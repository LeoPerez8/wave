# Wave v1 — Spec

Status: draft for review · Owner: @LeoPerez8

## Problem Statement

I keep my music as local files on my Android phone (Samsung Galaxy S24+). BlackPlayer has the features I want from a local player — folder-based library, gapless playback, lyrics, sleep timer, an equalizer and a tag editor — but its look and interaction model feel dated next to Apple Music, whose structure, gestures and "Liquid Glass" materials I much prefer. Apple Music, on the other hand, does not exist as a local-file player on Android. No app gives me both.

## Solution

**Wave** is a personal, Android-only local music player with a BlackPlayer-grade local-file engine and an interface of its own. Apple Music (iOS 26 era) is the reference for how it should feel, not a template to copy. It indexes the music folders I choose, plays them gaplessly through a native audio engine with full system integration (notification, lock screen, Bluetooth), and records every play so that rich listening statistics can be built later.

v1 delivers the core player and library. v2 adds the advanced audio and library-management features (see Out of Scope).

## Glossary

- **Track** — one song in the library: an audio file on the phone, or, while signed in to a Wave account, a missing song.
- **Wave account** — an optional sign-in (with Google) that keeps the Library's song list, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences on Wave's sync service, so another phone signed in to it gets them back. It never holds audio. _Avoid_: YouTube account (that's YT Music's sign-in), cloud library.
- **Recording key** — what makes two songs on different phones the same song for a Wave account: the album's identity (album title + album artist), the normalised title, and the disc and track number when the file has them. Length within 3 seconds only breaks a tie. Core's `recordingKey` decides.
- **Missing song** — a Track the Wave account has but this phone has no file for. It shows in the Library, dimmed, and tapping it downloads it. _Avoid_: ghost track, remote track.
- **Unsearched entry** — a playlist entry for a song a Spotify import listed and nothing has searched on YouTube Music yet. It keeps what Spotify listed (title, artists, album, length, Spotify's cover), as an unavailable entry does, and is stored the same way: no new column, no video id, marked unsearched. It looks, plays, queues and joins playlists as a YT Music entry does, and Wave searches it when it's about to play, or in the background while Wave runs (the background fill). Found, it becomes a YT Music entry in its place; not found, an unavailable entry; a search that fails leaves it unsearched. _Avoid_: pending song, placeholder.
- **Unavailable entry** — a playlist entry for a song a playlist import couldn't find: no match on YouTube Music or in the Library, or a Spotify local file with no artist, which is never searched. It keeps its place and what the source listed (title, artists, album, length, and Spotify's cover when there is one), shows dimmed with why ("Not found on YouTube Music", "Local file on Spotify"), never plays, and becomes the Library's song once the Library has it. _Avoid_: missing song (that's a Track with no file on this phone), not-found song.
- **Album** — tracks grouped by album title + album artist.
- **Credit** — a track's artist tag as written ("Ariana Grande, Doja Cat"); rows show it as is. A tag with several values (Vorbis comments, ID3v2.4) gives one credit per value, shown joined with commas; separate values always name separate artists.
- **Artist** — one artist a credit names. A track belongs to every artist its credit names, so the Artists list, Search, Android Auto and artist photos list individual artists, and a collaboration counts for, and shows on the page of, each of them. "feat." / "ft." / "featuring" (bracketed or not) always separate artists. `,` `·` `;` `/` "with" " x " "&" and "and" separate them too, unless that part of the credit is an artist in the Library on its own: some track's whole credit (or the part of it before a feat.) is exactly that name and none of its names is credited alone anywhere (alone, or featuring someone). So "Earth, Wind & Fire", "AC/DC", "Tyler, The Creator" and "Simon & Garfunkel" stay whole, and "Ariana Grande, Doja Cat" splits once "Ariana Grande" has a song of her own. A collaboration whose artists have no other song stays one artist until one of them does. Names are trimmed, and a name credited twice counts once (case and spacing aside). Core's `creditSplitter` decides; the split is recomputed when a scan or a deletion changes the Library's credits. Listening stats follow the credit too: a counted play of a collaboration counts in full for each artist it names (Stats' artist rankings, Home's Most played artists, the artist page's Stats block), while album rankings and Library-wide totals count it once. The artist page's "X of N songs" counts both over the songs whose credit names the artist (not songs that are only on an album filed under them). A deleted song's plays keep counting for every artist it named while that artist is in the Library.
- **Primary artist** — the first artist a credit names.
- **Go to Artist** — on a song or album credited to one artist, Go to Artist (the long-press menu, the selection bar) and the artist lines of Now Playing and an album's page open that artist. Credited to several, they open the artist choice card, listing each credited artist in credit order; picking one opens their page. The artist page you're on is left out, and a choice of one opens straight away. In selection, Go to Artist shows only when every checked item has the same set of artists. Core's `itemActions` and `artistLink` decide. A streamed song (a YT Music song, not downloaded) links to its artists too while YT Music is on, Now Playing's artist line and Go to Artist in the YT Music song's menu, and opens them integrated, with no Show on YouTube Music tap: a name the Library has (compared as artist photos compare names) opens their Library page, any other the YouTube Music artist page. Credited to several, the artist choice card lists them in credit order, the YouTube Music ones with "YouTube Music" under the name. The artist page you're on is left out here too, a YouTube Music artist page included (by the name it was opened with); when it's the song's only artist, the menu has no Go to Artist and Now Playing's artist line just closes Now Playing to reveal it, as for a Library song. With YT Music off the link is gone. Core's `streamedArtistLink` decides. Checked beside other songs or albums, a streamed song is by the artists its link resolves to (a name the Library has is that Library artist), so Go to Artist shows when every checked item shares them and leads where the first streamed song's link does.
- **Album artist** — the artist an album is filed under: the first artist its album artist credit names (the credit falls back to the track's; "Various Artists" for compilations). The album shows under that artist's Albums and under the other credited artists' Appears on.
- **Genre** — an alternative grouping of tracks.
- **Folder** — a directory on the phone. The index records the folder each track is in, and scan roots and excluded folders are folders, but the Library doesn't browse by folder.
- **Playlist** — a user-made, ordered list of tracks; with YT Music, it can also hold YT Music songs the library doesn't have yet (YT Music entries), streamed until the library has them.
- **Scan root** — a folder Wave indexes; **Excluded folder** — a folder under a root that Wave ignores.
- **Library scan** — walking the scan roots and reconciling files with the index (full on first run, incremental afterwards).
- **Play context** — what a playback was started from (album, artist, playlist, genre, search, all songs, favorites, home section). Plays recorded before the Library lost its Folders entry may say folder; they load and count like any other.
- **Queue** — the ordered list of tracks to be played, including the current one and **Up Next**.
- **Play event** — one record per time a track starts playing, with how long it was listened to and how it ended.
- **Counted play** — a play event that counts towards play counts: listened to for more than 15 seconds, or, for a song of 15 seconds or less, played to the end. Each play event counts at most once, however long it goes on.
- **Sidecar** — a `.lrc` lyrics file beside an audio file (`Song.lrc` or `Song.mp3.lrc`).
- **Favorite** — a starred track (Apple Music's star).
- **Artist image** — the circle an artist is shown with: their **artist photo** (downloaded online, see Artist photos) when there is one, else the cover of the album that represents them, else their initial.
- **Now Playing** — the full-screen player; **Mini player** — the floating glass pill above the tab bar; **Toast** — a line on a small glass pill above the mini player that goes away on its own, or on a tap or a swipe.
- **YT Music song** — a song found on YouTube Music rather than in the Library. It is identified by its YouTube video id (`yt:<videoId>` wherever a track id is expected, so it can't collide with a Library id), can be streamed or queued, and becomes a Track only once it's downloaded into the Library. A YT Music song the Library already has (see YT Music) is never offered as one. _Avoid_: online track, remote track, stream.

## User Stories

### First run and library

1. As a user, I want Wave to ask for all-files access on first launch, so that it can read every audio file in my music folders.
2. As a user, I want Wave to index my `Music` folder by default and let me choose the folders my music is actually in with the system folder picker, so that I get a working library whatever my folders are called.
3. As a user, I want to add and remove scan roots and exclude sub-folders in Settings, so that only the music I care about appears.
4. As a user, I want to see scan progress (files found / indexed) during the first scan, so that I know the app is working on a large library.
5. As a user, I want to start browsing while the first scan is still running, so that I don't wait for tens of thousands of files.
6. As a user, I want Wave to pick up new, changed and deleted files automatically each time I open it, so that my library stays current without a manual full rescan.
7. As a user, I want to pull to refresh in the Library, so that I can force a rescan after copying new music.
8. As a user, I want MP3, FLAC, M4A/AAC/ALAC, OGG/Opus and WAV files to be supported, so that my whole collection plays.
9. As a user, I want Wave to read title, artist, album, album artist, genre, year, track and disc number, duration, embedded artwork and embedded lyrics from tags, so that my library is organised the way I tagged it.
10. As a user, I want files without tags to still appear (using the file name as title and "Unknown Artist/Album"), so that nothing goes missing.
11. As a user, I want a renamed or moved file to keep its play history and favorite status, so that my statistics survive reorganising folders.

### Browsing (Library tab)

12. As a user, I want the Library tab to list Playlists, Artists, Albums, Songs, Genres and Favorites as entry rows like Apple Music, so that I can browse the way I think.

17. As a user, I want genre screens listing their tracks, so that I can browse by genre.
18. As a user, I want long lists (tens of thousands of songs) to scroll smoothly at 120 Hz, so that the app feels native on my phone.
19. As a user, I want a fast-scroll alphabet index on long lists, so that I can jump to a letter.
20. As a user, I want long-press context menus on tracks, albums, artists and playlists (Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Favorite, Go to Album/Artist), so that I can act without opening screens. ### Playback

21. As a user, I want tapping a track to play it and queue the rest of its context after it, so that playback continues naturally.
22. As a user, I want gapless playback between consecutive tracks, so that live albums and mixes play without silence.
    22a. As a user, I want songs to crossfade into each other when I choose to, so that a playlist flows without a hard cut. Settings › Playback › Crossfade sets the overlap, 0 to 15 s in 0.5 s steps; 0 (Off, the default) keeps gapless playback exactly as before. With it on, every song that ends on its own overlaps the next one for that long (at most half the song), the outgoing one fading out as the next fades in, songs of one album and streamed YT Music songs alike. Skip, previous and picking a song switch at once, without a fade, as do repeat one and the last song. As the overlap starts, Now Playing, the notification, the lock screen and Android Auto move to the next song; pausing pauses both. The outgoing song's play ends when its fade does. The sleep timer's end of current track stops after the song, with nothing of the next one. Core's `crossfadeDuration` turns the setting into the overlap; the playback service decides which changes fade. The setting syncs with the Wave account.

24. As a user, I want "previous" to restart the current track if more than 3 seconds have played, so that it behaves like Apple Music.

    25a. As a user, I want to choose between two kinds of shuffle by long-pressing any shuffle button: Real Shuffle, where every order is as likely, and Smart Shuffle, which mixes the songs I play most with the ones I play less, so that a shuffle can lean on what I like without repeating it in a row. Real Shuffle is the default. Smart Shuffle ranks the shuffled songs by all-time counted plays; the top half of the played ones (most played) are spread evenly among the rest (less played, never-played songs included), at random within each side, so neither side bunches up. The choice is saved (and syncs with the Wave account), and every shuffle uses it: Now Playing's button, the Shuffle buttons on album, playlist, artist, genre and song pages, YT Music album, artist and playlist pages, Discover mix pages, and Android Auto. Picking a kind on the card turns shuffle on and shuffles Up Next that way. Every shuffle button reads Smart Shuffle, with a single spark replacing one shuffle arrowhead in the same line style as the Tabler icons, while Smart Shuffle is chosen. Each button's accessibility hint explains the long press. A tap keeps its usual shuffle action. Core's queue (`reduceQueue` with a `Shuffle`) decides.

## Implementation Decisions

### Platform and distribution

- Android only. Minimum Android 12 (API 31, required for native blur), target the latest SDK. Tuned for the Galaxy S24+ (120 Hz).
- App name **Wave**, application id `dev.leoperez.wave` (permanent).
- Personal use, sideloaded APK. No Play Store, so `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` (all-files access) is acceptable.
- The Library works offline; the online features are listed under Network. Wave's one backend is the sync service behind the optional Wave account (see Wave account and sync), and nothing else in Wave needs it.

### Network

Wave uses the network for three features, each behind its own Settings switch (on by default) that stops every request it makes, and for the Wave account, whose switch is signing in.

- **Wave account** (Wave's sync service, see Wave account and sync): while signed in, sends the Library's song list (each song's tags and, for a downloaded one, its YouTube video id, never the file or its path), playlists, favorites, play history and preferences, and the Google ID token once at sign-in. Signed out, Wave sends it nothing, and the Library, play history and stats never leave the phone.

- **Artist photos** (Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends artist names.
- **Lyrics and genre** (LRCLIB and Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends a song's title, artist, album and duration.
- **YT Music** (YouTube Music, below): `music.youtube.com/youtubei/v1/*` for search (and, as later tickets land, browsing, song details and the player), `www.youtube.com` for the player script, visitor data and BotGuard's proof-of-origin endpoints (`api/jnn/v1/*`), `*.googlevideo.com` for audio, `*.googleusercontent.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com` and `i.ytimg.com` for covers, `accounts.google.com` for sign-in. It sends the query in Search when Search YouTube Music is tapped (and the refined query after it, until the field is cleared) or when the Library has nothing for it, the name of an artist whose page (or whose album's page) asks for its YT Music section (only when Show on YouTube Music is tapped), that artist's YouTube browse id and the id of their songs playlist (the "See all" on their page's Songs shelf) and, for an album page once that artist's answer is saved, the album's browse id, the browse id of a YouTube Music artist whose page is opened (an artist the Library doesn't have) and the id of their songs playlist, the id of a playlist imported from a link (see "Importing playlists from a link"), the video ids of songs it plays or downloads, YouTube's visitor data and, once signed in, the account's session cookie. It never sends the Library's contents, play history or stats. Offline, or when YouTube fails, the YT Music parts show a quiet line and the Library works as before.
  - Importing a Spotify playlist sits under the same switch, since its songs become YT Music entries. It sends Spotify the playlist id (`open.spotify.com/embed/playlist/<id>`, then `api-partner.spotify.com/pathfinder/v2/query` with the playlist's URI, the page asked for and the anonymous token the embed page gave) and, for a shared short link, its code (`spotify.link`, `spotify.app.link`). The card shows the cover from `i.scdn.co` or `image-cdn-ak.spotifycdn.com`, and so do the playlist's unsearched and unavailable entries. Import itself sends YouTube Music nothing. A song the Library doesn't have is searched when it's needed (about to play): one YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists. Nothing about the Library, play history or stats goes to either (see "Importing playlists from a link").

### Artist photos

- Wave downloads a photo of each artist from **Deezer's open API** (no account or key): it searches the artist's name and takes the best match's picture. Artist names are the only thing sent. A "Download artist photos" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no requests and artists show their album artwork. "Clear artist photos" deletes the downloaded ones.
- **Matching** is Core's (`artistPhoto.ts`): names compare with case, accents, a leading "The", punctuation and "&"/"and" ignored; only an exact match counts, and of namesakes the most followed one (its photo or none). "Various Artists", "Unknown Artist" and names without letters are never looked up.
- **Caching.** Lookups run in the background after each library scan, three at a time, and stop after a few failures in a row (offline). Photos are saved beside the album artwork (`files/artwork/artists/`) as a thumbnail (lists, menus) and a large image (artist page), and are never fetched again. A lookup with no match is retried after a week, a failed one after a day (`artist_photos` table, by artist key).
- **Fallback.** Until a photo arrives, without a match, offline or with downloads off, the artist's album-artwork image shows.

### Lyrics and genre

- **Fix Song** (a song's long-press menu; **Fix Songs** in the selection bar with more than one song checked) looks up online what a song lacks of its lyrics, genre, track number (with the disc) and year, and writes only that into the file. It never replaces a value the song has, so a wrong match can't retag a song tagged by hand. It shows only on a song missing at least one of the four (Core's `canFixSong`); in the selection bar it shows when at least one checked song is, and works on those (Core's `songsToFix`). Settings › Lyrics and tags runs it on the whole Library in the background ("Fix songs in the whole Library"), with progress and Cancel. Every run ends with a summary: fixed, not found, failed, and how many songs were skipped as having everything. Wave says it on a toast, a line such as "Fixed 3 songs · 1 not found" (Core's `tagLookupsToast`), never a card to close. It stops early when the services can't be reached (three lookups failing in a row). This replaced Find Lyrics and Genre, which always replaced a song's lyrics and genre.
- **Only what's missing is fetched** (Core's `fixLookups` and `deezerFollowUps`), from a menu, the selection bar, the whole-Library run and the fill-in after a download alike: LRCLIB is searched only for a song without lyrics; Deezer is searched only for a song missing a genre, a track number or a year. After Deezer's search, the album is fetched only for a missing genre, or a missing year when the match is on the song's own album, and the tracklist only for a missing track number on the song's own album. A request whose answer couldn't fill anything isn't made.
- **The whole-Library run goes on in the background**, the way YT Music downloads do: a foreground service (`LibraryLookupService`, data sync) keeps Wave running with it in the background and the screen off, also while music plays, and shows a "Fix Songs" notification with the progress ("Fixing songs… N of M") and Cancel. The run itself is JavaScript; while it goes on, Wave's JS runtime is held (`JsRuntime`, as for downloads and Android Auto) along with a partial wake lock, so JS timers and lookups keep going without the Activity. Settings shows the same progress and Cancel, also when Wave is reopened while the run still goes on. Cancel in either place, or turning "Fix songs online" off, stops it; songs already done keep what was written. When it ends, the notification shows the summary in full, and Wave shows it too, on the same toast, at once when it's on screen, otherwise the next time it is (the summary is kept until it's seen; once the toast has gone, so does the notification). Android 13+ asks for notification permission at the first run; without it the run still goes on, with the notification hidden. Android 15 caps data-sync services at six hours a day; past that the notification goes and the run carries on while Wave stays alive.
- **Sources.** Lyrics come from **LRCLIB** (`lrclib.net`, free, no key): synced (LRC) lyrics are preferred, plain lyrics are taken when that's all there is. The genre is that of the song's album on **Deezer's open API**: a track search, then the matched album's first genre. The same album gives the year (of its release date), and its tracklist gives the song's track number, plus the disc for an album on more than one disc. Albums and tracklists are fetched once per album in a session, and a whole-Library run spends at least 400 ms on each song, which keeps its three Deezer requests at most (search, album, tracklist) under Deezer's limit of 50 every 5 seconds. A song's **title, artist, album and duration** are the only things sent, and "Unknown Artist" / "Various Artists" songs are never looked up. A "Fix songs online" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no lookups and Fix Song is gone from menus and the selection bar.
- **Matching** is Core's (`lyricsAndGenre.ts`): titles and artists compare like artist photos (case, accents, punctuation, a leading "The" ignored), plus featured artists ("feat. X") and version notes in brackets or after a dash ("(Live)", "- Remastered 2011"). Lyrics must be within 2 seconds of the song's length; then synced beats plain, the song's own album beats another, and the closest length wins. The genre's album is the matched track's, preferring the song's own album, then the closest length. The year and track number are taken only when that album is the song's own (titles compared as above), since a compilation or live record numbers and dates the song differently; a song without an album tag gets neither. A release date that's missing or malformed ("0000-00-00") gives no year. No match leaves that field alone; no match at all leaves the file untouched.
- **Writing** is native (`SongTagWriter`, jaudiotagger): only the lyrics, genre, track number, disc number and year fields change, and the audio and every other tag stay as they were (JVM tests check this for MP3, FLAC, M4A, Ogg Vorbis and WAV). Core decides what is sent to it (`missingTags`: only the fields the indexed song lacks, the disc going with the track number), and the writer checks the file too: a track number or year the file already has is kept, and the disc number is only written with the track number. A WAV file's lyrics and disc number go into an ID3 chunk (RIFF INFO has no field for them) and its genre, track number and year into both. A format Wave can't write tags to (Opus, raw AAC) gets the lyrics in a sidecar `.lrc` and no genre, track number or year, and the summary lists those songs. A track number or year it can't take isn't a failure on its own; a song whose track number and year were all that was written counts as fixed. A sidecar already beside a file is replaced too, since it would win over the tags in the Lyrics view.
- **Skipping.** Every run looks up only songs missing lyrics, a genre, a track number or a year (Core's `songsToFix`); songs with all four are skipped and counted. Run the whole-Library one again after adding music and it does the new and incomplete songs, plus any it found nothing for last time. Progress ("N of M") counts only those songs, and the summary says how many it skipped. Lyrics count whether they're in the tags or in a sidecar `.lrc`. The index records this per song (`tracks.has_lyrics`): scans set it, listing each folder's `.lrc` files once, and so do Fix Song's writes. Wave doesn't notice another app adding or deleting a sidecar until the song's own file changes. The update that added the column marks every indexed file as changed, so the next scan reads them all once.
- **After writing** the file is read again as a scan reads it and its index entry updated, so Genres, Search, the album page (track order, and the year when the album had none) and an open Lyrics view show the new values without a rescan.
- **Downloads.** The fill-in after a download is Fix Song on the new file: it looks up and writes only what the file lacks. A YT Music download's tags take the track number and year from its album page, and Deezer's stand in when the page doesn't give them.

### Lyrics on Android Auto

- **The Lyrics toggle** sits in the overflow menu of Auto's player screen ("Lyrics on" / "Lyrics off"), off on a fresh install and remembered between drives.
- **Lyrics are drawn into the artwork** (the owner's pick in #150). Auto draws the player screen itself, and Wave controls only the title, the artist and the artwork image there, so with the toggle on and synced lyrics Wave hands Auto a square image of three lines over the cover dimmed: the line being sung big and bold in the middle, the lines before and after it smaller and dimmed. The split-screen card shows it in its top area, and the full-screen player beside the title. The title and artist stay as they are, and Auto's progress bar and buttons are untouched. A sung line too wide for the image shrinks, then wraps onto two rows, then ends in "…"; lines sung together share one line (`Me / You`). The rows stay in the middle band of the square, in case Auto crops it to fit the card.
- **What shows when** is Core's call (`autoLyricLines`): which three lines, and when the next change is due. The neighbours skip pauses, so the line after a pause already shows below. The cover is back with the toggle off, with Auto disconnected, for a track with only plain lyrics or none (a streamed YT Music song has none), before the first line and in a pause between lines (an empty LRC line).
- **Timing.** JS follows the player and wakes at each line's start (`startAutoLyricLines`), sending the lines only when they change, so Auto gets one new image per line, as it got one new text line before. The playback service hands the session a new artwork URI per set of lines (`content://<package>.artwork/lyrics/<id>`, `LyricArtworks`); Wave's artwork provider draws the image (`LyricArtPainter`, 800 px JPEG) the first time Auto or the notification reads it, and keeps the latest four.
- **The phone** shares the artwork with Auto: while Auto is connected with the toggle on, the notification and lock screen show the lyrics image as the cover. Once Auto disconnects (or the toggle goes off) the service drops the lines at once, without waiting for JS, and they get the cover back. The artist line is never touched.

### YT Music

- YouTube Music has no official API. Wave talks to it the way Metrolist does: through **innertubex** (MetrolistGroup/innertubex, from JitPack), from the native module. innertubex sends requests and handles sessions, ciphers and stream extraction; reading the answers into songs is Wave's (`YtSearchParser`, after Metrolist's page parsers). Metrolist is the reference for anything the library leaves to the app.
- **Licence.** innertubex is GPL-3.0, and so is any Metrolist code Wave ports, so the APK that ships them is a GPL-3.0 combined work.
- **Wave is its own client, not YouTube's app.** Nothing YouTube serves as an ad is ever requested, shown or played (Wave plays the audio stream itself), there is no Premium upsell or nag, and nothing is limited because the account isn't Premium; the only difference is the audio bitrate YouTube's servers send. Wave doesn't report plays to YouTube.
- **Search.** When Search asks YouTube Music, it sends the query and nothing else, as three searches at once: "Songs", "Artists" and "Albums" (which lists singles and EPs too). What they find that the Library doesn't have joins the Library's results in the Songs, Artists and Albums groups, after the Library's rows (making the group when the Library has nothing of its kind), and the groups' counts, previews and Show all cover both. A song shows its artwork, title, artists · album and length. An artist shows their picture and name over "YouTube Music", and a tap opens the YT Music artist page. An album shows its cover, title and artists; a tap opens the YT Music album page, and a long-press opens the YT Music album menu with Download Album. An artist the Library has (named as artist photos compare names, Core's `libraryArtistFinder`) isn't repeated, nor is an album that one of the artists it credits has in the Library under its title (matched as the artist page matches releases, Core's `libraryReleaseFinder`): the Library's row already shows. The top result is the Library's best match when there is one. Otherwise it's YouTube Music's best: the result shown that matches the query best as Search ranks the Library's, or, when none matches word for word, YouTube Music's first song (then album, then artist). While Search shows YouTube Music's results it's an integrated view, so a Library album opened from it opens integrated. While YT Music's songs are among the results, the Library's show a small downloaded icon before their artist. Tapping any song plays the song results from it, the Library's files and YT Music's streamed. Typing searches the Library only: while it has results, a **Search YouTube Music** text button follows them, and nothing is asked until it's tapped. A tap asks for the current query at once; from then on the results stay merged while the query is refined, each new query asked once typing pauses (300 ms), until the field is cleared (or holds a single character), when the button is back. A query the Library has nothing for asks on its own, once typing pauses; once the Library has results again, that view ends and the button returns. Asking never blocks typing or the Library's results, keeps the last results up while the next query loads (with no YouTube Music top result until the new answer arrives, since they answer the last query) and drops answers to queries no longer typed. Under the results one quiet line says it's searching, that YouTube Music has nothing for the query or nothing the Library lacks, or that it's offline or YouTube failed; if any of the three searches fails, the line says so and none of their results show (`ytSectionView`). With nothing in the Library matching and nothing from YouTube Music, a "nothing in your library" line shows. With YT Music off there is no button and nothing is asked. When Search asks, and who asked, is Core's (`stepYtSection`).
- **Search allowance.** Every YouTube Music search Wave asks on its own shares one allowance: at most 1,000 in any 15 minutes, retries included (`SEARCH_ALLOWANCE`, `SEARCH_WINDOW_MS`; YouTube Music starts refusing near 2,300, see "Importing playlists from a link", Rate limits). That is the Songs search for an unsearched song about to play, a download's search for the song's audio track, a missing song's search for its recording, an unsearched song's download search for its video, Try Again's searches for a playlist's unavailable entries, the background fill's Songs search for an unsearched entry (see "Importing playlists from a link", Background fill), and a Discover mix's search for its seed (a Songs search for a song, an Artists search for an artist without a saved page, the same for a Library artist whose similar artists are read). A search typed in Search stays outside it. Each search waits its turn (`searchTurn` in `src/ytmusic/searchAllowance.ts`), and Core decides who goes next (`searchTurns`), by priority: playing (the unsearched songs among the playing one and the next 3, see Unsearched entries), then downloads and Try Again, then Discover mixes, then the background fill, which also takes none while the window holds 800, so 200 stay free for the others; within a priority, in the order they asked. A lower priority never takes a search a higher one is waiting for. A search given up while it waits spends nothing. The searches asked are kept on the phone (the key-value store, "spotifySearches", the name from when only imports kept it: when each of the last 1,000 went), so a restart doesn't start a fresh allowance; those waiting go with the process. A download waiting for its turn keeps its prepare decision: JS renews the journal's lease every 20 seconds while it works on a decision (`renewDecision`), up to the job's 20 minutes. The app log has a line when a wait starts and one when it ends, and one for each failed search (see App log).
- **Already in the Library.** A YT Music song is left out when a Library song has its title (case, accents, punctuation and a "feat." credit aside), shares an artist with it (credits split as the Library splits them, Core's `splitCredit`) and, when both lengths are known, is as long within 3 seconds, so a live take or an edit still shows (Core's `libraryMatcher`).
- **Streaming.** Tapping a YT Music result plays the section's songs from it; its long-press menu has Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Go to Album (when it has a YT Music album, opening that album's YT Music page; not on that page itself, and not while YT Music is off) and Go to Artist (and Download, below), so YT Music songs sit in the queue beside Library tracks (as `yt:<videoId>` track ids). Wherever playback shows (mini player, Now Playing with its tint, notification, lock screen, Bluetooth, Auto) a streamed song behaves like a Library track, except that it has no star, album link or lyrics; its artist line opens its artists integrated (Go to Artist, above). Its artists, as YouTube Music credits them, are kept with its details for that. The playback service streams a `yt:` item when the player loads it (`YtDataSource`), so the address is fresh at play time, mid-queue and after a cold start: `YtStreamResolver` asks innertubex's extractor for the best audio YouTube serves the account (a plain address: no HLS, SABR or ranged-only clients), with the proof-of-origin token YouTube's web client needs, minted by BotGuard in a hidden web view as Metrolist does (`YtPoTokens`), and visitor data kept across launches. Addresses are reused until ten minutes before they expire; one googlevideo rejects (403/410) is resolved again, twice at most, skipping the client it came from. The cover is cached beside the Library's artwork, so the notification, Auto and Now Playing's tint work as for a Library track. Playback and the cache take it at the size downloads embed (Core's `streamedCoverUrls`): a YouTube Music cover is asked for at 1200 px whatever size its row carried, and a video thumbnail (`i.ytimg.com`) as the video's largest (maxres, else sd, else hq, which every video has); lists keep their small thumbnails. Wave never reports plays to YouTube, and streamed plays aren't recorded (Core's `trackPlays`), so play counts and stats stay about the Library. A song that can't stream (offline, YT Music off, a song YouTube won't serve) pauses playback with a line saying why in place of the artist (Core's `playerErrorNotice`); skipping on plays the next song. The queue's YT Music songs' details are saved with it, so a cold start restores them, unless YT Music is off (Core's `restorableTrack`, `queuedYtTracks`).
- **Downloads.** The existing YT Music song menu keeps Download, Cancel Download and Retry Download, with compact row progress; a failed song offers Retry Download alone (Core's `ytDownloadActions`), and clearing it is Settings › Downloads' Clear All or the trash button on its row there. Each download has a source, which Core decides when it's asked for and again when it starts (`downloadSource`): Soulseek while Download in best quality is on, else YouTube Music while YT Music is on. With both off, the row fails with "Turn on YT Music or Download in best quality in Settings" and keeps Retry. **When Soulseek can't deliver the song**, the same job carries on from YouTube Music while YT Music is on, and the owner doesn't tap anything. That happens when Soulseek has no 320 kbps copy of the recording or every selected peer failed, when it rejects the account (or Download in best quality is turned off mid-download, as a new download would then come from YouTube Music), when its server can't be reached while the phone is online, and when the Soulseek attempt uses up its 20 minutes. Being offline, full storage, an unwritable folder, a failure while saving and Cancel don't fall back: YouTube would fail the same way, or the owner asked to stop. Core decides which failures fall back (`downloadFallback`). The prepare answer gives the journal Core's plan for the source's failures (`downloadFailurePlan`: which are tried again after a pause, which end the job, which fall back, and what the job fails with once nothing is left to try) and the M4A's place, so the journal switches the job's source where the failure happens, without waiting for JS and without a failure list of its own. The row keeps its place and shows the YouTube transfer's progress, and the song lands as a YouTube Music download does: the M4A in the Library's tree, tagged. The fallback gets 20 minutes of its own, and a job cut off by process death after falling back resumes from YouTube Music without going back to Soulseek. When YouTube Music fails as well, the row shows YouTube's failure with Retry, and Retry tries Soulseek first again. If YT Music is turned off before YouTube takes over, the row keeps Soulseek's failure. With YT Music off, Soulseek failures end the job as before; an unreachable server is retried like a dropped connection and then says "Download failed: Soulseek can't be reached". Whichever the source, there is one Download button, with no picker and no quality dialog. **From YouTube Music**, Wave saves the best M4A stream YouTube serves the account as it's served, never re-encoded: AAC at 256 kbps (itag 141) with Premium, about 128 kbps (itag 140) without. A Google sign-in isn't needed. It downloads the song's audio track: YT Music's album pages may list a song under its music video, whose audio runs longer or shorter than the album cut, so when YouTube Music's download starts (its prepare, or a Soulseek job's fallback, whose turn begins with a prepare of its own) Wave looks the song up in a Songs search and takes the result that is the same recording, by the rules imports use (Core's `ytAudioTrack`), else the song's own video. A Soulseek download that never falls back makes no Songs search. That search, and a missing song's search for its recording, wait their turn in the search allowance with downloads' priority (see Search allowance), so a large playlist's downloads keep within it. An audio-track search that fails still leaves the song's own video; either search's failure is logged. The Library Track keeps the video downloaded. The address comes from playback's resolver (`YtStreamResolver`, asking for M4A), and the audio arrives in 1 MiB ranges into the hidden part file, a request that breaks off going on from its last byte. An address googlevideo turns down (403/410) is resolved again, three times at most: the download carries on from where it was when the fresh address serves the same stream, and starts over when it serves another (`YtAudioDownload`). The file is published only once it is the length YouTube announced and, read through to its last sample, plays as long as the song, within 2 s or 2% (Core's `ytAudioMatches`). Each check writes a line to the app log (Core's `ytDownloadReport`): the video asked for and the one YouTube served, the player client, the video type (ATV, OMV, UGC), YouTube's announced length, the song's length and where it came from, and how long the file plays. Otherwise, or when YouTube won't serve the song, the row fails with "This song is unavailable on YouTube Music" and keeps Retry. A transfer that breaks off is started again after a pause that grows each time (it downloads the whole song again), up to six attempts in all, then fails with "Download failed"; offline it fails with "Download failed: offline". **From Soulseek**, with Download in best quality on (see "The Soulseek account"), Wave automatically selects the same recording and at most three suitable peers. Core combines exact normalized title, the main artist, complete edition evidence and duration; a faster live version, remix, edit or different remaster cannot replace the requested recording. Only advertised original MP3 320 candidates are eligible in the proven native path; there is no lossless encoder or lower-bitrate fallback. Native code checks every MPEG frame, completeness, bitrate and actual decoded duration. Contradictory received title, artist or edition tags reject the peer before trusted metadata overwrites them. With YT Music off, failure says "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and leaves Retry available. Either way, the destination is the Library's own tree, `<album artist>/<album>/<NN> <title>.mp3` from Soulseek or `.m4a` from YouTube Music, directly under an included root (`<title>.mp3` or `<title>.m4a` without a track number; names made safe for the storage), so a song whose album folder the Library already has lands next to its songs. Core's `ytDownloadTarget` names it, the same function the PC job that organizes the music folder uses. The root and folder are checked against current exclusions. Existing files are never replaced: a taken name gets the next free one ("Name (2).m4a"), and so does a name another file takes between choosing it and moving the download there. A job whose saved file is gone when it's imported again, with another file at its name, fails with "Download failed" and never touches that file; Retry downloads the song again and saves it under a free name. The existing tag writer keeps supplied title, artists, album, album artist, year, track/disc numbers and artwork; optional genre/lyrics metadata failures never reject usable audio. YouTube's M4A is fragmented MP4 (an empty sample table in the `moov`, the audio in `moof`/`mdat` fragments), which jaudiotagger can't grow, so the tags go into its `moov` directly and the fragments' absolute offsets move with it (`FragmentedMp4`). A download fails with "Download failed: couldn't save the file" only for full storage or a folder Wave can't make or write to; anything else that goes wrong while saving or importing (a file the tagger or scanner can't read, a Library error, an unexpected error in JS's answer) fails with its own reason, keeping any retry or YouTube Music fallback its plan has. Each failure keeps its cause (the exception's class and message, `cause` on the journal's job) in the journal and in Logcat (`WaveDownloads`); Retry clears it. The existing scanner and LibraryStore immediately import the complete file into Library, Search and Android Auto and refresh YT Music Library matching. Repeated taps and positively matched result ids share a job. Neither source needs a Google session or a paid account, and downloads never share or upload the Library. No PC, server, daemon or separate downloader app is involved. Up to three songs download at once, and the next starts as one of them finishes or waits (a peer's queue, say), so one song never holds up the rest; they still join the Library one at a time. A native journal owns the queue (see Native shape), so downloads keep going with the screen off and while music plays, and survive Android recreating Wave's process: the request, its source, the chosen recording, candidate peers, current peer, the policy (MP3 320, or YouTube's M4A) and deadlines are persisted; a transfer cut off by process death restarts from zero with the same peer and what was left of its time, or from YouTube's stream with a fresh window to start in, within the job's own remaining time. Rows say "Waiting to download", "Finding this recording", "Connecting", "Downloading · NN%", "Checking the download" and "Saving to the Library"; a YouTube Music download waits instead of finding a recording or connecting. The notification looks the same whichever the source. Internally, for Soulseek, a dropped connection reconnects the saved account and retries (searching three times, transferring up to six attempts), a peer that keeps Wave in its queue over 2 minutes, doesn't start within 3 or goes quiet for 1 is replaced by the next selected peer, and each job has 20 minutes from leaving the queue; when peers run out the job falls back to YouTube Music, or with YT Music off the row fails with "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and Retry, which searches again. Network loss, full storage, an unwritable folder, a rejected account or audio that fails validation never touch existing files and never publish partial audio. Cancel Download in the menu or Cancel in the notification deletes the hidden part files; once it is saving to the Library it can't be cancelled. Repeated completion is idempotent (upsert by path), so no duplicate Tracks.
- **Album downloads.** A YT Music album (not a Library album) can be downloaded in one go: **Download Album** in a YT Music album tile's long-press menu (after Play Next and Add to Queue for the album's songs; the album's page is read first, once a session, so the card opens with all its entries; offline or without an answer, a message says so instead), and a **Download** button beside Play and Shuffle on the album's page. Every song of the album the Library doesn't already have (`libraryFinder`, as Search decides) joins the download queue in album order; a song already queued or downloading keeps its job, and one that is positively the same recording as a job on its way (or as an earlier song of the same album) shares it. Each song then goes through the single-song pipeline above, unchanged: the same source (Soulseek's MP3 320, or YouTube Music's M4A with Download in best quality off), the same fallback song by song when Soulseek can't deliver one, the same matching and tags, `<album artist>/<album>/`. The album page is read once a session and shared by the screen, its menu and every song's tags, so the songs land as one album with the album's title, album artist, year, track numbers and cover. While any of the album's songs are queued or downloading, the menu offers **Cancel Album Download** (the album's songs leave their jobs that aren't saving yet; a job shared with a song asked for elsewhere goes on for it) and the page head says "Downloading · N of M" (M: the album's songs downloaded or asked for this session, N: those saved). Each song row keeps its own progress line. Once nothing is on its way, songs that failed keep their own Retry Download and the album offers **Retry Failed** (a song that fell back to YouTube Music has failed only once both sources have, so Retry Failed asks again only for those); with every song in the Library, Download Album is gone. The page shows one button, the album's first entry ("Download", "Retry Failed" or "Cancel"). Play, Shuffle and the button stay on one line: when the button doesn't fit beside Play and Shuffle with its label (a narrow screen, large system text, Smart Shuffle's longer label), it drops the label and shows its teal icon alone at the end of the line, still a 48 dp target and still named in full for TalkBack ("Download Album", "Retry Failed", "Cancel Album Download"). Every label the button can say is measured ahead, unseen, so a change of state shows the right form on its first frame (Core's `lastListActionForm`). The download notification counts the songs downloading and the songs left ("3 downloading · 9 left") while there is more than one. What the album offers and queues is Core's (`ytAlbumDownload`, `ytAlbumDownloadButton`), built from the album's songs, the Library matches and the journal's snapshot. The decision itself takes any list of songs (`ytSongsDownload`), so playlists share it.
- **Playlist downloads.** A playlist with YT Music entries can download them in one go, as an album does: a **Download** button beside Play and Shuffle on the playlist's page, and **Download Playlist** in its long-press menu (after Add to Playlist; the playlist's entries are read first, so the card opens with all its entries). Every YT Music entry the Library doesn't already have joins the download queue in playlist order; the Library's songs are skipped, a song already queued or downloading keeps its job, and one that is positively the same recording as a job on its way (or as an earlier entry) shares it. Each goes through the single-song pipeline unchanged (the same source, the same matching and tags, `<album artist>/<album>/`, the album page read from the entry's album browse id when it has one). As each song lands, the store turns its entry, in every playlist that holds it, into the Library's song in the same place (see Playlists). While any entry is queued or downloading, the button is **Cancel** and the menu offers **Cancel Playlist Download**, and the page head says "Downloading · N of M" (M: the playlist's songs downloaded or asked for this session, N: those saved, including entries that have already become the Library's song: a Library entry whose file a download saved this session). Once nothing is on its way and some failed, the button and the menu offer **Retry Failed**; when every entry is the Library's, the button and the menu entries are gone. The button keeps to Play and Shuffle's line as an album's does; with no room for its label, its icon alone sits just before the playlist's actions button (⋯). Downloads need no Google session, and with Download in best quality on they don't need YT Music either: with YT Music off the button and menu entries stay and the songs come from Soulseek, tagged from what the playlist kept and Deezer, since the album page can't be read. With it off the songs come from YouTube Music while YT Music is on; with it on, each song Soulseek can't deliver falls back to YouTube Music on its own, as a song's download does, and Retry Failed asks again only for the songs both sources failed. With both off, each entry's row fails with "Turn on YT Music or Download in best quality in Settings", as a song's download does, and the playlist then offers Retry Failed. What the playlist offers and queues is Core's (`ytPlaylistDownload`, `ytPlaylistDownloadButton`), from its entries, the Library matches and the journal's snapshot.
  - **Unsearched entries download too**, so a Spotify playlist downloads whole right after its import, without anyone playing it first. Download Playlist, Retry Failed and the selection bar's Download take an unsearched entry as they take a missing song: it joins the queue in its place in playlist order, as a job named after what Spotify listed (`unsearchedSongJob`, the entry's `unsearchedTrackId`), and Core's rule for a song without a video picks its source (`downloadSource`). With Download in best quality on it comes from Soulseek, searched by its title and artists and tagged from what the entry kept (title, artists, album, length, Spotify's cover) and Deezer; a song Soulseek has downloads with no YouTube Music search, and stays unsearched until its file lands. When Soulseek can't deliver it, the job falls back to YouTube Music on its own, as a song's download does, and only then asks YouTube Music's search. With it off, YouTube Music's search is the source. With both off, its row fails with "Turn on YT Music or Download in best quality in Settings". The search is the Songs search playing asks (`unsearchedSearchQuery`, matched by `spotifyYtMatch`), with downloads' priority in the search allowance, and it settles the entry as playing's does (Core's `settleUnsearched`, the app's `settleUnsearchedSong`): found, the entry becomes that YT Music entry in its place in every playlist, and the job takes that song too, so its row keeps the job's progress and nothing asks for it twice; not found, the entry becomes unavailable ("Not found on YouTube Music") and its row fails; a failed search (offline, refused) leaves it unsearched and fails its row (Core's `unsearchedDownload`). The found result is a Songs search result, the song's audio track, so the download makes no second audio-track search: one YouTube Music search per song. Found, the file is tagged as a YT Music entry's download is, from what the entry kept and Deezer, with the album page YouTube Music files the video under. Once the file lands, the entry, found or still unsearched, becomes the Library's song in its place in every playlist that holds it (`playlistEntryLinks`). An unsearched entry's row shows its download as a YT Music entry's does, and "Downloading · N of M" counts it as any song asked for. An entry that became unavailable this way stays the playlist's download while its failed job is in the journal: the playlist offers Retry Failed, which asks again only for failed rows and searches each such song once more; found then, its file turns the entry into the Library's song. Cancel Playlist Download ends a found song's job too, not only its place on it.

- **Discover mixes.** Home's Discover mixes section (after Most played artists by default) shows up to six mixes of songs the owner doesn't know yet, made from YouTube Music's radios. It needs YT Music on but no sign-in. The seeds are the owner's all-time most played artists and songs, as Stats ranks them (Core's `discoverMixSeeds`): three artists and three songs, taking turns from the top artist. A seed song is never by one of the seed artists, the same recording seeds once, and names that aren't an artist's ("Various Artists") seed nothing. With too few of either, the other makes up the six. When plays make fewer than six seeds (a fresh install, a Library just filled), the Library fills the rest after them, under the same rules: its artists with the most songs (of its 20 with the most), then its newest songs, artists and songs taking turns, with as many artists as bring the seeds to three. An artist credited on a seed song doesn't seed too. If those artists' songs would leave too few songs to seed, fewer artists seed and more songs do. So mixes show with no plays at all, and the plays' seeds come first as the owner listens; with six seeds from plays, the Library adds none. When the Library's artists are too few for that, artists similar to the Library's come after its own: the ones YouTube Music lists on a Library artist's page ("Fans might also like"), less those the Library has. Core says whose page to read next (`discoverSimilarSource`): the owner's most played artists first, then the Library's with the most songs, one page at a time while the seeds have room for similar artists, three pages at most. The page is the one their artist page saved when it has the similar artists, else it's read, its artist found as an artist seed's is. The pages take turns giving their next artist, and an artist another page gave, or a name that isn't an artist's, seeds nothing. Similar artists take no songs from the rest, so when fewer of the Library's artists seed to make room for songs, the similar ones stay. So a Library of two artists and no plays gets its two artists and up to four similar ones. A similar artist's mix starts from the Start radio of their own page, and its tile reads "<artist> mix". A page that can't be read or lists no one leaves the other seeds as they are. The mixes span the Library's genres (Core's `discoverMixSeeds`): mix 1 is **varied**, the first of the seeds above; mix 2 is seeded from the most listened Library genre, mix 3 from the second, and so on. Genres are ranked by the all-time counted plays of their Library songs, then by how many songs they have (the ranking alone while nothing is played). A genre counts only with at least 5 Library songs, its name told apart without case. Genre comes from the Library's own tags (the file's, or what Fix Song wrote from Deezer); YouTube Music songs carry none. A genre's mix starts from its artist the seeds before don't have and no seed song credits, the one most played in the genre (then the one with most songs in it), or, with none left, from its most played (then newest) song by none of the seeds' artists, and then grows through that seed's radio as any mix does. When every genre has had a mix, they seed again in the same order with other seed artists, and when no genre has a seed left, the seeds above fill the six around the ones taken. With no genre of 5 songs, the mixes are seeded as above. Similar artists' pages are read while these seeds have room for them. An artist's radio starts from the Start radio button on their YouTube Music page. That page is the match their artist page saved, or the one an Artists search finds (`pickYtArtist`). A song's radio starts from the first result of a songs search for its title and artist that is the song, as the Library finds a YT Music song (`discoverSeedVideo`). A mix keeps the radio's songs in order and leaves out music videos, the seed song, songs the Library has (`libraryFinder`), songs the owner has played and songs an earlier mix has (`discoverMixSongs`). Played songs are found by the recording keys of every play recorded, so a song played and deleted since stays out too. Its title (a feat. credit and a remaster note aside) must match, and so must its album artist or, on a compilation, its album (`playedFinder`). A mix reads up to three pages of its radio, until it has 25 songs, and keeps 50 at most. A mix with fewer than ten songs isn't shown. Each tile is the first song's cover over "<seed> mix", its first three artists and its song count; a genre's mix reads "<genre> mix", its seed's lead artist first of the three. A saved mix keeps its genre and its seed's artists. It opens the mix's page (`/library/discover-mix/<id>`), the YT Music list page with Play, Shuffle and Download (every song, as an album downloads). Songs downloaded since play their files. The mixes are saved on the phone (not synced), so Home shows the last ones at once. They're built again when Home comes into focus with YT Music on and they weren't built that calendar day, and also once the first counted plays arrive if the last build had no seeds from plays, so they turn toward the owner's plays without waiting a day (`discoverMixesDue`; the saved mixes keep whether their build had play seeds, and mixes saved before Library seeds existed count as having them). A build that makes no usable mixes (offline, an empty answer, or too few songs left after filtering) keeps the saved mixes and is tried again ten minutes later at the earliest. An empty or unreadable saved set does not count as having built mixes today, so the next visit tries again. With an empty Library, nothing is asked. While no mixes are saved (the first build after install, or after clearing data), the section shows its title over a skeleton: placeholder tiles in the mixes' row, at their size, with fill boxes for the cover and the caption lines, holding still (a pulse would make the glass chrome re-blur every frame, and reduce motion wants none). A failed build keeps the skeleton until a later one saves mixes, and a build that finds nothing to build from (no plays and an empty Library) hides the section instead. With mixes saved, a rebuild never shows it: the saved mixes stay until new ones are saved (Core's `discoverMixesSection`). Discover mixes doesn't wait for a play on Home: the line asking for a first play sits before the first other section that plays fill. Only artist names, song titles with their artist, artist page ids and radio tokens are sent. With YT Music off the section is hidden (see Editable Home).

The artist's **Albums** from the same page that the Library doesn't have join the Albums shelf after the Library's, and their **Singles & EPs** it doesn't have make a shelf of their own, each newest first (Core's `ytArtistShelves`). A release the Library already has, an album whose album artist credit names this artist (the Library's artist identity, so a collaboration counts for each of its artists) with the release's title, case, accents, punctuation and version notes ("Deluxe Edition", "Remastered", "- Single", a featured credit) aside, is left out: the Library's album already shows (Core's `libraryAlbumFinder`); a live or remix album is another release. Any other release opens the YT Music album page, where songs the Library has play its files as in Search, and long-presses to the YT Music album menu with Download Album. The answer is saved in the database with when it came (`yt_artists`, by artist key, the whole page kept: top songs, every song of the artist, albums and singles), and the match goes with it, so a later load skips the search (Core's `ytArtistLookup`). For a week, opening the artist shows the saved section at once, with no request and no button; after that the button is back and tapping asks again. An answer saved before Wave read the artist's full list, or saved by a Library album's page (which reads the artist's page only), counts as expired on the artist page: the button is back and a tap asks again (a Library album's page still finds its release in it). "No match" is saved too and shows a quiet "Not found on YouTube Music" line for a week, then the button returns and the search is tried again. Offline, or when YouTube fails, the section shows a quiet line with the button to try again, and nothing is saved. With YT Music off the section and the button are gone and nothing is asked; what was saved stays. Only the opened artist's name, their browse id and the id of their songs playlist are sent; the Library's contents never are, and nothing is fetched in the background: leaving the page stops its request, so no further playlist page is asked for and nothing is saved. The section's states (button, loading, saved, expired, no match, offline) and how long an answer stands are Core's (`stepYtArtistSection`, `ytArtistSectionView`, `YT_ARTIST_FRESH_MS`).
- **YT Music artist page.** An artist the Library doesn't have gets a page too, opened from their YouTube Music browse id (`/library/yt-artist/<UC…>?name=<name>`, `openYtArtist`, as Search's YT Music artists open it) or, from a streamed song, whose artists are only names, by name (`/library/yt-artist/by-name?name=<name>`, `openYtArtistNamed`). - **Playlists.** A playlist holds Library songs, YT Music songs the Library doesn't have (YT Music entries), the songs a Spotify import listed that nothing has searched yet (unsearched entries, below) and the songs an import couldn't find (unavailable entries, below), side by side in the order they were added. **Add to Playlist** in a YT Music song's menu (Search, Discover mixes, YT Music album and artist pages) adds it as a Library song is added: at the end, skipped when the playlist already holds it (by its video id), or as the Library's song when the Library already has it (`libraryFinder`). A playlist keeps what shows an entry without asking YouTube: its video id, title, artists, album (and the album's browse id, for a download's tags), length, cover and explicit mark (`playlist_items.yt_song`, Core's `writePlaylistYtSong`; an item is a Library track or a stored song, never both). The playlist page lists YT Music entries in place as stream rows (YouTube's cover, title, artists and album) with the YT Music song's menu plus Remove from Playlist; while any is in the list, the Library's songs carry the downloaded icon. Play, Shuffle and taps play the playlist's whole list in order, the Library's files and the rest streamed, as an integrated view does; with YT Music off the entries show but don't play (a tap says YT Music is off) and Play plays the Library's songs (Core's `playlistPlayback`). The playlist's long-press menu queues and adds its entries the same way. Reorder, swipe to remove, rename and delete work on YT Music entries as on any other, and selection mode checks them as it checks the Library's songs. The mosaic takes the covers of what the playlist holds, YouTube's for its YT Music entries, and its count and length include them. With YT Music off Wave asks YouTube for nothing, covers included: the entries' rows, their menu and their mosaic tiles show the placeholder instead. Android Auto's Playlists list the same entries, unsearched ones included, and the YT Music ones stream there as streamed songs do (with YT Music off they don't play). Once the Library has a song that is positively the same recording as an entry (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover mixes decide), the entry becomes that Library song in the same place, in every playlist that holds it, and its plays count for it from then on: the store does this when it saves a finished download, and once against the whole Library when a scan ends (Core's `playlistEntryLinks`; it costs one small query while no playlist holds a YT Music entry). A playlist never holds a song twice, so an entry whose Library song its playlist already holds, or that an earlier entry of it has just become, is removed instead. Deleting a Library file still takes its entries out of playlists. Streamed plays from a playlist aren't recorded, as elsewhere.
- **Unavailable entries.** A playlist import keeps each song it couldn't find in its place as an unavailable entry (see Glossary, and "Importing playlists from a link"), and an unsearched entry whose search finds nothing becomes one in its place (see Unsearched entries). It is stored as a YT Music entry is (`playlist_items.yt_song`), marked by why it's unavailable and with no video id (Core's `writePlaylistEntrySong`, `readPlaylistEntrySong`), so no column changed and a version of Wave that reads only YT Music entries leaves it out (a Wave account carries it like any entry). The playlist page lists it in place, dimmed, with Spotify's cover (shown, like YouTube's covers, only while YT Music is on), its title, and why it's unavailable as its second line ("Not found on YouTube Music", "Local file on Spotify"; Core's `unavailableEntryView`). It never plays: Play, Shuffle and Next skip it, the playlist's long-press menu queues the rest without it (Core's `playlistPlayback`), and tapping it says why instead of playing. Play or Shuffle on a playlist with nothing else to play (its missing songs aside) says why too, for them all (Core's `playlistUnplayableNotice`). Download Playlist and Retry Failed skip it (`ytPlaylistDownload`), except one a download's own search just found nothing for, whose failed row Retry Failed asks again (see Playlist downloads); selection mode doesn't check it, and Android Auto, which lists what plays, leaves it out. Its menu offers Remove from Playlist and, while Search is on the tab bar, **Search**, which opens Search with its title and main artist (Core's `unavailableEntryActions`, `unavailableSearchQuery`); swipe to remove and reorder work as on any entry. Nothing searches an unavailable entry again on its own. **Try Again**, under the playlist's count line while YT Music is on, searches YouTube Music once more for each of the playlist's unavailable entries a source listed, never a local file with no artist, in the playlist's order and four at a time (Core's `tryAgainSearches`). It asks the Songs search an unsearched entry gets (the title and artists) and takes the result that search would (`spotifyYtMatch`), waiting its turn in the search allowance with downloads' priority (see Search allowance). Found, the entry becomes that YT Music entry in its place, or is removed when the playlist already holds that song (a playlist never holds a song twice); not found, it stays unavailable; a search that fails (offline, refused) leaves it unavailable and counts as failed (`tryAgainSettles`). The answers are saved about 25 at a time, so a long run fills the playlist as it goes. While it runs, the button reads "Searching…" and can't be tapped, including after the page is left and opened again. When it ends, a short notice says how many were found, and how many searches failed, if any ("Found 2 of 3", "Found 1 of 3 · 1 search failed"; `tryAgainNotice`). A playlist with nothing to search again (no unavailable entries, or only local files) shows no Try Again. Once the Library has the song (a scan, a download), the entry becomes the Library's song in its place, as a YT Music entry does (`playlistEntryLinks`, with the same rule that a playlist never holds a song twice); a local file with no artist never matches. The playlist's count includes its unavailable entries, as its length does theirs, and its page says how many can't play ("2,451 songs · 3 unavailable", Core's `playlistSongsLine`). Only unavailable entries count as unavailable there: an unsearched entry is a song like any other, so the number grows as searches settle songs as not found. Existing playlists, and imports with every song found, are unchanged.
- **Unsearched entries.** A Spotify import puts each song the Library doesn't have in its place as an unsearched entry (see Glossary, and "Importing playlists from a link"). It is stored as an unavailable entry is (`playlist_items.yt_song`, no video id), marked unsearched instead of by a reason (Core's `writePlaylistEntrySong`, `readPlaylistEntrySong`). It goes wherever a YT Music entry goes: Play, Shuffle and a tap play it in its place, and the queue, Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist (a copy stays unsearched, and a playlist never takes it twice), the playlist's long-press menu, selection mode and the selection bar take it as a YT Music entry. Its own menu offers Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Remove from Playlist, Go to Artist (its artists' names, as a streamed song's link resolves them) and Select. Download Playlist, Retry Failed and the selection bar's Download download it, searching it first when YouTube Music is its source (see Playlist downloads); its row then shows its download. With YT Music off it shows but doesn't play, as a YT Music entry: a tap says YT Music is off, and Play plays the Library's songs (Core's `playlistPlayback`).
  - **Searched as it's about to play.** Whenever the queue changes, Wave searches the unsearched songs among the playing one and the next 3 in play order, the shuffled order when shuffle is on and the top again after the end with repeat all, each once, with the playing priority in the search allowance (Core's `playingSearches`, `PLAYING_SEARCHES`; see Search allowance). It is the Songs search an import used to ask (the title and artists, `unsearchedSearchQuery`), and the first result that is the recording wins (`spotifyYtMatch`; Core's `settleUnsearched` decides what an answer settles). Found, the entry becomes that YT Music entry in its place in every playlist that holds it, or is removed from a playlist that already holds that song (Core's `unsearchedSettles`); the queue's entries become the YT Music song and the player takes them under their own keys. Not found, the entry becomes an unavailable entry ("Not found on YouTube Music") in its place in every playlist, keeping Spotify's cover; it leaves the queue, and a short notice names the song skipped (`Skipped "Touch": it isn't on YouTube Music.`). A search that fails (offline, refused, an answer Wave can't read) never makes a song unavailable: it stays unsearched, and the queue asks it again only after 30 seconds (`PLAYING_SEARCH_RETRY_MS`). The app does this in `src/playback/playingSearches.ts`, through `searchUnsearchedSong` and `settleUnsearchedSong` (`src/library/unsearchedEntries.ts`), which the background fill and downloads use too, and the store's `settleUnsearchedEntries` writes the change, stamped for the Wave account.

  - **The player and the playing song.** The native player never holds an unsearched entry (Core's `playerQueueList`), so it plays along without one still unsearched; when it passes over one, the same kind of notice names it (`Skipped "Touch": YouTube Music didn't answer.`, `passedOverNotice`). When the playing entry itself is unsearched (a tap, Play or Shuffle on one, Next or Up Next onto one), the queue waits for its search, the usual loading: the song before it pauses, the queue shows it as current, and once found it plays as the YT Music song. Not found or failed, the next song goes the same way, with the notice, or on Previous the one before it, so Previous keeps going back. Once the queue runs out or goes round on songs that didn't play, it stops at a song the player holds. Tapped, Play, Shuffle or chosen in Up Next while offline or with YT Music off, it says what a YT Music song says then ("You're offline. YT Music songs need the network.", "YT Music is off in Settings.") and the queue stays as it was. Skipped to then (Next, Previous), it says so once and playback stops at the next song the player holds (on Previous, the one before): the unsearched songs after it aren't searched or named one by one (Core's `playingAnswerStep`, `startPlayingWalk`, `answerPlayingWalk`). While the queue waits for a search, the queue's other commands and the player's own moves go on: a newer command takes the wait's place (the search answers the queue as a song coming up's does), and one that leaves the same song playing (an Up Next change, a song coming up settled) waits for the same search. A queue picked back up after a restart starts at the next song the player holds when its playing song is unsearched (`withHeldCurrent`), and so does one saved while its playing song was searched when JS restarts while the player kept going (`keptQueue`); its unsearched songs come back with it while YT Music is on (`queuedYtTracks`, `restorableTrack`).
  - **Android Auto** lists it with the playlist's songs (no cover, as a YT Music entry) and plays it: a play that starts on one waits for its search as the phone does, and the queue searches the next ones as above.
  - **Sync.** The Wave account carries it as it carries an unavailable entry. The first phone to search it settles it for the others: the entry's new song (YT Music or unavailable) is stamped and pulled like any edit.
- **Selecting YT Music songs.** A YT Music song's menu ends with **Select** where its list can select, as a Library song's does, and YT Music rows check and uncheck in selection wherever they list beside Library songs: Search, playlists, the artist page, YT Music artist and album pages, and an album page with YouTube Music's songs joined in. The selection bar offers what every checked item supports, Library and YT Music songs mixed: Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Remove from Playlist (in a playlist), **Download** while any checked song isn't a file yet (a missing song downloads into its Library entry, a YT Music song as its menu's Download does), and Go to Artist when they share their artists. Favorite, Find Lyrics and Genre and Delete act on files, so they show only when every checked item is a Library song. A YT Music song downloaded since it was checked is queued and added as the Library's song. Core's `itemActions` decides.
- **Integrated playback.** Search's song results, an artist's page and an album's page decide what Play, Shuffle and a tapped song play by one rule (Core's `pagePlayback`): with YouTube Music's songs among the page's (an integrated view), the page's whole list in page order, the Library's files and the rest streamed; with none, the Library's songs alone, as before, so nothing is streamed or queued from YouTube Music outside an integrated view.
- **Rows stay in place.** A download finishing doesn't move its song in a mixed list (Search's song results, an artist's page, a Library album's page). For the rest of the visit to the screen, a YT Music song the list showed streamed keeps its row: it turns downloaded at once and plays the Library's file, and the Library's part of the list leaves that file out, so it shows once (Core's `songsInPlace`). In Search it doesn't become the top result, reorder the groups or count as the Library having results for the query, so YT Music's results stay (`heldTracks`, a skipped search). An album page showing its tracklist keeps it when the last song is downloaded, instead of showing the album as it is (`keepTracklist`). Leaving the screen (another screen over it, another tab; not Now Playing) and opening it again, or typing another query, lays the list out by its rule again, each song once. YT Music's album and artist pages, Discover mixes and playlists already keep their rows.
- **Off switch.** Settings › Accounts › YT Music › Use YT Music (on by default). Off, the section isn't shown and the native client refuses every request before it reaches the network; the switch is kept natively too, so the playback service obeys it without JS.
- **Account.** Signing in is optional; everything YT Music does also works signed out. Settings › Accounts › YT Music › Sign In opens Google's sign-in page in a web view inside Wave (`YtSignInActivity`, as Metrolist does). Once the page reaches YouTube Music signed in, Wave takes the session cookie and the page's account (`YtSignInPage`), asks YouTube Music's account menu whose account it is (`YtAccountMenu`) and keeps the session; Settings then shows the account's picture, name and email, and Sign Out. The session is handed to innertubex (`replaceSession`), so every request the `YtClient` sends (Search, playback, optional download metadata, Discover mixes) goes out as the account; a Premium account therefore gets the audio YouTube serves Premium. Visitor data isn't the account's and is left as it is.
- **Session privacy.** The cookie lives only in the native module: in one file in the app's no-backup storage (`noBackupFilesDir`, never in an Android backup), never in the JS key-value store, never handed to JS (which only sees `{ status, account: { name, email, photoUrl } }`) and never logged. The sign-in web view starts and ends empty: its cookies and site storage are cleared before the page opens and when it closes, so its only copy is the native one. Sign Out deletes the file and clears the web view's cookies and storage again.
- **Expiry.** A session YouTube stops accepting expires instead of failing: when a request comes back HTTP 401, or the account menu has no account (checked each time Settings opens), the session's credentials are dropped, the request is asked again signed out (once), and Settings shows "signed out" with Sign In Again. Signed out, nothing can be rejected again, so there's no error loop. Without an answer (offline, YouTube failing) the session stays as it was. What Settings shows is Core's (`stepYtAccount`, `ytAccountView`); with YT Music off there is no Sign In, but a signed-in account can still be signed out of.
- **Native shape.** One process-wide `YtClient` (package `expo.modules.wavenative.yt`) owns the HTTP client, innertubex's session and the stream resolver (`YtClient.get(context).streams`, used by playback); the `WaveYtMusic` Expo module exposes it to JS (`setEnabled`, `searchSongs`, `searchArtists`, `searchAlbums`, `artist`, `playlist`, `playlistMore`, `songRadio`, `artistRadio`, `radioMore`, `session`, `checkSession`, `signIn`, `signOut`, `cacheArtwork`, `libraryPlaylists` and the `onSessionChanged` event; failures reject with `ERR_YT_OFFLINE`, `ERR_YT_FAILED` or `ERR_YT_DISABLED`, and `libraryPlaylists` with `ERR_YT_SIGNED_OUT` too), wrapped by the app's `YtMusic` Effect service. `libraryPlaylists` reads the signed-in account's library playlists (browse `FEmusic_liked_playlists`, following its continuations, at most 100 pages, `YtLibraryParser`): for each, its id, name, cover, song count and kind (the account's own, by the Edit entry in its menu; saved; or generated: `LM`, `SE`, the `RD…` mixes but `RDCLAK5uy_…`, podcasts). It needs the session: signed out it rejects with `ERR_YT_SIGNED_OUT` before asking, and a session YouTube refuses (HTTP 401, or a page asking to sign in) expires, then rejects the same way. YouTube Music sends a playlist a hundred rows a page: `playlist` reads its first page (its head, its songs and the token for the next page) and `playlistMore` the page a token leads to, both by `YtPlaylistParser`, which leaves out the rows YouTube Music greys out (removed, blocked in the country). Core's `readYtPlaylist` follows the pages to the last, keeping YouTube Music's order, and keeps a song listed twice once. A read stops at 5,000 songs or 100 pages, whichever comes first, and then says `truncated`; Liked Songs' import reads `LM` with `uncapped` instead, to the last page, ended early only by a token it already followed or a page of nothing but songs it already has. `YtMusic.playlist` runs it: a page that fails fails the whole read, so the import never gets part of a playlist that looks complete, while a YT Music playlist's page hears each page as it comes (see YT Music list pages). Downloads belong to the native `AndroidDownloads` owner (package `download`): `DurableDownloads` keeps the queue as an atomically rewritten journal in no-backup storage and drives it from `YtDownloadService`, three songs at a time in the order they were asked for (a song waiting in a peer's queue, for a pause before trying again, or over 30 seconds for an answer from JS leaves its slot to the next, up to six started at once), each song's transfer, tagging and publication on a worker thread of its own, while songs join the Library one at a time (publish decision, publication, import), a `dataSync` foreground service with a partial wake lock and a Wi-Fi lock that shows progress and Cancel in its notification, restarts sticky after process death and stops when the queue is empty. Each job carries its source, and the transport hands a Soulseek candidate to the Soulseek client and YouTube's to `YtAudioDownload` (package `yt`). A Soulseek job also carries the fallback its prepare answer gave (the failures Core hands over and the M4A's place); every failure goes through one place in the journal, which switches such a job to YouTube Music with a fresh deadline instead of failing it. The Soulseek client tells an unreachable server (`ERR_SLSK_UNREACHABLE`) from being offline by asking whether the phone is online. The journal leases the decisions that need the Library and Core to JS (prepare: the source, for Soulseek the search and Core's match, and the destination; confirm: received tags, or the length YouTube's audio plays for; publish: the current folder policy; import: the LibraryStore upsert); the download service starts the headless JS runtime for them, and a lease that isn't answered expires and is offered again. `WaveSoulseek` exposes the session, Download in best quality (`turnOn`, `turnOff`; the account it makes or reuses never reaches JS), search and the journal (`enqueueJob`, `jobsSnapshot`, `claimDecision`, `submitDecision`, `cancelJob`, `resumeJobs`, `onJobsChanged`); `WaveYtDownloads` keeps the optional album, watch and lyrics pages; the app's `ytmusic/YtDownloads.ts` answers decisions and mirrors the journal into rows. Building it needs Kotlin 2.4 (innertubex's), set through `expo-build-properties`.

### Wave account and sync

- **What it's for.** Changing phones without losing the Library. The account keeps what Wave knows about the music, not the music: the song list, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences. Audio files never go up. A new phone gets the files back from Syncthing, or downloads them as missing songs.
- **Who can sign in.** Any Google account. The app is sideloaded and shared with a few people, so the service limits each account instead of keeping an allowlist: a maximum request size, a maximum number of songs, playlists and plays, a request rate per session, and a sign-in rate per address and per Google account (constants in the service, `SYNC_LIMITS`). An optional allowlist of emails (`ALLOWED_EMAILS`, unset by default) closes sign-in to everyone else if it's ever needed.
- **Signing in.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account › Sign In with Google uses Android's Google sign-in (the account picker, not a web view, unlike YT Music's sign-in), with Wave's OAuth client, which Google ties to the APK's package name and signing certificate. The first time, a card says what goes up and what never does (story 80) before the picker opens. The app sends the Google ID token to the service once. The service checks it against Google's keys and Wave's client id and answers with its own session token. The ID token is then dropped. The session token is kept like YT Music's cookie, in the native module's no-backup storage, never logged. It lasts until sign-out, account deletion or the service revoking it. A revoked or expired session signs the app out, and Settings says so. JS never holds the token: the native module (`WaveAccount`) sends pushes and pulls for it, and only native code reaches the Soulseek account's route. The service's URL and the web client id are `syncUrl` and `googleWebClientId` in `app.config.ts` extra; empty, Settings › Accounts › Wave Account says the account isn't set up on this build.
- **Signing out** keeps the Library, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences on the phone and stops syncing. Missing songs go, since the phone has nothing of them. A playlist entry for a missing song with a YouTube video id becomes a YT Music entry. Without a video id it leaves the playlist, as a deleted file's entry does. Signing in again merges, as the first sign-in does.
- **Deleting the account.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account › Delete Account asks first, then the service deletes every row it holds for the account and every session, and the app signs out. Nothing on the phone is deleted.
- **What syncs.**
  - Songs: the recording key, title, credit, album, album artist, disc and track number, length, year, genre, the YouTube video id when the song was downloaded from YouTube Music, and when it was added.
  - Playlists and their entries, including YT Music entries.
  - Favorites.
  - Play events, with the song's recording key, including imported BlackPlayer plays.
  - Preferences: the appearance, the theme choice, glass, the tap and long-press timings, the tab bar, the kind of shuffle, Crossfade, the Artist photos, Lyrics and genre and YT Music switches, Download in best quality, Auto lyrics and the notification accent. Each goes up as what the key-value store holds for it (null at its default), and one pulled applies at once, through its setting, without counting as a change on that phone. The notification accent follows the theme and the appearance, so it comes with them. Download in best quality's switch lives in the native module: Settings sets the synced copy once the switch has moved (stamped even when the copy already said so), and one pulled turns the native switch on or off at the end of the pull, again at each pull until it has (turning it on fails offline; Core's `settleBestQuality`, see "The Soulseek account"). Auto lyrics is the car screen's own toggle, kept by the native playback service rather than as a setting, and doesn't sync yet.
  - Never: scan roots and excluded folders (they're paths on one phone), the queue and resume point, recent searches, artwork, artist photos, playlists' own images and other caches, the YouTube session, the download journal, and which YouTube Music playlist a From YT Music playlist came from.
- **The Soulseek account.** Settings has no Soulseek sign-in, only a **Download in best quality** switch (off on a new install). Turning it on gives the phone a Soulseek account if it has none: a random username that doesn't name Wave (a name every Wave user shared would let peers block them all, and Wave shares no files) and a random password. Logging in with an unused name is how Soulseek creates an account, so a taken name (`ERR_SLSK_AUTH`) just means drawing another, five names at most before Settings says it couldn't make an account. Offline, or with Soulseek's server down, the switch goes back off with a message. Turning the switch off keeps the account for the next time. An account Soulseek later rejects is forgotten and the switch goes off, its synced copy too at the next pull; turning it on again makes a new one. A phone already connected to an account of its own keeps it, with the switch on. While signed in to a Wave account, the Soulseek account belongs to it: the first phone with one sends it up, and every phone signed in afterwards uses the Wave account's, replacing its own. The native module asks for it when the switch turns on and after each pull (the first after sign-in too): when the Wave account has one and the phone's is different, the phone switches to it and forgets its own (downloads running on the old one finish first, and the next search with none running connects with the new one); when it has none, the phone's goes up. An account the switch makes while signed in goes up at once, and turning the switch on with no account of the phone's uses the Wave account's instead of making one. When Wave's server can't be asked, turning it on goes on as if signed out, and the next pull settles it. Signing out keeps the phone's Soulseek account. Without a Wave account, the phone keeps its own. The username and password go between the native module and the sync service only (`PUT` and `GET /v1/soulseek`, sent by the native module with the session token it holds), never through JS, as the Soulseek credentials never are. The service keeps the password encrypted with a key only the Worker has (`SOULSEEK_KEY`). Deleting the Wave account deletes the service's copy, and the phone keeps its own.
- **Song identity.** A song's local id and path mean nothing on another phone, so the account knows songs by their recording key. The Library store keeps each Track's recording key and, for a song downloaded from YouTube Music, its video id (until now the video id was dropped once the file was saved). The service never sees local ids. The app translates them to recording keys and back as it pushes and pulls. Two files on one phone with the same recording key are one song for the account. Plays already recorded get their song's recording key when the update lands. Plays of a song deleted before then can't be keyed and stay on the phone only.
- **Merging.** Every synced row carries when it last changed and which phone changed it. The later change wins, row by row; the phone id breaks a tie (Core's `mergeRows`). Removing something leaves a tombstone, so a removal travels like any other change. A playlist's order is a fractional index per entry (Core's `orderKeyBetween`), so entries two phones add to one playlist offline both stay. Play events are only ever added, and a play is never duplicated (each has an id made on the phone that recorded it). It relies on the phones' clocks. They're set by the network on Android, which is close enough for one person's phones.
- **First sign-in on a phone with a Library.** The two sides are joined, never replaced. The account's songs the phone has (by recording key) become its Tracks, the rest come in as missing songs. The phone's songs, playlists, favorites and plays go up. Two playlists with the same name stay two playlists. A song both sides have keeps the account's tags: the phone's go up only once they change on the phone (a retag), so two phones whose files are tagged differently don't overwrite each other forever (Core's `planSongSync`). Signing out leaves the account, so the next sign-in, to the same account or another, joins again: everything goes up again and the account is pulled from the start, and merging makes that harmless.
- **When it talks to the service.** A change is pushed 3 seconds after the last one (debounced, so a run of edits is one push). The app pulls when it opens, when it comes back to the foreground and after each push. Any network will do, as the rows are small, and the first push of a large history goes in batches of at most 1,000 rows and 1.5 MB (Core's `pushBatches`), each marked as sent as soon as the service has it, so 18,000 BlackPlayer plays go up in 18 requests and an interrupted first push goes on where it stopped. A play goes up once it ends, or once the next one starts (Wave's process died under it). A request refused for the request rate is sent again 20 seconds later, up to four times. There's no background job, and Android Auto's headless start doesn't sync: a phone in a drawer catches up the next time Wave opens. Offline, changes wait in the store and go up later; a sync that fails tries again at the next trigger. The service is never in the way of the Library. The app's side is `WaveSync` (`src/sync`), which runs `syncCycle` through the store and `WaveAccount.request`.
- **Account songs the phone lacks.** Every song pulled is kept in the store's `account_songs`, with its tags, whether the phone has a file for it or not. A star, an entry or a play of a song the phone has no Track for waits in `sync_pending` (as does an entry whose playlist hasn't come yet), and goes in once a Track with its key is in the Library (a scan finds the file, or it comes in as a missing song). Nothing is pulled twice for it.
- **Missing songs.** While signed in, a scan that no longer finds a file keeps the song as a missing song instead of removing it, with its favorites, playlist entries and plays. Signed out, a scan removes it, as before. A missing song shows wherever its Track would (albums, artists, genres, playlists, favorites, Home, Search), dimmed, with the look a not-downloaded YT Music entry has. Tapping one, or Download in its menu, downloads it. Its menu also has **Remove from Library**, and Delete on a song that has a file deletes the file and removes the song from the account too, with its star and playlist entries (Core's `songsLeaving`). Those are the only ways a song leaves the account (story 86), besides a retag: a file retagged to another recording key is the same Track under a new key, so its old version leaves the account and its star and entries go up under the new key; its plays keep the old key, as plays are only ever added. Another phone loses a removed song at its next pull if it's a missing song there; a phone that has the file keeps it, as a pull never takes a file's song away, but loses its star and entries. A file a scan finds with a missing song's recording key fills that song in (length within 3 seconds breaks a tie), so files Syncthing brings never show twice.
- **Downloading missing songs.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account counts the missing songs and offers **Download All**, which queues them as an album download does, with the same progress, Retry Failed and Cancel. A Library album's long-press menu does the same for its own missing songs: **Download Missing Songs** queues them in album order, Cancel Download shows while they download, and Retry Failed once the rest has ended with failures, as a YT Music album's menu does. Albums checked together offer **Download** on the selection bar, for the missing songs of all of them (Core's `itemActions`). An album with no missing songs offers none of these. One song, or all of them, downloads through the download pipeline and its source rule (Core's `downloadSource`): a song with a video id downloads that video while YT Music is on, whatever Download in best quality says, and gets the cover of that video's album; with YT Music off it goes to Soulseek while Download in best quality is on. Any other song goes to Soulseek while Download in best quality is on, else to YouTube Music's search, matched by the rules downloads already use. A downloaded song keeps its place in playlists, favorites and play history, since it's the same Track.
- **The sync service** (`apps/sync`) is a Cloudflare Worker in front of one Turso (libSQL) database, with the account's id on every row. The app never holds a database credential: only the Worker has Turso's token. The Worker imports Core, so it merges by the same rules the app does. Its routes:
  - `POST /v1/session`: Google ID token in, session token out.
  - `DELETE /v1/session`: sign out.
  - `POST /v1/push`: changed rows in, merged on arrival.
  - `GET /v1/pull?since=<cursor>`: every row that changed after the cursor, and the next cursor. The service numbers each accepted change, so a pull never misses one.
  - `PUT /v1/soulseek`: the account's Soulseek account in (the password kept encrypted under `SOULSEEK_KEY`); `GET /v1/soulseek`: it, or none. Only the native module sends them.
  - `DELETE /v1/account`: every row, session and the Soulseek account.
- **Deploying it** is done by hand with `wrangler deploy`. A setup wizard walks through creating the Turso database, Google's OAuth clients (an Android client with the release and debug signing certificates' SHA-1, and the web client id the service checks ID tokens against) and the Worker's secrets.

### Modules

1. **Core** (workspace package, pure TypeScript, **zero runtime dependencies**). Holds all decision-heavy domain logic behind a small public API, so it can be tested anywhere with plain Node:
   - _Queue engine_ — a pure state + command reducer: start playback from a context at a given track; next/previous (with the 3-second restart rule); seek; shuffle on/off (current track stays first, original order restorable; Real Shuffle or Smart Shuffle, see story 25); repeat off/all/one; Play Next; Add to Queue; reorder; remove; track-ended transitions. It outputs the ordered list the native player should hold.
   - _Play-event classifier_ — given start, listened time, duration and end reason, decides whether a play is counted.
   - _Library model_ — grouping (album identity = album title + album artist, album-artist fallback, compilations), sort keys (articles and accents ignored), disc/track ordering, unknown-tag fallbacks, and splitting credits into artists (`creditSplitter`).
   - _Scan reconciliation_ — given the known index and a scan result, produces adds/updates/removals, and matches moved/renamed files (same size + duration + title + artist) so history and favorites carry over.
   - _Search_ — normalisation (case, accents, punctuation), word-prefix matching and ranking across entity types.
   - _Lyrics_ — LRC parsing (timestamps, multiple stamps per line, offsets) and current-line lookup.

   - _BlackPlayer stats import_ — parsing `.bpstat` track stats, ordering snapshots, dating plays, matching rows to Library tracks, skipping plays imported before, and the summary (`blackPlayer.ts`; see "Importing BlackPlayer stats").
   - _App log_ — what a log line looks like, what never reaches it (redaction), which entries go when it's full, and the exported file's header and name (`appLog.ts`; see "App log").
   - _Sync_ (`sync.ts`, `orderKey.ts`) — a song's recording key and which Track a scanned file or an account song is (`recordingKey`, `findRecording`), merging rows (`mergeRows`: the later change wins, tombstones, the phone id breaking ties) and play events (`mergePlays`: by id only), playlist order keys, the order they list entries in and the keys for entries added or moved to a place (`orderKeyBetween`, `compareOrderKeys`, `placeEntries`), what the first sign-in joins (`joinLibraries`), what a scan does with a vanished file while signed in and which missing songs new files fill in (`vanishedFile`, `fillMissingSongs`), what signing out leaves (`signOutPlan`), the rows the app and the service send each other, cut into pushes the service takes and read from a pull (`syncWire.ts`: `pushBatches`, `readPullPage`), which Track an account song is, which of the phone's songs go up and an imported play's id (`syncPlan.ts`: `accountTrackIds`, `planSongSync`, `blackPlayerPlayId`), and Settings › Accounts › Wave Account (`waveAccount.ts`: `stepWaveAccount`, `waveAccountView`, the first sign-in's card, and the build's setup from `app.config.ts` extra, `waveAccountConfig`) (see "Wave account and sync").
2. **Native player** (local Expo module, Kotlin, Media3). A `MediaSessionService` hosting ExoPlayer, so notification, lock screen, Bluetooth/headset buttons, audio focus and becoming-noisy handling come from Media3. The JS side owns ordering (from the queue engine) and pushes the ordered item list; the native side plays it gaplessly and reports state, throttled position, item transitions with a reason (auto-advance, skip, seek, error, crossfade) and errors. Native shuffle is not used. Crossfade plays the outgoing song's last seconds on a second ExoPlayer (the tail): it opens on the same song shortly before the overlap, silent, is brought in step with the session's player, and takes over that song as the session's player moves to the next one, so the media session, notification, queue sync and Android Auto still have one current song. The service keeps the overlap JS last set, so it fades without JS too. The audio pipeline keeps a hook for custom audio processors so equalizer and ReplayGain (v2) slot in without restructuring.
3. **Native scanner** (same Kotlin module). Walks the scan roots minus exclusions, reads tags with a JVM tag library that also supports writing (needed by the v2 tag editor), extracts embedded artwork once per unique image (hashed, cached as files) together with a small colour palette (Android Palette) for the Now Playing tint, and finds sidecar `.lrc` files. It streams results to JS in batches of ~500 and supports an incremental mode driven by known path + modification time + size. The same module holds the YT Music client (`yt` package, see YT Music).
4. **Library store** (app, Drizzle on expo-sqlite). The single owner of the schema and migrations. Tables for tracks, albums, artists (with each track's and album's credited artists in credit order), genres, folders, artwork (path + palette), playlists and playlist items, favorites, play events, scan roots/exclusions, recent searches, and persisted playback state. Home sections and play counts are queries over play events.
   - _What the Wave account needs_ (`db/sync.ts`). Nothing syncs from the store itself; it keeps what sync works from. The `phone` table holds this install's phone id, 32 random hex digits made by the migration (or the first launch), never changed. A Track has `recording_key` (Core's `recordingKey`, computed whenever its file is saved; after the update that brought it in, the store keys the existing Library at launch, `keyRecordings`) and `video_id` (set when a YouTube Music download is imported and kept when the file is saved again; null for Soulseek downloads and scanned files). Its `path` is null for a missing song. Song lists, playback, the scan, imports and lookups read only Tracks with a file (`hasFile`, `withFile` in `db/tracks.ts`). A play event has `recording_key`, written when the play is recorded. Plays of songs deleted before the update have none and stay on the phone.
   - Playlists and playlist entries have a `sync_id` made on the phone that created them. An entry has an `order_key` (Core's `orderKeyBetween`) instead of a position, and a playlist lists its entries by `order_key`, then `sync_id`. The update gave existing entries the keys that adding them one by one at the end would have made, in their old order.
   - Playlists, entries and favorites carry `changed_at` (ms, this phone's clock) and `phone_id`. A removal sets `deleted` and leaves the row as a tombstone, which no list, count or total shows. A phone never stamps two changes of one row with the same time: a change made in the same millisecond as the last one takes the next. `preference_changes` holds when each synced setting (`persistedSetting`'s `synced`) last changed and on which phone; the values stay in the key-value store. `selectChangesSince` reads every playlist, entry, favorite and preference changed after a time, tombstones included.
   - Every synced row also has `pushed`: false from a change made here until a push takes it, true for a change pulled from the account. A play event has a `sync_id`, the same on every phone: random for Wave's own plays, `blackplayer:<n>:<recording key>` for a song's n-th imported BlackPlayer play (Core's `blackPlayerPlayId`), so importing the same stats on two phones uploads them once; and `ongoing` for the play going on now, which `latestPlay` picks back up and which waits to go up until it ends. `account_songs` holds the account's songs as the phone knows them (and what its own file with each key was at the last sync), `sync_pending` the pulled rows waiting for a song or playlist, and `sync_state` the account the Library is joined with and the pull cursor (`db/syncRows.ts`: `joinAccount`, `selectUnpushed`, `markPushed`, `planSongs`, `applyPulledPage`, `applyPending`).
5. **App services** (app, Effect). Wrap the native modules as typed services and event streams; orchestrate scans (core reconciliation → store); drive the queue engine and sync it to the native player; record play events through the classifier; persist and restore playback state.
6. **UI** (app, Expo Router). Tabs the owner arranges — Home, then any of Library, Search and the Library's sections (Search rendered as a separate glass pill; see "Editable tab bar"). A custom glass tab bar, a floating glass mini player and a full-screen Now Playing presented over the tabs with an interactive drag-to-dismiss gesture. Detail screens for album, artist, playlist and genre. Settings, a list of pages (see "Settings").
7. **Sync service** (`apps/sync`, Cloudflare Worker, Turso). Checks Google ID tokens, holds sessions, merges pushed rows by Core's rules, serves pulls and deletes accounts. The app's side is an Effect service (`WaveSync`) that hears of local changes, pushes and pulls, and translates local ids to recording keys through the store (see "Wave account and sync").

### Play event shape

One row per playback start: track, started-at, listened milliseconds, track duration, end reason (`completed` | `skipped` | `stopped` | `interrupted`), counted flag, and play context (type + id). Append-only; nothing is aggregated away.

### Importing playlists from a link

- Library › Playlists › Import Playlists asks where the playlist comes from: **From a Link**, **From Files** or **From YT Music**. From Files is the M3U import (story 56). With YT Music off there is neither From a Link nor From YT Music: Import Playlists opens the file picker at once, as before, and nothing is asked of YouTube (Core's `playlistImportSources`).
- **From YT Music** shows whenever YT Music is on, signed in or not. Signed out (or with a session that expired), it opens the YouTube Music sign-in Settings › Accounts uses, then the picker; leaving the sign-in opens nothing. The picker is a full-screen sheet that rises from the bottom (`YtLibraryPicker.tsx`): **Cancel** at the top, **Import** and **Import & Download** side by side at the bottom, and **Select All** above the list, which reads **Deselect All** while every row is ticked. Its first row is **Liked Songs**, YouTube Music's Liked music (read as the playlist `LM`), always listed, even for an account with no playlists; its cover and count come from the library page's Liked Music when it lists one. Under it is a switch, **Also add to Favorites**: off when the sheet opens, and enabled only while Liked Songs is ticked (unticked, it shows off and dimmed, and ticking Liked Songs again brings back what it was; Core's `toggleYtLibraryFavorites`, `ytLibraryPickerView`). After it come the playlists in the account's YouTube Music library (`YtMusic.libraryPlaylists`, see YT Music, Native shape), in YouTube Music's order: the ones the account made and the ones it saved from others, YouTube Music's editorial playlists (`RDCLAK5uy_…`) included. What YouTube Music generates (Liked Music, which is the Liked Songs row, Episodes for Later, Supermix, My Mix and the other mixes, podcasts) is left out. Each row shows the cover, the name and "N songs", all from the library page; nothing is read per playlist before Import. Every row starts ticked, Liked Songs included, except those already imported on this phone: they show **Imported** and start unticked. Import and Import & Download do nothing while no row is ticked; Cancel, Back or dragging the sheet down imports nothing. While the library loads the sheet shows a spinner. A failed read gives the reason and **Try Again**; a session YouTube no longer accepts says so and offers **Sign In Again** (Core's `ytLibraryFailure`). The rows, the ticks and Select All are Core's (`ytLibraryPicker`, `toggleYtLibraryRow`, `pressYtLibrarySelectAll`, `ytLibraryPickerView`).
  - **Import.** The sheet goes down and the user stays on Playlists. Each ticked playlist is made at once, empty, in the picker's order and named as a link import names it, Liked Songs as "Liked Songs"; a name any other playlist has (or one made just before it in the same Import) goes in under Keep Both's name ("Name 2") without asking (Core's `planYtLibraryImport`). Wave then reads them one at a time in the background, in that order, and fills each as a YouTube Music link import does (`YtMusic.playlist`, `planLinkImport`, `finishPlaylistImport`): a song the Library has as the Library's track, any other as a YT Music entry. Liked Songs has no song cap: it's read whole, however many songs the account liked (`readYtPlaylist`'s `uncapped`). The other playlists stop at the 5,000-song cap (`YT_PLAYLIST_SONG_CAP`), and the page of one that goes on past it says "Only its first 5,000 songs came from YouTube Music" (see Listed twice, below). Nothing is downloaded or searched; there is no notification and no service. The app log gets the import's line per playlist (`importLogLine`), a line for each read that fails (`ytLibraryReadLogLine`), and one each time the batch pauses (`ytLibraryPauseLogLine`). With Also add to Favorites on, Liked Songs' read gives the Favorite star to each liked song that is a Library track at that moment, the ones `libraryFinder` matched for the playlist (Core's `ytLibraryFavorites`), in one write, before its songs go in; the Favorites list shows them, and the star syncs like any other. Songs that go in as YT Music entries get no star, even if they're downloaded later, and the import never takes a star away. With it off, no star changes.
  - **Import & Download.** Imports exactly as Import does, and as soon as each playlist has been read and filled, its downloads join the queue as its own Download Playlist would queue them: the songs the Library doesn't have, in playlist order, sharing jobs already on their way (`runYtPlaylistDownloadAction`, Core's `ytPlaylistDownload`, on the playlist as it now stands). A playlist whose read fails queues nothing, and Try Again on its page imports it without downloading (Core's `ytLibraryReadFailed` drops the choice). The choice is each playlist's own (`YtLibraryRead.download`): a playlist refreshed by a later Import while still in the batch takes that Import's choice. Import alone downloads nothing.
  - **Kept on the phone.** The batch is kept in the key-value store ("ytLibraryBatch", never synced; Core's `writeYtLibraryBatch`, `readYtLibraryBatch`), each playlist with its source, its Wave playlist, its name, whether its read stops at the cap (Liked Songs' doesn't) whether it stars (Liked Songs' with Also add to Favorites on, so a resumed read or Try Again still stars; a playlist kept without it stars nothing) and whether it downloads (Import & Download's, so the playlists read after Wave starts again still queue their downloads; a playlist kept without it downloads nothing), added as it's made; a playlist refreshed while still in the batch leaves its old place and is read once, in the new order. A read still running when its playlist is refreshed or deleted gives its answer to nothing: no songs, no stars, and the newer read stays as it was (each read carries a token while Wave runs, Core's `isYtLibraryReadQueued`), so a playlist is never filled twice. If Android stops Wave partway through, the playlists not read yet are read once Wave starts again with all-files access, after the first screen shows its content, in the same order; one being read when Wave stopped is read again first (Core's `nextYtLibraryRead`). A playlist leaves the batch once it's read, its songs then going in as a link import's do, or once it's deleted, here or on another phone (`pruneYtLibraryBatch`, as each run of the batch starts; one deleted while the batch runs is read, and its link import ends at once).
  - **Offline, or YT Music off.** A read that finds Wave offline or YT Music turned off pauses the batch (Core's `ytLibraryReadFailed`): that playlist and the rest stay waiting, in order, with no line on their pages, and the batch goes on when Wave starts again, comes back to the foreground or imports again, from the playlist it paused at.
  - **A failed read.** If a playlist's read fails otherwise (refused, an answer Wave can't read), the playlist stays, empty, and the rest of the batch goes on. Its page says "Couldn't read it from YouTube Music" under its length, with **Try Again** while YT Music is on (`YtLibraryReadLine.tsx`, Core's `ytLibraryReadView`). Try Again reads it again at once and fills it as the batch would have (Liked Songs whole, and starred when the switch was on); it reads "Reading…", not tappable, until the read ends, and another failure, offline included, leaves the line as it was. A failed playlist is kept in the batch, so the line is still there after Wave starts again, but the batch doesn't read it again on its own.
  - **Importing again.** Wave remembers which YouTube Music playlist each playlist From YT Music made came from (its id; `LM` for Liked Songs), in the key-value store ("ytLibraryOrigins", Core's `readYtLibraryOrigins`, `writeYtLibraryOrigins`). It stays on this phone: the Wave account doesn't sync it, so on another phone the playlist arrives without its origin, shows no Imported label there, and a new import there only falls back to Keep Both by name. Ticking a row marked Imported refreshes that playlist with a fresh copy, as Replace does: the old playlist keeps its name and is emptied at once (an import still filling it ends), then filled again from the source in its turn, and no second playlist is made (Core's `planYtLibraryImport`). Deleting the playlist forgets its origin, so its row is ticked and unmarked again; one deleted on another phone is forgotten when the picker next opens (`liveYtLibraryOrigins`). A playlist whose origin is gone by Import is made again, as a new one.
  - **What is sent.** The library page's browse requests (and each ticked playlist's id when it's read), with the session, as every signed-in YouTube Music request goes. Nothing of the Library goes out. Wave asks for the library page only when the picker opens.
- From a Link opens a card with a field for the link. When the clipboard holds a playlist link, the field starts with it (`WaveNative.readClipboardText`, read only when From a Link is chosen; Android shows its own "pasted" notice). Wave takes a YouTube Music or YouTube playlist link (`music.youtube.com/playlist?list=…`, `www.youtube.com/…`, `youtube.com/…`, `m.youtube.com/…`, with or without the scheme, extra parameters such as `si` ignored), the first address in text with words around it (as apps share a link with its title), or the bare playlist id (`PL…`, `OLAK5uy_…`, `RDCLAK5uy_…`, `list=` or `VL` before it allowed). It also takes a Spotify playlist link (`open.spotify.com/playlist/<id>`, with or without an `intl-xx` locale, `/embed` or an old link's `/user/<user>` before it, parameters such as `si` ignored), a Spotify URI (`spotify:playlist:<id>`, `spotify:user:<user>:playlist:<id>`) and a Spotify short link (`spotify.link/<code>`, `spotify.app.link/<code>`), which the card follows before reading. Anything else gets a reason under the field and no Continue: not a link, a site other than YouTube or Spotify, a link to a song (`/watch`, `youtu.be`, even one played from a playlist, or a Spotify track or episode) or another page (an album, an artist, a profile) (Core's `recognisePlaylistLink`).
- Continue opens the **import card**, which reads the whole playlist at once (`YtMusic.playlist`, see Native shape) and then shows its cover, name, how many songs it has and how many of them the Library already has ("24 songs · 10 in your Library", "all", "none"), with **Import** and **Cancel**. Every song the source lists is accounted for: when some don't go in as listed, a line under it gives the source's own count and why ("2,451 songs on Spotify · 21 listed twice go in once"; "· 1 isn't a song" for items such as podcast episodes), so the songs imported, those listed twice and those that aren't songs add up to the source's count. The count is Spotify's own (`totalCount`) or the YouTube Music playlist's rows, a video listed again included (`readYtPlaylist` keeps it once and names the rows listed again). A playlist with nothing left out shows no such line. A playlist past the read's cap says only its first songs come, and counts only those. Offline, or when the read fails, the card says so with **Try Again**. YouTube Music answers a private, deleted or wrong playlist as it answers a page it can't serve, so those share the failure message ("It may be private or deleted, or YouTube Music didn't answer."). A playlist with no songs has nothing to import. What the card shows is Core's (`linkImportView`).
- **Import** makes a Wave playlist named after the source (trimmed to a playlist name, "Imported Playlist" when it has none), gives it every song at once and opens it; it is in Playlists from then on. A name already in use asks Replace or Keep Both first, as the M3U import does (Replace empties the old playlist). The songs go in in the source's order: a song the Library has (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover mixes decide) as the Library's, a YouTube Music playlist's other songs as YT Music entries that stream and can be downloaded later (see YT Music, Playlists), a Spotify playlist's other songs as unsearched entries, searched on YouTube Music when they're about to play or by the background fill (below; see YT Music, Unsearched entries), and a Spotify local file with no artist as an unavailable entry in its place (see YT Music, Unavailable entries). Import searches nothing, so a 2,451-song playlist is whole within seconds, with no wait, no progress line, no notification and no service (Core's `planLinkImport`, `finishPlaylistImport`); its songs settle afterwards, as they play or through the background fill. A playlist of only Library songs is an ordinary playlist. A song that comes twice, or two that are one Library song, goes in once, and counts as **listed twice**: the import never puts a song in a playlist twice. On Spotify that is usually a re-release under another Spotify id with the same title, artists, album and length.
  - **Listed twice.** With none listed twice and the source read whole, the page shows nothing of the import. Otherwise it says "N songs were listed twice" under the playlist's length, with **Show**, which lists them by title and artist under **Listed twice**, scrolling when they don't fit, and **Dismiss**, which takes the line away for good. A playlist past the read's cap (YouTube Music's 5,000 songs, Spotify's 100 when only the embed page answered) says so first: "Only its first 5,000 songs came from YouTube Music", followed by "· N songs were listed twice" when some were; with none listed twice there's nothing to Show, only Dismiss. The playlist's songs (unsearched and unavailable entries included), those listed twice and the items that aren't songs add up to the source's count. The import is kept on the phone until it's dismissed or its playlist is deleted or replaced (the key-value store, "playlistImports"; Core's `writePlaylistImports`, `readPlaylistImports`). What the page shows is Core's (`playlistImportView`).
  - **Imports from before.** Spotify imports used to search each song in the background before putting it in, with a notification, and could be stopped. An import a version from before kept going on, waiting or stopped ends once Wave starts with all-files access: the songs it had found stay as they were found, the songs it hadn't searched go in as unsearched entries in their places, and a song it gave the playlist already isn't given again. One kept by a version from before songs listed twice were named shows none. One kept by a version from before unavailable entries (kept with no version, where today's are kept as version 2) gave the playlist nothing for the songs it didn't find, so the line still names them: "N songs weren't found" (with "· M listed twice" when some were), and Show lists them under **Not found**, before any **Listed twice**; such an import done with nothing to name is gone once Wave starts.
  - **In the app log.** Each import writes one line (Core's `importLogLine`, tag `WaveImport`): the source, the name, how many songs went in and where from (the Library's, YouTube Music's, unsearched, unavailable) and how many were listed twice. An import kept from before says it ended as Wave started.
  - **Background fill.** While Wave runs, on screen or playing in the background, it searches the unsearched entries nobody has played or downloaded, so an imported playlist settles over time and its count line ("2,451 songs · N unavailable") becomes right. It has no notification and no service of its own, and stops when Android stops Wave; in the app it starts after the first screen shows its content. Core decides which song goes next, when it waits and what a failure does (`backgroundFill.ts`: `nextFillStep`, `fillAnswered`); the app runs it in `src/library/backgroundFill.ts`.
    - **Pace.** One search a second (`FILL_PACE_MS`), as the fill, the lowest asker in the search allowance: it takes none while the last 15 minutes already hold 800 searches, so 200 stay free for playing and downloads (see YT Music, Search allowance). A 2,451-song playlist settles at about one song a second until the window holds 800, then as the window frees.
    - **Downloads first.** It searches nothing while any download is queued or downloading (Core's `queuedDownloads` over the download journal), gives up a turn it was still waiting for when one starts, and goes on once the queue is empty. With YT Music off it searches nothing and looks again a minute later (`FILL_LOOK_AGAIN_MS`).
    - **Order.** The playlist opened or played from last, top to bottom, then the other playlists with unsearched entries, the most recently opened or played first; a playlist never opened or played comes after those, the newest first. Opening a playlist moves the fill to it at once (a playlist imported since the fill last read the playlists is read then). "Played from" is the play history's (`selectPlaylistsLastPlayed`); "opened" counts while Wave runs. The fill reads every playlist's unsearched entries in one query (`selectUnsearchedEntries`), drops each song it settles from what it read, and reads them again at most every 15 seconds while the playlists change.
    - **Answers.** Found and not found settle the entry as playing does (`searchUnsearchedSong`: a YT Music entry in its place in every playlist, or an unavailable entry). No notice is shown: nobody was waiting for it. The playlist page shows nothing while the fill runs, only the count line changing.
    - **Failures.** A failed search leaves the entry unsearched and puts it at the back of its playlist's line, asked again no sooner than a minute later (`FILL_RETRY_MS`), so a song whose search keeps failing never becomes unavailable and never stops the others. Offline, the entry keeps its place and the fill looks again a minute later. Only several different searches refused in a row (`FILL_REFUSALS`, 3) pause the fill, with growing pauses: 2, 5, 10 and 20 seconds, then 1, 2, 5, 10 and 15 minutes, 15 minutes again after that, each further refusal before an answer pausing it longer (`FILL_PAUSES_MS`). An answer ends the run. Each pause writes a line to the app log (tag `WaveSearch`, Core's `fillPauseLine`): "The background fill pauses for 5 s: YouTube Music refused 4 different searches in a row; it goes on at 13:14:09 UTC".
- **Spotify playlists.** The card's heading says "Import from Spotify". Wave reads the playlist as `docs/spotify-playlists.md` (#156) recommends, keyless and without an account (Core's `readSpotifyPlaylist`). It loads the playlist's embed page, which says whether the playlist is public and gives its name, cover, first 100 songs and an anonymous token. With that token it then reads every song (and its album's cover) through the web player's own `fetchPlaylist` query, 500 a page. A short link is followed first (Core's `spotifyShortLinkTarget`), and one that leads to a song or a profile says so.
  - **Limits.** `fetchPlaylist` is an internal Spotify API: it can change without notice, and its persisted-query hash is a constant in Core. When that call fails (a changed hash answers 412, a refused token 401, or Spotify throttles), the card uses the embed page's songs instead, which stop at 100, and says the playlist goes on past them. Neither source gives the ISRC, so songs are matched by title, artists and length. `fetchPlaylist` has no song limit of Wave's: a 10,000-song playlist is read whole, 20 pages. A private, deleted or unknown playlist says "This playlist doesn't exist or isn't public." Spotify out of reach, or an answer that isn't the playlist, gets Try Again. Using the internal API goes against Spotify's User Guidelines on automated access. The owner accepted that on #156, as YT Music's `youtubei` API already stands.
  - **Finding the songs.** Each song is looked for in the Library before Import (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover mixes decide). A song the Library doesn't have goes in unsearched, and is searched when it's needed or by the background fill (see YT Music, Unsearched entries, and Background fill below): one YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists. Wave takes only the first result that is the same recording, by the rules downloads use (Core's `spotifyYtMatch`). The normalized title must be the same once guest credits are set aside ("(feat. B)", Spotify's "(with B)", or a note naming only the song's artists). Each side's main artist must be credited on the other, with credits split as the Library splits them and "The" ignored. The edition notes must be the same, and so must any guests. Both lengths must be known and within 3 seconds. So a live version, a remix or another edit doesn't count, while a remaster note, an album's edition ("Deluxe") or a soundtrack's "From …" note doesn't tell recordings apart. A song with no match becomes an unavailable entry ("Not found on YouTube Music") in its place. A local file with no artist is never searched and goes in unavailable at once ("Local file on Spotify"). A song listed twice goes in once, so it's searched once. Every search waits its turn in the search allowance every automatic search shares (see YT Music, Search allowance); the Library's songs are never searched, so they spend none of it.
  - **Rate limits, measured from a PC on 2026-10-14**, when imports still searched every song. Four searches at once: a 495-song playlist took 85 seconds, about 6 searches a second, with no refusals. YouTube Music began refusing about one search in eight (HTTP 403, never more than three in a row) only after about 2,300 searches in 15 minutes, and still refused a few for over half an hour after. During that time every refused search went through within three tries. Under that throttling the 495-song playlist still finished, in 188 seconds, with 40 of 513 searches refused and all recovered. 97% of that playlist's songs were found. That is why the allowance is 1,000 in any 15 minutes, and why a 2,451-song import, which used to wait about 12 minutes near song 920, now searches nothing. YouTube Music playlist imports search nothing either and keep their own cap (`readYtPlaylist`).
  - **The card and Import.** Once read, the card shows the cover, the name and "N songs · M in your Library", as for YouTube Music, with Spotify's own count under it when some songs are listed twice or some items aren't songs ("2,451 songs on Spotify · 21 listed twice go in once"); nothing is searched. Cancel, or closing the card, stops the reading: no Spotify request starts after it. Import makes the playlist whole at once, in Spotify's order: the Library's songs as its tracks and the others unsearched (a local file with no artist unavailable), so the playlist counts as many songs as the card did. There is no progress or summary card over the screen: the playlist page shows the songs listed twice, if any (above).
- The import is a one-time copy: the playlist doesn't follow its source afterwards.
- **What is sent.** Reading a YouTube Music playlist sends YouTube Music the playlist id, as every YT Music request goes (with YouTube's visitor data and, once signed in, the session), and nothing else: not the link's other parameters, the Library or the playlists. Reading a Spotify playlist sends Spotify the playlist id, the page asked for and the token Spotify itself gave (and a short link's code, to follow it); Import sends YouTube Music nothing, and each song the Library doesn't have is searched later, when it's needed: its title and artists. Matching against the Library happens on the phone. An unsearched or unavailable entry's cover is asked of Spotify's image server when it shows, as a YT Music entry's is asked of YouTube's.
- The card opens from anywhere (`openLinkImport(text)` in `library/importPlaylistLink.tsx`). With YT Music off it says so and asks nothing.
- **Sharing to Wave.** Wave is listed in Android's share sheet for shared text and links, and for nothing else (a `SEND` intent filter for `text/plain` on the main activity, from `android.intentFilters` in `app.config.ts`). Wave also publishes an **Import to Wave** sharing shortcut, with the icon that is on, so the share sheet (Android's and Samsung's) shows it in the direct-share row at the top for shared text, without "..." or "see other apps"; another app's own share panel, such as YouTube Music's, still lists whatever that app picks. It is published each time Wave's screen opens, and again after the launcher icon is switched (Android drops a shortcut whose launcher entry is turned off); it keeps one id, so publishing again never adds a second. From Android 13 it isn't in the long-press menu on Wave's icon; before that Android has no way to keep it out. Picking it sends the same share as picking Wave from the app list. Sharing a playlist from the YouTube Music app, which shares a bare link, from the Spotify app (an `open.spotify.com` link or a `spotify.link` short link, with or without words around it), or from a browser, which adds the page title around it, opens Wave on the import card for that playlist, with Import and Cancel as from the Library. It works from a cold start and with Wave already open (the main activity is `singleTask`, so a share reaches the running Wave). Wave takes the first web address (or `spotify:` URI) in the shared text, drops the punctuation after it, and lets `recognisePlaylistLink` name the playlist (Core's `sharedTextImport`). Anything else shared (a song, an album, an artist, another site, text with no link, a bare playlist id) shows a short message saying Wave imports playlists from YouTube Music and Spotify links, and nothing is requested. A Spotify short link names its playlist only once followed, so a short link to a song opens the card, which then says it's a song. With YT Music off, any share shows a message saying to turn it on in Settings instead. Cancel only closes the card, so Wave stays where the share opened it. A share made before Wave has all-files access waits until it has (the import matches against the Library).
- **The sharing shortcut.** The `withShareShortcut` config plugin writes `res/xml/shortcuts.xml` with a share target for `text/plain` into `MainActivity`, and points every launcher icon's alias at it (`android.app.shortcuts` meta-data: Android reads it only from launcher entries, and MainActivity isn't one). `ShareShortcut` pushes the dynamic shortcut with `ShortcutManagerCompat`, long-lived, in the share target's category, tied to the launcher alias that is on. Picking it sends `MainActivity` the share target's `SEND` intent, which arrives like any other share.
- **How a share arrives.** `WaveNativePackage` passes the main activity's intents to `SharedTextInbox`: the one that started it (not when the activity is recreated, and not a share Android replays when Wave is reopened from Recents) and each new one. The inbox keeps the latest shared text until JS takes it with `WaveNative.takeSharedText()`, and `onShareReceived` tells JS a share came in. The root layout's `useOpenSharedText` takes it on start and on each event, and `openSharedText(text)` opens the card or the message.

### Importing BlackPlayer stats

- Settings › Library › Import BlackPlayer Stats opens the file picker for any file type, several at once, and adds the plays in BlackPlayer's track-stat files (`BlackPlayer EX Track Stat Autosave.bpstat`) to the play history. Each file is a cumulative snapshot: one song per line, `playCount;unknown;title;artist;album;path;addedAtMs;lastPlayedAtMs`, UTF-8. The second field isn't understood and is ignored. A title, album or path can hold a `;` of its own.
- **Dating.** Snapshots are ordered by content: a snapshot's time is its latest last play. The plays a song gained between two consecutive snapshots are spread evenly over that interval (from when it was added, if later); those already in the earliest are spread from when it was added to that snapshot's time. No play is dated after the song's last play. A song's count is the highest any snapshot gave, so a lower count later adds nothing.
- **Matching.** A row is the Library song at its path (case-insensitively, as Android's storage is), else the one with its title and artist as `libraryFinder` compares a YT Music song's, so a moved file still matches. A row has no length, so it can't tell recordings apart: when several Library songs have its title and artist it takes the only one on its album, and otherwise counts as not in the Library rather than guess.
- **Imported plays** are counted plays, listened for the song's length, with no play context, and marked `imported_from = 'blackplayer'`. They count in Stats, Home's Most Played, the artist page's Stats block and play counts like Wave's own. An import adds to each song only the plays beyond those it already has from BlackPlayer, dated after the latest of them, so importing the same or newer snapshots again only adds what's new. They are never the play Wave picks back up after a restart.
- The summary (the M3U import's card) says how many new plays and songs were imported and lists the songs that matched nothing in the Library, and the files that weren't track stats. Core's `blackPlayer.ts` decides all of this.

### App log

- Wave keeps a log on the phone so a friend can send what went wrong. It records unhandled JS errors and promise rejections with their stack, the errors React reports (a render error shows a screen with Try Again and Export Logs instead of closing Wave), every `console.warn` and `console.error`, and a line each time Wave starts. A message keeps 8,000 characters at most; an object given to `console` is written as JSON, eight levels deep at most, and read only as far as those characters go, so logging a huge one costs no more than a small one (Core's `describeLogArgs`).
- It's one UTF-8 file in Wave's private storage, `files/logs/wave.log`, kept across restarts. Each entry is `<UTC time, ISO 8601 with ms> <I|W|E|F> <tag>: <message>`; a stack goes on the lines after, each indented by two spaces, so only an entry's first line starts with a time (Core's `formatLogEntry`). Native code writes the same shape to the same file (`AppLogFile`).
- Native code logs there too, from the start of every process, before JS (`NativeLog`, started at Application.onCreate). Its warnings go through `WaveLog` (`WaveLog.w(tag, message, error?)`), which writes to logcat and to the log under the same tags (`WaveDownloads`, `WaveYtPoToken`, `WaveAuto`, `WaveResume`, …), redacted the way Core's `redactSecrets` does (`AppLogEntry`, a Kotlin port to keep in step with Core). A call never waits for the file: entries queue for one writer thread of Wave's log (`AppLogWriter`), so a caller on the main thread isn't held up by JS reading or trimming the log; a fatal entry, and Export Logs, write what's queued first. Past 1,000 waiting entries, new ones are dropped and a line says how many.
- An uncaught Kotlin exception, which is also how a fatal JS error ends a release build, is written with its thread and stack before the process dies, and the next launch starts with "The previous session ended in a crash". Each launch also writes what Android recorded about sessions that ended badly since the last launch (`ApplicationExitInfo`; only the latest on the first launch): a native crash, an ANR with the main thread's stack, a failed start, too many resources. Quitting, swiping away and other ordinary ends say nothing.
- It records YouTube Music's search allowance (see YT Music, Search allowance), a few lines per event and never one per search answered. Each wait for it writes a line as it starts and one as it ends (tag `WaveSearch`, Core's `searchWaitLine`): who waited (the song about to play, a download, Try Again, a Discover mix, the background fill), from when until when (UTC, as the log's own times), and how many searches the last 15 minutes held. Each failed search writes who asked, the search and how it failed: offline, YT Music off, or failed (`searchFailedLine`). Each pause the background fill takes after refusals writes how long, why and when it goes on (`fillPauseLine`; see "Importing playlists from a link", Background fill). Native code writes what it saw of the same failure on a line of its own through `WaveLog` (tag `WaveYtMusic`, `YtFailure.failedLine`): the HTTP status YouTube answered, or the exception's class and message, so a failure in the background is logged however slow JS is. Every failed Songs or Artists search gets that native line, a search typed in Search included. A link import has a line of its own (see "Importing playlists from a link", In the app log).
- Android ending a foreground service on its time limit (Android 15+ limits data-sync services: the playlist import, downloads, and lyrics and genre services) writes a line with how long the service ran (`ServiceTimeout`).
- While Wave is on screen (one of its activities started), a watchdog pings the main thread and the JS thread once a second (`FreezeWatchdog`); in the background, background playback included, it doesn't run; one that doesn't answer for 3 s is written as a freeze, with the route showing and, for the main thread, its stack, then again with how long it was stuck once it answers. A tick that comes 3 s late means Android paused the whole process, which isn't a freeze.
- Release builds keep Wave's exception class names, and source files and line numbers, through R8, so the log's stacks stay readable. Settings › Developer has debug-build triggers for a Kotlin crash and a 5 s JS freeze.
- Past 2 MiB the oldest entries go, whole, until it's down to 1.5 MiB (Core's `trimLog`).
- Passwords, tokens, cookies, Authorization headers, bearer tokens, JWTs and a URL's password never reach it (Core's `redactSecrets`); song titles, artists and paths can.
- Entries wait in memory and are written together every two seconds, off the JS thread, and whenever Wave leaves the screen; a fatal error is written at once.
- Settings › Developer › Export Logs shares it through the share sheet as `wave-log-<date>-<version>.txt`, which starts with Wave's version and build, Android's version, the phone and its font scale (Core's `logHeader`).

Fog ground in light (`#e0ecef`), true black in dark (`#000000`); on it, dark grouped areas (`#0a1113`, a ramp step only dark mode uses, so light mode's text is untouched), cards and fills, and separators climb one ramp step each. Every neutral comes from one tonal ramp; teal accent, ember highlight. Text, accent and highlight meet WCAG AA on the ground and cards in both themes. ### Build and CI

- GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) on every PR and on `main` and `ti`. Checks installs, typechecks, lints, checks formatting and runs the tests; on PRs it typechecks and tests only the packages the PR changed and the ones that depend on them. wave-native's Kotlin unit tests run in their own job beside the APK build, and on PRs only when something they test changed. The Android APK job builds the APK I install on the phone, uploads it as a build artifact and publishes it to a prerelease (`pr-<n>`, or `latest` for `main`). On a PR it skips the build when nothing in the APK changed, and repacks when only JS changed since a kept full build with the same native side: it swaps the new JS bundle into that APK without Gradle (`scripts/ci/repack.ts`). Anything else builds in full, and so do pushes to `main` and stable release tags. What counts as changed is the change rule in `scripts/ci/changes.ts`. Each job posts a sticky PR comment with its step durations; the Android APK one says whether the build was skipped, repacked or full.
- **Releases.** Wave's version lives only in the `version` field of `apps/mobile/package.json` (`major.minor.patch`, optionally ending in `-ti`, minor and patch under 100), which `app.config.ts` reads. Android's versionCode follows from it (major × 10000 + minor × 100 + patch, so 0.5.0 is 500), so each version installs over the one before and keeps the Library and settings. Nobody bumps or tags a version by hand: every push to `main` or `ti` that changes the app releases the next one (`ti`'s end in `-ti`, see Test branch and prereleases). The CI's Version job works it out first (`scripts/ci/next-version.ts`): the highest version on any `v*` tag (a `-ti` tag counts by its base), bumped by the PRs merged since the channel's previous tag. The PRs that count are the ones that channel's patch notes list (see Patch notes), so a PR that changes only CI, docs or specs doesn't, less any whose merge commit a `v*` tag of either channel already contains: the shared counter already bumped for it, so a `feat/` PR released on `ti` doesn't bump the minor again when `ti` reaches `main`. If any of them has a branch starting with `feat/`, the bump is minor and the patch goes back to 0 (0.5.3 becomes 0.6.0); otherwise it's a patch bump. The Version job's log names the PR that made the bump minor, or says it's a patch. Major is never automatic. Both branches count from these same tags. A version in `package.json` already above every tag is released as it is, which is how a major bump made by hand ships; with no version tag yet, the one in `package.json` is the first. A patch bump that would reach 100 fails the Version job and asks for a minor or major bump by hand, and a minor bump that would reach 100 asks for a major one. The Version job writes the version into `apps/mobile/package.json`, commits that file alone to the pushed branch as the workflow bot ("Release Wave <version>") and pushes it before anything is checked, together with its `v<version>` tag in one atomic push: both land or neither does (`scripts/ci/bump-version.sh`). The tag takes the number as soon as it's picked, so the next bump on either branch counts past it, even while this run is still building. The rest of the run checks, builds and publishes that commit, so its APK reports the new version. The workflow's token pushes the commit and the tag, so neither starts another run. A push whose changes since its branch's previous release (on `main`, the newest stable tag in its history) are only CI, docs or specs, by the change rule that also leaves PRs out of the patch notes (`scripts/ci/changes.ts`), or only the `version` in `apps/mobile/package.json` (the other branch's version bump merged in), makes no commit and no release: it publishes only the rolling `latest` (or `ti-latest`) build, and the Version job's log says why. Runs on `main` or `ti` don't cancel each other: one runs at a time, the newest push waits, and a push replaced while it waits is released by the next run, since that counts from the previous release. Since both branches share the counter, the Version job also runs one at a time across `main` and `ti`, so a `main` push and a `ti` push close together never pick the same number. If the branch moves on, or the tag is taken, before the bump is pushed, the Version job starts over from the new tip, so the bump commit always lands on top; a push rejected for any other reason fails the job with git's error. A bump whose run fails after the push keeps its tag but has no release: re-running the run's failed jobs publishes it, and so does re-running all of them, since a branch tip that already carries its channel's tag is released as it is, with nothing pushed; the next push to the branch releases the next number. The release job then publishes, on that tag, the GitHub Release "Wave <version>" with that run's `wave-<version>.apk` and patch notes. A stable one becomes the repo's latest release; a `-ti` one is a prerelease and leaves Latest alone. If `v<version>` has since been moved to another commit, the release job publishes nothing, with a warning. Pushing `v<version>` by hand still works (`git tag v0.5.0 <commit> && git push origin v0.5.0`): CI checks the tag against the app's version and that it's on its release branch before building (a mismatch fails the Checks job and says which version the app is at), then publishes as above. Releases are signed with the same debug keystore as every other build. In `.github/workflows/ci.yml` a hand-pushed tag's run has `IS_RELEASE == 'true'`, and a stable release always builds fully. Release publication waits for the APK, native tests and patch notes; when the Patch notes job fails, the release job fails instead of publishing without them. PR builds (`pr-<n>`) and main's rolling `latest` prerelease are unchanged.
- **Test branch and prereleases.** Daily test work targets `ti`, created from `main`. Its app version ends in `-ti`, starting at `0.5.1-ti` (Android versionName `0.5.1-ti`, versionCode 501). Every push to `ti` that changes the app releases the next test version, the way `main` releases (see Releases). The next `ti` version comes from the same counter as `main`'s: the highest version on any `v*` tag, stable or `-ti` (counted by its base), bumped by the PRs merged since `ti`'s previous release as `main`'s is, with the `-ti` suffix. So the sequence runs like 0.5.4-ti, 0.5.5, 0.5.6-ti, and every new APK from either channel has a higher versionCode than the ones before it and installs over them. The bot commits "Release Wave 0.5.6-ti" to `ti`'s `apps/mobile/package.json` and tags it `v0.5.6-ti` in the same push, and the run publishes the GitHub prerelease "Wave 0.5.6-ti" with `wave-0.5.6-ti.apk`, without replacing stable Latest. `ti`'s previous release, which its changes and patch notes count from, is the newest `-ti` tag in its history, or the newest stable one before `ti`'s first. A push that changes only CI, docs or specs since then, or only the version (merging `main` brings its bot bump), makes no commit and no release. Merging `ti` into `main` releases `main`'s next number, a patch bump unless it brings a `feat/` PR no tag holds yet; it doesn't promote ti's version. A conflict on the version line during that merge can be resolved either way, because CI overwrites the version. Pushes to `ti` also publish the rolling `ti-latest` prerelease with `wave-ti.apk`. A hand-pushed `v<version>-ti` tag still publishes too, as long as it names the app version and belongs to `ti`. ti keeps fast builds: ti pushes, ti tags and PRs may repack an exact-key kept APK. Since tags cannot read ti's branch-scoped cache, successful full CI pushes on ti also upload the APK and debug keystore as a `kept-<native key>` artifact for 30 days. A ti tag or PR can restore that artifact only from this repository's successful CI push on ti (`scripts/ci/kept-apk.ts`); a miss or failed download builds fully. The key includes native inputs and Metro resources but not the app version, so a version bump alone doesn't need a full build. The repack writes the run's versionCode and versionName (prebuild's, from `android/app/build.gradle`) into the kept APK's compiled `AndroidManifest.xml` and into Expo's embedded app config, `assets/app.config`, then signs the APK again (`scripts/ci/apk-version.ts`). Main and ti save Gradle, Metro and C++ caches; PRs and tags only read them. Both stable and ti releases wait for native tests before publication. The `-ti` suffix doesn't change the numeric versionCode; the shared counter keeps each release's above the last.
- **Patch notes.** Each release's notes say what's new and what's fixed since the previous version: the PRs merged since the previous tag in that release channel, under "New" and "Fixes" (an empty section is left out), one line per PR, the PR's title linked to the PR. A PR is a fix when its branch starts with `fix/` or an issue it closes has the `bug` label. PRs that change only CI, docs or specs (by the change rule, `scripts/ci/changes.ts`) aren't listed, nor is a feature branch's PR into `main` when its tickets' PRs into that branch are. So PR titles are written as one line a user understands. Stable notes exclude `-ti` tags; ti notes use the previous `-ti` tag, falling back to the last stable tag for the first ti version. The version bump counts from the same previous tag (`scripts/ci/previous-tag.ts` picks it for both). The PRs come from GitHub's search (`scripts/ci/merged-prs.sh`), which lags merges by a minute or so; a commit on the branch's first-parent line that no PR it found was merged by is asked about directly (`commits/<sha>/pulls`), so the PR a push just merged still counts. With no previous version tag (including 0.5.0), notes are the hand-written `docs/first-release-notes.md`. `scripts/ci/patch-notes.sh` builds them (`patch-notes.ts` decides); the CI's Patch notes job shows them in every run's summary, and a run that releases publishes them. A push that releases already has its own tag on its version bump, so its notes leave that tag out and count from the previous tag of its channel, the same as a hand-pushed tag's.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test exercises a module only through its public interface and asserts observable behaviour (resulting queue, counted/not counted, search results order, reconciled changes) — never internals. Tests should survive refactors of the implementation.
- **Primary seam — Core.** Every decision-heavy rule lives in Core and is tested there with `node:test`, run directly by Node's TypeScript type-stripping. Because Core has zero dependencies, these tests run anywhere, including environments without package-registry access. This is where most tests live.
- **Library store.** Migrations and the Home/play-count queries are tested in CI against a real SQLite database through Drizzle, seeded with fixture data.
- **Sync service.** Its routes are tested in CI against a local libSQL database. Merging is Core's, so the rules themselves are tested there.
- **Native module.** Kotlin JVM unit tests in CI for tag reading (small fixture audio files per format) and for scan diffing; Media3's test utilities for the player command/event mapping where practical.
- **UI.** No automated UI tests in v1. Each ticket has acceptance criteria verified manually on the S24+ using the CI-built APK.
- Prior art: none yet (new repository). The first ticket establishes the test setup for each seam.

## Out of Scope

- v2 features: equalizer and presets, ReplayGain, tag editor, smart playlists, M3U export, Last.fm scrobbling, home-screen widgets, album artwork download, statistics/metrics screens (v1 only records the data).
- iOS, desktop, web.
- Streaming services other than YouTube Music (see YT Music), cloud libraries, network shares. Spotify is only read for playlists to import, and nothing streams from it.
- Uploading audio to the Wave account, a web or desktop view of it, sharing between accounts, and signing in with anything but Google.
- Play Store distribution.
- Chromecast / casting; Android Auto beyond what Media3 provides for free. Android Auto is one of the two primary listening scenes, so a browsable Auto library is the first v2 candidate.
- Localisation (English only).

## Further Notes

- **Glass on Android** (no longer a default material; see Visual design) **was the main technical risk.** Sampling the native views behind a surface is not straightforward in React Native Skia. The first UI work is a spike comparing approaches (for example, Android `RenderEffect` blur with an AGSL refraction shader versus a Skia snapshot of the backdrop) for visual quality and 120 Hz performance on the S24+. Acceptable fallback if refraction is too costly: blur + tint + specular rim highlight.
- **Track identity** on the phone is the file path, with moved/renamed files matched by Core's reconciliation so history survives. Between phones it's the recording key (see Wave account and sync).
- **Development environment constraint:** the agent session working on this repository cannot reach the npm registry or the Android/Gradle repositories. GitHub Actions is the build and test environment of record; Core's zero-dependency design keeps its tests runnable inside the agent session as well.
- Library sizes in the tens of thousands of tracks are the design target for scan batching, list virtualisation and query indexes.
