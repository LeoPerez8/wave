# Wave v1 — Spec

Status: draft for review · Owner: @LeoPerez8

## Problem Statement

I keep my music as local files on my Android phone (Samsung Galaxy S24+). BlackPlayer has the features I want from a local player — folder-based library, gapless playback, lyrics, sleep timer, an equalizer and a tag editor — but its look and interaction model feel dated next to Apple Music, whose structure, gestures and "Liquid Glass" materials I much prefer. Apple Music, on the other hand, does not exist as a local-file player on Android. No app gives me both.

## Solution

**Wave** is a personal, Android-only local music player with a BlackPlayer-grade local-file engine and an interface of its own. Apple Music (iOS 26 era) is the reference for how it should feel, not a template to copy. It indexes the music folders I choose, plays them gaplessly through a native audio engine with full system integration (notification, lock screen, Bluetooth), and records every play so that rich listening statistics can be built later.

v1 delivers the core player and library. v2 adds the advanced audio and library-management features (see Out of Scope).

## Glossary

- **Track** — one audio file in the library.
- **Album** — tracks grouped by album title + album artist.
- **Credit** — a track's artist tag as written ("Ariana Grande, Doja Cat"); rows show it as is. A tag with several values (Vorbis comments, ID3v2.4) gives one credit per value, shown joined with commas; separate values always name separate artists.
- **Artist** — one artist a credit names. A track belongs to every artist its credit names, so the Artists list, Search, Android Auto and artist photos list individual artists, and a collaboration counts for, and shows on the page of, each of them. "feat." / "ft." / "featuring" (bracketed or not) always separate artists. `,` `·` `;` `/` "with" " x " "&" and "and" separate them too, unless that part of the credit is an artist in the Library on its own: some track's whole credit (or the part of it before a feat.) is exactly that name and none of its names is credited alone anywhere (alone, or featuring someone). So "Earth, Wind & Fire", "AC/DC", "Tyler, The Creator" and "Simon & Garfunkel" stay whole, and "Ariana Grande, Doja Cat" splits once "Ariana Grande" has a song of her own. A collaboration whose artists have no other song stays one artist until one of them does. Names are trimmed, and a name credited twice counts once (case and spacing aside). Core's `creditSplitter` decides; the split is recomputed when a scan or a deletion changes the Library's credits. Listening stats follow the credit too: a counted play of a collaboration counts in full for each artist it names (Stats' artist rankings, Home's Most played artists, the artist page's Stats block), while album rankings and Library-wide totals count it once. The artist page's "X of N songs" counts both over the songs whose credit names the artist (not songs that are only on an album filed under them). A deleted song's plays keep counting for every artist it named while that artist is in the Library.
- **Primary artist** — the first artist a credit names.
- **Go to Artist** — on a song or album credited to one artist, Go to Artist (the long-press menu, the selection bar) and the artist lines of Now Playing and an album's page open that artist. Credited to several, they open the artist choice card, listing each credited artist in credit order; picking one opens their page. The artist page you're on is left out, and a choice of one opens straight away. In selection, Go to Artist shows only when every checked item has the same set of artists. Core's `itemActions` and `artistLink` decide. A streamed song (a YT Music song, not downloaded) links to its artists too while YT Music is on, Now Playing's artist line and Go to Artist in the YT Music song's menu, and opens them integrated, with no Show on YouTube Music tap: a name the Library has (compared as artist photos compare names) opens their Library page, any other the YouTube Music artist page. Credited to several, the artist choice card lists them in credit order, the YouTube Music ones with "YouTube Music" under the name. The artist page you're on is left out here too, a YouTube Music artist page included (by the name it was opened with); when it's the song's only artist, the menu has no Go to Artist and Now Playing's artist line just closes Now Playing to reveal it, as for a Library song. With YT Music off the link is gone. Core's `streamedArtistLink` decides.
- **Album artist** — the artist an album is filed under: the first artist its album artist credit names (the credit falls back to the track's; "Various Artists" for compilations). The album shows under that artist's Albums and under the other credited artists' Appears on.
- **Genre** — an alternative grouping of tracks.
- **Folder** — a directory on the phone. The index records the folder each track is in, and scan roots and excluded folders are folders, but the Library doesn't browse by folder.
- **Playlist** — a user-made, ordered list of tracks; with YT Music, it can also hold YT Music songs the library doesn't have yet (YT Music entries), streamed until the library has them.
- **Scan root** — a folder Wave indexes; **Excluded folder** — a folder under a root that Wave ignores.
- **Library scan** — walking the scan roots and reconciling files with the index (full on first run, incremental afterwards).
- **Play context** — what a playback was started from (album, artist, playlist, genre, search, all songs, favorites, home section). Plays recorded before the Library lost its Folders entry may say folder; they load and count like any other.
- **Queue** — the ordered list of tracks to be played, including the current one and **Up Next**.
- **Play event** — one record per time a track starts playing, with how long it was listened to and how it ended.
- **Counted play** — a play event that counts towards play counts: listened to for more than 15 seconds, or, for a song of 15 seconds or less, played to the end. Each play event counts at most once, however long it goes on.
- **Sidecar** — a `.lrc` lyrics file beside an audio file (`Song.lrc` or `Song.mp3.lrc`).
- **Favorite** — a starred track (Apple Music's star).
- **Artist image** — the circle an artist is shown with: their **artist photo** (downloaded online, see Artist photos) when there is one, else the cover of the album that represents them, else their initial.
- **Now Playing** — the full-screen player; **Mini player** — the floating glass pill above the tab bar.
- **YT Music song** — a song found on YouTube Music rather than in the Library. It is identified by its YouTube video id (`yt:<videoId>` wherever a track id is expected, so it can't collide with a Library id), can be streamed or queued, and becomes a Track only once it's downloaded into the Library. A YT Music song the Library already has (see YT Music) is never offered as one. _Avoid_: online track, remote track, stream.

## User Stories

### First run and library

1. As a user, I want Wave to ask for all-files access on first launch, so that it can read every audio file in my music folders.
2. As a user, I want Wave to index my `Music` folder by default and let me choose the folders my music is actually in with the system folder picker, so that I get a working library whatever my folders are called.
3. As a user, I want to add and remove scan roots and exclude sub-folders in Settings, so that only the music I care about appears.
4. As a user, I want to see scan progress (files found / indexed) during the first scan, so that I know the app is working on a large library.
5. As a user, I want to start browsing while the first scan is still running, so that I don't wait for tens of thousands of files.
6. As a user, I want Wave to pick up new, changed and deleted files automatically each time I open it, so that my library stays current without a manual full rescan.
7. As a user, I want to pull to refresh in the Library, so that I can force a rescan after copying new music.
8. As a user, I want MP3, FLAC, M4A/AAC/ALAC, OGG/Opus and WAV files to be supported, so that my whole collection plays.
9. As a user, I want Wave to read title, artist, album, album artist, genre, year, track and disc number, duration, embedded artwork and embedded lyrics from tags, so that my library is organised the way I tagged it.
10. As a user, I want files without tags to still appear (using the file name as title and "Unknown Artist/Album"), so that nothing goes missing.
11. As a user, I want a renamed or moved file to keep its play history and favorite status, so that my statistics survive reorganising folders.

### Browsing (Library tab)

12. As a user, I want the Library tab to list Playlists, Artists, Albums, Songs, Genres and Favorites as entry rows like Apple Music, so that I can browse the way I think.
13. As a user, I want albums shown as an artwork grid with title and album artist, so that I can browse visually.
14. As a user, I want artists sorted alphabetically ignoring leading "The"/"A"/"An" and accents, so that sorting matches my expectations.
15. As a user, I want an album screen with large artwork, title, album artist, year, genre, track list ordered by disc and track number, and Play / Shuffle buttons, so that I can play an album properly.
16. As a user, I want an artist screen listing their albums and songs, so that I can explore an artist.
17. As a user, I want genre screens listing their tracks, so that I can browse by genre.
18. As a user, I want long lists (tens of thousands of songs) to scroll smoothly at 120 Hz, so that the app feels native on my phone.
19. As a user, I want a fast-scroll alphabet index on long lists, so that I can jump to a letter.
20. As a user, I want long-press context menus on tracks, albums, artists and playlists (Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Favorite, Go to Album/Artist), so that I can act without opening screens. The finger that long-pressed can stay down and slide onto an entry: lifting it there runs that entry, so one gesture does it (lifting anywhere else, or without sliding, leaves the menu open to tap; the list underneath holds still meanwhile).

### Playback

21. As a user, I want tapping a track to play it and queue the rest of its context after it, so that playback continues naturally.
22. As a user, I want gapless playback between consecutive tracks, so that live albums and mixes play without silence.

32. As a user, I want playback to pause when headphones are unplugged or Bluetooth disconnects, so that music doesn't blast from the speaker.
33. As a user, I want Wave to pause or duck for calls and other apps' audio and resume afterwards where appropriate, so that it behaves well with the system.
34. As a user, I want my queue, current track, position, shuffle and repeat restored (paused) when I reopen Wave, so that I can pick up where I left off.

### Mini player and Now Playing

57. As a user, I want every play recorded with start time, listened time, how it ended (completed, skipped, stopped, interrupted) and its play context, so that statistics can be computed later over my full history.

## Implementation Decisions

### Platform and distribution

- Android only. Minimum Android 12 (API 31, required for native blur), target the latest SDK. Tuned for the Galaxy S24+ (120 Hz).
- App name **Wave**, application id `dev.leoperez.wave` (permanent).
- Personal use, sideloaded APK. No Play Store, so `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` (all-files access) is acceptable.
- No backend of Wave's own. The Library works offline; the online features are listed under Network.

### Network

Wave uses the network for three features, each behind its own Settings switch (on by default) that stops every request it makes.

- **Artist photos** (Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends artist names.
- **Lyrics and genre** (LRCLIB and Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends a song's title, artist, album and duration.
- **YT Music** (YouTube Music, below): `music.youtube.com/youtubei/v1/*` for search (and, as later tickets land, browsing, song details and the player), `www.youtube.com` for the player script, visitor data and BotGuard's proof-of-origin endpoints (`api/jnn/v1/*`), `*.googlevideo.com` for audio, `*.googleusercontent.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com` and `i.ytimg.com` for covers, `accounts.google.com` for sign-in. It sends the query in Search when Search YouTube Music is tapped (and the refined query after it, until the field is cleared) or when the Library has nothing for it, the name of an artist whose page (or whose album's page) asks for its YT Music section (only when Show on YouTube Music is tapped), that artist's YouTube browse id and the id of their songs playlist (the "See all" on their page's Songs shelf) and, for an album page once that artist's answer is saved, the album's browse id, the browse id of a YouTube Music artist whose page is opened (an artist the Library doesn't have) and the id of their songs playlist, the id of a playlist opened from Discover or imported from a link (see "Importing playlists from a link"), the video ids of songs it plays or downloads, YouTube's visitor data and, once signed in, the account's session cookie. It never sends the Library's contents, play history or stats. Offline, or when YouTube fails, the YT Music parts show a quiet line and the Library works as before.
  - Importing a Spotify playlist sits under the same switch, since its songs become YT Music entries. It sends Spotify the playlist id (`open.spotify.com/embed/playlist/<id>`, then `api-partner.spotify.com/pathfinder/v2/query` with the playlist's URI, the page asked for and the anonymous token the embed page gave) and, for a shared short link, its code (`spotify.link`, `spotify.app.link`). The card shows the cover from `i.scdn.co` or `image-cdn-ak.spotifycdn.com`. It sends YouTube Music one Songs search per song the Library doesn't have: the song's title and artists. Nothing about the Library, play history or stats goes to either (see "Importing playlists from a link").

### Artist photos

- Wave downloads a photo of each artist from **Deezer's open API** (no account or key): it searches the artist's name and takes the best match's picture. Artist names are the only thing sent. A "Download artist photos" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no requests and artists show their album artwork. "Clear artist photos" deletes the downloaded ones.
- **Matching** is Core's (`artistPhoto.ts`): names compare with case, accents, a leading "The", punctuation and "&"/"and" ignored; only an exact match counts, and of namesakes the most followed one (its photo or none). "Various Artists", "Unknown Artist" and names without letters are never looked up.
- **Caching.** Lookups run in the background after each library scan, three at a time, and stop after a few failures in a row (offline). Photos are saved beside the album artwork (`files/artwork/artists/`) as a thumbnail (lists, menus) and a large image (artist page), and are never fetched again. A lookup with no match is retried after a week, a failed one after a day (`artist_photos` table, by artist key).
- **Fallback.** Until a photo arrives, without a match, offline or with downloads off, the artist's album-artwork image shows.

### Lyrics and genre

- **Find Lyrics and Genre** (a song's long-press menu and the selection bar) looks a song up online and writes what it finds into the file, replacing the lyrics and genre there and filling in its track number (with the disc) and year when the file has none. A file that has them keeps its values, so a wrong match can't renumber an album tagged by hand. Settings › Lyrics and genre runs it on the whole Library in the background, with progress and Cancel, and ends with a summary: updated, not found, failed, and how many songs were skipped. It stops early when the services can't be reached (three lookups failing in a row).
- **The whole-Library run goes on in the background**, the way YT Music downloads do: a foreground service (`LibraryLookupService`, data sync) keeps Wave running with it in the background and the screen off, also while music plays, and shows a "Lyrics and genre" notification with the progress ("Finding lyrics and genre… N of M") and Cancel. The run itself is JavaScript; while it goes on, Wave's JS runtime is held (`JsRuntime`, as for downloads and Android Auto) along with a partial wake lock, so JS timers and lookups keep going without the Activity. Settings shows the same progress and Cancel, also when Wave is reopened while the run still goes on. Cancel in either place, or turning "Find lyrics and genre online" off, stops it; songs already done keep what was written. When it ends, the notification shows the summary, and Wave shows it too, at once when it's on screen, otherwise the next time it is (the summary is kept until it's seen; seeing it takes the notification away). Android 13+ asks for notification permission at the first run; without it the run still goes on, with the notification hidden. Android 15 caps data-sync services at six hours a day; past that the notification goes and the run carries on while Wave stays alive.
- **Sources.** Lyrics come from **LRCLIB** (`lrclib.net`, free, no key): synced (LRC) lyrics are preferred, plain lyrics are taken when that's all there is. The genre is that of the song's album on **Deezer's open API**: a track search, then the matched album's first genre. The same album gives the year (of its release date), and its tracklist gives the song's track number, plus the disc for an album on more than one disc. The tracklist is fetched only for a song without a track number. Albums and tracklists are fetched once per album in a session, and a whole-Library run spends at least 400 ms on each song, which keeps its three Deezer requests at most (search, album, tracklist) under Deezer's limit of 50 every 5 seconds. A song's **title, artist, album and duration** are the only things sent, and "Unknown Artist" / "Various Artists" songs are never looked up. A "Find lyrics and genre online" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no lookups and the menu entry is gone.
- **Matching** is Core's (`lyricsAndGenre.ts`): titles and artists compare like artist photos (case, accents, punctuation, a leading "The" ignored), plus featured artists ("feat. X") and version notes in brackets or after a dash ("(Live)", "- Remastered 2011"). Lyrics must be within 2 seconds of the song's length; then synced beats plain, the song's own album beats another, and the closest length wins. The genre's album is the matched track's, preferring the song's own album, then the closest length. The year and track number are taken only when that album is the song's own (titles compared as above), since a compilation or live record numbers and dates the song differently; a song without an album tag gets neither. A release date that's missing or malformed ("0000-00-00") gives no year. No match leaves that field alone; no match at all leaves the file untouched.
- **Writing** is native (`SongTagWriter`, jaudiotagger): only the lyrics, genre, track number, disc number and year fields change, and the audio and every other tag stay as they were (JVM tests check this for MP3, FLAC, M4A, Ogg Vorbis and WAV). Core decides what is sent to it (`tagsToWrite`: the track number and year only when the indexed song lacks them), and the writer checks the file too: a track number or year the file already has is kept, and the disc number is only written with the track number. A WAV file's lyrics and disc number go into an ID3 chunk (RIFF INFO has no field for them) and its genre, track number and year into both. A format Wave can't write tags to (Opus, raw AAC) gets the lyrics in a sidecar `.lrc` and no genre, track number or year, and the summary lists those songs. A track number or year it can't take isn't a failure on its own; a song whose track number and year were all that was written counts as updated. A sidecar already beside a file is replaced too, since it would win over the tags in the Lyrics view.
- **Skipping.** The whole-Library run looks up only songs missing lyrics, a genre, a track number or a year (Core's `libraryLookupSongs`); songs with all four are skipped and counted. A song it picks only for lacking a track number or year is filled in, as a download is: it gets the track number (with the disc) and year it lacks, and its lyrics and genre are left alone. A song it picks for lacking lyrics or a genre gets both replaced, as from a menu (Core's `libraryTagsToWrite`). Run it again after adding music and it does the new and incomplete songs, plus any it found nothing for last time. Progress ("N of M") counts only those songs, and the summary says how many it skipped. Lyrics count whether they're in the tags or in a sidecar `.lrc`. The index records this per song (`tracks.has_lyrics`): scans set it, listing each folder's `.lrc` files once, and so do Find Lyrics and Genre's writes. Wave doesn't notice another app adding or deleting a sidecar until the song's own file changes. The update that added the column marks every indexed file as changed, so the next scan reads them all once. From a menu or the selection bar, Find Lyrics and Genre still looks up every song it's given.
- **After writing** the file is read again as a scan reads it and its index entry updated, so Genres, Search, the album page (track order, and the year when the album had none) and an open Lyrics view show the new values without a rescan.
- **Downloads.** The fill-in after a download writes only what the file lacks: lyrics, genre, track number and year alike. A YT Music download's tags take the track number and year from its album page, and Deezer's stand in when the page doesn't give them.

### Lyrics on Android Auto

- **The Lyrics toggle** sits in the overflow menu of Auto's player screen ("Lyrics on" / "Lyrics off"), off on a fresh install and remembered between drives.
- **Lyrics are drawn into the artwork** (the owner's pick in #150). Auto draws the player screen itself, and Wave controls only the title, the artist and the artwork image there, so with the toggle on and synced lyrics Wave hands Auto a square image of three lines over the cover dimmed: the line being sung big and bold in the middle, the lines before and after it smaller and dimmed. The split-screen card shows it in its top area, and the full-screen player beside the title. The title and artist stay as they are, and Auto's progress bar and buttons are untouched. A sung line too wide for the image shrinks, then wraps onto two rows, then ends in "…"; lines sung together share one line (`Me / You`). The rows stay in the middle band of the square, in case Auto crops it to fit the card.
- **What shows when** is Core's call (`autoLyricLines`): which three lines, and when the next change is due. The neighbours skip pauses, so the line after a pause already shows below. The cover is back with the toggle off, with Auto disconnected, for a track with only plain lyrics or none (a streamed YT Music song has none), before the first line and in a pause between lines (an empty LRC line).
- **Timing.** JS follows the player and wakes at each line's start (`startAutoLyricLines`), sending the lines only when they change, so Auto gets one new image per line, as it got one new text line before. The playback service hands the session a new artwork URI per set of lines (`content://<package>.artwork/lyrics/<id>`, `LyricArtworks`); Wave's artwork provider draws the image (`LyricArtPainter`, 800 px JPEG) the first time Auto or the notification reads it, and keeps the latest four.
- **The phone** shares the artwork with Auto: while Auto is connected with the toggle on, the notification and lock screen show the lyrics image as the cover. Once Auto disconnects (or the toggle goes off) the service drops the lines at once, without waiting for JS, and they get the cover back. The artist line is never touched.

### YT Music

- YouTube Music has no official API. Wave talks to it the way Metrolist does: through **innertubex** (MetrolistGroup/innertubex, from JitPack), from the native module. innertubex sends requests and handles sessions, ciphers and stream extraction; reading the answers into songs is Wave's (`YtSearchParser`, after Metrolist's page parsers). Metrolist is the reference for anything the library leaves to the app.
- **Licence.** innertubex is GPL-3.0, and so is any Metrolist code Wave ports, so the APK that ships them is a GPL-3.0 combined work.
- **Wave is its own client, not YouTube's app.** Nothing YouTube serves as an ad is ever requested, shown or played (Wave plays the audio stream itself), there is no Premium upsell or nag, and nothing is limited because the account isn't Premium; the only difference is the audio bitrate YouTube's servers send. Wave doesn't report plays to YouTube.
- **Search.** When Search asks YouTube Music, it sends the query and nothing else, as three searches at once: "Songs", "Artists" and "Albums" (which lists singles and EPs too). What they find that the Library doesn't have joins the Library's results in the Songs, Artists and Albums groups, after the Library's rows (making the group when the Library has nothing of its kind), and the groups' counts, previews and Show all cover both. A song shows its artwork, title, artists · album and length. An artist shows their picture and name over "YouTube Music", and a tap opens the YT Music artist page. An album shows its cover, title and artists; a tap opens the YT Music album page, and a long-press opens the YT Music album menu with Download Album. An artist the Library has (named as artist photos compare names, Core's `libraryArtistFinder`) isn't repeated, nor is an album that one of the artists it credits has in the Library under its title (matched as the artist page matches releases, Core's `libraryReleaseFinder`): the Library's row already shows. The top result is the Library's best match when there is one. Otherwise it's YouTube Music's best: the result shown that matches the query best as Search ranks the Library's, or, when none matches word for word, YouTube Music's first song (then album, then artist). While Search shows YouTube Music's results it's an integrated view, so a Library album opened from it opens integrated. While YT Music's songs are among the results, the Library's show a small downloaded icon before their artist. Tapping any song plays the song results from it, the Library's files and YT Music's streamed. Typing searches the Library only: while it has results, a **Search YouTube Music** text button follows them, and nothing is asked until it's tapped. A tap asks for the current query at once; from then on the results stay merged while the query is refined, each new query asked once typing pauses (300 ms), until the field is cleared (or holds a single character), when the button is back. A query the Library has nothing for asks on its own, once typing pauses; once the Library has results again, that view ends and the button returns. Asking never blocks typing or the Library's results, keeps the last results up while the next query loads (with no YouTube Music top result until the new answer arrives, since they answer the last query) and drops answers to queries no longer typed. Under the results one quiet line says it's searching, that YouTube Music has nothing for the query or nothing the Library lacks, or that it's offline or YouTube failed; if any of the three searches fails, the line says so and none of their results show (`ytSectionView`). With nothing in the Library matching and nothing from YouTube Music, a "nothing in your library" line shows. With YT Music off there is no button and nothing is asked. When Search asks, and who asked, is Core's (`stepYtSection`).
- **Already in the Library.** A YT Music song is left out when a Library song has its title (case, accents, punctuation and a "feat." credit aside), shares an artist with it (credits split as the Library splits them, Core's `splitCredit`) and, when both lengths are known, is as long within 3 seconds, so a live take or an edit still shows (Core's `libraryMatcher`).
- **Streaming.** Tapping a YT Music result plays the section's songs from it; its long-press menu has Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist and Go to Artist (and Download, below), so YT Music songs sit in the queue beside Library tracks (as `yt:<videoId>` track ids). Wherever playback shows (mini player, Now Playing with its tint, notification, lock screen, Bluetooth, Auto) a streamed song behaves like a Library track, except that it has no star, album link or lyrics; its artist line opens its artists integrated (Go to Artist, above). Its artists, as YouTube Music credits them, are kept with its details for that. The playback service streams a `yt:` item when the player loads it (`YtDataSource`), so the address is fresh at play time, mid-queue and after a cold start: `YtStreamResolver` asks innertubex's extractor for the best audio YouTube serves the account (a plain address: no HLS, SABR or ranged-only clients), with the proof-of-origin token YouTube's web client needs, minted by BotGuard in a hidden web view as Metrolist does (`YtPoTokens`), and visitor data kept across launches. Addresses are reused until ten minutes before they expire; one googlevideo rejects (403/410) is resolved again, twice at most, skipping the client it came from. The cover is cached beside the Library's artwork, so the notification, Auto and Now Playing's tint work as for a Library track. Wave never reports plays to YouTube, and streamed plays aren't recorded (Core's `trackPlays`), so play counts and stats stay about the Library. A song that can't stream (offline, YT Music off, a song YouTube won't serve) pauses playback with a line saying why in place of the artist (Core's `playerErrorNotice`); skipping on plays the next song. The queue's YT Music songs' details are saved with it, so a cold start restores them, unless YT Music is off (Core's `restorableTrack`, `queuedYtTracks`).
- **Downloads.** The existing YT Music song menu keeps Download, Cancel Download and Retry Download, with compact row progress. Each download has a source, which Core decides when it's asked for and again when it starts (`downloadSource`): Soulseek while Settings has a Soulseek account, else YouTube Music while YT Music is on. With neither, the row fails with "Connect YT Music or Soulseek in Settings" and keeps Retry. **When Soulseek can't deliver the song**, the same job carries on from YouTube Music while YT Music is on, and the owner doesn't tap anything. That happens when Soulseek has no 320 kbps copy of the recording or every selected peer failed, when it rejects the account (or the account is signed out mid-download, as a new download would then come from YouTube Music), when its server can't be reached while the phone is online, and when the Soulseek attempt uses up its 20 minutes. Being offline, full storage, an unwritable folder, a failure while saving and Cancel don't fall back: YouTube would fail the same way, or the owner asked to stop. Core decides which failures fall back (`downloadFallback`). The prepare answer gives the journal Core's plan for the source's failures (`downloadFailurePlan`: which are tried again after a pause, which end the job, which fall back, and what the job fails with once nothing is left to try) and the M4A's place, so the journal switches the job's source where the failure happens, without waiting for JS and without a failure list of its own. The row keeps its place and shows the YouTube transfer's progress, and the song lands as a YouTube Music download does: the M4A in the Library's tree, tagged. The fallback gets 20 minutes of its own, and a job cut off by process death after falling back resumes from YouTube Music without going back to Soulseek. When YouTube Music fails as well, the row shows YouTube's failure with Retry, and Retry tries Soulseek first again. If YT Music is turned off before YouTube takes over, the row keeps Soulseek's failure. With YT Music off, Soulseek failures end the job as before; an unreachable server is retried like a dropped connection and then says "Download failed: Soulseek can't be reached". Whichever the source, there is one Download button, with no picker and no quality dialog. **From YouTube Music**, Wave saves the best M4A stream YouTube serves the account as it's served, never re-encoded: AAC at 256 kbps (itag 141) with Premium, about 128 kbps (itag 140) without. A Google sign-in isn't needed. The address comes from playback's resolver (`YtStreamResolver`, asking for M4A), and the audio arrives in 1 MiB ranges into the hidden part file, a request that breaks off going on from its last byte. An address googlevideo turns down (403/410) is resolved again, three times at most: the download carries on from where it was when the fresh address serves the same stream, and starts over when it serves another (`YtAudioDownload`). The file is published only once it is the length YouTube announced and, read through to its last sample, plays as long as the song, within 2 s or 2% (Core's `ytAudioMatches`). Otherwise, or when YouTube won't serve the song, the row fails with "This song is unavailable on YouTube Music" and keeps Retry. A transfer that breaks off is started again after a pause that grows each time (it downloads the whole song again), up to six attempts in all, then fails with "Download failed"; offline it fails with "Download failed: offline". **From Soulseek**, after one-time free connection in Settings, Wave automatically selects the same recording and at most three suitable peers. Core combines exact normalized title, the main artist, complete edition evidence and duration; a faster live version, remix, edit or different remaster cannot replace the requested recording. Only advertised original MP3 320 candidates are eligible in the proven native path; there is no lossless encoder or lower-bitrate fallback. Native code checks every MPEG frame, completeness, bitrate and actual decoded duration. Contradictory received title, artist or edition tags reject the peer before trusted metadata overwrites them. With YT Music off, failure says "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and leaves Retry available. Either way, the destination is the Library's own tree, `<album artist>/<album>/<NN> <title>.mp3` from Soulseek or `.m4a` from YouTube Music, directly under an included root (`<title>.mp3` or `<title>.m4a` without a track number; names made safe for the storage), so a song whose album folder the Library already has lands next to its songs. Core's `ytDownloadTarget` names it, the same function the PC job that organizes the music folder uses. The root and folder are checked against current exclusions. Existing files are never replaced: a taken name gets a free one. The existing tag writer keeps supplied title, artists, album, album artist, year, track/disc numbers and artwork; optional genre/lyrics metadata failures never reject usable audio. The existing scanner and LibraryStore immediately import the complete file into Library, Search and Android Auto and refresh YT Music Library matching. Repeated taps and positively matched result ids share a job. Neither source needs a Google session or a paid account, and downloads never share or upload the Library. No PC, server, daemon or separate downloader app is involved. A native journal owns the queue (see Native shape), so downloads keep going with the screen off and while music plays, and survive Android recreating Wave's process: the request, its source, the chosen recording, candidate peers, current peer, the policy (MP3 320, or YouTube's M4A) and deadlines are persisted; a transfer cut off by process death restarts from zero with the same peer and what was left of its time, or from YouTube's stream with a fresh window to start in, within the job's own remaining time. Rows say "Waiting to download", "Finding this recording", "Connecting", "Downloading · NN%", "Checking the download" and "Saving to the Library"; a YouTube Music download waits instead of finding a recording or connecting. The notification looks the same whichever the source. Internally, for Soulseek, a dropped connection reconnects the saved account and retries (searching three times, transferring up to six attempts), a peer that keeps Wave in its queue over 2 minutes, doesn't start within 3 or goes quiet for 1 is replaced by the next selected peer, and each job has 20 minutes from leaving the queue; when peers run out the job falls back to YouTube Music, or with YT Music off the row fails with "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and Retry, which searches again. Network loss, full storage, an unwritable folder, a rejected account or audio that fails validation never touch existing files and never publish partial audio. Cancel Download in the menu or Cancel in the notification deletes the hidden part files; once it is saving to the Library it can't be cancelled. Repeated completion is idempotent (upsert by path), so no duplicate Tracks.
- **Album downloads.** A YT Music album (not a Library album) can be downloaded in one go: **Download Album** in a YT Music album tile's long-press menu (after Play Next and Add to Queue for the album's songs; the album's page is read first, once a session, so the card opens with all its entries; offline or without an answer, a message says so instead), and a **Download** button beside Play and Shuffle on the album's page. Every song of the album the Library doesn't already have (`libraryFinder`, as Discover decides) joins the download queue in album order; a song already queued or downloading keeps its job, and one that is positively the same recording as a job on its way (or as an earlier song of the same album) shares it. Each song then goes through the single-song pipeline above, unchanged: the same source (Soulseek's MP3 320, or YouTube Music's M4A without a Soulseek account), the same fallback song by song when Soulseek can't deliver one, the same matching and tags, `<album artist>/<album>/`. The album page is read once a session and shared by the screen, its menu and every song's tags, so the songs land as one album with the album's title, album artist, year, track numbers and cover. While any of the album's songs are queued or downloading, the menu offers **Cancel Album Download** (the album's songs leave their jobs that aren't saving yet; a job shared with a song asked for elsewhere goes on for it) and the page head says "Downloading · N of M" (M: the album's songs downloaded or asked for this session, N: those saved). Each song row keeps its own progress line. Once nothing is on its way, songs that failed keep their own Retry Download and the album offers **Retry Failed** (a song that fell back to YouTube Music has failed only once both sources have, so Retry Failed asks again only for those); with every song in the Library, Download Album is gone. The page shows one button, the album's first entry ("Download", "Retry Failed" or "Cancel"). The download notification counts the songs left ("N songs left") while more than one is. What the album offers and queues is Core's (`ytAlbumDownload`, `ytAlbumDownloadButton`), built from the album's songs, the Library matches and the journal's snapshot. The decision itself takes any list of songs (`ytSongsDownload`), so playlists share it.
- **Playlist downloads.** A playlist with YT Music entries can download them in one go, as an album does: a **Download** button beside Play and Shuffle on the playlist's page, and **Download Playlist** in its long-press menu (after Add to Playlist; the playlist's entries are read first, so the card opens with all its entries). Every YT Music entry the Library doesn't already have joins the download queue in playlist order; the Library's songs are skipped, a song already queued or downloading keeps its job, and one that is positively the same recording as a job on its way (or as an earlier entry) shares it. Each goes through the single-song pipeline unchanged (the same source, the same matching and tags, `<album artist>/<album>/`, the album page read from the entry's album browse id when it has one). As each song lands, the store turns its entry, in every playlist that holds it, into the Library's song in the same place (see Playlists). While any entry is queued or downloading, the button is **Cancel** and the menu offers **Cancel Playlist Download**, and the page head says "Downloading · N of M" (M: the playlist's songs downloaded or asked for this session, N: those saved, including entries that have already become the Library's song: a Library entry whose file a download saved this session). Once nothing is on its way and some failed, the button and the menu offer **Retry Failed**; when every entry is the Library's, the button and the menu entries are gone. Downloads need no Google session, and with a Soulseek account they don't need YT Music either: with YT Music off the button and menu entries stay and the songs come from Soulseek, tagged from what the playlist kept and Deezer, since the album page can't be read. Without a Soulseek account the songs come from YouTube Music while YT Music is on; with one, each song Soulseek can't deliver falls back to YouTube Music on its own, as a song's download does, and Retry Failed asks again only for the songs both sources failed. With neither, each entry's row fails with "Connect YT Music or Soulseek in Settings", as a song's download does, and the playlist then offers Retry Failed. What the playlist offers and queues is Core's (`ytPlaylistDownload`, `ytPlaylistDownloadButton`), from its entries, the Library matches and the journal's snapshot.
- **Discover.** While YT Music is on, the Library lists a Discover entry after Favorites. It opens the signed-in account's recommendations: YouTube Music's home (`FEmusic_home` and the next three pages it links to), read by `YtHomeParser` after Metrolist's `HomePage`. Only songs, albums and playlists are kept; the rest of the home (artists, moods, podcasts, the taste builder, banners) has no place in Wave and is never shown. A shelf of only songs is laid out as song rows, any other as a row of covers (Core's `discoverView`). Tapping a song plays the shelf's songs from it; an album (its page, as downloads read it) or a playlist (`VL<id>` and the pages it links to, up to 5,000 songs, Core's `readYtPlaylist`) opens a list of its songs with Play and Shuffle. A playlist's list shows its first page of songs as soon as it arrives and adds each later page as it comes, with "Loading more songs…" under them meanwhile. If a later page fails, the songs shown stay and the line says the rest didn't load (offline, or go back and open it again) (Core's `ytPlaylistRestLine`). Play, Shuffle and taps play the songs shown. Songs the Library already has (Core's `libraryFinder`, the matching `libraryMatcher` uses) play the Library's file and show as Library rows; the others stream and have the YT Music song's menu (Play Next, Add to Queue, Go to Artist, Download). Pull to refresh asks again, keeping the shelves up meanwhile, and answers to an older question are dropped (Core's `stepDiscover`). Discover asks YouTube only when an account is signed in: signed out (or expired) it explains that recommendations come from the YouTube Music account and links to Settings, an account sign-in, never a Premium upsell; offline, or when YouTube fails, it shows a quiet empty state.
- **Artist page.** The artist page stays the Library's: it opens from the Library alone, with no YouTube request. Its album shelves (Albums, Singles & EPs, Appears on) are each one row of covers under their heading that scrolls sideways, laid out as Home's and Discover's rows are, and the Songs follow straight after; a shelf with nothing in it is left out (Core's `artistPageItems`). While YT Music is on it ends with a **Show on YouTube Music** text button ("Various Artists", "Unknown Artist" and names without letters get none, Core's `ytArtistQuery`), and nothing is asked until it's tapped. The one exception is the artist opened from a streamed song (an integrated view, the artist route's `integrated=1` param, `openArtist`'s `{ integrated: true }`): with no answer standing it asks at once, once, as a tap would, showing the looking line instead of the button; a failure leaves the quiet line and the button (Core's `ytArtistSectionAsks`). Tapping finds the artist on YouTube Music, an Artists search for the name (`YtArtistParser`) and Core's match (`pickYtArtist`: names compare like artist photos, only an exact match counts, and among namesakes the most followed one, by subscribers or monthly audience, wins), then reads the artist's page (`YtArtistParser`, after Metrolist's `ArtistPage`): the top songs, albums and singles, and the Songs shelf's "See all" link, a playlist of every song of the artist. That playlist is read next, page by page as any YT Music playlist is (Core's `readYtPlaylist`, with its cap); a page without the link keeps its top songs as the list, and a playlist page that fails fails the whole request, as being offline does, so a half-read list is never saved. Nothing new gets a section of its own: **every song of the artist the Library doesn't have** (`libraryMatcher`, as Search decides) joins the page's Songs, the Library's songs first in the sort chosen for artists, then the missing ones in YouTube Music's order, and they stream on tap with the YT Music song's menu (Play Next, Add to Queue, Go to Artist, Download); while they show, the Library's songs carry the downloaded icon, and Play, Shuffle and tapping any song play the whole list (a tap from that song). When the Library has every song of the artist, a quiet line at the end says so. The artist's **Albums** from the same page that the Library doesn't have join the Albums shelf after the Library's, and their **Singles & EPs** it doesn't have make a shelf of their own, each newest first (Core's `ytArtistShelves`). A release the Library already has, an album whose album artist credit names this artist (the Library's artist identity, so a collaboration counts for each of its artists) with the release's title, case, accents, punctuation and version notes ("Deluxe Edition", "Remastered", "- Single", a featured credit) aside, is left out: the Library's album already shows (Core's `libraryAlbumFinder`); a live or remix album is another release. Any other release opens the YT Music album page, where songs the Library has play its files as in Discover, and long-presses to the YT Music album menu with Download Album. The answer is saved in the database with when it came (`yt_artists`, by artist key, the whole page kept: top songs, every song of the artist, albums and singles), and the match goes with it, so a later load skips the search (Core's `ytArtistLookup`). For a week, opening the artist shows the saved section at once, with no request and no button; after that the button is back and tapping asks again. An answer saved before Wave read the artist's full list counts as expired on the artist page: the button is back and a tap asks again (a Library album's page still finds its release in it). "No match" is saved too and shows a quiet "Not found on YouTube Music" line for a week, then the button returns and the search is tried again. Offline, or when YouTube fails, the section shows a quiet line with the button to try again, and nothing is saved. With YT Music off the section and the button are gone and nothing is asked; what was saved stays. Only the opened artist's name, their browse id and the id of their songs playlist are sent; the Library's contents never are, and nothing is fetched in the background. The section's states (button, loading, saved, expired, no match, offline) and how long an answer stands are Core's (`stepYtArtistSection`, `ytArtistSectionView`, `YT_ARTIST_FRESH_MS`).
- **YT Music artist page.** An artist the Library doesn't have gets a page too, opened from their YouTube Music browse id (`/library/yt-artist/<UC…>?name=<name>`, `openYtArtist`, as Search's YT Music artists open it) or, from a streamed song, whose artists are only names, by name (`/library/yt-artist/by-name?name=<name>`, `openYtArtistNamed`). Opened by name, it finds the artist as a Library artist's page does (an Artists search and `pickYtArtist`) and saves the answer under the name as a Library artist's is (`yt_artists`, by artist key); "no match" is saved too and shows a quiet "Not found on YouTube Music" line for a week. It is always integrated and shows what the integrated artist page shows, with nothing from the Library but what it already has: the artist's image (the page header's, asked for as a square, `YtArtistParser`) and name (the opener's until the page is read), Play and Shuffle, their **Albums** and **Singles & EPs** shelves, each one row of covers that scrolls sideways as on the artist page, newest first, where a release the Library has is its Library album and opens it (the Library artist the YouTube Music name is, compared as artist photos compare names, Core's `libraryArtistFinder`, then `libraryAlbumFinder`), and **every song of the artist** in YouTube Music's order, read as a Library artist's page reads them (the Songs shelf's "See all" playlist, page by page with Core's `readYtPlaylist` and its cap; a page without the link keeps its top songs; a playlist page that fails fails the whole read), where a song the Library has (`libraryFinder`) plays its file and carries the downloaded icon and the rest stream with the YT Music song's menu. Play, Shuffle and taps play the page's songs (Core's `pagePlayback`, an integrated view). Opening the page is asking for it, so it's read as it opens, with no button (Core's `ytArtistPageAsks`), and saved like a Library artist's answer (`yt_artists`, keyed `yt:<browse id>`, the whole page): reopening within a week shows the saved page with no request, and after that it's read again. An answer saved before Wave read the artist's full list counts as expired, so the page is read again as it opens. Offline, or when YouTube fails, a quiet line with **Try Again** takes the page's place and nothing is saved; with YT Music off the page says YT Music is off and asks nothing. Only the browse id and the id of their songs playlist are sent (opened by name, the name first). What the page shows is Core's (`ytArtistPageView`).
- **Album page.** A Library album opens as the Library's: its tracks, and while YT Music is on, **Show on YouTube Music** at the end, with nothing asked, even when its artist has a saved answer. It merges YouTube Music in on its own only when it's opened from a view that already does (an **integrated view**): the artist page while it shows their YouTube Music answer (Core's `ytArtistIntegrated`), or Search while it shows YouTube Music's results (the album route's `integrated=1` param, `openAlbum`'s `{ integrated: true }`). Opened from anywhere else (the Albums grid, an artist page without its answer, Search without YouTube Music, Now Playing), it waits for the button. Integrated, by that opening or by the button, the album goes by its first album artist's saved answer (`yt_artists`, a week), the same one their page shows; with nothing saved, or a week on, it asks for the artist at once, once, as their page would, and saves the answer for both pages. With the answer, the album is looked for among the artist's Albums and Singles & EPs, matched as the artist page matches releases (Core's `ytArtistRelease`), and the release's page (`MPREb_…`, read once a session, as downloads read it) is read at once, with no further tap. Its songs then join the album's tracks in one list in the release's order: each song is the album's own track when it has it, else the Library's file of it from another album, else streamed with the YT Music song's menu (Play Next, Add to Queue, Go to Artist, Download); album tracks the release doesn't list keep their place by number (Core's `ytAlbumTracklist`). Rows are numbered by their place on the release, the Library's carry the downloaded icon, and Play, Shuffle and taps play the whole list. **Download** beside Play and Shuffle downloads the rest of the album, as a YT Music album page does, with its progress under the furniture line. With every song of the release in the Library, the album shows as before. Under the tracks a quiet line says when it's looking, when the artist or the release isn't on YouTube Music ("Not found on YouTube Music"), or when it's offline or YouTube fails (with the button to try again). What the page shows, and when it asks on its own, is Core's (`ytLibraryAlbumView`).
- **Playlists.** A playlist holds Library songs and YT Music songs the Library doesn't have (YT Music entries), side by side in the order they were added. **Add to Playlist** in a YT Music song's menu (Search, Discover, YT Music album and artist pages) adds it as a Library song is added: at the end, skipped when the playlist already holds it (by its video id), or as the Library's song when the Library already has it (`libraryFinder`). A playlist keeps what shows an entry without asking YouTube: its video id, title, artists, album (and the album's browse id, for a download's tags), length, cover and explicit mark (`playlist_items.yt_song`, Core's `writePlaylistYtSong`; an item is a Library track or a YT Music song, never both). The playlist page lists YT Music entries in place as stream rows (YouTube's cover, title, artists and album) with the YT Music song's menu plus Remove from Playlist; while any is in the list, the Library's songs carry the downloaded icon. Play, Shuffle and taps play the playlist's whole list in order, the Library's files and the rest streamed, as an integrated view does; with YT Music off the entries show but don't play (a tap says YT Music is off) and Play plays the Library's songs (Core's `playlistPlayback`). The playlist's long-press menu queues and adds its entries the same way. Reorder, swipe to remove, rename and delete work on YT Music entries as on any other; selection mode checks only the Library's songs. The mosaic takes the covers of what the playlist holds, YouTube's for its YT Music entries, and its count and length include them. With YT Music off Wave asks YouTube for nothing, covers included: the entries' rows, their menu and their mosaic tiles show the placeholder instead. Android Auto's Playlists list the same entries, and the YT Music ones stream there as streamed songs do (with YT Music off they don't play). Once the Library has a song that is positively the same recording as an entry (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover decide), the entry becomes that Library song in the same place, in every playlist that holds it, and its plays count for it from then on: the store does this when it saves a finished download, and once against the whole Library when a scan ends (Core's `playlistEntryLinks`; it costs one small query while no playlist holds a YT Music entry). A playlist never holds a song twice, so an entry whose Library song its playlist already holds, or that an earlier entry of it has just become, is removed instead. Deleting a Library file still takes its entries out of playlists. Streamed plays from a playlist aren't recorded, as elsewhere.
- **Integrated playback.** Search's song results, an artist's page and an album's page decide what Play, Shuffle and a tapped song play by one rule (Core's `pagePlayback`): with YouTube Music's songs among the page's (an integrated view), the page's whole list in page order, the Library's files and the rest streamed; with none, the Library's songs alone, as before, so nothing is streamed or queued from YouTube Music outside an integrated view.
- **Off switch.** Settings › YT Music › Use YT Music (on by default). Off, the section isn't shown and the native client refuses every request before it reaches the network; the switch is kept natively too, so the playback service obeys it without JS.
- **Account.** Signing in is optional; everything YT Music does also works signed out. Settings › YT Music › Sign In opens Google's sign-in page in a web view inside Wave (`YtSignInActivity`, as Metrolist does). Once the page reaches YouTube Music signed in, Wave takes the session cookie and the page's account (`YtSignInPage`), asks YouTube Music's account menu whose account it is (`YtAccountMenu`) and keeps the session; Settings then shows the account's picture, name and email, and Sign Out. The session is handed to innertubex (`replaceSession`), so every request the `YtClient` sends (Search, playback, optional download metadata, Discover) goes out as the account; a Premium account therefore gets the audio YouTube serves Premium. Visitor data isn't the account's and is left as it is.
- **Session privacy.** The cookie lives only in the native module: in one file in the app's no-backup storage (`noBackupFilesDir`, never in an Android backup), never in the JS key-value store, never handed to JS (which only sees `{ status, account: { name, email, photoUrl } }`) and never logged. The sign-in web view starts and ends empty: its cookies and site storage are cleared before the page opens and when it closes, so its only copy is the native one. Sign Out deletes the file and clears the web view's cookies and storage again.
- **Expiry.** A session YouTube stops accepting expires instead of failing: when a request comes back HTTP 401, or the account menu has no account (checked each time Settings opens), the session's credentials are dropped, the request is asked again signed out (once), and Settings shows "signed out" with Sign In Again. Signed out, nothing can be rejected again, so there's no error loop. Without an answer (offline, YouTube failing) the session stays as it was. What Settings shows is Core's (`stepYtAccount`, `ytAccountView`); with YT Music off there is no Sign In, but a signed-in account can still be signed out of.
- **Native shape.** One process-wide `YtClient` (package `expo.modules.wavenative.yt`) owns the HTTP client, innertubex's session and the stream resolver (`YtClient.get(context).streams`, used by playback); the `WaveYtMusic` Expo module exposes it to JS (`setEnabled`, `searchSongs`, `searchArtists`, `searchAlbums`, `artist`, `home`, `playlist`, `playlistMore`, `session`, `checkSession`, `signIn`, `signOut`, `cacheArtwork` and the `onSessionChanged` event; failures reject with `ERR_YT_OFFLINE`, `ERR_YT_FAILED` or `ERR_YT_DISABLED`), wrapped by the app's `YtMusic` Effect service. YouTube Music sends a playlist a hundred rows a page: `playlist` reads its first page (its head, its songs and the token for the next page) and `playlistMore` the page a token leads to, both by `YtPlaylistParser`, which leaves out the rows YouTube Music greys out (removed, blocked in the country). Core's `readYtPlaylist` follows the pages to the last, keeping YouTube Music's order, and keeps a song listed twice once. A read stops at 5,000 songs or 100 pages, whichever comes first, and then says `truncated`. `YtMusic.playlist` runs it: a page that fails fails the whole read, so the import never gets part of a playlist that looks complete, while Discover hears each page as it comes (see Discover). Downloads belong to the native `AndroidDownloads` owner (package `download`): `DurableDownloads` keeps the queue as an atomically rewritten journal in no-backup storage and drives it one job at a time from `YtDownloadService`, a `dataSync` foreground service with a partial wake lock and a Wi-Fi lock that shows progress and Cancel in its notification, restarts sticky after process death and stops when the queue is empty. Each job carries its source, and the transport hands a Soulseek candidate to the Soulseek client and YouTube's to `YtAudioDownload` (package `yt`). A Soulseek job also carries the fallback its prepare answer gave (the failures Core hands over and the M4A's place); every failure goes through one place in the journal, which switches such a job to YouTube Music with a fresh deadline instead of failing it. The Soulseek client tells an unreachable server (`ERR_SLSK_UNREACHABLE`) from being offline by asking whether the phone is online. The journal leases the decisions that need the Library and Core to JS (prepare: the source, for Soulseek the search and Core's match, and the destination; confirm: received tags, or the length YouTube's audio plays for; publish: the current folder policy; import: the LibraryStore upsert); the download service starts the headless JS runtime for them, and a lease that isn't answered expires and is offered again. `WaveSoulseek` exposes the session, search and the journal (`enqueueJob`, `jobsSnapshot`, `claimDecision`, `submitDecision`, `cancelJob`, `resumeJobs`, `onJobsChanged`); `WaveYtDownloads` keeps the optional album, watch and lyrics pages; the app's `ytmusic/YtDownloads.ts` answers decisions and mirrors the journal into rows. Building it needs Kotlin 2.4 (innertubex's), set through `expo-build-properties`.

### Modules

1. **Core** (workspace package, pure TypeScript, **zero runtime dependencies**). Holds all decision-heavy domain logic behind a small public API, so it can be tested anywhere with plain Node:
   - _Queue engine_ — a pure state + command reducer: start playback from a context at a given track; next/previous (with the 3-second restart rule); seek; shuffle on/off (current track stays first, original order restorable); repeat off/all/one; Play Next; Add to Queue; reorder; remove; track-ended transitions. It outputs the ordered list the native player should hold.
   - _Play-event classifier_ — given start, listened time, duration and end reason, decides whether a play is counted.
   - _Library model_ — grouping (album identity = album title + album artist, album-artist fallback, compilations), sort keys (articles and accents ignored), disc/track ordering, unknown-tag fallbacks, and splitting credits into artists (`creditSplitter`).
   - _Scan reconciliation_ — given the known index and a scan result, produces adds/updates/removals, and matches moved/renamed files (same size + duration + title + artist) so history and favorites carry over.
   - _Search_ — normalisation (case, accents, punctuation), word-prefix matching and ranking across entity types.
   - _Lyrics_ — LRC parsing (timestamps, multiple stamps per line, offsets) and current-line lookup.
   - _Sleep timer_ — deadline/end-of-track logic.
   - _YT Music_ — YT Music song ids (`ytTrackId`, `parseTrackId`), whether a song is already in the Library and which Library song it is (`libraryMatcher`, `libraryFinder`), which list a page's Play, Shuffle and taps play (`pagePlayback`: the mixed list only in an integrated view), Discover (`ytDiscover.ts`: its states, the load lifecycle, each shelf's layout, what tapping an item does, which songs play the Library's file, an album page's songs), reading a playlist page by page with its cap and what a playlist shows while the rest loads (`readYtPlaylist`, `ytPlaylistRestLine`, `ytPlaylist.ts`), when Search asks YouTube Music, on a tap or on its own, and what it shows: the songs, artists and albums the Library lacks, YouTube Music's best result and whether Search is integrated (`stepYtSection`, `ytSectionView`, `libraryArtistFinder`, `libraryReleaseFinder`), the artist page's section (`ytArtist.ts`: who gets the button, which YouTube Music artist the Library's artist is, what a tap asks, how long a saved answer stands (a week, and only with the artist's full list), what the section shows (every song of the artist the Library lacks, from their songs playlist, or the quiet line), its albums and singles shelves and which releases the Library already has, and a Library album's section: which release it is and which of its songs the Library lacks; when an artist page opened integrated asks on its own, `ytArtistSectionAsks`, and the YouTube Music artist page: when it asks, what it shows and which Library artist a YouTube Music name is, `ytArtistPageAsks`, `ytArtistPageView`, `libraryArtistFinder`), which artist page a streamed song's artists open (`streamedArtistLink`, offered by the YT Music song's menu, `ytSongActions`), the account in Settings (`stepYtAccount`, `ytAccountView`), streamed songs in the queue (`ytQueue.ts`: what a saved queue restores, which details are kept with it, which ids are the Library's, the line a song that can't stream shows) and downloads: which source a download uses, what the download journal does when a source fails, which Soulseek failures fall back to YouTube Music and whether YouTube's audio is the song (`downloadSource`, `downloadFailurePlan`, `downloadFallback`, `ytAudioMatches`), where the file goes (`ytDownloadRoot`, `ytDownloadTarget`), its tags and lookup (`ytDownloadTags`, `ytDownloadLookup`) and downloads as rows and menus show them (`ytDownloadsFromJournal`, `ytDownloadSharedJob`, `ytDownloadActions`, `ytDownloadLine`), and whole-album downloads (`ytAlbumDownload.ts`: which songs to queue and which jobs they share, the album's progress line, what Retry Failed and Cancel Album Download act on, and the entries its menu and page offer).
   - _Link import_ — which links name a playlist and why others don't (`recognisePlaylistLink`), what Import offers (`playlistImportSources`), what a read playlist becomes (`planLinkImport`: its name, its songs in order, the Library's as its tracks), what the import card shows (`linkImportView`) and the summary of songs left out (`linkImportSummary`) (`playlistLinks.ts`); reading a Spotify playlist through a transport the app gives (`readSpotifyPlaylist`, `spotifyEmbed`, `spotifyPlaylistPage`, `spotifyPlaylist.ts`) and following a short link (`spotifyShortLinkTarget`); finding its songs and what the import makes (`findSpotifySongs`, `spotifyYtMatch`, `spotifySearchQuery`, `planSpotifyImport`, `spotifyImport.ts`) (see "Importing playlists from a link").
   - _M3U import_ — parsing `.m3u` / `.m3u8` (`#EXTM3U`, `#EXTINF`, comments), resolving entries (absolute, relative to the playlist file, `file://`, Windows separators) and matching them to Library tracks by path (case-insensitively, as Android's storage is), the playlist's name, Keep Both's numbered name and the import summary (`m3u.ts`). The native module's file picker reads the files and their paths.
   - _BlackPlayer stats import_ — parsing `.bpstat` track stats, ordering snapshots, dating plays, matching rows to Library tracks, skipping plays imported before, and the summary (`blackPlayer.ts`; see "Importing BlackPlayer stats").
2. **Native player** (local Expo module, Kotlin, Media3). A `MediaSessionService` hosting ExoPlayer, so notification, lock screen, Bluetooth/headset buttons, audio focus and becoming-noisy handling come from Media3. The JS side owns ordering (from the queue engine) and pushes the ordered item list; the native side plays it gaplessly and reports state, throttled position, item transitions with a reason (auto-advance, skip, seek, error) and errors. Native shuffle is not used. The audio pipeline keeps a hook for custom audio processors so equalizer, crossfade and ReplayGain (v2) slot in without restructuring.
3. **Native scanner** (same Kotlin module). Walks the scan roots minus exclusions, reads tags with a JVM tag library that also supports writing (needed by the v2 tag editor), extracts embedded artwork once per unique image (hashed, cached as files) together with a small colour palette (Android Palette) for the Now Playing tint, and finds sidecar `.lrc` files. It streams results to JS in batches of ~500 and supports an incremental mode driven by known path + modification time + size. The same module holds the YT Music client (`yt` package, see YT Music).
4. **Library store** (app, Drizzle on expo-sqlite). The single owner of the schema and migrations. Tables for tracks, albums, artists (with each track's and album's credited artists in credit order), genres, folders, artwork (path + palette), playlists and playlist items, favorites, play events, scan roots/exclusions, recent searches, and persisted playback state. Home sections and play counts are queries over play events.
5. **App services** (app, Effect). Wrap the native modules as typed services and event streams; orchestrate scans (core reconciliation → store); drive the queue engine and sync it to the native player; record play events through the classifier; persist and restore playback state.
6. **UI** (app, Expo Router). Three tabs — Home, Library, Search (Search rendered as a separate glass pill). A custom glass tab bar, a floating glass mini player and a full-screen Now Playing presented over the tabs with an interactive drag-to-dismiss gesture. Detail screens for album, artist, playlist and genre. Settings for scan roots, exclusions and rescan.

### Play event shape

One row per playback start: track, started-at, listened milliseconds, track duration, end reason (`completed` | `skipped` | `stopped` | `interrupted`), counted flag, and play context (type + id). Append-only; nothing is aggregated away.

### Importing playlists from a link

- Library › Playlists › Import Playlists asks where the playlist comes from: **From a Link** or **From Files**. From Files is the M3U import (story 56). With YT Music off there is no From a Link: Import Playlists opens the file picker at once, as before, and nothing is asked of YouTube (Core's `playlistImportSources`).
- From a Link opens a card with a field for the link. When the clipboard holds a playlist link, the field starts with it (`WaveNative.readClipboardText`, read only when From a Link is chosen; Android shows its own "pasted" notice). Wave takes a YouTube Music or YouTube playlist link (`music.youtube.com/playlist?list=…`, `www.youtube.com/…`, `youtube.com/…`, `m.youtube.com/…`, with or without the scheme, extra parameters such as `si` ignored), the first address in text with words around it (as apps share a link with its title), or the bare playlist id (`PL…`, `OLAK5uy_…`, `RDCLAK5uy_…`, `list=` or `VL` before it allowed). It also takes a Spotify playlist link (`open.spotify.com/playlist/<id>`, with or without an `intl-xx` locale, `/embed` or an old link's `/user/<user>` before it, parameters such as `si` ignored), a Spotify URI (`spotify:playlist:<id>`, `spotify:user:<user>:playlist:<id>`) and a Spotify short link (`spotify.link/<code>`, `spotify.app.link/<code>`), which the card follows before reading. Anything else gets a reason under the field and no Continue: not a link, a site other than YouTube or Spotify, a link to a song (`/watch`, `youtu.be`, even one played from a playlist, or a Spotify track or episode) or another page (an album, an artist, a profile) (Core's `recognisePlaylistLink`).
- Continue opens the **import card**, which reads the whole playlist at once (`YtMusic.playlist`, see Native shape) and then shows its cover, name, how many songs it has and how many of them the Library already has ("24 songs · 10 in your Library", "all", "none"), with **Import** and **Cancel**. A playlist past the read's cap says only its first songs come. Offline, or when the read fails, the card says so with **Try Again**. YouTube Music answers a private, deleted or wrong playlist as it answers a page it can't serve, so those share the failure message ("It may be private or deleted, or YouTube Music didn't answer."). A playlist with no songs has nothing to import. What the card shows is Core's (`linkImportView`).
- **Import** makes a Wave playlist named after the source (trimmed to a playlist name, "Imported Playlist" when it has none) with every song in the source's order. A song the Library has (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover decide) goes in as the Library's, the rest as YT Music entries that stream and can be downloaded later (see YT Music, Playlists). A playlist of only Library songs is therefore an ordinary playlist. A song that comes twice, or two that are one Library song, goes in once (Core's `planLinkImport`). A name already in use asks Replace or Keep Both, as the M3U import does, and then the new (or replaced) playlist opens.
- **Spotify playlists.** The card's heading says "Import from Spotify". Wave reads the playlist as `docs/spotify-playlists.md` (#156) recommends, keyless and without an account (Core's `readSpotifyPlaylist`). It loads the playlist's embed page, which says whether the playlist is public and gives its name, cover, first 100 songs and an anonymous token. With that token it then reads every song through the web player's own `fetchPlaylist` query, 500 a page. A short link is followed first (Core's `spotifyShortLinkTarget`), and one that leads to a song or a profile says so.
  - **Limits.** `fetchPlaylist` is an internal Spotify API: it can change without notice, and its persisted-query hash is a constant in Core. When that call fails (a changed hash answers 412, a refused token 401, or Spotify throttles), the card uses the embed page's songs instead, which stop at 100, and says the playlist goes on past them. Neither source gives the ISRC, so songs are matched by title, artists and length. Wave reads at most the first 1,000 songs of a playlist (`SPOTIFY_SONG_CAP`) and says so when there are more. A private, deleted or unknown playlist says "This playlist doesn't exist or isn't public." Spotify out of reach, or an answer that isn't the playlist, gets Try Again. Using the internal API goes against Spotify's User Guidelines on automated access. The owner accepted that on #156, as YT Music's `youtubei` API already stands.
  - **Finding the songs.** While the card reads "Finding songs… N of M", each song is looked for in the Library first (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover decide). A song the Library doesn't have gets one YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists. Wave takes only the first result that is the same recording, by the rules downloads use (Core's `spotifyYtMatch`). The normalized title must be the same once guest credits are set aside ("(feat. B)", Spotify's "(with B)", or a note naming only the song's artists). Each side's main artist must be credited on the other, with credits split as the Library splits them and "The" ignored. The edition notes must be the same, and so must any guests. Both lengths must be known and within 3 seconds. So a live version, a remix or another edit doesn't count, while a remaster note, an album's edition ("Deluxe") or a soundtrack's "From …" note doesn't tell recordings apart. A song with neither match is left out, and so is a local file with no artist, which is never searched. A song listed twice is searched once (Core's `findSpotifySongs`).
  - **Rate limits, measured from a PC on 2026-10-14.** Four searches run at once (`SPOTIFY_SEARCHES_AT_ONCE`): a 495-song playlist took 85 seconds, about 6 searches a second, with no refusals. YouTube Music began refusing about one search in eight (HTTP 403, never more than three in a row) only after about 2,300 searches in 15 minutes, and still refused a few for over half an hour after. During that time every refused search went through within three tries. So a refused search is asked again after 2, 5, 10 and 20 seconds, and only a fifth refusal stops the finding with "YouTube Music stopped answering while finding the songs." and Try Again. Offline, or with YT Music turned off, it stops at once. Under that throttling the 495-song playlist still finished, in 188 seconds, with 40 of 513 searches refused and all recovered. The 1,000-song cap keeps one import well under the volume where the refusals began. 97% of that playlist's songs were found.
  - **The card and Import.** Once found, the card shows the cover, the name and "N songs · M in your Library", with a second line counting the songs not found ("3 songs weren't found."). With none found there is nothing to import. Cancel, or closing the card, stops the finding: no search or Spotify request starts after it. Import makes the playlist as a YouTube Music import does, in Spotify's order without the songs not found, the Library's songs as its tracks and the rest as YT Music entries (Core's `planSpotifyImport`). After it opens, a card lists the songs left out by title and artist, scrolling when they don't fit (Core's `linkImportSummary`).
- The import is a one-time copy: the playlist doesn't follow its source afterwards.
- **What is sent.** Reading a YouTube Music playlist sends YouTube Music the playlist id, as every YT Music request goes (with YouTube's visitor data and, once signed in, the session), and nothing else: not the link's other parameters, the Library or the playlists. Reading a Spotify playlist sends Spotify the playlist id, the page asked for and the token Spotify itself gave (and a short link's code, to follow it), and sends YouTube Music one search per song the Library doesn't have: its title and artists. Matching against the Library happens on the phone.
- The card opens from anywhere (`openLinkImport(text)` in `library/importPlaylistLink.tsx`). With YT Music off it says so and asks nothing.
- **Sharing to Wave.** Wave is listed in Android's share sheet for shared text and links, and for nothing else (a `SEND` intent filter for `text/plain` on the main activity, from `android.intentFilters` in `app.config.ts`). Sharing a playlist from the YouTube Music app, which shares a bare link, from the Spotify app (an `open.spotify.com` link or a `spotify.link` short link, with or without words around it), or from a browser, which adds the page title around it, opens Wave on the import card for that playlist, with Import and Cancel as from the Library. It works from a cold start and with Wave already open (the main activity is `singleTask`, so a share reaches the running Wave). Wave takes the first web address (or `spotify:` URI) in the shared text, drops the punctuation after it, and lets `recognisePlaylistLink` name the playlist (Core's `sharedTextImport`). Anything else shared (a song, an album, an artist, another site, text with no link, a bare playlist id) shows a short message saying Wave imports playlists from YouTube Music and Spotify links, and nothing is requested. A Spotify short link names its playlist only once followed, so a short link to a song opens the card, which then says it's a song. With YT Music off, any share shows a message saying to turn it on in Settings instead. Cancel only closes the card, so Wave stays where the share opened it. A share made before Wave has all-files access waits until it has (the import matches against the Library).
- **How a share arrives.** `WaveNativePackage` passes the main activity's intents to `SharedTextInbox`: the one that started it (not when the activity is recreated, and not a share Android replays when Wave is reopened from Recents) and each new one. The inbox keeps the latest shared text until JS takes it with `WaveNative.takeSharedText()`, and `onShareReceived` tells JS a share came in. The root layout's `useOpenSharedText` takes it on start and on each event, and `openSharedText(text)` opens the card or the message.

### Importing BlackPlayer stats

- Settings › Import BlackPlayer Stats opens the file picker for any file type, several at once, and adds the plays in BlackPlayer's track-stat files (`BlackPlayer EX Track Stat Autosave.bpstat`) to the play history. Each file is a cumulative snapshot: one song per line, `playCount;unknown;title;artist;album;path;addedAtMs;lastPlayedAtMs`, UTF-8. The second field isn't understood and is ignored. A title, album or path can hold a `;` of its own.
- **Dating.** Snapshots are ordered by content: a snapshot's time is its latest last play. The plays a song gained between two consecutive snapshots are spread evenly over that interval (from when it was added, if later); those already in the earliest are spread from when it was added to that snapshot's time. No play is dated after the song's last play. A song's count is the highest any snapshot gave, so a lower count later adds nothing.
- **Matching.** A row is the Library song at its path (case-insensitively, as Android's storage is), else the one with its title and artist as `libraryFinder` compares a YT Music song's, so a moved file still matches. A row has no length, so it can't tell recordings apart: when several Library songs have its title and artist it takes the only one on its album, and otherwise counts as not in the Library rather than guess.
- **Imported plays** are counted plays, listened for the song's length, with no play context, and marked `imported_from = 'blackplayer'`. They count in Stats, Home's Most Played, the artist page's Stats block and play counts like Wave's own. An import adds to each song only the plays beyond those it already has from BlackPlayer, dated after the latest of them, so importing the same or newer snapshots again only adds what's new. They are never the play Wave picks back up after a restart.
- The summary (the M3U import's card) says how many new plays and songs were imported and lists the songs that matched nothing in the Library, and the files that weren't track stats. Core's `blackPlayer.ts` decides all of this.

### Visual design

- The visual world is **The Quiet Catalog** (ECM Records' cover program as the reference): artwork in calm, tinted fields, small and exact type. The decision, its direction contract and the product record live in `apps/mobile/PRODUCT.md` and `apps/mobile/.impeccable/`; `apps/mobile/DESIGN.md` documents the built system.
- Components are fully custom (Apple Music is inspiration only); system and predictive Back always work.
- Theme follows the system. Fog ground in light (`#e0ecef`), true black in dark (`#000000`); on it, dark grouped areas (`#0a1113`, a ramp step only dark mode uses, so light mode's text is untouched), cards and fills, and separators climb one ramp step each. Every neutral comes from one tonal ramp; teal accent, ember highlight. Text, accent and highlight meet WCAG AA on the ground and cards in both themes. Tokens live in `apps/mobile/global.css`, mirrored and contrast-checked in Core (`packages/core/src/theme.ts`); the launch splash uses the same grounds. Settings › Theme picks a background per mode (true black is the dark default; Slate, the night slate ground Wave shipped before, is still offered and looks as it did), a primary and a secondary from preset swatches; a background chosen before the default changed is kept. For the other backgrounds Core (`themeChoice.ts`) derives cards, groups, fills, separators, text and the dialog scrim by keeping each token's contrast against the ground, and deepens or brightens inks that wouldn't read. The choice is applied at runtime over the CSS defaults. The launch splash shows the chosen backgrounds from the next cold start: Android draws it before Wave's code runs, so the app ships a splash style per pair of preset backgrounds and, whenever the theme changes, saves the choice natively (Core's `launchTheme`: the style to open with and each mode's ground, primary and secondary) and picks that style with `SplashScreen.setSplashScreenTheme`. That needs Android 13; on Android 12 the splash keeps the default theme. The splash ships a style per choice, so its logo wears the chosen primary (top wave) and secondary (bottom wave) too, and the logo moves: its waves flow from the first frame until the first screen is ready, then the splash fades out, never before the first sweep ends; with Remove animations on, the logo stands still (Core's `launchLogo.ts`). The launcher icon follows the primary (top wave) and secondary (bottom wave) on black: Wave ships an icon per pair as activity aliases and switches when it leaves the screen, warning first if the launcher drops the home-screen icon on a switch.
- Inter as the typeface, with a light large title and tracked-caps catalog lines; Tabler line icons.
- Signature move: a thin A–Z spine on long lists, every letter in a fixed place, that sets the letter on a plate beside the finger while scrubbing, with a haptic tick per letter, and leaves the list in view. Where each letter sits and which row it jumps to are Core (`spine.ts`).
- Every tap animates through one press primitive whose duration comes from Settings (0–600 ms, default 140 ms) and respects the system's reduce-motion setting.
- Glass is reserved for what floats: the tab bar, the mini player, and dialogs and menus, which are floating glass cards (rounded, sized to their content, clear of the system bars) over a lightly dimmed app. They are drawn at the app root, not in a separate window, so the blur shows the app behind live. Below Android 12 (no blur) the cards are an opaque surface. The row, tile or button a card was opened from stays clear of the dimming, washed with the accent, while that card (or one following up on it) is open. The highlight covers it as it sits at rest (not shrunk by its press feedback), square like the row, and never over the floating chrome: a tile partly under the mini player or tab bar shows only its visible part (Core's `visibleHighlight`). So a long-press menu card has no heading: it starts straight at its entries, with Play Next nearest the finger. A Library entry's menu (Songs, Albums…) is the exception: its heading counts the songs it acts on, which the row doesn't say. A menu opened with nothing highlighted (no press, or what was pressed is off screen or entirely under the floating chrome) still starts with the item it acts on: artwork, title and subtitle, ruled off from the entries (Core's `cardShowsHeading`).
- Now Playing background: gradient from the artwork palette, animated on track change; controls use the app palette.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test exercises a module only through its public interface and asserts observable behaviour (resulting queue, counted/not counted, search results order, reconciled changes) — never internals. Tests should survive refactors of the implementation.
- **Primary seam — Core.** Every decision-heavy rule lives in Core and is tested there with `node:test`, run directly by Node's TypeScript type-stripping. Because Core has zero dependencies, these tests run anywhere, including environments without package-registry access. This is where most tests live.
- **Library store.** Migrations and the Home/play-count queries are tested in CI against a real SQLite database through Drizzle, seeded with fixture data.
- **Native module.** Kotlin JVM unit tests in CI for tag reading (small fixture audio files per format) and for scan diffing; Media3's test utilities for the player command/event mapping where practical.
- **UI.** No automated UI tests in v1. Each ticket has acceptance criteria verified manually on the S24+ using the CI-built APK.
- Prior art: none yet (new repository). The first ticket establishes the test setup for each seam.

## Further Notes

- **Glass on Android** (no longer a default material; see Visual design) **was the main technical risk.** Sampling the native views behind a surface is not straightforward in React Native Skia. The first UI work is a spike comparing approaches (for example, Android `RenderEffect` blur with an AGSL refraction shader versus a Skia snapshot of the backdrop) for visual quality and 120 Hz performance on the S24+. Acceptable fallback if refraction is too costly: blur + tint + specular rim highlight.
- **Track identity** is the file path in v1, with moved/renamed files matched by Core's reconciliation so history survives.
- **Development environment constraint:** the agent session working on this repository cannot reach the npm registry or the Android/Gradle repositories. GitHub Actions is the build and test environment of record; Core's zero-dependency design keeps its tests runnable inside the agent session as well.
- Library sizes in the tens of thousands of tracks are the design target for scan batching, list virtualisation and query indexes.
