# Wave v1 — Spec

Status: draft for review · Owner: @LeoPerez8

## Problem Statement

I keep my music as local files on my Android phone (Samsung Galaxy S24+). BlackPlayer has the features I want from a local player — folder-based library, gapless playback, lyrics, sleep timer, an equalizer and a tag editor — but its look and interaction model feel dated next to Apple Music, whose structure, gestures and "Liquid Glass" materials I much prefer. Apple Music, on the other hand, does not exist as a local-file player on Android. No app gives me both.

## Solution

**Wave** is a personal, Android-only local music player with a BlackPlayer-grade local-file engine and an interface of its own. Apple Music (iOS 26 era) is the reference for how it should feel, not a template to copy. It indexes the music folders I choose, plays them gaplessly through a native audio engine with full system integration (notification, lock screen, Bluetooth), and records every play so that rich listening statistics can be built later.

v1 delivers the core player and library. v2 adds the advanced audio and library-management features (see Out of Scope).

## Glossary

- **Track** — one song in the library: an audio file on the phone, or, while signed in to a Wave account, a missing song.
- **Wave account** — an optional sign-in (with Google) that keeps the Library's song list, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences on Wave's sync service, so another phone signed in to it gets them back. It never holds audio. _Avoid_: YouTube account (that's YT Music's sign-in), cloud library.
- **Recording key** — what makes two songs on different phones the same song for a Wave account: the album's identity (album title + album artist), the normalised title, and the disc and track number when the file has them. Length within 3 seconds only breaks a tie. Core's `recordingKey` decides.
- **Missing song** — a Track the Wave account has but this phone has no file for. It shows in the Library, dimmed, and tapping it downloads it. _Avoid_: ghost track, remote track.
- **non-D** — short for non-downloaded; a song this phone has no audio file for: YT Music songs, unsearched entries and missing songs.
- **Unsearched entry** — a playlist entry for a song a Spotify import listed and nothing has searched on YouTube Music yet. It keeps what Spotify listed (title, artists, album, length, Spotify's cover), as an unavailable entry does, and is stored the same way: no new column, no video id, marked unsearched. It looks, plays, queues and joins playlists as a YT Music entry does, and Wave searches it when it's about to play, or in the background while Wave runs (the background fill). Found, it becomes a YT Music entry in its place; not found, an unavailable entry; a search that fails leaves it unsearched. _Avoid_: pending song, placeholder.
- **Unavailable entry** — a playlist entry for a song a playlist import couldn't find: no match on YouTube Music or in the Library, or a Spotify local file with no artist, which is never searched. It keeps its place and what the source listed (title, artists, album, length, and Spotify's cover when there is one), shows dimmed with why ("Not found online", "Local file on Spotify"), never plays, and becomes the Library's song once the Library has it. _Avoid_: missing song (that's a Track with no file on this phone), not-found song.
- **Album** — tracks grouped by album title + album artist.
- **Credit** — a track's artist tag as written ("Ariana Grande, Doja Cat"); rows show it as is. A tag with several values (Vorbis comments, ID3v2.4) gives one credit per value, shown joined with commas; separate values always name separate artists.
- **Artist** — one artist a credit names. A track belongs to every artist its credit names, so the Artists list, Search, Android Auto and artist photos list individual artists, and a collaboration counts for, and shows on the page of, each of them. "feat." / "ft." / "featuring" (bracketed or not) always separate artists. `,` `·` `;` `/` "with" " x " "&" and "and" separate them too, unless that part of the credit is an artist in the Library on its own: some track's whole credit (or the part of it before a feat.) is exactly that name and none of its names is credited alone anywhere (alone, or featuring someone). So "Earth, Wind & Fire", "AC/DC", "Tyler, The Creator" and "Simon & Garfunkel" stay whole, and "Ariana Grande, Doja Cat" splits once "Ariana Grande" has a song of her own. A collaboration whose artists have no other song stays one artist until one of them does. Names are trimmed, and a name credited twice counts once (case and spacing aside). Core's `creditSplitter` decides; the split is recomputed when a scan or a deletion changes the Library's credits. Listening stats follow the credit too: a counted play of a collaboration counts in full for each artist it names (Stats' artist rankings, Home's Most played artists, the artist page's Stats block), while album rankings and Library-wide totals count it once. The artist page's "X of N songs" counts both over the songs whose credit names the artist (not songs that are only on an album filed under them). A deleted song's plays keep counting for every artist it named while that artist is in the Library.
- **Primary artist** — the first artist a credit names.
- **Go to Artist** — on a song or album credited to one artist, Go to Artist (the long-press menu, the selection bar) and the artist lines of Now Playing and an album's page open that artist. Credited to several, they open the artist choice card, listing each credited artist in credit order; picking one opens their page. The artist page you're on is left out, and a choice of one opens straight away. In selection, Go to Artist shows only when every checked item has the same set of artists. Core's `itemActions` and `artistLink` decide. A streamed song (a YT Music song, not downloaded) links to its artists too while YT Music is on, Now Playing's artist line and Go to Artist in the YT Music song's menu, and opens them integrated, with Online already selected on the page's Library | Online toggle: a name the Library has (compared as artist photos compare names) opens their Library page, any other the YouTube Music artist page. Credited to several, the artist choice card lists them in credit order, the YouTube Music ones with "YouTube Music" under the name. The artist page you're on is left out here too, a YouTube Music artist page included (by the name it was opened with); when it's the song's only artist, the menu has no Go to Artist and Now Playing's artist line just closes Now Playing to reveal it, as for a Library song. With YT Music off the link is gone. Core's `streamedArtistLink` decides. Checked beside other songs or albums, a streamed song is by the artists its link resolves to (a name the Library has is that Library artist), so Go to Artist shows when every checked item shares them and leads where the first streamed song's link does.
- **Album artist** — the artist an album is filed under: the first artist its album artist credit names (the credit falls back to the track's; "Various Artists" for compilations). The album shows under that artist's Albums and under the other credited artists' Appears on.
- **Genre** — an alternative grouping of tracks.
- **Style** — a group of genre tags that sound alike, finer than a genre family (Heavy: Nu Metal, Alternative Metal, Rap Metal; Soft / acoustic: Soft Rock, Pop Rock, Acoustic), so a seed's style tells its sound apart where its family can't. Core's style table sits beside the families (`genreStyle`); a family that doesn't split keeps one style of its own. A generic tag ("Rock", "Alternative", "Pop") and a Deezer profile (always coarse) know a family only, never a style, and Wave doesn't guess a style from a family. _Avoid_: sub-genre, subgenre.
- **Folder** — a directory on the phone. The index records the folder each track is in, and scan roots and excluded folders are folders, but the Library doesn't browse by folder.
- **Playlist** — a user-made, ordered list of tracks; with YT Music, it can also hold YT Music songs the library doesn't have yet (YT Music entries), streamed until the library has them.
- **Scan root** — a folder Wave indexes; **Excluded folder** — a folder under a root that Wave ignores.
- **Library scan** — walking the scan roots and reconciling files with the index (full on first run, incremental afterwards).
- **Play context** — what a playback was started from (album, artist, playlist, genre, search, all songs, favorites, home section). Plays recorded before the Library lost its Folders entry may say folder; they load and count like any other.
- **Queue** — the ordered list of tracks to be played, including the current one and **Up Next**.
- **Magic Queue** — a queue started from a seed (a song, playlist, album or artist) that keeps adding songs from the Library and YouTube Music on its own, 10 at a time, and learns from what you skip, favorite, download, play through and edit. It lasts a session: until something else plays. _Avoid_: radio, autoplay.
- **Taste profile** — what the owner likes beyond one Magic Queue session: a score for each song, artist and genre family, derived from the play history, favorites and downloads, each signal losing half its weight in 30 days. It keeps no state of its own, so it syncs with the history. _Avoid_: taste model, preferences.
- **Candidate catalog** — the YT Music songs YouTube Music has already shown Wave (its radios and artist pages), up to 5,000, each with its metadata and whose radios brought it (their seed artists), kept on the phone so recommendations can be built without the network. A cache: not synced, rebuilt as Wave reads YouTube Music. _Avoid_: song pool, candidate cache.
- **Artist graph** — which artists are close to which, derived from what Wave already keeps: who shows up in whose YouTube Music radio (the candidate catalog), the artists a saved YT Music artist page lists as "Fans might also like", and the artists the owner plays one after another. No request and no storage of its own. _Avoid_: artist map, similarity graph.
- **Harvest** — the background job that refills the candidate catalog by reading the radios of the seeds whose candidates run low, apart from building Home's mixes (which read only the phone): fully on Wi-Fi, on mobile data only for a seed with no candidates left, within about 5 MB a day. _Avoid_: crawl, prefetch (that's the Wi-Fi pre-download of audio).
- **Play event** — one record per time a track starts playing, with how long it was listened to and how it ended.
- **Counted play** — a play event that counts towards play counts: listened to for more than 15 seconds, or, for a song of 15 seconds or less, played to the end. Each play event counts at most once, however long it goes on.
- **Recap** — a Wrapped-style look back at one calendar month or year of listening (local time), computed on the phone from counted plays and watched as full-screen stories. A month has one once it has ended and holds at least 3 hours of counted listening; a year from 1 December with at least 20 hours (see "Recap"). _Avoid_: Wrapped, summary.
- **Sidecar** — a `.lrc` lyrics file beside an audio file (`Song.lrc` or `Song.mp3.lrc`).
- **Favorite** — a starred track (Apple Music's star).
- **Artist image** — the circle an artist is shown with: their **artist photo** (downloaded online, see Artist photos) when there is one, else the cover of the album that represents them, else their initial.
- **Now Playing** — the full-screen player; **Mini player** — the floating glass pill above the tab bar; **Toast** — a line on a small glass pill at the top of the screen, just under the status bar, that goes away on its own, or on a tap or a swipe up.
- **YT Music song** — a song found on YouTube Music rather than in the Library. It is identified by its YouTube video id (`yt:<videoId>` wherever a track id is expected, so it can't collide with a Library id), can be streamed or queued, and becomes a Track only once it's downloaded into the Library. A YT Music song the Library already has (see YT Music) is never offered as one. _Avoid_: online track, remote track, stream.

## User Stories

### First run and library

1. As a user, I want Wave to ask for all-files access on first launch, so that it can read every audio file in my music folders.
2. As a user, I want Wave to index my `Music` folder by default and let me choose the folders my music is actually in with the system folder picker, so that I get a working library whatever my folders are called.
3. As a user, I want to add and remove scan roots and exclude sub-folders in Settings, so that only the music I care about appears.
4. As a user, I want to see scan progress (files found / indexed) during the first scan, so that I know the app is working on a large library.
5. As a user, I want to start browsing while the first scan is still running, so that I don't wait for tens of thousands of files.
6. As a user, I want Wave to pick up new, changed and deleted files automatically each time I open it, so that my library stays current without a manual full rescan.
7. As a user, I want to pull to refresh in the Library, so that I can force a rescan after copying new music.
8. As a user, I want MP3, FLAC, M4A/AAC/ALAC, OGG/Opus and WAV files to be supported, so that my whole collection plays.
9. As a user, I want Wave to read title, artist, album, album artist, genre, year, track and disc number, duration, embedded artwork and embedded lyrics from tags, so that my library is organised the way I tagged it.
10. As a user, I want files without tags to still appear (using the file name as title and "Unknown Artist/Album"), so that nothing goes missing.
11. As a user, I want a renamed or moved file to keep its play history and favorite status, so that my statistics survive reorganising folders.

### Browsing (Library tab)

12. As a user, I want the Library tab to list Playlists, Artists, Albums, Songs, Genres and Favorites as entry rows like Apple Music, so that I can browse the way I think.
13. As a user, I want albums shown as an artwork grid with title and album artist, so that I can browse visually.
14. As a user, I want artists sorted alphabetically ignoring leading "The"/"A"/"An" and accents, so that sorting matches my expectations.
15. As a user, I want an album screen with large artwork, title, album artist, year, genre, track list ordered by disc and track number, and Play / Shuffle buttons, so that I can play an album properly.
16. As a user, I want an artist screen listing their albums and songs, so that I can explore an artist.
17. As a user, I want genre screens listing their tracks, so that I can browse by genre.
18. As a user, I want long lists (tens of thousands of songs) to scroll smoothly at 120 Hz, so that the app feels native on my phone.
19. As a user, I want a fast-scroll alphabet index on long lists, so that I can jump to a letter.
20. As a user, I want long-press context menus on tracks, albums, artists and playlists (Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Favorite, Go to Album/Artist), so that I can act without opening screens. The finger that long-pressed can stay down and slide onto an entry: lifting it there runs that entry, so one gesture does it (lifting anywhere else, or without sliding, leaves the menu open to tap; the list underneath holds still meanwhile).

### Playback

21. As a user, I want tapping a track to play it and queue the rest of its context after it, so that playback continues naturally.
    21a. As a user, I want a toast saying "New queue playing", with Undo, whenever a play replaces a queue that was playing, so that a mistaken tap doesn't lose my queue. It shows for a play started on the phone (a song tapped, Play, Shuffle, an album, playlist, mix or radio) over a queue with a song current (the queue picked back up at launch counts), once the new queue plays (after its unsearched song's search), saying any song skipped on the way (a tapped explicit song, one that couldn't be searched) after a "·"; not when there was no queue, for a play from Android Auto, when every song was skipped and nothing plays, or for a play whose song couldn't be searched (the queue stays). A newer play, from the phone or Android Auto, withdraws the Undo on show. Undo brings the old queue back as it was (Up Next, Play Next and Add to Queue entries, shuffle order, shuffle and repeat, and where its playback was started from) on the same song at the same position (from its start if the player had gone on to another song), playing; it shows no toast of its own. Undo starts a new play of the old song from that position; the new queue's song keeps the play it had, counted or not as usual. A toast with an action stays two seconds longer (Core's `toastDuration`); a tap on the action runs it, a tap elsewhere or a swipe up dismisses, and TalkBack offers it as an action. Core's `queueUndo` decides whether a play offers Undo (not one whose every song Don't play explicit passes over: the queue stays), `undoneQueue` what it goes back to, and `newQueueToast` the line.
22. As a user, I want gapless playback between consecutive tracks, so that live albums and mixes play without silence.
    22a. As a user, I want songs to crossfade into each other when I choose to, so that a playlist flows without a hard cut. Settings › Playback › Crossfade sets the overlap, 0 to 15 s in 0.5 s steps; 0 (Off, the default) keeps gapless playback exactly as before. With it on, every song that ends on its own overlaps the next one for that long (at most half the song), the outgoing one fading out as the next fades in, songs of one album and streamed YT Music songs alike. Skip, previous and picking a song switch at once, without a fade, as do repeat one and the last song. As the overlap starts, Now Playing, the notification, the lock screen and Android Auto move to the next song, with a continuous position: the next song, opened well ahead on a second player, keeps playing on that player, which becomes the media session's (queue, audio focus, position reports), while the player it replaces fades the outgoing song out and is released. Nothing seeks at the handoff, so no audio is replayed or skipped. Pausing pauses both. The outgoing song's play ends when its fade does. The next song is made ready well before the overlap, about 30 s ahead, on its own player (a streamed YT Music song has its address resolved and its start buffered, an unsearched playlist entry is searched again even if its last search just failed). A next song still not ready when the overlap should start is late: the outgoing song keeps playing at full volume, and the two overlap for whatever time is left once it's ready. An unsearched next song still being searched counts as not ready, as does one whose search failed and is queued to be asked again (Core's `searchingAfter`, which JS tells the service as `setSearchingAfter`), so the overlap waits for it instead of fading into the song after it (and being cut when it's found), and the log says it was still being searched; one ready only in the last second, or after the outgoing song ended, follows gaplessly. The outgoing song never fades out on its own while it has time left. Once the overlap has started it runs to its end unless the song being faded into changes (a skip, previous, picking a song, or the next song replaced in the queue), the owner seeks or the player fails: a queue sync that leaves the next song as it was, a brief rebuffer, or a late tick from a busy main thread (the fades follow the songs' positions, so their volumes catch up) doesn't end it. It is bounded by real time too: once it has run its length plus a second since the incoming song sounded (paused time left out), the second player closes and the incoming song plays at full volume, whatever the players' positions say. A queue sync that replaces the next song while it waits opens the new one instead. The app log says what crossfade did at every song change, in exactly one line: skipped, naming the rule that skipped it (crossfade off, the song buffering, paused, paused while the song to play was searched or held back by a call, the player stopped, unknown length, past the handoff point, no next song, the next song not ready or still being searched, with how late, the song changing before its overlap was due or while the next song waited for it, or the waiting next song called off and why); done, with the overlap it had (and how much shorter a late song made it); or cut, with why, how long it had run, and both players' positions, playback state, `playWhenReady`, `isPlaying` and suppression reason. Skipped and cut lines name what changed the song ("song changed by Next"): the song ending, repeat one, Next, Previous, a tap in the queue, a new queue, Undo, another queue change, or a media session command or media button with the controller it came from (the notification, Android Auto, another app's package); a seek names where it came from (the scrubber, a lyric tap, Previous restarting the song, a session command), so a skip or a seek can't be taken for a failure. JS sends the cause with each sync and seek (Core's `songChangeCause`, `SeekSource`), and the service notes session commands and media buttons itself; the log is all that changes. The sleep timer's end of current track stops after the song, with nothing of the next one. Core's `crossfadeDuration` turns the setting into the overlap, `crossfadePrefetchAt` says when the next song is made ready and `lateCrossfadeOverlap` how long a late one overlaps; the playback service decides which changes fade. The setting syncs with the Wave account.
    22b. As a user, I want to choose how much streamed audio Wave keeps loaded, so that a dead zone doesn't stop playback. Settings › Playback › Buffer offers 1, 2, 5, 10, 15 or 30 minutes, 2 by default (1 is the floor: anything less is worse than the player's own 50 s). While a streamed YT Music song loads, the player keeps that much audio loaded ahead of what's playing, carrying on past the current song into the next ones in Up Next, so with 30 minutes the next several songs are already loaded. Library files keep the player's default, as before. What's loaded for songs a queue change moves out of next place (a reorder, Up Next replaced) is dropped. A change applies from the player's next buffering decision, without restarting playback; the playback service keeps the choice on disk, so it holds without JS too (headless Auto, the media controls). About 40-60 MB at 30 minutes. Crossfade's early load of the next song works as before. Core's `readStreamBuffer` reads the choice, `streamBufferMs` is what JS hands the service (`setStreamBuffer`) and `bufferAheadMs` how far ahead it loads, which the service mirrors (Kotlin `StreamBuffer`, `StreamLoadControl`, a full APK build). The setting syncs with the Wave account.
23. As a user, I want play/pause, next, previous and seek, so that I control playback.
24. As a user, I want "previous" to restart the current track if more than 3 seconds have played, so that it behaves like Apple Music.
25. As a user, I want shuffle that keeps the current track playing and shuffles the rest, so that toggling shuffle doesn't interrupt me.
    25a. As a user, I want to choose between two kinds of shuffle by long-pressing any shuffle button: Real Shuffle, where every order is as likely, and Smart Shuffle, which mixes the songs I play most with the ones I play less, so that a shuffle can lean on what I like without repeating it in a row. Real Shuffle is the default. Smart Shuffle ranks the shuffled songs by all-time counted plays; the top half of the played ones (most played) are spread evenly among the rest (less played, never-played songs included), at random within each side, so neither side bunches up. The choice is saved (and syncs with the Wave account), and every shuffle uses it: Now Playing's button, the Shuffle buttons on album, playlist, artist, genre and song pages, YT Music album, artist and playlist pages, Discover mix pages, and Android Auto. Picking a kind on the card turns shuffle on and shuffles Up Next that way. Every shuffle button reads Smart Shuffle, with a single spark replacing one shuffle arrowhead in the same line style as the Tabler icons, while Smart Shuffle is chosen. Each button's accessibility hint explains the long press. A tap keeps its usual shuffle action. Core's queue (`reduceQueue` with a `Shuffle`) decides.
26. As a user, I want turning shuffle off to restore the original context order from the current track, so that I can go back to album order.
27. As a user, I want repeat off / repeat all / repeat one, so that I can loop a context or a track.
28. As a user, I want Play Next and Add to Queue, so that I can line up tracks without losing the current context.
29. As a user, I want to see, reorder (drag) and remove items in Up Next, so that I can curate what plays.
    29a. As a user, I want to start a Magic Queue from a song, playlist, album or artist, so that the music keeps going in the direction I picked and gets better the more I listen. "Magic Queue" sits in the long-press menu of a Library song (one with a file here), album, artist or playlist, after Add to Queue; not on a genre, a missing or YT Music song, or the selection bar. It replaces the queue with the existing "New queue playing" toast and Undo, and plays the song itself for a song seed, or a random song of the seed's otherwise, with 9 recommendations after it, which join at once when the candidate catalog has enough of the seed's songs, else once the seed's YouTube Music radio was read (15 s at most); shuffle is off. Up Next's heading reads "Magic Queue · from <seed>" while it lasts. Once the last recommendation in Up Next starts playing, the next 10 join the end of the queue. While a batch is picked, the first as the next ones, Up Next shows still skeleton rows in an Up Next row's shape (cover, two lines, the space of its end cells) where it will land: in place of "Nothing up next.", or under the songs already lined up, which stay put; TalkBack hears them as one busy group ("Loading songs"). They go the moment the batch lands, and when the pick fails, times out or something else plays (Core's `magicBatchLoading`; see Skeletons). The pick first waits for every signal so far to be learned (the end of the song before, a Next on it say; 15 s at most), so it weighs the previous batch's songs up to the one now playing; that last song's own outcome is weighed into the batch after. Recommendations come from the Library's songs with a file and from YouTube Music, whose songs come from the candidate catalog (see Candidate catalog) by Wave's recommendation engine, as Home's mixes do: its seed is the radio artists (an artist seed's artist, else the seed songs' two most credited primary artists; the session keeps which artists, and the seed songs' genre profile, through a restart) and the seed songs' genre profile, so its candidates are the catalog songs those artists' radios brought or by them, then those of their neighbourhood in the artist graph, each scored by the engine's signals (closeness, genre affinity, the taste profile, newness, popularity, variety), what the session learned of their artists and genres added on top (a quarter of its weight's binary log). A batch picks its YouTube Music songs from the best 50 of them (`MAGIC_CATALOG_POOL`), keeping only songs whose artist passes the shared genre affinity check against the seed's genre profile (an artist with no genre known stays out), and leaving out music videos, songs the Library has, songs the session queued, songs by an artist it excluded and a recording the catalog has twice (Core's `magicCatalogCandidates`). **Library songs pass the same check:** a batch picks only Library songs whose genre passes it against the seed's profile at Genre faithfulness's threshold (Core's `magicLibraryCandidates`, `magicLibraryThreshold`), except at its loosest step, Wander, where the Library keeps every genre as before (YouTube Music's candidates are still held at 0.2): a song's genre is its genre tag's, else (no tag) its primary artist's profile (the Library's, else Deezer's); a song with no genre known anywhere stays out, as an unknown YouTube Music artist does, and its artist's genres are looked up on Deezer in the background for later batches (10 artists a batch at most, those with the most such songs first, `magicLibraryLookups`). When the filter leaves the Library short, YouTube Music makes up for it. **Styles at Same genres and Close:** these two steps compare styles (Glossary: style; Core's `styleAffinity`, `magicCloseArtists`) instead of the families' profile. A song of a known style matches the seed's style at Same genres, and at Close a neighbouring one too (Heavy and Hard rock / grunge sound near each other, Heavy never near Soft / acoustic). A song of an unknown style (a generic tag, a Deezer profile, no tag) joins only when its artist is one of the seed's artists, in their neighbourhood in the artist graph, or (a YouTube Music song) brought by the seed's radio; a seed whose own style is unknown holds all its candidates to that closeness. A Library song's style is its own tags'; a YouTube Music song's its first artist's Library style, Deezer giving none. Balanced, Neighbours and Wander keep the families' rule above. **The seed's genre profile** is the seed songs' genre tags'; when none of them has one, it's its radio artists' genre profiles together (the Library's, else Deezer's; Core's `magicSeedProfile`), the batch first looking up on Deezer the radio artists nothing is known of yet (`magicSeedLookups`, 10 s at most, once per artist as every lookup is kept). Magic Queue additionally requires the shared genre mass (the sum of minimum family shares) to reach the same threshold, preventing a minor shared family from admitting a mostly incompatible profile. With no seed genre known anywhere, non-Wander batches withhold recommendations rather than silently accepting unrelated songs. Wander admits every Library genre, while YouTube Music still requires known seed and candidate profiles and at least 0.2 affinity/shared mass. The engine's weights, threshold and neighbourhood are a parameter, for Discover's sliders. **A batch makes no request while the catalog has enough:** only when the session runs low (fewer than 20 candidates left, `MAGIC_CATALOG_LOW`) does it read its radio artists' radios first (two pages each, at once, 15 s at most; each written into the catalog as it comes), and look up on Deezer the genres of the outside artists that decide its next 20 candidates (Core's `magicRadioLookups`), under the harvest's network policy (`magicRefillDue`, through `harvestNext`): on Wi-Fi whenever it runs low, on mobile data only when none is left and within the harvest's ~5 MB a day (the same count), and never when its radios were all read, or its refill reached YouTube Music, in the last 12 hours (a refill that brought nothing, for an artist YouTube Music doesn't have say, waits as long; the session keeps when it tried for the launch). A read that didn't reach YouTube Music (offline, say) is tried again by the next batch. Each session's batches start at the YouTube Music share Discover's New music level gives (below; 60% YouTube Music at the default level, all YouTube Music at All new), whether it was started from Home or from a long-press menu; a favorite or a download of a YouTube Music song moves YouTube Music's share up (a favorite more), a skip or a removal of one moves it down, always between 20% and 100%, so it never contradicts the slider; either source makes up for the other running short, and the two's songs are spread through each other. With the YT Music switch off, batches come from the Library alone; offline (Android says the network doesn't reach the internet), they come from the Library and the catalog's songs the audio cache holds whole (`cachedVideos`), which play without the network, and read nothing. Each song is picked at random, weighed by how much its artists and its genre were liked, starting with the seed's artists and genres, and by the taste profile (below) from the first batch on, so a session starts from what the owner likes rather than from zero: a song's own score moves its weight between a quarter and 4 times (a favorite starred today nearly triples it), its artists' and its genre family's between half and twice each, a skipped one's down; what the session learns comes on top, and its rules stay its own (a skip excludes an artist for the rest of the session, Play Next, Add to Queue and removals count within it). An artist is one and the same in the Library and on YouTube Music when their names match as artist photos compare them, so a skip excludes them from both sources and the seed's artists weigh on their YouTube Music songs too. Genres weigh by family (Genre mixes' families): a Library song's is its genre tag's, a YouTube Music song's its artist's dominant family (the Library's profile, else Deezer's), so its signals move that genre's weight for both sources. What it learns, from YouTube Music songs too (though they're never in play history): Next within a song's first 30 s is a skip (Next in Wave, the notification or a headset: moving on to the following entry; Previous, picking another Up Next entry and replaying the song say nothing), which excludes that song's primary artist for the rest of the session and halves its genre's weight, never down to nothing (a one-genre seed would run dry); a favorite, a download and a song played to the end are positive, strongest first; skipping after 30 s says nothing. Up Next takes edits as usual: a song you add with Play Next or Add to Queue counts as positive, and removing a recommendation counts as a skip. No song comes twice in a session. Playing anything else ends the session (Undo on its toast goes back to it); the session is saved beside the queue, so it goes on after Wave restarts, as the queue does. **Taste profile:** Wave remembers what the owner likes from playback anywhere in the app, not only the Magic Queue or mixes. A song played to the end is positive; one skipped within its first 30 s (the play ended `skipped` before `MAGIC_SKIP_MS`) negative; a download (a song downloaded from YouTube Music) more positive; a favorite most (3, 2, 1 and −1 points when fresh). Each signal loses half its weight every 30 days, counted from when it happened: the play's start, the star's last change, the file's date (when it entered the Library, or the file's own date when older, so editing its tags doesn't make it new). An artist's score, and a genre family's, add up their songs' signals (a song gone from the Library counts for none). Core derives it from the play events, favorites and downloads (`tasteProfile.ts`: `tasteProfile`, `tasteLearned`, `tasteWeight`, `tastePlaySignal`), so it adds no state and syncs with the history; YouTube Music songs, never in play history, count once downloaded. The app derives it once per launch, the first time something asks for it (usually Home's first visit, for the Magic Queue row's picks; else a mix build or a Magic Queue batch), then keeps it up to date as Library plays end, songs are starred and YT Music songs are starred or downloaded (`playback/TasteProfile.ts`, `playback/liveTaste.ts`). A streamed YT Music song's play end doesn't count, since it would be gone after a restart; an unstar has the profile derived again at the next ask; a signal that comes while it's being derived counts once it is. **Discover's Magic Queue row** (the first of Home's Discover section; see Editable Home) shows nine of the Library's songs with a file, in a three-column, three-row grid at the default phone font scale, to start a Magic Queue from with one tap. Each square cover holds its readable title on a dark caption plate, including over bright or missing artwork. At 1.3x font scale it uses two columns, and at 2x one; narrow widths reduce columns too, keeping touch targets usable. The full accessible name includes title, artist, album and the seed action. They're picked at random weighed by the taste profile (`tasteWeight`: favorites and songs played through likelier, songs the owner skips less likely; alike while the profile can't be read), each once. A new list comes each calendar day; a song gone from the Library is replaced in its place, and a list short of nine fills up as the Library grows. Pulling the row right past its start brings another list at once, of songs the section didn't show that day where others are left (those come back only to make up the nine), with a "New songs" toast whose Undo puts the previous list back while it shows, as the mix rows' pull does. Tapping a song starts a Magic Queue from it, as its long-press menu's Magic Queue does (the "New queue playing" toast and its Undo); its long-press opens the song's menu. The row shows with YT Music off too, its Magic Queues then playing from the Library alone. Discover's settings card (see Editable Home) sets it with the mix rows: New music, on the mixes' scale, is the YouTube Music share every Magic Queue starts at, from Home or any long-press menu, from Mostly familiar (20%) through More familiar (40%), Balanced (60%, the default) and Mostly new (80%) to All new (100%; `magicQueueShare`); at All new a Magic Queue plays only YouTube Music songs while YouTube Music has candidates, and with YT Music off or offline it still plays from the Library (and fully cached songs). Signals then move the share within 20-100% as above. Genre faithfulness and Popularity tune how the engine ranks every Magic Queue's YouTube Music candidates from the catalog, from Home or any long-press menu, and Genre faithfulness's threshold holds its Library songs too, Wander aside; each batch reads them again, so a move applies to a running Magic Queue from its next batch. A refill's Deezer lookups (`magicRadioLookups`) still aim at the default threshold. What it shows is kept on the phone (not synced). Core's `magicQueueSection.ts` decides (`magicQueuePicks`, `magicQueuePicksDue`, `refreshedMagicQueuePicks`, `undoneMagicQueueRefresh`); the app's is `src/home/magicQueueSection.ts`. Core's `magicQueue.ts` (`startMagicQueue`, `magicBatch`, `magicSignal`, `magicPlaySignal`, `magicQueueEdits`, `wantsMagicBatch`, `magicBatchLoading`, `magicRadioArtists`, `magicLibrarySongs`, `magicYtSong`, `magicArtistKey`) decides, with `playEvents.ts` telling a Next apart (`movedToFollowing`) and whether a play's end is still to come (`playEndPending`); a batch joins with the queue's `append` command, as the session's own entries rather than songs lined up by hand. Its YouTube Music candidates are `magicQueueCatalog.ts`'s (`magicCatalogCandidates`, `magicRefillDue`); the app's are `playback/magicCatalogRead.ts` and `playback/magicQueueRadio.ts`.

## Implementation Decisions

### Platform and distribution

- Android only. Minimum Android 12 (API 31, required for native blur), target the latest SDK. Tuned for the Galaxy S24+ (120 Hz).
- App name **Wave**, application id `dev.leoperez.wave` (permanent).
- Personal use, sideloaded APK. No Play Store, so `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` (all-files access) is acceptable.
- The Library works offline; the online features are listed under Network. Wave's one backend is the sync service behind the optional Wave account (see Wave account and sync), and nothing else in Wave needs it.

### Network

Wave uses the network for three features, each behind its own Settings switch (on by default) that stops every request it makes, for the Wave and Spotify accounts, whose switch is signing in, and for lyrics translation, whose switch is the Lyrics view's Translate button (off by default).

- **Wave account** (Wave's sync service, see Wave account and sync): while signed in, sends the Library's song list (each song's tags and, for a downloaded one, its YouTube video id, never the file or its path), playlists, favorites, play history and preferences, and the Google ID token once at sign-in. Signed out, Wave sends it nothing, and the Library, play history and stats never leave the phone.

- **Artist photos** (Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends artist names.
- **Lyrics and genre** (LRCLIB and Deezer's open API, free and keyless; below): sends a song's title, artist, album and duration. Home's mixes' harvest and the Magic Queue send Deezer the names of radio artists outside the Library, for their genres, and the Magic Queue also those of Library artists whose songs have no genre tag (see Artist mixes, Genre affinity, Magic Queue). The Lyrics view sends LRCLIB the same about a non-D song, or a Library song whose file has no lyrics, when it searches for its lyrics (see "Lyrics online").
- **Lyrics translation** (Google Translate's keyless web endpoint, `clients5.google.com/translate_a/t`; see "Lyrics translation"): sends the text of a song's lyric lines (each line with words, nothing else about the song) and the target language (the phone's), only while Translate is on and the Lyrics view shows that song's lyrics, and only when the phone has no saved translation for them. It doesn't obey "Fix songs online": tapping Translate is the consent.
- **YT Music** (YouTube Music, below): `music.youtube.com/youtubei/v1/*` for search (and, as later tickets land, browsing, song details and the player), `www.youtube.com` for the player script, visitor data and BotGuard's proof-of-origin endpoints (`api/jnn/v1/*`), `*.googlevideo.com` for audio, `*.googleusercontent.com`, `yt3.ggpht.com` and `i.ytimg.com` for covers, `accounts.google.com` for sign-in. It sends the query in Search when Online is tapped on its Library | Online toggle (and the refined query after it, until the field is cleared) or when the Library has nothing for it, the name of an artist whose page (or whose album's page) asks for its YT Music section (only with Online selected on the page's toggle), that artist's YouTube browse id and the id of their songs playlist (the "See all" on their page's Songs shelf, also saved for Popular's order; the read waits for Online, settled page content, usable Android connectivity and the shared allowance) and, for an album page once that artist's answer is saved, the album's browse id, the browse id of a YouTube Music artist whose page is opened (an artist the Library doesn't have) and the id of their songs playlist, the id of a playlist imported from a link (see "Importing playlists from a link"), the names of the artists a Magic Queue's radio grows from (in an Artists search, unless their artist page saved the match) and their pages' browse ids, for their radios (see Magic Queue), the video ids of songs it plays or downloads (or fetches ahead on Wi-Fi for Home's mixes, see Wi-Fi pre-download) or whose lyrics the Lyrics view looks for, a shared playlist's name and its songs' video ids (and the title and artists of each it searches), see "Sharing playlists", YouTube's visitor data and, once signed in, the account's session cookie. It never sends the Library's contents (beyond a playlist the owner shares), play history or stats. Offline, or when YouTube fails, the YT Music parts show a quiet line and the Library works as before.
  - Sharing a song's links (see "Sharing a song") sits under the same switch. Its YT Music and song.link links are built from the song's YouTube video id, with no request; a Library song without one, or an unsearched entry, is first searched once on YouTube Music by its title and artists. Odesli's API (`api.song.link`) is no longer called.
  - Importing a Spotify playlist sits under the same switch, since its songs become YT Music entries. It sends Spotify the playlist id (`open.spotify.com/embed/playlist/<id>`, then `api-partner.spotify.com/pathfinder/v2/query` with the playlist's URI, the page asked for and the anonymous token the embed page gave) and, for a shared short link, its code (`spotify.link`, `spotify.app.link`). The card shows the cover from `i.scdn.co` or `image-cdn-ak.spotifycdn.com`, and so do the playlist's unsearched and unavailable entries. Import itself sends YouTube Music nothing. A song the Library doesn't have is searched when it's needed (about to play): one YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists. Nothing about the Library, play history or stats goes to either (see "Importing playlists from a link").
- **Spotify account** (Spotify's web-player endpoints, see Spotify account): only while signed in. `accounts.spotify.com` gets whatever is typed into Spotify's sign-in page in the web view; `open.spotify.com` the session cookie (`sp_dc`), two TOTP codes and the device time, for a token (and, when Spotify changes its web player, a plain fetch of that page and its script); `api-partner.spotify.com` the token, for the account's name and photo, when a song's share sheet opens with YT Music on (see "Sharing a song") that song's title and artists as a search term, and, when a playlist is shared on Spotify (see "Sharing playlists"), each of its songs' title and artists as a search term, the shared playlist's URI and page offsets to read it and the account's playlists back, the track URIs it adds and the entry uids it removes or moves; `spclient.wg.spotify.com` the token, the shared playlist's name, its URI and its public and link permission. Every request carries the phone's IP address and a desktop browser's User-Agent. Nothing else about the Library, and nothing about play history, stats or the YT Music account, goes to Spotify.

### Artist photos

- Wave downloads a photo of each artist from **Deezer's open API** (no account or key): it searches the artist's name and takes the best match's picture. Artist names are the only thing sent. A "Download artist photos" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no requests and artists show their album artwork. "Clear artist photos" deletes the downloaded ones.
- **Matching** is Core's (`artistPhoto.ts`): names compare with case, accents, a leading "The", punctuation and "&"/"and" ignored; only an exact match counts, and of namesakes the most followed one (its photo or none). "Various Artists", "Unknown Artist" and names without letters are never looked up.
- **Caching.** Lookups run in the background after each library scan, three at a time, and stop after a few failures in a row (offline). Photos are saved beside the album artwork (`files/artwork/artists/`) as a thumbnail (lists, menus) and a large image (artist page), and are never fetched again. A lookup with no match is retried after a week, a failed one after a day (`artist_photos` table, by artist key).
- **Fallback.** Until a photo arrives, without a match, offline or with downloads off, the artist's album-artwork image shows.

### Find in Playlist

A song can be found inside a playlist (#477): a local playlist, a YT Music playlist and Favorites.

- **The field.** "Find in Playlist" sits at the top of the list, under the page's header (Back, or Favorites' title). The list opens scrolled just past it, so it's hidden until the list is pulled down, as in Apple Music. However short the list, it can always scroll the field out of sight.
- **Matching.** Typing narrows the list at once, by each song's title, artist and album, as Search matches (case, accents and punctuation folded, every word of the query starting a word of the song, in any order; Core's `createPlaylistFinder`, `findInPlaylist.ts`). The songs found keep the playlist's order: nothing is ranked. While a query narrows the list, the page's head (cover, title, Play / Shuffle) steps aside so the songs found sit under the field, a playlist's rows can't be dragged to a new place (a left swipe still removes one; see Swipe actions), and Favorites' A–Z spine is gone.
- **Results.** Tapping a song found plays the whole playlist from that song, in the playlist's order (shuffled when shuffle is on), as tapping it in the full list does.
- **YT Music playlists** read all their pages as they open (up to the 5,000-song cap), so a query typed meanwhile has the rest already on its way, and it narrows every page read. While pages are still arriving, the songs found so far show with skeleton rows under them, and "No songs match" waits until every page is in (Core's `findInPlaylistHead`); pages that arrive join the matches.
- **No match.** A query no song matches shows "No songs match" under the field. Clearing the field (its × or deleting the text) brings back the full list, its head and the scroll position it had before the query.
- **Library › Songs and Artists** (#530) have the same field, "Find in Songs" and "Find in Artists", placed, hidden and cleared the same way. Songs narrows by each song's title, artist and album; Artists by the artist's name alone (Core's `artistFindText`), with the same matching and not a second copy of it (`createFindableList`). What's found keeps the list's order, in its current sort. Tapping a song found plays the whole Songs list from it, as tapping it in the full list does; tapping an artist found opens the artist's page. While a query narrows either list, its head (the count, Play / Shuffle, the sort control) steps aside and the A–Z index is hidden; they return once the field is cleared. The Songs list, read in pages (#310), reads what finds each song (title, artist credit, album) only once a query is first typed, then follows Library changes; until it's read, nothing shows under the field. A query that finds nothing says "No songs match" or "No artists match" (`findNoMatch`).

### Playlist recommendations

An editable playlist ends with up to five distinct matching song suggestions (#558–562). The existing recommendation engine, candidate catalog and Artist graph supply them. Playlist similarity leads: compatible songs, artists and genres. The same album or artist is strong evidence, not automatic eligibility. Already-present recordings and duplicate Library/YouTube Music representations stay out; an empty or exhausted candidate pool shows a truthful short or empty section. Loading or failure never prevents using the playlist, and failure offers recovery. With YT Music off or offline, eligible Library songs still qualify.

Among eligible candidates, favorites and downloads have priority. If five slots can hold all qualifying favorites/downloads, all are included; if more than five qualify, the most compatible win. An unrelated favorite or download does not qualify just because of its status. The next renewal reflects changes to those statuses.

**Add** inserts the suggestion once, removes it from the suggestions and records one positive signal for this playlist only, after the addition succeeds. **Dismiss** removes the suggestion and records mild disinterest for this playlist only. Either action fills just the freed slot when another candidate exists, leaving the other visible suggestions in place; acceptance ranks the replacement using the updated playlist contents. A dismissal does not reoffer that recording in the same renewal cycle, and mildly lowers related song/artist/genre priority without permanently banning an artist or genre. Rapid repeated taps and failed saves cannot duplicate playlist entries or feedback, or silently claim success.

Feedback persists across reopening and restarting and belongs to the playlist's identity. The baseline Artist graph can supply similarity, but acceptance/dismissal never writes global graph weights, changes the global Taste profile, or adjusts other playlists, Discover, mixes or Magic Queue. An owner can love Taylor Swift globally and dismiss her for one alternative-pop playlist. Adding a suggestion neither favorites nor downloads it; later explicit favorite, download and playback actions keep their independent semantics.

### Swipe actions

A song row swipes sideways to run an entry of its own long-press menu (#529), one shared gesture for every list of song rows. Settings › Swipe actions turns the swipes off or picks what each direction runs (#531).

- **Where.** Library › Songs, an album's, artist's and genre's songs, a playlist, Favorites, Home's song lists, Discover mixes, Search results, YT Music albums, playlists and artist pages, and Stats lists. Not Up Next, which keeps its × button and drag to reorder.
- **Right** adds the song to the queue by default, as its menu's Add to Queue does, with the same toast.
- **Left** removes it by default: inside a playlist it leaves the playlist at once (also while Find narrows the list); elsewhere it's the menu's destructive entry, Delete for a song with a file or Remove from Library for a missing song, with the confirmation the menu asks (Cancel leaves the song).
- **Settings › Swipe actions** has a **Swipe actions** switch (on on a fresh install) and, while it's on, a **Swipe right** and a **Swipe left** list, each picking one of a song row's long-press actions (Core's `SWIPE_CHOICES`): Play Next, Add to Queue, Magic Queue, Add to Playlist, Favorite, Download, Go to Album, Go to Artist, Fix Song, Share and Remove (Remove from Playlist inside a playlist, Delete or Remove from Library elsewhere). Favorite runs the star as the row's menu offers it (Remove from Favorites on a starred song), and Download the song's download as its menu does (Retry Download after a failure). Off, no song row swipes anywhere, playlists included, screen readers lose the swipe actions too, and every action stays in the row's menu; the Settings row then says "Off". The choices are kept as one value (`settings.swipeActions`, Core's `readSwipeSettings` / `storedSwipeSettings`, nothing kept at the defaults; a choice this Wave doesn't know reads as its default) and sync with the Wave account. A row renders again when they change, and only then; a swipe reads them again as it runs.
- **Fit.** Each direction runs an entry the row's menu offers (Core's `rowSwipeActions`, from the row's menu actions: `itemActions`, `ytSongActions` with its download entries, `unavailableEntryActions`), so a swipe never does what the menu wouldn't. A direction the menu has no entry for is inert and the row doesn't move that way: by default a missing song or a playlist's unavailable entry doesn't swipe right, a YT Music song outside a playlist doesn't swipe left; with other choices, Download on a song with a file, Go to Album on a YT Music song without one or on that album's own page, Magic Queue on a YT Music song, Fix Song on a song with every tag it looks up, Favorite or Share on an unavailable entry. A row works out what only some choices need only while a direction is set to them, without following it: a download's state (as the journal is when the row renders) and a streamed song's artists for Go to Artist (once the Library's artists are read; a row set to it starts that read). Library › Songs' rows read their album, primary artist and whether Fix Song can do anything for them with what they show. Whatever opens something (Add to Playlist's picker, the Delete confirmation, Go to Album) opens it as the menu does; the entry is planned again from the menu as it is when the swipe ends, so a direction whose entry has gone since the row rendered does nothing.
- **Gesture.** The row follows the finger and uncovers, on the side it leaves, the action's icon and short label (teal, red for a removal). Past a third of the row it arms, full strength and with a light haptic tick; letting go then runs the action, letting go sooner (or flicking back) springs back with nothing done (Core's `rowSwipeArmed`, `resolveRowSwipe`). Under reduce motion the row follows the finger and goes back without the spring. Vertical scrolling, taps, long-press and a playlist's drag to reorder are unaffected; no row swipes while selecting or while a held long-press freezes the list.
- **Screen readers** get each direction's action as a custom action on the row, named as the menu names it.

### Lyrics and genre

- **Fix Song** (a song's long-press menu; **Fix Songs** in the selection bar with more than one song checked, and in an album's, artist's, playlist's or genre's long-press menu) looks up online what a song lacks of its lyrics, genre, track number (with the disc), year and explicit flag, and writes only that into the file. It never replaces a value the song has, so a wrong match can't retag a song tagged by hand. It shows only on a song missing at least one of the five (Core's `canFixSong`), and a song whose explicit flag was never checked (unknown) counts as missing it; in the selection bar it shows when at least one checked song is, and works on those (Core's `songsToFix`). A collection's menu (album, artist, playlist, genre) offers Fix Songs when at least one of its Library songs is (Core's `itemActions` over its songs) and runs the same Fix Songs on those, the others counted as skipped; a playlist's YT Music, unsearched and unavailable entries and missing songs have no file and are left out. It sits where a song's menu places Fix Song, after the play cards and queue entries and before the destructive entries. Settings › Lyrics and tags runs it on the whole Library in the background ("Fix songs in the whole Library"), with progress and Cancel. Every run ends with a summary: fixed, not found, failed, and how many songs were skipped as having everything. Wave says it on a toast, a line such as "Fixed 3 songs · 1 not found" (Core's `tagLookupsToast`), never a card to close. It stops early when the services can't be reached (three lookups failing in a row). This replaced Find Lyrics and Genre, which always replaced a song's lyrics and genre.
- **Only what's missing is fetched** (Core's `fixLookups` and `deezerFollowUps`), from a menu, the selection bar, the whole-Library run and the fill-in after a download alike: LRCLIB is searched only for a song without lyrics; Deezer is searched only for a song missing a genre, a track number or a year. After Deezer's search, the album is fetched only for a missing genre, or a missing year when the match is on the song's own album, and the tracklist only for a missing track number on the song's own album. A request whose answer couldn't fill anything isn't made.
- **The whole-Library run goes on in the background**, the way YT Music downloads do: a foreground service (`LibraryLookupService`, data sync) keeps Wave running with it in the background and the screen off, also while music plays, and shows a "Fix Songs" notification with the progress ("Fixing songs… N of M") and Cancel. The run itself is JavaScript; while it goes on, Wave's JS runtime is held (`JsRuntime`, as for downloads and Android Auto) along with a partial wake lock, so JS timers and lookups keep going without the Activity. Settings shows the same progress and Cancel, also when Wave is reopened while the run still goes on. Cancel in either place, or turning "Fix songs online" off, stops it; songs already done keep what was written. When it ends, the notification shows the summary in full, and Wave shows it too, on the same toast, at once when it's on screen, otherwise the next time it is (the summary is kept until it's seen; once the toast has gone, so does the notification). Android 13+ asks for notification permission at the first run; without it the run still goes on, with the notification hidden. Android 15 caps data-sync services at six hours a day; past that the notification goes and the run carries on while Wave stays alive.
- **Sources.** Lyrics come from **LRCLIB** (`lrclib.net`, free, no key): synced (LRC) lyrics are preferred, plain lyrics are taken when that's all there is. The genre is that of the song's album on **Deezer's open API**: a track search, then the matched album's first genre. The same album gives the year (of its release date), and its tracklist gives the song's track number, plus the disc for an album on more than one disc. The matched track itself says whether the song is explicit (Deezer's `explicit_lyrics`, whichever album it's on), so the flag costs no request of its own: a song missing only the flag makes the search alone. Albums and tracklists are fetched once per album in a session, and a whole-Library run spends at least 400 ms on each song, which keeps its three Deezer requests at most (search, album, tracklist) under Deezer's limit of 50 every 5 seconds. A song's **title, artist, album and duration** are the only things sent, and "Unknown Artist" / "Various Artists" songs are never looked up. A "Fix songs online" switch in Settings (on by default) says so; off, Wave makes no lookups and Fix Song is gone from menus and the selection bar.
- **Matching** is Core's (`lyricsAndGenre.ts`): titles and artists compare like artist photos (case, accents, punctuation, a leading "The" ignored), plus featured artists ("feat. X") and version notes in brackets or after a dash ("(Live)", "- Remastered 2011"). Lyrics must be within 2 seconds of the song's length; then synced beats plain, the song's own album beats another, and the closest length wins. The genre's album is the matched track's, preferring the song's own album, then the closest length. The year and track number are taken only when that album is the song's own (titles compared as above), since a compilation or live record numbers and dates the song differently; a song without an album tag gets neither. A release date that's missing or malformed ("0000-00-00") gives no year. No match leaves that field alone; no match at all leaves the file untouched.
- **Writing** is native (`SongTagWriter`, jaudiotagger): only the lyrics, genre, track number, disc number and year fields change, and the audio and every other tag stay as they were (JVM tests check this for MP3, FLAC, M4A, Ogg Vorbis and WAV). Core decides what is sent to it (`missingTags`: only the fields the indexed song lacks, the disc going with the track number), and the writer checks the file too: a track number or year the file already has is kept, and the disc number is only written with the track number. A WAV file's lyrics and disc number go into an ID3 chunk (RIFF INFO has no field for them) and its genre, track number and year into both. A format Wave can't write tags to (Opus, raw AAC) gets the lyrics in a sidecar `.lrc` and no genre, track number or year, and the summary lists those songs. A track number or year it can't take isn't a failure on its own; a song whose track number and year were all that was written counts as fixed. A sidecar already beside a file is replaced too, since it would win over the tags in the Lyrics view.
- **Skipping.** Every run looks up only songs missing lyrics, a genre, a track number, a year or a checked explicit flag (Core's `songsToFix`); songs with all five are skipped and counted. Since every song's flag starts unknown, the first whole-Library run after the update that added it revisits every song once; a song Deezer doesn't find stays unknown and is tried again next run. Run the whole-Library one again after adding music and it does the new and incomplete songs, plus any it found nothing for last time. Progress ("N of M") counts only those songs, and the summary says how many it skipped. Lyrics count whether they're in the tags or in a sidecar `.lrc`. The index records this per song (`tracks.has_lyrics`): scans set it, listing each folder's `.lrc` files once, and so do Fix Song's writes. Wave doesn't notice another app adding or deleting a sidecar until the song's own file changes. The update that added the column marks every indexed file as changed, so the next scan reads them all once.
- **After writing** the file is read again as a scan reads it and its index entry updated, so Genres, Search, the album page (track order, and the year when the album had none) and an open Lyrics view show the new values without a rescan.
- **Downloads.** The fill-in after a download is Fix Song on the new file: it looks up and writes only what the file lacks. A YT Music download's tags take the track number and year from its album page, and Deezer's stand in when the page doesn't give them.

### Lyrics online

Opening Lyrics on a non-D song searches for its lyrics online instead of leaving the view blank (#415, #416). The search runs when the Lyrics view opens, never on play.

- **While it searches** the view shows a still skeleton of placeholder lyric lines.
- **Order** (Core's `nonDLyrics`, `onlineLyrics.ts`): LRCLIB first, by the song's title, first artist, album and length, matched as Fix Song matches (synced preferred, within 2 s; Core's `nonDLyricsSong` builds the lookup); then, when LRCLIB has nothing, YT Music's own lyrics for the song's video (its watch page says where they are; `ytMusicFoundLyrics`), only while YT Music is on. A source that can't be reached doesn't stop the next one.
- **Found lyrics** show as a Library song's do (synced ones follow playback, plain ones scroll) and are saved on the phone against the song's YouTube video id (`saved_lyrics`, this phone only, never synced), so reopening is instant and works offline.
- **No lyrics anywhere** shows "No lyrics found", remembered for a week (`LYRICS_MISS_RETRY_MS`), then searched again on the next open.
- **A network failure** (a source that couldn't be reached while none had lyrics) isn't remembered: the view says it couldn't search and offers Try Again.
- **Switch.** The search obeys "Fix songs online" (Settings › Lyrics and tags). Off, a non-D song shows "No lyrics" and nothing is asked; lyrics already saved on the phone still show.
- **No video yet.** An unsearched entry still being searched has no video: it searches LRCLIB only and saves nothing until it has a video id.
- **Details first.** A streamed song whose title, artists and length the queue doesn't know yet waits for them before searching, so LRCLIB is always asked before YT Music's plain lyrics are saved. A song no source could be asked about (no lookup, YT Music off) shows "No lyrics found" and remembers nothing.
- **Downloads reuse them** (#418). Downloading a song whose lyrics are saved for its video writes those into the file, with no LRCLIB or YT Music lyrics request (Deezer is still asked for the genre and the rest; Core's `ytDownloadHas`, `ytDownloadTags`). A remembered miss doesn't stop the download's own search. Once the file is in the Library, what was saved for the song's video is dropped, and the Lyrics view, still open on the streamed song in the queue, reads the file (the Library song it now is, `libraryFinder`) and searches it online as a Library song when it has no lyrics. Until the Library has been read, a streamed song's search waits, so a song with a file is never searched as non-D.
- **Share** works on lyrics found this way.
- **Library songs** (#417). A Library song whose file has no lyrics (none in its tags, no sidecar `.lrc`) is searched the same way when Lyrics opens, with the same skeleton, order and Try Again (Core's `libraryLyrics`, `libraryLyrics.ts`): LRCLIB by the song's title, artist credit, album and length, as Fix Song sends them (`libraryLyricsSong`), then YT Music's lyrics only for a song downloaded from a YT Music video (`tracks.video_id`). A hit is written into the file as Fix Song writes lyrics (its tags, or a sidecar `.lrc` for a format without tag writing; `writeFoundLyrics`) and shows; from then on it comes from the file, with no request. A file that can't be written still shows the lyrics this once and keeps nothing. A miss shows today's "No lyrics" with its `.lrc` hint and is remembered for the track for a week (`lyrics_misses`, this phone only, never synced; deleting the song drops it). Lyrics already in the file or a sidecar never trigger a search, and with "Fix songs online" off the view shows today's "No lyrics" and asks nothing.

### Lyrics translation

The Lyrics view can show a song's lyrics translated into the phone's language (#527). Deezer's app translates popular songs' lyrics, but only for a signed-in Deezer session, so Wave asks Google Translate.

- **Translate** sits in the Lyrics view's header beside Share, for a song with lyrics to share (`shareableLyricLines`). Graphite while off, teal while on. Turning it on keeps it on for the next songs and through restarts until it's turned off (`settings.translateLyrics`, this phone only, never synced).
- **Service.** Google Translate's keyless web endpoint (`clients5.google.com/translate_a/t?client=dict-chrome-ex&sl=auto&tl=<target>`, one POST per song with a form field `q` per line with words). It's unofficial and can change, so a failure only ever degrades quietly. Core builds the request (`lyricsTranslationRequest`) and reads the reply, one `[translation, language found]` per line, back into one translation per original line, empty lines and instrumental gaps kept in place (`parseLyricsTranslation`, `lyricsTranslation.ts`; real replies in `docs/google-translate-samples`). A reply that doesn't answer every line counts as a failure.
- **Target language.** The phone's (`Intl`'s locale), as Google names it: the language without its region, but Chinese keeps its script (`zh-CN`, `zh-TW`) and European Portuguese its region (`pt-PT`) (`lyricsTranslationTarget`). There's no setting for it.
- **Display.** Each original line keeps its place, with its translation under it in Body, dimmed. Synced lyrics stay synced: the sung line's translation lights with it (tertiary to ink at 72%), and a tap on either seeks to the line. A translation Google gives back unchanged isn't shown. While the request runs, a still skeleton line sits under each line with words.
- **Same language.** When the language Google found for most lines is the phone's (regions aside), the view shows the original with a quiet Footnote line above it, "These lyrics are already in <language>." (`lyricsTranslationLanguageName`, in English), and that answer is saved too, so the song isn't asked again.
- **Saved.** A song's translation is saved in the key-value store (this phone only, never synced) under the song and target language (`lyricsTranslationKey`: a Library track by its id, a non-D song by its track id), with a fingerprint of the lyrics it translated (`storedLyricsTranslation`). Reopening the song's lyrics shows it with no request, offline too. When the song's lyrics change (Fix Song, a new file) the fingerprint no longer matches (`savedLyricsTranslation`), so the saved translation is dropped and the new lyrics are translated afresh.
- **Failure.** Offline, after 15 s without an answer, on an error status or an unreadable reply, the original lyrics show unchanged with a quiet line above them, "Couldn't translate the lyrics.", and Try Again. Nothing is saved, and translations already saved still show.

### Lyrics on Android Auto

- **The Lyrics toggle** sits in the overflow menu of Auto's player screen ("Lyrics on" / "Lyrics off"), off on a fresh install and remembered between drives.
- **Lyrics are drawn into the artwork** (the owner's pick in #150). Auto draws the player screen itself, and Wave controls only the title, the artist and the artwork image there, so with the toggle on and synced lyrics Wave hands Auto a square image of three lines over the cover dimmed: the line being sung big and bold in the middle, the lines before and after it smaller and dimmed. The split-screen card shows it in its top area, and the full-screen player beside the title. The title and artist stay as they are, and Auto's progress bar and buttons are untouched. A sung line too wide for the image shrinks, then wraps onto two rows, then ends in "…"; lines sung together share one line (`Me / You`). The rows stay in the middle band of the square, in case Auto crops it to fit the card.
- **What shows when** is Core's call (`autoLyricLines`): which three lines, and when the next change is due. The neighbours skip pauses, so the line after a pause already shows below. The cover is back with the toggle off, with Auto disconnected, for a track with only plain lyrics or none (Auto reads a file's lyrics only, so a non-D song has none there even when the Lyrics view found some online), before the first line and in a pause between lines (an empty LRC line).
- **Timing.** JS follows the player and wakes at each line's start (`startAutoLyricLines`), sending the lines only when they change, so Auto gets one new image per line, as it got one new text line before. The playback service hands the session a new artwork URI per set of lines (`content://<package>.artwork/lyrics/<id>`, `LyricArtworks`); Wave's artwork provider draws the image (`LyricArtPainter`, 800 px JPEG) the first time Auto or the notification reads it, and keeps the latest four.
- **The phone** shares the artwork with Auto: while Auto is connected with the toggle on, the notification and lock screen show the lyrics image as the cover. Once Auto disconnects (or the toggle goes off) the service drops the lines at once, without waiting for JS, and they get the cover back. The artist line is never touched.

### Sharing lyrics

A few lines of a song's lyrics go out as an image card, as Spotify does it (#400).

- **Share** sits at the right of the Lyrics view's header, only for a song with lyrics to share (synced or plain, with at least one line of words; Core's `shareableLyricLines`). A non-D song gets Share too once its lyrics are found online (see "Lyrics online").
- **Picking lines.** Share turns the lyrics into a selection: the header becomes Cancel, how many lines are picked ("2 of 5") and Next, enabled once a line is picked. A tap on a line starts the block, a tap on the line next to it extends it, and a tap on an end line shrinks it; up to **5 contiguous lines** (`MAX_SHARED_LYRIC_LINES`). A tap that would break the block or go past five does nothing but a light haptic. Blank lines (a pause, a gap between verses) can't be picked and are stepped over: the next verse's first line counts as next to the block, and they don't count toward the five. Core's `tapLyricLine` decides. While picking, synced lyrics stop following the song and a tap no longer seeks; Cancel or Back leaves picking, and so does another song.
- **The card** (`share/LyricCard.tsx`) fits its text, as Spotify's does (#480): a solid background, the cover small at the top with the title and artist, the picked lines large and bold, and a discreet Wave mark (the logo's waves and "Wave"). It's fixed-width (1080 px) and its height follows the lines, never shorter than square (1:1) nor taller than 9:16. The lines take the largest of Core's sizes whose wrapped rows fit a 9:16 card, and the card is as tall as they then need: one short line gets a large font on a square card, five long lines a smaller font on a taller one (Core's `lyricCardLayout`, which gives both the shape and the size). Everything scales with the card's width and ignores the font scale, so the image is the same on every phone. The text is white or Wave's ink, whichever contrasts more with the background (`lyricCardInk`).
- **Colours.** The card starts in the cover's dominant colour; a tap on the card moves to the next of the cover's palette swatches, then round to the first (`lyricCardColours`, `nextLyricCardColour`: dominant, vibrant, dark vibrant, dark muted, muted, light vibrant, light muted, each colour once). A song without a palette gets Wave's graphite.
- **The preview** covers Now Playing after Next. Close or Back goes back to picking. The previewed card is the very view the image is drawn from, laid out at 1080 px wide and scaled down to fit, so it shows the card's real shape.
- **Destinations**, a row under the preview (`share/ShareDestinations.tsx`, `share/useImageShare.ts`, which a Recap's share preview and the song share sheet use too): **Save image** copies the card, on its own at its own size, into the gallery (Pictures/Wave) and a toast confirms it; **More** opens Android's share sheet with the card as a PNG. **Instagram Stories** and **WhatsApp** come first, as in Spotify, each only when its app is installed (Core's `lyricShareDestinations`; the module's manifest queries both packages, `shareApps` checks that each takes the intent). The row stays hidden, keeping its place, until a check has answered; the preview checks again each time it opens, laying the row out as the last check found meanwhile with its buttons disabled until its own check answers. An app that still can't be opened (removed in between) rejects the call, and the toast says Wave couldn't open it. Instagram Stories opens Instagram's Stories editor through Meta's Stories intent (`com.instagram.share.ADD_TO_STORY`): the card is the sticker (`interactive_asset_uri`), drawn with the preview's rounded corners (Save image and More stay square), which the user can move and resize, at its own shape over a background of the colour picked in the preview top and bottom (`lyricStoryBackground`), and only Instagram is granted to read it. Instagram requires a Facebook App ID as the source application: it's `facebookAppId` in app.config.ts's extra (`instagramStoriesAppId`), and a build without one has no Instagram Stories destination. WhatsApp sends the card on its own, at its own size, as an image straight to WhatsApp's share flow (`com.whatsapp`, which alone can read it).
- **The image.** Skia's `makeImageFromView` draws the card to a PNG (`share/shareImage.ts`, `writeViewPng`), so the cover is React Native's `Image`, whose bitmaps a software canvas can draw (expo-image's may be hardware bitmaps). The native `WaveShare` module writes it to the cache's `shared-images` folder (`SharedImages`, keeping images for ten minutes for the app they went to), copies it to the gallery through MediaStore, and hands it to other apps as a content URI from `ShareImageProvider`. The file is named `Wave - <title> - <yyyyMMdd-HHmmss>.png` (`lyricCardFileName`).

### Sharing playlists

A playlist goes out as a playlist on another service that anyone with its link can open (#483 for YT Music, #487 for Spotify).

- **Share** sits in a playlist's menu (⋯ on its page, or its long press; not the selection bar), after its image entries, always, since Spotify needs no switch (Core's `itemActions`). It opens a card of the services it can go to (`playlistShareServices`): **YT Music** while YT Music is on, and **Spotify**, whose only switch is signing in.
- **Signed in.** Each service needs its account (see YT Music, Spotify account). Signed out, it asks "Sign In to YT Music" or "Sign In to Spotify" with Sign In (`shareSignInAlert`), which opens that sign-in page (the one From YT Music and From Spotify use, `signInToYtMusic`, `signInToSpotify`) and then shares.
- **Creating.** Wave makes an **unlisted** playlist on the account with the playlist's name and its songs in order (`playlist/create` with the first 100 songs, the rest added 100 per `browse/edit_playlist` request), opens Android's share sheet with its link (`https://music.youtube.com/playlist?list=<id>`, `ytMusicPlaylistUrl`), and a toast says how many were found: "12 of 15 songs found" (`sharedSongsLine`). A toast says it's sharing while it works.
- **Matching** (`ytShareSong`, `matchSharedSongs`). A YT Music entry goes by its video id, and so does a Library song downloaded from YouTube (its stored video id). A Library song without one, and an unsearched entry, is searched once: a YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists in the search allowance (downloads' priority, "sharing a playlist" in the log), taking the first result that is the same recording, as imports decide (`ytShareMatch`, `spotifyYtMatch`). An unsearched entry searched this way is settled in its playlists too. An unavailable entry isn't searched again and counts as not found. A song in the playlist twice goes in once. With none found, nothing is made and a message says so. A search that gets no answer stops the share with a message rather than leave the song out.
- **Sharing again** updates the same playlist and keeps its link. Wave remembers the playlist it made for each Wave playlist, on this phone only (the key-value store, `share.ytMusic.<playlist id>`). It reads that playlist back (each entry's `playlistSetVideoId`), renames it to the playlist's name, removes the songs no longer in it and a song's later copies, adds the new ones (`sharedPlaylistChanges`), reads it again and moves the songs out of place, the fewest moves, keeping the longest run already in order (`sharedPlaylistMoves`). Core's `syncSharedPlaylist` runs these steps for both services, each giving it its requests (a `SharedPlaylistService`). A playlist deleted on YouTube Music, or one the signed-in account can't edit (another account's), is made again and remembered in its place. There's no background sync: only Share updates it.
- **Spotify.** Wave makes a **public** playlist on the account (Spotify's private playlists don't open for anyone else) and shares its link (`https://open.spotify.com/playlist/<id>`, `spotifyPlaylistUrl`), with the same toasts. Every song is searched once with Spotify's own search as the signed-in account (`searchTracks` for its title and artists, one at a time), unavailable entries too, since YouTube Music not having a song says nothing about Spotify; a song with no artist isn't searched (`spotifyShareSong`). The first result that is the same recording, by the rules imports use (title, artists, edition notes and a length within 3 seconds), and that Spotify plays, goes in (`spotifyShareMatch`). Creating posts the playlist with its name to `spclient`'s `playlist/v2/playlist`, adds it to the account's library (`rootlist/changes`), marks it public there and sets its link permission to `VIEWER`, then adds the songs 100 per `addToPlaylist`. Sharing again remembers it as `share.spotify.<playlist id>` and first looks for it in the account's library (`libraryV3`, `spotifyLibraryHas`): deleting a playlist on Spotify only takes it out of the library, so one missing there, one Spotify doesn't have, or one the account can't edit, is made again. Otherwise it reads it back (`fetchPlaylist`, each entry's `uid`; `readSpotifySharedPlaylist`), renames it, makes it public again, and brings its songs up to date as YT Music's (`removeFromPlaylist`, `addToPlaylist`, then one `moveItemsInPlaylist` per move). The native `SpotifyPlaylistWrites` sends these through `SpotifyClient`; Core reads the answers (`spotifyShare.ts`). A Spotify failure stops the share with its reason (no connection, signed out, Spotify changed something, or Spotify refused); both services' messages are Core's `sharePlaylistFailureLine`.
- **Requests.** innertubex's own `createPlaylist` makes private playlists and adds one song per request, so the native `YtPlaylistWrites` sends `playlist/create` and `browse/edit_playlist` itself, with the headers innertubex sends and the account's `SAPISIDHASH`; reading the playlist back is innertubex's `browse`. A request YouTube refuses as signed out expires the session.

### Sharing a song

The song you're listening to, or any other, goes to Instagram Stories as a card, as Spotify does it (#481), or out as a link that opens on any platform (#482).

- **Share** sits at the right of Now Playing's top bar, and "Share" is in every song's menu, before Select: a Library song's (a missing one too), a YT Music song's and a playlist's unsearched entry's (Core's `itemActions`, `ytSongActions`; never the selection bar). Both open one share sheet (`share/SongSharePreview.tsx`) over Now Playing and the app, with the same links: Now Playing's looks the playing song up as its menu's Share would, a Library track by its id and a streamed one by its video id (`shareNowPlaying`). Close or Back closes it.
- **The card** (`share/SongCard.tsx`) is the song's own cover large, the title and artist under it and the Wave mark, 1080 px wide and 1.4 times as tall, everything scaled with its width and ignoring the font scale. It sits on the cover's dark muted colour (else its dark vibrant one, else the next of its colours), its text white or Wave's ink for contrast; a song without a palette gets Wave's graphite (Core's `songCardColours`). A Library song's cover and palette are the Library's; a non-D song's cover is YouTube's, cached on the phone as playback caches it, which gives the palette (with YT Music off it has no cover, as nothing is asked of YouTube). The sheet shows what it has meanwhile.
- **The sheet** previews the card as a sticker on a 9:16 Story fading from the cover's dominant colour down to the card's, with the destinations row under it (`songShareDestinations`): **Instagram Stories** and **WhatsApp**, each only when its app is installed, then **Copy Link**, **Spotify** and **YT Music** while YT Music is on (#482), then **More**. The row scrolls sideways when it doesn't fit.
- **Instagram Stories** gets the card as the movable sticker, with the preview's rounded corners, over that same fade (top and bottom colours), through the lyrics card's Stories intent. **WhatsApp** and **More** get the card on its own, square, as an image, as the lyrics card goes. The file is named `Wave - <title> - <yyyyMMdd-HHmmss>.png` (`songCardFileName`).
- **Links** (#482, #504; Core's `lookUpSongVideo`, `lookUpSpotifyTrack`, `songLinkView`). Wave finds each link itself; song.link's API (Odesli) closed to the public and isn't asked. Copy Link and YT Music go by the song's YouTube video id: a YT Music song's own, a Library song's stored one. A Library song without one, and an unsearched entry, is first searched once on YouTube Music by its title and artists (in the search allowance, as Share), taking the first result that is the same recording (`ytShareMatch`). **YT Music** opens Android's share sheet with `https://music.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`; **Copy Link** copies the song.link page built from the video id, `https://song.link/y/<id>` (song.link's website resolves it on any platform), and a toast says "Link copied". **Spotify** searches once with Spotify's own search as the signed-in account (`searchTracks` for the title and artists), taking the first result that is the same recording, by the rules sharing a playlist uses (title, artists, edition notes, a length within 3 seconds, a track Spotify plays; `spotifyShareMatch`), and opens Android's share sheet with `https://open.spotify.com/track/<id>`; signed out, nothing is sent. Each destination stands on its own lookup: while it loads it shows a spinner, and one with no link is disabled and says why under its label: "No Match" (no search result is the song; for Copy Link and YT Music, no video id), "Signed Out" (Spotify only: no session, or one Spotify refused), "Offline" (its request couldn't be sent) or "Unavailable" (any other failure). A failure on one service leaves the others working. With YT Music off the three are hidden and nothing is asked.

### YT Music

- YouTube Music has no official API. Wave talks to it the way Metrolist does: through **innertubex** (MetrolistGroup/innertubex, from JitPack), from the native module. innertubex sends requests and handles sessions, ciphers and stream extraction; reading the answers into songs is Wave's (`YtSearchParser`, after Metrolist's page parsers). Metrolist is the reference for anything the library leaves to the app.
- **Licence.** innertubex is GPL-3.0, and so is any Metrolist code Wave ports, so the APK that ships them is a GPL-3.0 combined work.
- **Wave is its own client, not YouTube's app.** Nothing YouTube serves as an ad is ever requested, shown or played (Wave plays the audio stream itself), there is no Premium upsell or nag, and nothing is limited because the account isn't Premium; the only difference is the audio bitrate YouTube's servers send. Wave doesn't report plays to YouTube.
- **Search.** When Search asks YouTube Music, it sends the query and nothing else, as three searches at once: "Songs", "Artists" and "Albums" (which lists singles and EPs too). What they find that the Library doesn't have joins the Library's results in the Songs, Artists and Albums groups, after the Library's rows (making the group when the Library has nothing of its kind), and the groups' counts, previews and Show all cover both. A song shows its artwork, title, artists · album and length. An artist shows their picture and name over "YouTube Music", and a tap opens the YT Music artist page. An album shows its cover, title and artists; a tap opens the YT Music album page, and a long-press opens the YT Music album menu with Download Album. An artist the Library has (named as artist photos compare names, Core's `libraryArtistFinder`) isn't repeated, nor is an album that one of the artists it credits has in the Library under its title (matched as the artist page matches releases, Core's `libraryReleaseFinder`): the Library's row already shows. With Online, once YouTube Music's answer to the query shows and the top result is a Library artist, Search reads that artist's online page as their page does: the saved answer while it stands, else asked for and saved as their page's Online would, sending only the artist's name, browse id and songs playlist id, the read taking its turn in the search allowance as Search's own searches do. That artist's "N songs" then counts the Library's songs plus every song of theirs it lacks, each once; until then, on no match or a failure, and with Library, it's the Library's count (Core's `ytSearchArtistToRead`, `ytSearchTopArtistSongs`). Each artist's page is read once a visit; one whose read failed isn't read again as the query is refined, only once Online is tapped (`ytSearchArtistsKnownAfter`), so a failing artist doesn't spend the search allowance keystroke by keystroke. Other rows, YouTube Music's artists among them, are unchanged. The top result is the Library's best match when there is one. Otherwise it's YouTube Music's best: the result shown that matches the query best as Search ranks the Library's, or, when none matches word for word, YouTube Music's first song (then album, then artist). While Search shows YouTube Music's results it's an integrated view, so a Library album opened from it opens integrated. While YT Music's songs are among the results, the Library's show a small downloaded icon before their artist. Tapping any song plays the song results from it, the Library's files and YT Music's streamed. Typing searches the Library only: while YT Music is on, a **Library | Online** toggle sits at the end of the search field's row (two segments, "Library" and "Online" with a globe, on a `fill` pill, the selected one a teal pill; where the row is tight they show their icons alone, still named for TalkBack, `src/ytmusic/OnlineToggle.tsx`, Core's `listActionsForms`), Library selected, and nothing is asked until Online is tapped (with Search online set to Always, Online is selected and every query is asked; see Search online below). Online asks for the current query at once; from then on the results stay merged while the query is refined, each new query asked once typing pauses (300 ms), until the field is cleared, when the toggle is back on Library (a single character asks nothing but keeps what was picked). A query the Library has nothing for asks on its own, once typing pauses, and the toggle shows Online; once the Library has results again, that view ends and the toggle shows Library, unless Online was tapped. Tapping Library hides YouTube Music's results with no request and shows the Library's alone; nothing asks on its own while Library is picked, until the field is cleared. Tapping Online again shows the answer already fetched when the query is still the one it answers, and asks for the query at once otherwise. After a failure, tapping Online asks again. Asking never blocks typing or the Library's results and drops answers to queries no longer typed. While a query loads, three still track-row skeletons stand below the Library's results where the online ones will go (one group TalkBack reads as busy, "Loading online results"); a changed query swaps the last query's online rows for them at once, and the Library's results update as usual (see Skeletons). Under the results one quiet line says that YouTube Music has nothing for the query or nothing the Library lacks, or that it's offline or YouTube failed; if any of the three searches fails, the line says so and none of their results show (`ytSectionView`). With nothing in the Library matching and nothing from YouTube Music, a "nothing in your library" line shows. With YT Music off there is no toggle and nothing is asked. When Search asks, who asked and what the toggle shows are Core's (`stepYtSection`, `ytSectionToggle`).
- **Search allowance.** Every YouTube Music search Wave asks on its own shares one allowance: at most 1,000 in any 15 minutes, retries included (`SEARCH_ALLOWANCE`, `SEARCH_WINDOW_MS`; YouTube Music starts refusing near 2,300, see "Importing playlists from a link", Rate limits). That is the Songs search for an unsearched song about to play, a download's search for the song's audio track, a missing song's search for its recording, an unsearched song's download search for its video, Try Again's searches for a playlist's unavailable entries, Share's searches for a playlist's songs without a video id (see "Sharing playlists"), the background fill's Songs search for an unsearched entry (see "Importing playlists from a link", Background fill), a Library artist page's Artists search with Online selected (including its Popular ranking, after settlement; at Search's priority), and a mix's search for a seed artist (an Artists search for an artist without a saved page, for an Artist mix's seed, each of a Genre mix's up to three and a Library artist whose similar artists are read). A search typed in Search stays outside it, unless Search online is set to Always (see Search online): then each of Search's three searches takes its turn, with the playing priority. Each search waits its turn (`searchTurn` in `src/ytmusic/searchAllowance.ts`), and Core decides who goes next (`searchTurns`), by priority: playing (the unsearched songs among the playing one and the next 3, see Unsearched entries) and Search under Always, then downloads, Try Again and Share, then Discover mixes, then the background fill, which also takes none while the window holds 800, so 200 stay free for the others; within a priority, in the order they asked. A lower priority never takes a search a higher one is waiting for. A search given up while it waits spends nothing. The searches asked are kept on the phone (the key-value store, "spotifySearches", the name from when only imports kept it: when each of the last 1,000 went), so a restart doesn't start a fresh allowance; those waiting go with the process. A download waiting for its turn keeps its prepare decision: JS renews the journal's lease every 20 seconds while it works on a decision (`renewDecision`), up to the job's 20 minutes. The app log has a line when a wait starts and one when it ends, and one for each failed search (see App log).
- **Already in the Library.** A YT Music song is left out when a Library song has its title (case, accents, punctuation and a "feat." credit aside), shares an artist with it (credits split as the Library splits them, Core's `splitCredit`) and, when both lengths are known, is as long within 3 seconds, so a live take or an edit still shows (Core's `libraryMatcher`).
- **Streaming.** Tapping a YT Music result plays the section's songs from it; its long-press menu has Play Next, Add to Queue, Add to Playlist, Go to Album (when it has a YT Music album, opening that album's YT Music page; not on that page itself, and not while YT Music is off) and Go to Artist (and Download, below), so YT Music songs sit in the queue beside Library tracks (as `yt:<videoId>` track ids). Wherever playback shows (mini player, Now Playing with its tint, notification, lock screen, Bluetooth, Auto) a streamed song behaves like a Library track, except that it has no album link or lyrics, and its star (Now Playing's included) downloads it (see A star means downloaded, below); its artist line opens its artists integrated (Go to Artist, above). Its artists, as YouTube Music credits them, are kept with its details for that. The playback service streams a `yt:` item when the player loads it (`YtDataSource`), so the address is fresh at play time, mid-queue and after a cold start: `YtStreamResolver` asks innertubex's extractor for the best audio YouTube serves the account (a plain address: no HLS, SABR or ranged-only clients), with the proof-of-origin token YouTube's web client needs, minted by BotGuard in a hidden web view as Metrolist does (`YtPoTokens`), and visitor data kept across launches. Addresses are reused until ten minutes before they expire; one googlevideo rejects (403/410) is resolved again, twice at most, skipping the client it came from. **Audio cache:** every song the player streams goes through a 1 GB audio cache on the phone (`YtAudioCache`, a Media3 `SimpleCache` in the app's cache folder, the least recently played out first), so playing a song again, seeking back or going back to it doesn't download its audio again. A song's bytes are keyed by its video id and format (itag), not by its address, so a fresh address finds them. A song cached whole plays from the cache without asking YouTube, so it plays offline too (YT Music still has to be on); one cached in part (a song left halfway, whose bytes are kept) is resolved as usual and reads what the cache has, the rest coming from googlevideo and joining the cache as it plays. When a song resolves to another format (or the same format at another length), what the cache had of it is dropped. Familiar songs the phone has no file for stream through it like any YT Music song. It is invisible: no setting, no screen, not a Library download (the star's download still fetches its own M4A, while streaming plays Opus), not synced; clearing Wave's storage or its cache clears it, and if it can't open, songs stream without it. **Wi-Fi pre-download:** on Wi-Fi, the start of each of the day's mixes goes into the audio cache, so playing a mix on mobile data starts without spending data: the first 5 songs that stream in each mix Home shows (Artist mixes and Genre mixes, as blended at Discover's level; about 40 songs, ~200 MB a day), the mixes taking turns (every mix's first song, then every mix's second, and so on) and a song in two mixes fetched once (Core's `mixPreDownload`). Home hands the list to the phone whenever it changes (a build or refresh saved, Undo, a level changed; an empty list when YT Music is off), and a JobScheduler job (`PreDownloads`, `PreDownloadJob`) fetches it on an unmetered network with battery not low, stopping when either goes (or Android's time for a job is up) and going on when they're back; joining Wi-Fi with the list not fetched yet is what starts it, also after a reboot. (Android's JobScheduler stands in for WorkManager, which the build doesn't carry; it needs no notification.) Each song is read whole through the audio cache as the player reads it (`YtDataSource`), skipped when the cache has it whole already; a song stopped halfway keeps its bytes, and one that won't stream is skipped. A song fetched ahead is marked so in the cache until the player loads it; when the cache needs room, songs fetched ahead that the current list no longer has and that haven't played leave first, then the least recently played (`YtAudioEvictor`), so earlier days' fetches go before songs the owner played. It is silent: no notification, no screen, one line in the app log per run (see App log). Each load of a streamed song writes one line to the app log as it ends (tag `WaveYtAudio`): "Streamed <video id>: 4.1 MB from the network, 0.0 MB from the cache" (`YtAudioCache.loadLine`), the network bytes being those read from googlevideo, never those read from the cache. A lookup that fails (for playback or a download) writes one line to the app log under `WaveYtStreams`, and only that one: the song's video id, the failure (offline, unavailable, failed) with innertubex's reason, how long it took, and for each player request the client it went as, the HTTP status (or the exception when no answer came), the playability status and reason YouTube gave, whether it carried a proof-of-origin token, and its time (`YtPlayerRecorder` notes the requests, `YtLookupLog` writes the line); when no player request was seen, innertubex's own per-client outcomes go instead. Nothing is logged while YT Music is off, nor offline when nothing was asked; tokens, cookies and visitor data are never kept, and the line is redacted as every line is. A lookup that succeeds logs nothing new. The cover is cached beside the Library's artwork, so the notification, Auto and Now Playing's tint work as for a Library track: the tapped song's is waited for briefly before it plays, the others' are cached a few at a time once the play starts, and each cover that lands reaches the player's items for its song (Core's `playerCoverUpdates`, swapped in place without interrupting playback), so they show the file rather than YouTube's address. Now Playing's big cover is keyed to its song: it never shows the previous song's picture, and while the full-size cover loads (or for good if it fails) it shows the song's own thumbnail, or the music placeholder when there is none. Playback and the cache take it at the size downloads embed (Core's `streamedCoverUrls`): a YouTube Music cover is asked for at 1200 px whatever size its row carried, and a video thumbnail (`i.ytimg.com`) as the video's largest (maxres, else sd, else hq, which every video has); lists keep their small thumbnails. Wave never reports plays to YouTube, and streamed plays aren't recorded (Core's `trackPlays`), so play counts and stats stay about the Library. Wave only notes, on the phone (not synced), which YT Music songs were streamed, how often and since when (`streamed_songs`), so Home's mixes know them (see Artist mixes). A song that can't stream (offline, YT Music off, a song YouTube won't serve) pauses playback with a line saying why in place of the artist (Core's `playerErrorNotice`); skipping on plays the next song. The queue's YT Music songs' details are saved with it, so a cold start restores them, unless YT Music is off (Core's `restorableTrack`, `queuedYtTracks`).
- **Downloads.** The existing YT Music song menu keeps Download, Cancel Download and Retry Download, with compact row progress; a failed song offers Retry Download alone (Core's `ytDownloadActions`), and clearing it is Settings › Downloads' Clear All, its reason group's Remove, or Remove on its row there. Each download has a source, which Core decides when it's asked for and again when it starts (`downloadSource`): Soulseek while Download in best quality is on, else YouTube Music while YT Music is on. With both off, the row fails with "Turn on YT Music or Download in best quality in Settings" and keeps Retry. **When Soulseek can't deliver the song**, the same job carries on from YouTube Music while YT Music is on, and the owner doesn't tap anything. That happens when Soulseek has no 320 kbps copy of the recording or every selected peer failed, when it rejects the account (or Download in best quality is turned off mid-download, as a new download would then come from YouTube Music), when its server can't be reached while the phone is online, when no peer has started sending 2 minutes after the job went to Soulseek, and when the Soulseek attempt uses up its 20 minutes. Being offline, full storage, an unwritable folder, a failure while saving and Cancel don't fall back: YouTube would fail the same way, or the owner asked to stop. Core decides which failures fall back (`downloadFallback`). The prepare answer gives the journal Core's plan for the source's failures (`downloadFailurePlan`: which are tried again after a pause, which end the job, which fall back, and what the job fails with once nothing is left to try) and the M4A's place, so the journal switches the job's source where the failure happens, without waiting for JS and without a failure list of its own. The row keeps its place and shows the YouTube transfer's progress, and the song lands as a YouTube Music download does: the M4A in the Library's tree, tagged. The fallback gets 20 minutes of its own, and a job cut off by process death after falling back resumes from YouTube Music without going back to Soulseek. When YouTube Music fails as well, the row shows YouTube's failure with Retry, and Retry tries Soulseek first again. If YT Music is turned off before YouTube takes over, the row keeps Soulseek's failure. With YT Music off, Soulseek failures end the job as before; an unreachable server is retried like a dropped connection and then says "Download failed: Soulseek can't be reached". Whichever the source, there is one Download button, with no picker and no quality dialog. **From YouTube Music**, Wave saves the best M4A stream YouTube serves the account as it's served, never re-encoded: AAC at 256 kbps (itag 141) with Premium, about 128 kbps (itag 140) without. A Google sign-in isn't needed. It downloads the song's audio track: YT Music's album pages may list a song under its music video, whose audio runs longer or shorter than the album cut, so when YouTube Music's download starts (its prepare, or a Soulseek job's fallback, whose turn begins with a prepare of its own) Wave looks the song up in a Songs search and takes the result that is the same recording, by the rules imports use (Core's `ytAudioTrack`), else the song's own video. A Soulseek download that never falls back makes no Songs search. That search, and a missing song's search for its recording, wait their turn in the search allowance with downloads' priority (see Search allowance), so a large playlist's downloads keep within it. An audio-track search that fails still leaves the song's own video; either search's failure is logged. The Library Track keeps the video downloaded. The address comes from playback's resolver (`YtStreamResolver`, asking for M4A), and the audio arrives in 1 MiB ranges into the hidden part file, a request that breaks off going on from its last byte. An address googlevideo turns down (403/410) is resolved again, three times at most: the download carries on from where it was when the fresh address serves the same stream, and starts over when it serves another (`YtAudioDownload`). The file is published only once it is the length YouTube announced and, read through to its last sample, plays as long as the song, within 2 s or 2% (Core's `ytAudioMatches`). Each check writes a line to the app log (Core's `ytDownloadReport`): the video asked for and the one YouTube served, the player client, the video type (ATV, OMV, UGC), YouTube's announced length, the song's length and where it came from, and how long the file plays. Otherwise, or when YouTube clearly won't serve the song, the row fails with "This song is unavailable on YouTube Music" and keeps Retry. Only clear answers fail it at once: a player answer with status ERROR (the video is unavailable or removed), a private, removed or age-restricted video, or a song innertubex won't serve for what it is (age-restricted, explicit content it can't serve, no music video). Any other refusal (an UNPLAYABLE answer, a bot check, no answer at all) is tried again after 30 s, 90 s and 4 minutes, and fails as unavailable only when every try is refused (Core's `downloadFailurePlan`, `refused`); these tries count apart from the six attempts below. Offline is always offline, whatever YouTube's last answer said, so it stays retryable. A transfer that breaks off, or goes quiet for 4 minutes (longer than the range requests' own retries, so the journal only steps in on a transfer that's truly dead), is tried again after a pause that grows each time, up to six attempts in all, then fails with "Download failed"; offline it fails with "Download failed: offline". **When YouTube limits downloads**, it refuses song after song, fast and in queue order, until the session changes (Oct 18: a network drop brought downloads back; a restart didn't). Core's `ytThrottle` reads the journal's marks (when YouTube last refused each job's song, and last served a stream): 5 or more different songs refused within a minute, after a YouTube download worked this session (or during a backoff), pause every YouTube download for 2 minutes, doubled by each burst that follows (4, 8, 16, then 30 minutes at most); a download that works starts the backoff over. While paused, no YouTube transfer starts, no YouTube song waiting in line starts, and the songs waiting for a transfer leave their slots and don't count against the songs started at once, so Soulseek's jobs go on however many YouTube songs wait ahead of them; a song refused during the pause isn't counted and waits for its end, a new pause gives back the refusals songs counted before it, and the pause isn't charged to a song's 20 minutes. A new pause renews YouTube's visitor data and proof-of-origin tokens, so the downloads after it go out with a fresh session. A pause under way survives a restart, and its backoff does even once the pause is over (YouTube may still be limiting downloads, so the next burst pauses longer); a stream the journal shows served counts as a download that worked this session (Core's `ytThrottleRestored`). Before Wave told a refusal from a clear answer, each refused song failed as unavailable at its first attempt (about 655 of Oct 18's Free up space run), so once an install, at the first launch after that update with YT Music on, Wave asks again for every YouTube download that failed as unavailable at its first attempt and was never tried again, as Retry would, so they follow the pause and try-again rules; a song downloaded since, a missing song found since, and a replacement whose Track was removed, replaced or found to have no better copy since (or whose direction Download in best quality no longer gives) are left alone (Core's `throttledFailures`). The app log says how many went back, and the phone remembers it ran (the key-value store, "downloads.throttledFailuresRequeued"); a read or request that fails leaves it for the next launch. Settings › Downloads says "Paused: YouTube is limiting downloads. Next try in N min" under the Downloading summary (Core's `ytThrottleNotice`), and the notification's title says "Paused: YouTube is limiting downloads" with a countdown to the next try, over the run's bar and counts during a run, with no time left until the pause is over. Each attempt goes on from the bytes its part file already holds (checked against the stream first, else it starts over), and cancelling the job stops its attempt wherever it waits, resolving the address included, so the next one isn't held up behind it. **From Soulseek**, with Download in best quality on (see "The Soulseek account"), Wave automatically selects the same recording and at most three suitable peers. Core combines exact normalized title, the main artist, complete edition evidence and duration; a faster live version, remix, edit or different remaster cannot replace the requested recording. Only advertised original MP3 320 candidates are eligible in the proven native path; there is no lossless encoder or lower-bitrate fallback. Native code checks every MPEG frame, completeness, bitrate and actual decoded duration. Contradictory received title, artist or edition tags reject the peer before trusted metadata overwrites them. With YT Music off, failure says "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and leaves Retry available. Either way, the destination is the Library's own tree, `<album artist>/<album>/<NN> <title>.mp3` from Soulseek or `.m4a` from YouTube Music, directly under an included root (`<title>.mp3` or `<title>.m4a` without a track number; names made safe for the storage), so a song whose album folder the Library already has lands next to its songs. Core's `ytDownloadTarget` names it, the same function the PC job that organizes the music folder uses. The root and folder are checked against current exclusions. Existing files are never replaced: a taken name gets the next free one ("Name (2).m4a"), and so does a name another file takes between choosing it and moving the download there. A job whose saved file is gone when it's imported again, with another file at its name, fails with "Download failed" and never touches that file; Retry downloads the song again and saves it under a free name. The existing tag writer keeps supplied title, artists, album, album artist, year, track/disc numbers and artwork; optional genre/lyrics metadata failures never reject usable audio. YouTube's M4A is fragmented MP4 (an empty sample table in the `moov`, the audio in `moof`/`mdat` fragments), which jaudiotagger can't grow, so the tags go into its `moov` directly and the fragments' absolute offsets move with it (`FragmentedMp4`). A download fails with "Download failed: couldn't save the file" only for full storage or a folder Wave can't make or write to; anything else that goes wrong while saving or importing (a file the tagger or scanner can't read, a Library error, an unexpected error in JS's answer) fails with its own reason, keeping any retry or YouTube Music fallback its plan has. Each failure keeps its cause (the exception's class and message, `cause` on the journal's job) in the journal and in Logcat (`WaveDownloads`); Retry clears it. The existing scanner and LibraryStore immediately import the complete file into Library, Search and Android Auto and refresh YT Music Library matching. Repeated taps and positively matched result ids share a job. Neither source needs a Google session or a paid account, and downloads never share or upload the Library. No PC, server, daemon or separate downloader app is involved. Up to five songs download at once, and the next starts as one of them finishes or waits (a peer's queue, say), so one song never holds up the rest; they still join the Library one at a time. Only the songs started count as downloading, wherever a count shows (the notification, Settings › Downloads, the app log; Core's `queuedDownloadCounts` and native `DownloadCounts` share the rule): at most ten, the five with a slot and those waiting on a peer, a pause or JS's answer. Songs downloaded and waiting their turn to join the Library count as saving; in a big run they pile up by the hundred (#466). **How landings reach the lists.** A song landing in the Library (an import, a quality replacement, and the lyrics and genre its fill-in writes a moment later) doesn't make every list re-read on its own: it names its Track, and landings are gathered and announced together as one burst at most every 2 seconds, as a scan's batches are (Core's `createLandings`, `LANDING_BURST_MS`), so lists in sight re-read at that pace and hidden ones catch up when shown. A downloaded song and its fill-in make one change: the imported song waits for its fill-in, 2 seconds at most, holding the burst back with it (`Landings.filled`); a fill-in done later is a change of its own only when it wrote something. The run's last song is announced at once, with what's gathered, as soon as its fill-in is done (or its 2 seconds are over), rather than waiting for another landing, and so is what's gathered when the journal shows nothing on its way any more (the last songs answered in one poll, or a run ending in a failure or a cancel). Stars a download settles and the Downloads page don't wait for the burst. A native journal owns the queue (see Native shape), so downloads keep going with the screen off and while music plays, and survive Android recreating Wave's process: the request, its source, the chosen recording, candidate peers, current peer, the policy (MP3 320, or YouTube's M4A) and deadlines are persisted; a transfer cut off by process death restarts from zero with the same peer and what was left of its time, or from YouTube's stream with a fresh window to start in, within the job's own remaining time. A song started before Wave was killed comes back as waiting (`resumed` on its job), at the front of the line, until the queue takes it up again (its next step, or JS's decision). **The journal is written in small, coalesced writes**, so a big queue doesn't saturate the phone's storage (#452): it is a snapshot of every job (with its song, candidates and tags, lyrics included) and, beside it, an append-only log of what changed since. The changes of a second share one write, at most about one a second (`WRITE_MS`), which appends only each changed job's changed fields (a new job whole) and syncs it, so a job's tags and lyrics are written once, when its prepare answer brings them. Once the log outgrows the snapshot, the next write folds it into a new snapshot (`JournalStore`). Only new jobs and publication write at once. A song asked for is in the journal before the request returns, so a crash loses no job; an album, a playlist, a replacement run or Retry All asks for all its songs in one write (`enqueueAll`), not one synced write per song. Publication writes before a file is copied into or moved to the Library folder, so a crash never loses or duplicates a song, and so does the download service as it stops (what waits is written, and songs Android's time limit sent back in line); otherwise a crash loses at most the last second of changes, the job going on from the step it last wrote, and a part file a lost step made is deleted as the journal opens. A journal from before reads as a snapshot with no log. **Download work yields to the rest of the phone** (#455). The threads that download, tag, hash and publish songs (Soulseek's peer threads that receive a song's bytes included; a search's peers keep theirs), and the journal's writer, run at Android's background thread priority (`BackgroundThreads`), as does writing a song's lyrics and genre; playback and the UI keep theirs. Each file is gone over as few times as it can be: its digest (what tells a published file is still the download's) is taken while the file is written, by the tag writer as it rewrites a YouTube download's M4A or a Soulseek download's MP3 (written whole into a copy hashed as it's written: the new ID3v2 tag, the audio from its first frame, the ID3v1 tag it has; `HashedMp3`), or by the copy when the Library's folder changed since the download started, not by reading the file again. Only a file neither writer rewrote whole (a plain M4A, an MP3 without an ID3v2 tag to write) is read once for it, and a quality replacement's copy is read once to check it before it moves over the old file. Android's media scanner is told about files in batches, at most every 5 seconds and once more when the download service stops (`MediaScanBatch`): a downloaded file waits until its fill-in is done (a minute at most), and a quality replacement's new file goes in the same batch as the old file its import deleted, so each file is scanned once, with its tags. Deleting songs and Fix Song hand their files to the same batches. A job's time is only charged while the download service runs: the journal keeps a clock that stands still while Wave is closed or the service is stopped, and from Wave opening until the service starts (`BudgetClock`, written at least every 30 seconds while jobs are charged), so relaunching Wave hours later doesn't expire the jobs that were mid-download; each goes on with the time it had left. The job's time ends once its file is downloaded: checking it, tagging, waiting its turn and saving to the Library finish however long they take, and a save that fails still fails with its own cause. Rows say "Waiting to download", "Finding this recording", "Connecting", "Downloading · NN%", "Checking the download" and "Saving to the Library"; a YouTube Music download waits instead of finding a recording or connecting. The notification looks the same whichever the source. Internally, for Soulseek, a dropped connection reconnects the saved account and retries (searching three times, transferring up to six attempts), a peer that keeps Wave in its queue over 2 minutes, doesn't start within 3 or goes quiet for 1 is replaced by the next selected peer, and each job has 20 minutes from leaving the queue until its file is downloaded. While YouTube Music can take over, Soulseek has 2 minutes from the job leaving the queue (its search, connecting, peers' queues and pauses included) for a peer to start sending, counted on the same clock as the job's, as behind mobile NAT peers mostly can't connect back to the phone; a transfer started by then runs on under these rules (`SOULSEEK_START_MS`, a rule of its own beside the job's 20 minutes). When peers run out, or those 2 minutes pass with nothing sent, the job falls back to YouTube Music, or with YT Music off the row fails with "This recording is unavailable in 320 kbps" and Retry, which searches again. Network loss, full storage, an unwritable folder, a rejected account or audio that fails validation never touch existing files and never publish partial audio. Cancel Download in the menu or Cancel in the notification deletes the hidden part files; once it is saving to the Library it can't be cancelled. Repeated completion is idempotent (upsert by path), so no duplicate Tracks.
- **Explicit songs.** Wave only uses a flag a source already gives, never the audio or the lyrics, and a song with none (unknown) counts as clean everywhere (Core's `isExplicit`). YT Music songs carry the explicit mark the parsers read (`MUSIC_EXPLICIT_BADGE`). A download writes it into the file's advisory tag (`ITUNESADVISORY` for ID3 and Vorbis comments, `rtng` for MP4): 1 for explicit, 0 for checked and clean, so a song YT Music doesn't mark is written as 0 (Core's `advisoryTag`); a song downloaded as a listing gave it (Spotify's, an unsearched entry's) or a missing song's download writes the flag Deezer's match gives, and none without a match (Core's `listedSongTags`, `missingSongTags`), since nothing else checked it. **Fix Song** fills the flag in for a song whose index says unknown, from Deezer's `explicit_lyrics` on the track it already matches, writing the same tag (1 or 0); a flag the file already has, from a YT Music download or another app, is never replaced, and a format Wave can't write tags to (Opus) keeps it unknown and, told by its file's extension (`.opus`, `.aac`), doesn't count as lacking it, so Fix Song isn't offered or run again for it (an Opus stream in an `.ogg` file can't be told apart by its name). A song whose only change was the flag counts as fixed in the summary. Scans read the tag into the index (`tracks.explicit`: explicit, clean or unknown, Core's `explicitFromAdvisory`), so the flag survives a rescan, a reinstall and an index rebuild; the update that added the column marks every indexed file as changed, so the next scan reads them all once. A tapped explicit song gives way to the next clean one after it (else the last before it) with a toast ("Skipped "<title>": it's explicit."), and a play or a queuing of explicit songs alone leaves the queue as it was and says so (`explicitPlayNotice`). Turning the switch on takes the queue's explicit songs out, the playing one too (`queuedPassedOver`); turning it off doesn't put them back. Artist mixes leave explicit songs out of both their new songs and their familiar share, streamed familiar songs included (`discoverMixKeep`, the `keep` of `blendDiscoverMix`; a share keeps each song's flag as the build saw it, and the mix's page goes by the Library's for Library songs), so turning the switch over re-blends the saved mixes with no rebuild. Lists (the Library, playlists, search) still show explicit songs, with their badge, whichever way the switch is set.

- **Download quality.** Download in best quality only decides where new downloads come from, so flipping it offers to bring the songs already in the Library in line (Core's `qualityReplacement.ts`).
  - **Turning it on** offers to replace the Library's low-quality songs with MP3 320 copies from Soulseek: every file under 256 kbps, whoever put it there (YouTube's ~128k, MP3s at 192k or less), as the file's header gives its bitrate, with a few kbps of slack for AAC files only, so an AAC 256k file whose header averages a little under stays (an MP3 or any other file under 256 is offered). AAC 256k, MP3 320, lossless files (FLAC, WAV, AIFF, APE, WavPack, ALAC, DSD) and files whose bitrate isn't known stay (`qualityUpgradeSongs`).
  - **Turning it off** offers to free space by replacing the Library's MP3s at 320 kbps (the header may average a few kbps under), wherever they came from, with YouTube Music's copies (`qualityDowngradeSongs`). Lossless files, AAC copies and smaller MP3s are never offered, nor is a song whose length isn't known (YouTube Music's match needs it). It needs YT Music on: with it off, no card or row shows.
  - **The card.** Flipping the switch on shows a card, "Replace 342 songs with best quality? About +1.2 GB", with Replace and Not now (`qualityUpgradeOffer`). The estimate is the difference to 320 kbps over the songs' lengths (each file's own size, else its bitrate over its length). On mobile data (not Wi-Fi), for a large run (100 MB or more to download), the card adds what the run downloads and that the phone isn't on Wi-Fi ("It downloads about 3.4 GB, and the phone isn't on Wi-Fi."); once confirmed it runs anywhere. No card shows when no song qualifies. Turning it off shows "Free about 900 MB by replacing 410 songs with YouTube Music copies?", saying the copies' bitrate, with Replace and Not now (`qualityDowngradeOffer`). Its estimate is what the songs' files take now less YouTube Music's copies over their lengths, at the bitrate the owner's YouTube downloads get: 256 kbps with Premium, about 128 kbps without, told by the Library's latest YouTube download (`youTubeCopyKbps`; about 128 with none to tell by). Wave can't ask YouTube whether the account is Premium. The mobile-data note is the upgrade's, from the same size. No card shows when nothing would be saved.

  - **The run.** Replace queues each song as its own job (`upgrade:<trackId>`, `qualityReplacementJob`) under a **Quality upgrade** group in the download queue (`QUALITY_ORIGINS`), reorderable and cancellable like any other group. Each is a Soulseek search for the Track's title, artists, album and length, matched by today's rules; an upgrade never falls back to YouTube Music. Freeing space queues `downgrade:<trackId>` jobs under a **Free up space** group; each is a YouTube Music Songs search for the Track's title and artists, matched as a listing's song is (title, main artist, edition, and length within 3 seconds: `qualityDowngradeSearch`, `qualityDowngradeVideo`), and downloaded as the usual M4A. Flipping the switch cancels the other direction's replacements still waiting or downloading (`qualityJobsToCancel`); one already saving finishes.
  - **In place.** The new file goes beside the old one under its name (`qualityReplacementTarget`: `08 Song.m4a` gets `08 Song.mp3`); a file of the same kind takes the old one's very path, the journal moving it over the old file as it publishes (the publish answer's `replaces`), never as `Name (2)`. It gets the old file's tags as the Library has them, its cover (the Library's copy of it) and its lyrics, picked as the Lyrics view picks them (`qualityReplacementTags`). The tag writer also copies the old file's other tags into it (`keepTagsOf`: composer, lyricist, conductor, comment, BPM, grouping, mood, key, ISRC, label, copyright, subtitle, track and disc totals, compilation, original year, language, sort names, MusicBrainz ids) and every genre value it has; ReplayGain, encoder and gapless fields describe the old file's audio and aren't copied. The import then hands the file to the Track it replaces (the store's `replaceTrackFile`): the same Track id, and with it its favorites, plays, playlist entries and recording key, whatever the new tags say; a Track a scan made of the new file in between goes. YouTube Music's copy gives the Track the video it came from, as any YouTube download has. Only then is the old file deleted, when the new one didn't take its path: the swap records that it must go in the same transaction (the `file_deletions` table), and the record is cleared once the file is gone or missing. The old file is never touched before the new one is downloaded, validated and tagged, and a new file that takes its very path moves over it only once its digest checks out.
  - **Interrupted.** A run can be cut off at any moment (an ANR, a kill, the phone switching off, the owner cancelling) and goes on where it stopped, never losing a song or leaving it twice. Killed after the swap and before the deletion, the old file stays recorded: each launch (before its scan) and each run's start finish the recorded deletions (`finishFileDeletions`), writing how many and which to the app log (an import, which finishes them too, leaves out only its own old file, the usual end of a replacement, and writes the other runs' leftovers it finishes), and a scan leaves a recorded file out, so it never comes back as a second song. A file a Track holds is never deleted that way; its record goes. Killed after publication and before the import, the journal asks for the import again at the next launch; a replayed import whose Track already has the new file still finds the old one recorded. A replacement that fails after publishing (or whose import fails) has its new file removed (Core's `failedReplacementFiles`), and the song keeps its old file; its Retry downloads it again. When Android stops the download service on its time limit or refuses to start it, the songs not downloaded yet go back in line in their places, uncharged, instead of failing (the journal's `interrupt`), and a song already downloaded keeps its step: they wait for the next start, and the run keeps its order. When a run stops (its last song done, or the service stopped under it), a line with tag `WaveDownloads` says how many songs were replaced, kept with no better copy, failed and still waiting (and cancelled, if any). It counts that run alone: each replacement joins the run under way when it's asked for (or starts one), an earlier run's failures aren't counted again, and a run killed and resumed still counts what it replaced before the kill (carried on its remaining jobs as the journal forgets the imported ones).
  - **Left twice.** Before replacements recorded the old file's deletion, a kill between the swap and the deletion left the old file on disk, and a scan made it a second song (`Arctic Monkeys - 505.mp3` and `Arctic Monkeys - 505.m4a`: the replacement's Track, older, with the favorite, plays and playlist entries, on the new file, and a bare Track the scan made on the old one). A check finds those and puts each back to one song (`src/library/replacementDuplicates.ts`). It runs once after the update that brings it, and again after a replacement run that was interrupted (the first journal read of a launch finds replacements still on their way), each time once the launch scan is through and no replacement is on its way, at most once a launch (Core's `duplicateCheckStep`, `replacementsUnderway`; kept on this phone in the key-value store, "downloads.replacementDuplicatesCheck"); not on every launch. It only acts on what it can prove: a pair is an MP3 and an M4A of the same folder and name (what a replacement either way makes of a file, `qualityReplacementTarget`) that are one recording, by recording key or by title, artists and length within 3 seconds (`replacementDuplicates`). Which is the new file comes from the pair itself, not from Download in best quality's switch as it stands now: the older Track's file is the one its replacement moved it to, the newer Track's the file left behind. Anything else is left alone. Both files are read to the end of their audio (the native `readAudioLengths`; complete audio is readable and stops no more than 3 seconds before the length its tags say). The older Track stays and ends on the new file when that is complete audio and plays the song's full length (the old file's, within 3 seconds; its tags' when the old file is cut short), otherwise on the old file when that is complete; a pair with neither file complete is left as it is and listed in the log, so the only copy is never deleted (`replacementDuplicateFix`). When the second Track has history too, it's merged into the one kept: the song is starred when either was, every play moves to it, and its playlist entries name the kept Track, those in a playlist already holding it removed so no playlist lists the song twice (Core plans it, `DuplicateMerge`; the store applies it in the same transaction). The other Track then goes in that transaction, which also records the other file's deletion (the store's `fixReplacementDuplicate`, the `file_deletions` table; a pair whose star or playlists changed since the check read them is left and logged), then the recorded deletions are finished (`finishFileDeletions`). The app log (tag `WaveDownloads`) gets a line with how many pairs it found, fixed and left alone, and one line per song it changed or left (`replacementDuplicateLines`).
  - **No better copy.** A song Soulseek has no 320 copy of, or YouTube Music has no match for when freeing space, or whose replacement fails after its retries, keeps its file and is recorded as having no better copy (`noBetterCopies`, the `no_better_copies` table, this phone only, going with its Track), never offered in that direction again. A failure that's the phone's or the account's (offline, full storage, no folder, no source, the Soulseek account or server) isn't the song's, and it's offered again. No 320 copy (or no YouTube match) isn't a failed download: it leaves the queue without a row in the failed list. A failure after retries shows in the failed list as any download's does, and its Retry asks for the replacement again.
- **Discover.** Discover is Home's section of three rows, the Magic Queue (see the Magic Queue), Artist mixes and Genre mixes (below; Artist mixes was Discover mixes), under one header with one settings card (see Editable Home). The mix rows need YT Music on but no sign-in. The Library has no Discover entry, and Wave never asks YouTube Music for the account's home recommendations (`FEmusic_home`). An old link to the former Discover screen (`/library/discover`) opens Home. **Wave's own engine:** both sections build at once and offline. A build (the day's, a pull's) reads only the phone and makes no request (its app-log line says "none" for every host): each seed's songs are Wave's recommendation engine's ranking of the candidate catalog (Core's `recommend`, one engine for Artist mixes, Genre mixes and the Magic Queue). Its candidates for a seed are the catalog songs one of the seed artists' radios brought or by a seed artist (as close as can be), the songs of the artists in the seed artists' neighbourhood in the artist graph (as close as the graph says), and, for a genre mix, any song one of whose artists' genre profiles has a share of its family, whoever's radio brought it. Each candidate scores named signals, each from 0 to 1 (taste from -1 to 1), weighed by named weights (`RECOMMEND_WEIGHTS`): **closeness** to the seed (1), **genre affinity** to the seed's profile (`genreAffinity`, 0.5), **taste** (the owner's taste profile's `tasteWeight` of the song, its artists and its genre; a YT Music song starred or downloaded counts as the owner's, 0.5), **newness** (how recently the catalog first saw it, half every 14 days, `RECOMMEND_NEWNESS_HALF_LIFE_MS`, 0.25), **popularity** (how many distinct catalog radios carry it, 0: neutral, as YouTube's radio was; a negative weight favours deep cuts) and a random **variety** nudge each build (0.25), so a seed's mix isn't the same every day. The weights, the affinity threshold (0.5) and how far out the neighbourhood goes are parameters (`RecommendOptions`), so Discover's sliders tune them (below); the defaults keep a seed's mix to its radio and its closest artists, as YouTube Music's radio did. The songs the section showed today go after every other. What a mix keeps of the ranking stays the mixes' rules (`catalogMixRead`, `discoverMixesOfRadios`: no music video, no song the owner knows, none in two of the section's mixes, genre affinity's match, miss and unknown, 50 at most, ten at least, a refresh's avoided songs only to make up ten), and its familiar share looks first at the familiar songs its seed artists' radios brought, in the ranking's order (`discoverMixFamiliarPool`). A seed the catalog has no songs for yet makes no mix: the section shows what the other seeds make, and the harvest refills it. **The harvest** refills the catalog in the background, apart from building (`src/home/mixHarvest.ts`). It reads the radios of the seeds and genre families whose candidates run low: Core counts each seed's candidates (the new songs its mix would keep from the catalog now, at Discover's Genre faithfulness and Popularity, `harvestWants`) and says which to read next (`harvestNext`): **on Wi-Fi**, any with fewer than 50 (`HARVEST_LOW`), from every artist Artist mixes may pick (`artistMixSeedPool`: the played artists, the Library's while plays give fewer than four, then similar artists, whose pages it reads), the Genre mixes' families and the seeds the builds picked; **on mobile data** (read once a run, `onMobileData`), only the seeds each section's last build picked (after a relaunch, those its saved mixes were built from; `harvestSeedPool`) that have no candidates left, while the harvest's mobile data that day (every host's bytes, as the network measurement counts them, kept on the phone, `home.harvestMobileUse`, `withHarvestMobileUse`) is under about 5 MB (`HARVEST_MOBILE_BYTES`); a read that crosses it is the day's last. The emptiest seed goes first, then the least recently read; never one read or tried in the last 12 hours (`HARVEST_RETRY_MS`; a read that never reached YouTube Music, the phone offline say, isn't counted as tried), at most eight a run (`HARVEST_SEEDS`), one at a time, and a run every 15 minutes at most (`harvestDue`), after Home's builds or a Home visit with none due, and after a pull. A seed's read is what a build used to read: its radios at the same time (from where they started last time when that's remembered), a page each and again while its mix would have fewer than 25 songs, three pages at most, with the genre lookups the songs it would keep need (below); each page goes into the catalog at once, as its artist's radio. Its Artists searches share the search allowance at the Discover priority. A harvest that read something forgets the artist graph (`forgetArtistGraph`) and the builds' shared catalog, and a section with no mixes yet (a fresh install, whose catalog starts empty) builds at once. With YT Music off, or offline (`online`), nothing is harvested; the next Home visit online tries again.

- **Artist mixes.** Discover's Artist mixes row (after the Magic Queue's; it was the Discover mixes section, then the Artist mixes section) shows up to four mixes of songs the owner doesn't know yet, each grown from one artist (Wave's engine over the candidate catalog: the songs their YouTube Music radio brought, then their neighbourhood in the artist graph; see Discover), and they change from day to day. It needs YT Music on but no sign-in. The seeds are the owner's all-time most played artists, as Stats ranks them (the top 20), picked at random each build but weighted towards the most played: each pick's chance follows the square root of the artist's counted plays among those not picked yet, so the few most played don't take every day (Core's `artistMixSeeds`, `pickWeighted`, `playsWeight`). The picked artists' mixes show most played first. Names that aren't an artist's ("Various Artists") seed nothing, and an artist seeds once. There are no song seeds, varied mix or genre seeds (genres get their own mixes). When plays give fewer than four seeds (a fresh install, a Library just filled), the Library's artists with the most songs (of its 20 with the most) fill the rest after them. So mixes show with no plays at all, and the plays' seeds come first as the owner listens; with four seeds from plays, the Library adds none. When the Library's artists are too few for that, artists similar to the Library's come after its own: the ones YouTube Music lists on a Library artist's page ("Fans might also like"), less those the Library has. Core says whose page to read next (`artistMixSimilarSource`): the owner's most played artists first, then the Library's with the most songs, one page at a time while the seeds have room for similar artists, three pages at most. A build reads only the pages the phone has: the one their artist page saved when it has the similar artists, else the one the harvest read (kept on the phone by the Library artist, the latest 100, not synced; `similarArtistsKept.ts`). A page neither has lists no one for that build, and the harvest reads it, its artist found as an artist seed's is. The pages take turns giving their next artist, and an artist another page gave, or a name that isn't an artist's, seeds nothing. So a Library of two artists and no plays gets its two artists and up to two similar ones. A similar artist's mix starts from the Start radio of their own page. A page that can't be read or lists no one leaves the other seeds as they are. An artist's radio starts from the Start radio button on their YouTube Music page. That page is the match their artist page saved, or the one an Artists search finds (`pickYtArtist`). Where the radio starts (the button's playlist id and params) is remembered by the page's browse id, on the phone (not synced; the latest 500 artists, Core's `artistRadioStarts.ts`, `src/home/artistRadio.ts`), so a later build, and a Magic Queue, starts the radio there without reading the whole artist page again. A start YouTube Music refuses is forgotten and the page read once, its start remembered instead; offline or with YT Music off, it stays. A mix's new songs are songs the owner doesn't know: it keeps the engine's ranking in order and leaves out music videos, the songs the owner knows and songs an earlier mix of the section has (`discoverMixSongs`). **Genre affinity:** a mix keeps to its seed's style (Core's `genreAffinity.ts`). An artist's genre profile is the share of each genre family (Genre mixes' families) across their songs, every value of a multi-value genre tag counting and a song in n families adding 1/n to each (`genreProfile`; say alt rock 60%, pop 25%, rap 15%). Two profiles' affinity is the cosine of their shares (`genreAffinity`), so sharing one minor family isn't enough, and a radio song joins a mix only when one of its artists' affinity to the seed's profile reaches 0.5 (`GENRE_AFFINITY_THRESHOLD`, `mixAffinity`): in a twenty one pilots mix, an all-rap Dr. Dre falls out and an alt rock and pop artist stays. A Library artist's profile comes from their Library songs (`libraryGenreProfiles`). An artist outside the Library (a radio artist, or a similar artist's seed) gets one from Deezer's open API: an artist search for the exact name, then the genres of their albums (each album with a genre counting as one song; Deezer's own genre names Films/Games, Soul & Funk and Latin Music are filed under Soundtrack, R&B and Latin). It's looked up once and kept on the phone (the latest 2,000 artists, a miss included; a failed lookup is tried again on a later build, not in the same one), three at a time, and only while the Lyrics and genre switch is on (`deezerArtistProfile`). **Only the genres a mix needs** are looked up, as the harvest reads a seed's radio (a build looks nothing up: an artist whose genre isn't known yet is unknown): the seed's own artists first (an outside seed needs its profile to judge any song; `mixProfileLookups`), then, walking the radio in order, the artists of the songs the mix would keep next (music videos, songs the owner knows, songs played again and songs whose genre is decided already ask nothing; a song one of whose artists matches asks nothing for the others), only as many songs as would bring the mix to its 50 if they all matched, a round at a time, until 50 songs match or nothing is left to ask (Core's `mixProfileLookupsNext`). So mixes are as long as when every radio artist was looked up, and artists further down the radio stay unknown till a build needs them. A refresh's avoided songs ask only when the others leave the mix short of its ten songs. A song whose artists have no genre known only fills a mix up to its ten songs, after every matching song (and after the songs a refresh avoids). A seed with no genre known keeps every genre, and asks Deezer nothing about its radio. The owner knows (Core's `mixKnownSongs`) the songs the Library has (`libraryFinder`), the songs ever played, the YT Music songs streamed (by video, or the same recording as `libraryFinder` matches it) and the songs in any playlist: Library entries, YT Music entries (by video, or the same recording) and unsearched or unavailable entries (by title and artists, as the Library finds a YT Music song). Played songs are found by the recording keys of every play recorded, so a song played and deleted since stays out too. Its title (a feat. credit and a remaster note aside) must match, and so must its album artist or, on a compilation, its album (`playedFinder`). A build reads the Library, the plays, the songs streamed and the playlists' entries and indexes them once (`mixIndexes`), shared by every section's build that starts within a minute, and gives the JavaScript thread back between steps and after each seed, so Home doesn't stutter while mixes build (on a desktop, 10,000 Library songs, 30,000 plays and 5,000 playlist entries index in about 150 ms). The harvest reads up to three pages of a seed's radio, until its mix would have 25 songs; a mix keeps 50 at most. **Seeds in order:** the seeds take their songs in seed order (Core's `discoverMixesOfRadios`), so a new song two seeds' rankings hold stays in the earlier seed's mix and a familiar song in the earlier seed's share. Deezer lookups stay three at a time across every harvest and Magic Queue batch going on, and an artist another one is looking up is waited for rather than looked up again. A mix with fewer than ten new songs isn't shown. **How much of each mix is new** is Discover's New music level, one for both mix rows and the Magic Queue, kept on the phone (not synced), set from Discover's settings card (see Editable Home): 1 = 20% new songs (Mostly familiar), 2 = 40% (More familiar), 3 = 60% (Balanced, the default), 4 = 80% (Mostly new), 5 = 100% (All new) (Core's `DISCOVER_MIX_LEVELS`, `discoverLevelLabel`, `readDiscoverMixLevel`). **The level card** holds three sliders of five steps each, applied as they move: **New music** (the level, each step showing its name and share, "Balanced · 60% new"), **Genre faithfulness**, from Same genres (the seed's style only) through Close (the seed's style or a neighbouring one: Heavy sounds near Hard rock / grunge, Metalcore / post-hardcore and Extreme metal, never Soft / acoustic), Balanced, Neighbours to Wander (from Balanced on, the genre affinity a song's artist needs to the seed's profile to match: 0.5 = `GENRE_AFFINITY_THRESHOLD`, the default, 0.35, 0.2); a song of no known style gets in at Same genres and Close only when its artist is close to the seed, see Magic Queue, and **Popularity**, from Hits through Popular, Balanced, Lesser known to Deep cuts (the engine's popularity weight: 1, 0.5, 0, the default and neutral, -0.5, -1). Core's `mixSlidersEngine` maps them (at Same genres and Close the engine holds a song to its seed's style, the Magic Queue's rule: `styleAffinity`, a song of unknown style joining only when close to the seed); `readMixSliders` and `storedMixSliders` keep them on the phone, not synced, one set for Discover (`home.discoverLevel`, `home.discoverSliders`; the defaults store nothing; the three sections' own values from before Discover are left unread, so every slider started at its default after the update). At the defaults the engine ranks as it does without sliders. A move of Genre faithfulness or Popularity re-ranks both mix rows' saved mixes from the catalog at once and offline, with no new seed, no request and no rebuild: a build saves what it started from with its mixes (`DiscoverMixesBuild`: its seeds, those that made no mix too, a refresh's avoided songs, and its variety, what its random nudge is made from, `seededRandom`), and a re-rank ranks the catalog for them again (`discoverMixesRerun`, `rerankedDiscoverMixes`), so moving a slider back brings the mixes back. Mixes saved before builds kept that start re-rank from their mixes' seeds, a genre mix's artists found in the Library by name. The engine walks the catalog once per seed (`catalogMixReader`, `recommendCandidates`) and a move only weighs its songs again (`rankRecommended`); a re-rank waits for the finger to lift, and one at a time (sliders moved during a build or a re-rank re-rank once after it). The re-ranked mixes stay the same build (the next one is due as before), replace their set in what a pull avoids, crossfade in on their tiles and hand Wi-Fi pre-download their new first songs. The other songs are familiar: songs the owner knows, downloaded or not. A familiar song the phone has no file for (non-D) streams, as a YT Music entry does; an unavailable entry never joins, since it can't play, and neither do unsearched entries and songs only played (no video to stream). A build stores each mix's new songs (as many as level 5 plays, the mix's length) and its familiar share (its pool) next to them (`discoverMixFamiliarPool`): first the familiar songs its seed's radio brought into the catalog, in the ranking's order (the Library's as Library songs, the others streamed as the catalog has them), then the Library songs of its seed artist, the most played and then the newest first, then the non-D songs streamed or in a playlist by its seed artists (they carry no genre tag), the most streamed and then the newest (first streamed or added) first; a song is in one mix's share at most; the share holds what level 1 takes. So at 20% new a small Library's mix fills out with songs played and playlisted before it falls back to new ones. The level only re-blends what's stored (`blendDiscoverMix`, the one function every reading of a saved mix goes through): no network call, no skeleton, no wait for the next build. A mix keeps its length: its level's share of new songs (rounded), the rest from its pool in order, and new songs again for what the pool lacks; familiar and new songs are interleaved, from a new song, as evenly as their counts allow. Library songs the Library no longer has are left out of the pool before blending on the mix's page and menu. Every re-blend (a level change, opening a mix's page or menu, Home coming into focus) also reads the videos the owner knows now (`knownVideos`: songs streamed or in a playlist, and downloaded songs played or in a playlist) and leaves the new songs among them out, with no network (Core's `discoverMixKeep`), so a song from a mix played or added to a playlist after the build leaves its new songs without a rebuild. Don't play explicit leaves explicit familiar songs out too, streamed ones included. Mixes saved before Artist mixes (Discover mixes, with their song, varied and genre seeds, some saved before levels with no pool), and those saved before familiar songs could stream, are dropped on update: they read as none (Core's `parseDiscoverMixes`, by the saved format's version), so the section builds on the next Home visit, showing its skeleton meanwhile. Each tile follows what the level plays (Core's `discoverMixCaption`): "<artist> mix" over the first three artists of the blended mix (the familiar songs' too) and its song count, under the cover of the first song of the share the mix mostly plays: its first familiar song's (a Library song's cached artwork, which the pool keeps from the build, or a streamed one's cover) when familiar songs outnumber new ones (20% and 40% new), else its first new song's; a song without a cover passes to the next of that share, then to the mix's first song with one. So changing the level visibly changes the tiles, and each tile crossfades its cover and caption to the new ones (`src/motion/Crossfade.tsx`, 300 ms, a one-shot fade that never loops). The mix's page and menu head show the same. A saved mix keeps its seed's artist. It opens the mix's page (`/library/discover-mix/<id>`), the YT Music list page with Play, Shuffle and Download (every song it streams, new or familiar, as an album downloads), its Library songs among the new ones as Library rows. Songs downloaded since play their files. Its long-press menu (Play Next, Add to Queue, Save as Playlist, Download Mix) and Save as Playlist follow the blended list: a saved copy keeps its Library songs as Library entries. The mixes are saved on the phone (not synced), so Home shows the last ones at once. They're built again when Home comes into focus with YT Music on and they weren't built that calendar day, and also once the first counted plays arrive if the last build had no seeds from plays, so they turn toward the owner's plays without waiting a day (`discoverMixesDue`; the saved mixes keep whether their build had play seeds). A build that makes no usable mixes (the catalog has too few songs for its seeds yet, or too few are left after filtering) keeps the saved mixes and is tried again ten minutes later at the earliest. An empty or unreadable saved set does not count as having built mixes today, so the next visit tries again. With an empty Library, nothing is asked. While no mixes are saved (the first build after install, or after clearing data), the row shows its label over a skeleton: placeholder tiles in the mixes' row, at their size, with fill boxes for the cover and the caption lines, holding still (a pulse would make the glass chrome re-blur every frame, and reduce motion wants none). A failed build keeps the skeleton until a later one saves mixes, and a build that finds nothing to build from (no plays and an empty Library) hides the section instead, which the phone remembers, so a later launch doesn't show the skeleton before the next build. With mixes saved, a rebuild never shows it: the saved mixes stay until new ones are saved, the row's label saying a quiet "Updating…" (Footnote graphite after the label, read out by screen readers) while it runs, and then each tile crossfades to the new mix in its place (Core's `discoverMixesSection`). Nothing pulses or shimmers anywhere in the section, and under reduce motion every crossfade is a plain swap. **Pull for new mixes:** pulling the carousel right from its start, past its edge, shows a refresh arrow at the left edge; past a threshold (Core's `mixPull`, `MIX_PULL_THRESHOLD`) it turns into a spinner with a light haptic tick, and letting go rebuilds that section alone, at once, whether or not a build is due (`refreshMixes` in `src/home/discoverMixes.ts`). A pull while the section builds does nothing, and there's no daily cap. The saved mixes stay with "Updating…" and crossfade to the new ones once they're saved, which counts as the section's build for the day. The new mixes try to be different: each refresh avoids the seed artists and the new songs of every set the section showed today, where others are left (Core's `mixesShown`, `mixRefreshAvoid`, kept on the phone and started afresh by the day's automatic build); Artist mixes pick other artists (the similar artists the phone has read when the Library's are used up), and when nothing different is left the least recently shown seeds come back first; a song shown today comes back only to make up a mix's ten songs. A "New mixes" toast offers Undo, which puts the previous set back as it was (its familiar share and blend) while the toast shows (`undoneMixRefresh`). It reads only the phone too, so it waits for no search; the harvest after it refills what it used up. Artist mixes doesn't wait for a play on Home: the line asking for a first play sits before the first other section that plays fill. - **Genre mixes.** Discover's Genre mixes row (its last, after Artist mixes) shows up to four mixes that each sound like a genre family rather than one band. It needs YT Music on but no sign-in, and it builds, saves and refreshes on its own, beside Artist mixes, blended and ranked at Discover's one set of sliders. A genre build made once the owner has counted plays counts as one with seeds from plays, whichever families those plays fell in, so the first plays bring one rebuild and not one on every Home visit (`genreMixSeeds`, `discoverMixesDue`). The five-song threshold allows for the rounding of 1/n shares, so fifteen songs of three families each make five songs in each. **Families:** Core maps the Library's genre tags to families from a curated table (`genreFamily`), matched without case, spaces or punctuation, so Metalcore, Post-Hardcore, Alternative Metal and Nu Metal are Metal; Rock, Alternative, Indie Rock and Hard Rock are Rock; Rap/Hip Hop, Hip-Hop and Trap are Hip-Hop (and so on for Punk, Pop, Electronic, R&B, Jazz, Classical, Country, Folk, Reggae, Latin, Blues and Soundtrack). A tag the table doesn't know is its own family, named as typed. A family's own name is one of its tags. **Songs with several genres:** a song's genre is every value of its genre tag (a multi-value Vorbis comment or ID3v2.4 frame, which the scanner keeps beside the first, `tracks.genres`), and a value joined with ";" or "/" ("Metalcore; Rock", "Rock/Pop") is each of its parts, unless the table knows the value whole ("Rap/Hip Hop"). The parts are mapped to families first, so values of one family ("Rap/Hip Hop") count once. A song in n families adds 1/n of a song and 1/n of its counted plays to each, for the ranking and the five-song threshold, so a Library of multi-tagged songs inflates no family; it may join the familiar share of any of its families. This is for Genre mixes only: the Library's Genres list, genre counts and Search file a song under its tag's first value, as before. **Which families:** those with five or more Library songs, ranked by their songs' counted plays, then by how many songs they have; the first four that have an artist who may seed get a mix, one per family, never two (Core's `genreMixSeeds`). With no family of five songs the section is hidden (remembered on the phone, as Artist mixes' "nothing to build from" is; a Library without families is tried again ten minutes later at the earliest, since a build reads the whole Library). **Seeds:** each mix grows from up to three of the family's Library artists, picked at random each build, weighted towards those most played in the family (the square root of their counted plays in it, `pickWeighted`, `playsWeight`), the most played in it first; names that aren't an artist's ("Various Artists") seed nothing. **Songs:** a genre mix's candidates are any catalog song one of whose artists' genre profiles has a share of its family, whoever's radio brought it, besides its seed artists' radios and their neighbourhood in the artist graph (Core's `recommend` with the family; see Discover). The harvest reads its seed artists' Start radios (their artist pages found as an artist seed's is, from where they started last time when that's remembered), at the same time, the radios taking turns giving their next song in the seeds' order (`interleaveRadios`), a page each, again while the mix would have fewer than 25 songs, three pages at most; a radio that can't be read is left out. What a mix keeps of the ranking follows Artist mixes' rules (`discoverMixSongs`: no music videos, no song the owner knows, at most 50, at least ten, and genre affinity, the seed's profile being its seed artists' Library songs together, `mixSeedProfile`), and a new song is in one genre mix at most, but may also be in an artist mix. **Level:** Discover's New music level, as Artist mixes'. Its familiar share is first the familiar songs its seed artists' radios brought, in the ranking's order, then the Library songs of its family (`discoverMixFamiliarPool`, `ofGenreFamily`), the most played and then the newest first, then the non-D songs streamed or in a playlist by its seed artists, as Artist mixes'; a song is in one genre mix's share at most. **Tiles** read the family's name ("Metal mix"; an unknown tag typed all in lowercase gets capital initials) and otherwise follow Artist mixes' tiles: what the level plays, crossfading on a level change or a rebuild, with "Updating…" after the row's label while one runs. A genre mix's id is `genre:<family>`, and its page, menu, Save as Playlist and Download are an artist mix's. Building asks nothing; the harvest asks at most three Artists searches per genre family it reads (for artists without a saved page), within the search allowance. **Pull for new mixes** works as Artist mixes' does, for Genre mixes alone: a refresh keeps the families while there are four at most and grows them from other family artists (the ones it showed today filling up the three after them, least recently shown first), and with more families brings in those it didn't show today first (`genreMixSeeds` with the avoid-list).
- **Candidate catalog.** Wave keeps a local catalog of the songs YouTube Music has already shown it (see Glossary), so its own recommendations can later be built without the network. It makes no request of its own: every radio Wave reads (an artist mix's or a genre mix's radios, every page read of each, however the build ends; a Magic Queue's artist radios) and every YT Music artist page it reads (a Library artist's Online section, a YT Music artist page, the similar artists Artist mixes read) hands it what it brought. A song keeps its video id, title, artists, album (and its page), length, explicit mark and cover, when it was first and last seen, and its radios: each seed artist whose radio brought it (names compared as artist photos compare them, `catalogSeedKey`; a seed that isn't an artist's, "Various Artists", counts no radio), with when that radio last did. A song seen again is seen now, takes the latest page's metadata and counts a radio once however often that radio plays it; a new radio counts once more, so a song's radio count is how many distinct radios carry it (popularity's raw material, and the artist graph's). Music videos stay out (mixes never keep them), and an artist page adds its top songs only, with no radio: every song of a prolific artist would push the radios' songs out. **What leaves:** a song no radio or page brought in 60 days (`CATALOG_UNSEEN_MS`), and a radio that last brought a song that long ago stops counting for it; past 5,000 songs (`CATALOG_MOST`) the least recently seen leave first. Leaving is decided as the catalog grows, so a phone that reads nothing for a while keeps what it had. Core decides what a page adds, how a song seen again merges and what leaves (`catalogSeen`, `catalogLeaving`); the store keeps it in two tables (`candidate_songs`, and `candidate_radios` by song and seed artist), written in one transaction a radio or page on a free turn, so a read never waits on it or fails because of it. It is a cache, as the saved mixes are: on this phone only, never synced. Home's mixes and the Magic Queue's YouTube Music songs are built from it (see Discover, Magic Queue); the harvest and a Magic Queue's refill write each page they read at once, so what comes after has them.
- **Artist graph.** Wave knows which artists are close to which (see Glossary), from three sources it already has, with no request of its own. **Radio co-occurrence:** an artist whose songs a seed artist's radio brought (the candidate catalog's radios) is close to the seed, the more of the radio's songs are theirs the closer. **"Fans might also like":** an artist a saved YT Music artist page lists as similar is close to the page's artist, the earlier on the list the closer (only the pages' names and similar artists are read, not their songs). **The owner's sessions:** two artists the owner played one right after the other in the play history (the Library's songs; streamed songs aren't in it), within a listening session, are close, the more often the closer; a play starting 30 minutes or more after the one before ended starts a new session (`ARTIST_SESSION_GAP_MS`), and a song skipped within its first 30 s is left out. Each source's evidence is a closeness from 0 to 1 (a count's first piece is half, each more halves the rest; a similar list's first artist is 1, its last a little over half); two artists' closeness adds up the three, the radios and the pages counting fully and the sessions half (shuffle plays artists one after the other that aren't close; `ARTIST_GRAPH_WEIGHTS`), so it is 1 only when every source says they're as close as can be. Closeness goes both ways. Names are matched as the catalog matches seed artists (`catalogSeedKey`, as artist photos compare names), so a Library artist and their YouTube Music namesake are one artist, and a name that isn't an artist's ("Various Artists", "Unknown Artist") never joins. **Queries:** an artist's neighbours, closest first; and a neighbourhood of one or more seed artists a few hops out (two by default, `ARTIST_NEIGHBOURHOOD_HOPS`), the seeds left out: an artist is as close as the closest path from a seed makes them, its closenesses multiplied and each hop past the first keeping half (`ARTIST_HOP_DECAY`), and every artist of a hop ranks below every artist of the hop before (a hop that would reach the one before is scaled down below it). Core builds it (`artistGraph.ts`: `artistGraph`, `artistNeighbours`, `artistNeighbourhood`); the app derives it from the library database once per session, the first time it's asked for, and again after it's told what it's derived from grew (`forgetArtistGraph`; `src/home/artistGraph.ts`, `artistGraphNow.ts`). It stores nothing. Home's mixes draw from a seed's neighbourhood (see Discover), and a harvest that read something forgets it.
- **Artist page.** The artist page stays the Library's: it opens from the Library alone, with no YouTube request. Its album shelves (Albums, Singles & EPs, Appears on) are each one row of covers under their heading that scrolls sideways, laid out as Home's rows are, and the Songs follow straight after; a shelf with nothing in it is left out (Core's `artistPageItems`). Each cover on the shelves is the **album card** (see Kind cards): a square cover with 6dp corners and the title and artist under it, no year, for a Library album and a YT Music release alike, a selection check on the Library's. Each song is the **song card's row** (see Kind cards): a 40dp cover with 4dp corners and its play disc, the title over "Song · <artist>", the length still at the end; under Popular the rank leads in place of the cover, as it did. - **A star means downloaded.** A non-D song (one this phone has no audio file for: a YT Music song, streamed, or a missing song) can be starred wherever a Library song can: Now Playing's star, its long-press menu (**Favorite**, or **Remove from Favorites** once starred, after Add to Playlist; Core's `ytSongActions`), Search's results, and the selection bar, Library and YT Music songs checked together included (a playlist's unsearched entry has no video to star yet, so songs checked with one offer no Favorite; Core's `itemActions`). There's no waiting list for favorites: a star always means a download. The star fills at once everywhere the song shows (a streamed song's row in Search, a playlist, an artist's or album's page shows its pending favorite's star; Now Playing and the song's menu show it starred as either its pending favorite or the Library song it is by now, Core's `ytSongStarred`), and the song's download joins the queue as the song's own Download does (same source rules, retries and failed list), waiting in Settings › Downloads › Downloading under a group called **Favorites** (Core's `FAVORITES_DOWNLOAD_ORIGIN`). A YT Music song becomes a pending favorite (see "Importing playlists from a link", From YT Music), kept before its download is queued, so the save that brings its file in stars the Library track, which then syncs like any favorite and shows in Favorites; until then the star stays on this phone. A missing song is the Library's song already, so its star is a Library favorite from the start. Core decides what the star does to the download (`starredDownload`): a song never asked for (or downloaded once and gone from the Library since) is queued, and the star is the only one that asked; a failed one is asked for again, keeping where it was asked from; one already waiting, downloading or saving just gets the star. **Unstarring before the file arrives** removes the pending favorite (or the missing song's star) and cancels the download when only the star asked for it, waiting, downloading or failed (`unstarredDownload`); one already saving to the Library is too late to cancel. A download the owner asked for separately carries on: one asked for before the star, and one the owner asks for after it (the song's Download, an album's or playlist's, Download All), which takes it from the star (Core's `starAskedAfter`; each request settles who asked in turn with the stars' own, so a Download tapped right after the star still takes it). Now Playing's star on a streamed song waits for the Library's first read, so a song the Library has isn't taken for one it lacks and downloaded again. Which downloads only a star asked for is kept on this phone by job id (the key-value store's "downloads.starAsked", Core's `readStarDownloads`), so it survives Wave closing. **On every phone:** a star that comes from the Wave account on a missing song (starred on another phone, or a sign-in on a new phone, which brings them all) asks for its download as a star made here does, under Favorites, as do the liked songs of the YT Music import's Also add to Favorites (see From YT Music). Wave checks the starred missing songs after each pull that brought stars or missing songs, after the YT Music import's stars, and as it starts (Core's `syncedStarDownloads`, run by `downloadSyncedStars`): each one this phone hasn't seen starred yet is asked for, through `starredDownload`, so a song already on its way isn't queued twice; one seen before isn't asked for again, so a download cancelled or cleared since stays so. A missing song starred here counts as seen at once (Core's `starredHere`), and one unstarred here as seen no more. A star taken off, here or on another phone, before the file arrives cancels the download only it asked for (`unstarredDownload`), as does a missing song leaving the Library (signing out). Which starred missing songs this phone has seen is kept on this phone by Track id (the key-value store's "downloads.syncedStars", `readSyncedStars`).
- **Integrated playback.** Search's song results, an artist's page and an album's page decide what Play, Shuffle and a tapped song play by one rule (Core's `pagePlayback`): with YouTube Music's songs among the page's (an integrated view), the page's whole list in page order, the Library's files and the rest streamed; with none, the Library's songs alone, as before, so nothing is streamed or queued from YouTube Music outside an integrated view.
- **Rows stay in place.** A download finishing doesn't move its song in a mixed list (Search's song results, an artist's page, a Library album's page). For the rest of the visit to the screen, a YT Music song the list showed streamed keeps its row: it turns downloaded at once and plays the Library's file, and the Library's part of the list leaves that file out, so it shows once (Core's `songsInPlace`). In Search it doesn't become the top result, reorder the groups or count as the Library having results for the query, so YT Music's results stay (`heldTracks`, a skipped search). An album page showing its tracklist keeps it when the last song is downloaded, instead of showing the album as it is (`keepTracklist`). Leaving the screen (another screen over it, another tab; not Now Playing) and opening it again, or typing another query, lays the list out by its rule again, each song once. YT Music's album and artist pages, Discover mixes and playlists already keep their rows.
- **Off switch.** Settings › Accounts › YT Music › Use YT Music (on by default). Off, the section isn't shown and the native client refuses every request before it reaches the network; the switch is kept natively too, so the playback service obeys it without JS.
- **Account.** Signing in is optional; everything YT Music does also works signed out. Settings › Accounts › YT Music › Sign In opens Google's sign-in page in a web view inside Wave (`YtSignInActivity`, as Metrolist does). Once the page reaches YouTube Music signed in, Wave takes the session cookie and the page's account (`YtSignInPage`), asks YouTube Music's account menu whose account it is (`YtAccountMenu`) and keeps the session; Settings then shows the account's picture, name and email, and Sign Out. The session is handed to innertubex (`replaceSession`), so every request the `YtClient` sends (Search, playback, optional download metadata, Discover mixes) goes out as the account; a Premium account therefore gets the audio YouTube serves Premium. Visitor data isn't the account's and is left as it is.
- **Session privacy.** The cookie lives only in the native module: in one file in the app's no-backup storage (`noBackupFilesDir`, never in an Android backup), never in the JS key-value store, never handed to JS (which only sees `{ status, account: { name, email, photoUrl } }`) and never logged. The sign-in web view starts and ends empty: its cookies and site storage are cleared before the page opens and when it closes, so its only copy is the native one. Sign Out deletes the file and clears the web view's cookies and storage again.
- **Expiry.** A session YouTube stops accepting expires instead of failing: when a request comes back HTTP 401, or the account menu has no account (checked each time Settings opens), the session's credentials are dropped, the request is asked again signed out (once), and Settings shows "signed out" with Sign In Again. Signed out, nothing can be rejected again, so there's no error loop. Without an answer (offline, YouTube failing) the session stays as it was. What Settings shows is Core's (`stepYtAccount`, `ytAccountView`); with YT Music off there is no Sign In, but a signed-in account can still be signed out of.
- **Native shape.** One process-wide `YtClient` (package `expo.modules.wavenative.yt`) owns the HTTP client, innertubex's session and the stream resolver (`YtClient.get(context).streams`, used by playback); the `WaveYtMusic` Expo module exposes it to JS (`setEnabled`, `searchSongs`, `searchArtists`, `searchAlbums`, `artist`, `playlist`, `playlistMore`, `songRadio`, `artistRadio`, `radioMore`, `session`, `checkSession`, `signIn`, `signOut`, `cacheArtwork`, `libraryPlaylists` and the `onSessionChanged` event; failures reject with `ERR_YT_OFFLINE`, `ERR_YT_FAILED` or `ERR_YT_DISABLED`, and `libraryPlaylists` with `ERR_YT_SIGNED_OUT` too), wrapped by the app's `YtMusic` Effect service. `libraryPlaylists` reads the signed-in account's library playlists (browse `FEmusic_liked_playlists`, following its continuations, at most 100 pages, `YtLibraryParser`): for each, its id, name, cover, song count and kind (the account's own, by the Edit entry in its menu; saved; or generated: `LM`, `SE`, the `RD…` mixes but `RDCLAK5uy_…`, podcasts). It needs the session: signed out it rejects with `ERR_YT_SIGNED_OUT` before asking, and a session YouTube refuses (HTTP 401, or a page asking to sign in) expires, then rejects the same way. YouTube Music sends a playlist a hundred rows a page: `playlist` reads its first page (its head, its songs and the token for the next page) and `playlistMore` the page a token leads to, both by `YtPlaylistParser`, which leaves out the rows YouTube Music greys out (removed, blocked in the country). Core's `readYtPlaylist` follows the pages to the last, keeping YouTube Music's order, and keeps a song listed twice once. A read stops at 5,000 songs or 100 pages, whichever comes first, and then says `truncated`; Liked Songs' import reads `LM` with `uncapped` instead, to the last page, ended early only by a token it already followed or a page of nothing but songs it already has. `YtMusic.playlist` runs it: a page that fails fails the whole read, so the import never gets part of a playlist that looks complete, while a YT Music playlist's page hears each page as it comes (see YT Music list pages). Downloads belong to the native `AndroidDownloads` owner (package `download`): `DurableDownloads` keeps the queue as an atomically rewritten journal in no-backup storage and drives it from `YtDownloadService`, five songs at a time in the order they were asked for (a song waiting in a peer's queue, for a pause before trying again, or over 30 seconds for an answer from JS leaves its slot to the next, up to ten started at once), each song's transfer, tagging and publication on a worker thread of its own, while songs join the Library one at a time (publish decision, publication, import), a `dataSync` foreground service with a partial wake lock and a Wi-Fi lock that shows progress and Cancel in its notification, restarts sticky after process death and stops when the queue is empty. Each job carries its source, and the transport hands a Soulseek candidate to the Soulseek client and YouTube's to `YtAudioDownload` (package `yt`). A Soulseek job also carries the fallback its prepare answer gave (the failures Core hands over and the M4A's place); every failure goes through one place in the journal, which switches such a job to YouTube Music with a fresh deadline instead of failing it. The Soulseek client tells an unreachable server (`ERR_SLSK_UNREACHABLE`) from being offline by asking whether the phone is online. The journal leases the decisions that need the Library and Core to JS (prepare: the source, for Soulseek the search and Core's match, and the destination; confirm: received tags, or the length YouTube's audio plays for; publish: the current folder policy; import: the LibraryStore upsert); the download service starts the headless JS runtime for them, and a lease that isn't answered expires and is offered again. `WaveSoulseek` exposes the session, Download in best quality (`turnOn`, `turnOff`; the account it makes or reuses never reaches JS), search and the journal (`enqueueJob`, `jobsSnapshot`, `jobsChangedSince` (the jobs changed since a revision, which JS merges with Core's `applyJournalChanges` to follow the journal), `moveQueuedJobs` (waiting jobs in a new order, as Core's `moveQueuedJobs` does), `claimDecision`, `submitDecision`, `cancelJob`, `resumeJobs`, `onJobsChanged`); `WaveYtDownloads` keeps the optional album, watch and lyrics pages; the app's `ytmusic/YtDownloads.ts` answers decisions and mirrors the journal into rows. Building it needs Kotlin 2.4 (innertubex's), set through `expo-build-properties`.

### Spotify account

How it works and why is in `docs/spotify-account.md` (#484). There's no official Spotify Web API for this, so Wave uses the web player's own endpoints, which Spotify can change.

- **Sign-in.** Optional. Settings › Accounts › Spotify › Sign In opens Spotify's sign-in page (`accounts.spotify.com/login`, continuing to `open.spotify.com`) in a web view inside Wave (`SpotifySignInActivity`). Links to other apps (the Spotify app) don't open from it. Once the page reaches the web player, Wave takes the `sp_dc` cookie (`SpotifySignInPage`), asks `open.spotify.com/api/token` for a signed-in token with it, reads the account's name, photo and username (GraphQL `profileAttributes`) and keeps the session; Settings then shows the photo, the name (the username when Spotify has no display name) and Sign Out. A cookie that gets no token, or only an anonymous one, is "Spotify didn't confirm the sign-in". The note under Sign In says signing in lets Wave read the owner's Spotify playlists and share theirs there (see "Sharing playlists"), that email and password or an emailed code work, and that Continue with Google may not (Google blocks its sign-in in web views). A sign-in that didn't work says why the same way in Settings, From Spotify and sharing (Core's `SPOTIFY_SIGN_IN_FAILURE_LINES`).
- **Session privacy.** As YT Music's: the cookie lives only in the native module, in one file in no-backup storage (`spotify-session.json`), never in the JS key-value store, never handed to JS (which only sees `{ status, account: { name, photoUrl } }`) and never logged. Tokens stay in the native module's memory and are never written down. The web view starts and ends empty, and Sign Out deletes the file, drops the token and clears the web view's cookies and storage.
- **Token.** A token is asked for with the cookie and two RFC 6238 codes (HMAC-SHA1, 30 s, 6 digits; `totp` from the phone's clock and `totpServer` from `open.spotify.com/api/server-time`, or `unavailable`), keyed with the secret from the web player's script, which Wave ships with its version (`SpotifySecret`, `SpotifyTotp`). It's used until a minute before it expires; a 401 to a query gets one new token.
- **Expiry.** Only a 401 from `/api/token` means Spotify stopped accepting the session: it expires (the cookie is dropped, the account kept for the note), later requests are refused without being sent, and Settings shows "signed out" with Sign In Again. Each time Settings opens Wave asks Spotify whose session it is, picking up a new name or photo; no answer leaves it as it was.
- **When Spotify changes the web player.** A refused TOTP (400), a `totpVerExpired` flag or an unknown query hash (412) mean Wave needs an update, not that the session ended. Wave reads the secret and the operation hashes again from the live web player's script, at most once per run, and retries; if that doesn't help, the request fails with "Spotify changed something. Try again after updating Wave." and the session is kept.
- **Native shape.** One process-wide `SpotifyClient` (package `expo.modules.wavenative.spotify`, `SpotifyModule.clientFor(context)`) holds the session and the token; `query(operation, variables)` sends a GraphQL query as the account, which the import and the share build on, and `spclient(method, path, body)` a request to the web player's REST host (playlist writes). The `WaveSpotify` Expo module exposes `session`, `checkSession`, `signIn`, `signOut`, the `onSessionChanged` event and, for From Spotify, `library(offset)`, `likedSongs(offset)` and `playlist(uri, offset)`, each a page of its query's `data` in JSON for Core to read (`SpotifyLibraryQueries`); the share reads its playlist and the library back with the same two, and adds `searchTracks` (the query's `data` in JSON), `createPlaylist`, `makePlaylistPublic`, `renamePlaylist`, `addToPlaylist`, `removeFromPlaylist` and `movePlaylistEntry` (`SpotifyPlaylistWrites`). Failures reject with `ERR_SPOTIFY_OFFLINE`, `ERR_SPOTIFY_FAILED`, `ERR_SPOTIFY_SIGNED_OUT` or `ERR_SPOTIFY_CHANGED`. The app asks the module through the `Spotify` Effect service (`src/spotify/Spotify.ts`, `askSpotify`), whose reads parse the JSON and whose failures are a tagged `SpotifyError` carrying Core's `SpotifyAccountFailure`. What Settings shows is Core's (`stepSpotifyAccount`, `spotifyAccountView`).

### Wave account and sync

- **What it's for.** Changing phones without losing the Library. The account keeps what Wave knows about the music, not the music: the song list, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences. Audio files never go up. A new phone gets the files back from Syncthing, or downloads them as missing songs.
- **Who can sign in.** Any Google account. The app is sideloaded and shared with a few people, so the service limits each account instead of keeping an allowlist: a maximum request size, a maximum number of songs, playlists and plays, a request rate per session, and a sign-in rate per address and per Google account (constants in the service, `SYNC_LIMITS`). An optional allowlist of emails (`ALLOWED_EMAILS`, unset by default) closes sign-in to everyone else if it's ever needed.
- **Signing in.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account › Sign In with Google uses Android's Google sign-in (the account picker, not a web view, unlike YT Music's sign-in), with Wave's OAuth client, which Google ties to the APK's package name and signing certificate. The first time, a card says what goes up and what never does (story 80) before the picker opens. The app sends the Google ID token to the service once. The service checks it against Google's keys and Wave's client id and answers with its own session token. The ID token is then dropped. The session token is kept like YT Music's cookie, in the native module's no-backup storage, never logged. It lasts until sign-out, account deletion or the service revoking it. A revoked or expired session signs the app out, and Settings says so. JS never holds the token: the native module (`WaveAccount`) sends pushes and pulls for it, and only native code reaches the Soulseek account's route. The service's URL and the web client id are `syncUrl` and `googleWebClientId` in `app.config.ts` extra; empty, Settings › Accounts › Wave Account says the account isn't set up on this build.
- **Signing out** keeps the Library, playlists, favorites, play history and preferences on the phone and stops syncing. Missing songs go, since the phone has nothing of them. A playlist entry for a missing song with a YouTube video id becomes a YT Music entry. Without a video id it leaves the playlist, as a deleted file's entry does. Signing in again merges, as the first sign-in does.
- **Deleting the account.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account › Delete Account asks first, then the service deletes every row it holds for the account and every session, and the app signs out. Nothing on the phone is deleted.
- **What syncs.**
  - Songs: the recording key, title, credit, album, album artist, disc and track number, length, year, genre, the YouTube video id when the song was downloaded from YouTube Music, and when it was added.
  - Playlists and their entries, including YT Music entries.
  - Favorites.
  - Play events, with the song's recording key, including imported BlackPlayer plays.
  - Preferences: the appearance, the theme choice, glass, the tap and long-press timings, the tab bar, the kind of shuffle, Swipe actions, Crossfade, Buffer, Don't play explicit, the Artist photos, Lyrics and genre and YT Music switches, Download in best quality, Auto lyrics and the notification accent. Each goes up as what the key-value store holds for it (null at its default), and one pulled applies at once, through its setting, without counting as a change on that phone. The notification accent follows the theme and the appearance, so it comes with them. Download in best quality's switch lives in the native module: Settings sets the synced copy once the switch has moved (stamped even when the copy already said so), and one pulled turns the native switch on or off at the end of the pull, again at each pull until it has (turning it on fails offline; Core's `settleBestQuality`, see "The Soulseek account"). Auto lyrics is the car screen's own toggle, kept by the native playback service rather than as a setting, and doesn't sync yet.
  - Never: scan roots and excluded folders (they're paths on one phone), the queue and resume point, recent searches, artwork, artist photos, playlists' own images and other caches (the candidate catalog among them), the YouTube session, the download journal, and which YouTube Music or Spotify playlist a From YT Music or From Spotify playlist came from.
- **The Soulseek account.** Settings has no Soulseek sign-in, only a **Download in best quality** switch (off on a new install). Turning it on gives the phone a Soulseek account if it has none: a random username that doesn't name Wave (a name every Wave user shared would let peers block them all, and Wave shares no files) and a random password. Logging in with an unused name is how Soulseek creates an account, so a taken name (`ERR_SLSK_AUTH`) just means drawing another, five names at most before Settings says it couldn't make an account. Offline, or with Soulseek's server down, the switch goes back off with a message. Turning the switch off keeps the account for the next time. An account Soulseek later rejects is forgotten and the switch goes off, its synced copy too at the next pull; turning it on again makes a new one. A phone already connected to an account of its own keeps it, with the switch on. While signed in to a Wave account, the Soulseek account belongs to it: the first phone with one sends it up, and every phone signed in afterwards uses the Wave account's, replacing its own. The native module asks for it when the switch turns on and after each pull (the first after sign-in too): when the Wave account has one and the phone's is different, the phone switches to it and forgets its own (downloads running on the old one finish first, and the next search with none running connects with the new one); when it has none, the phone's goes up. An account the switch makes while signed in goes up at once, and turning the switch on with no account of the phone's uses the Wave account's instead of making one. When Wave's server can't be asked, turning it on goes on as if signed out, and the next pull settles it. Signing out keeps the phone's Soulseek account. Without a Wave account, the phone keeps its own. The username and password go between the native module and the sync service only (`PUT` and `GET /v1/soulseek`, sent by the native module with the session token it holds), never through JS, as the Soulseek credentials never are. The service keeps the password encrypted with a key only the Worker has (`SOULSEEK_KEY`). Deleting the Wave account deletes the service's copy, and the phone keeps its own.
- **Song identity.** A song's local id and path mean nothing on another phone, so the account knows songs by their recording key. The Library store keeps each Track's recording key and, for a song downloaded from YouTube Music, its video id (until now the video id was dropped once the file was saved). The service never sees local ids. The app translates them to recording keys and back as it pushes and pulls. Two files on one phone with the same recording key are one song for the account. Plays already recorded get their song's recording key when the update lands. Plays of a song deleted before then can't be keyed and stay on the phone only.
- **Merging.** Every synced row carries when it last changed and which phone changed it. The later change wins, row by row; the phone id breaks a tie (Core's `mergeRows`). Removing something leaves a tombstone, so a removal travels like any other change. A playlist's order is a fractional index per entry (Core's `orderKeyBetween`), so entries two phones add to one playlist offline both stay. Play events are only ever added, and a play is never duplicated (each has an id made on the phone that recorded it). It relies on the phones' clocks. They're set by the network on Android, which is close enough for one person's phones.
- **First sign-in on a phone with a Library.** The two sides are joined, never replaced. The account's songs the phone has (by recording key) become its Tracks, the rest come in as missing songs. The phone's songs, playlists, favorites and plays go up. Two playlists with the same name stay two playlists. A song both sides have keeps the account's tags: the phone's go up only once they change on the phone (a retag), so two phones whose files are tagged differently don't overwrite each other forever (Core's `planSongSync`). Signing out leaves the account, so the next sign-in, to the same account or another, joins again: everything goes up again and the account is pulled from the start, and merging makes that harmless.
- **When it talks to the service.** A change is pushed 3 seconds after the last one (debounced, so a run of edits is one push). The app pulls when it opens, when it comes back to the foreground and after each push. Any network will do, as the rows are small, and the first push of a large history goes in batches of at most 1,000 rows and 1.5 MB (Core's `pushBatches`), each marked as sent as soon as the service has it, so 18,000 BlackPlayer plays go up in 18 requests and an interrupted first push goes on where it stopped. A play goes up once it ends, or once the next one starts (Wave's process died under it). A request refused for the request rate is sent again 20 seconds later, up to four times. There's no background job, and Android Auto's headless start doesn't sync: a phone in a drawer catches up the next time Wave opens. Offline, changes wait in the store and go up later; a sync that fails tries again at the next trigger. The service is never in the way of the Library. The app's side is `WaveSync` (`src/sync`), which runs `syncCycle` through the store and `WaveAccount.request`.
- **Account songs the phone lacks.** Every song pulled is kept in the store's `account_songs`, with its tags, whether the phone has a file for it or not. A star, an entry or a play of a song the phone has no Track for waits in `sync_pending` (as does an entry whose playlist hasn't come yet), and goes in once a Track with its key is in the Library (a scan finds the file, or it comes in as a missing song). Nothing is pulled twice for it.
- **Missing songs.** While signed in, a scan that no longer finds a file keeps the song as a missing song instead of removing it, with its favorites, playlist entries and plays. Signed out, a scan removes it, as before. A missing song shows wherever its Track would (albums, artists, genres, playlists, favorites, Home, Search), dimmed, with the look a not-downloaded YT Music entry has. Tapping one, or Download in its menu, downloads it. A missing song with a star downloads on its own (see A star means downloaded): whoever starred it, on this phone or another, once this phone sees the star (after a pull that brought stars or missing songs, and as Wave starts), so signing in on a new phone queues every starred missing song under Favorites. Its menu also has **Remove from Library**, and Delete on a song that has a file deletes the file and removes the song from the account too, with its star and playlist entries (Core's `songsLeaving`). Those are the only ways a song leaves the account (story 86), besides a retag: a file retagged to another recording key is the same Track under a new key, so its old version leaves the account and its star and entries go up under the new key; its plays keep the old key, as plays are only ever added. Another phone loses a removed song at its next pull if it's a missing song there; a phone that has the file keeps it, as a pull never takes a file's song away, but loses its star and entries. A file a scan finds with a missing song's recording key fills that song in (length within 3 seconds breaks a tie), so files Syncthing brings never show twice.
- **Downloading missing songs.** Settings › Accounts › Wave Account counts the missing songs and offers **Download All**, which queues them as an album download does, with the same progress, Retry Failed and Cancel. A Library album's long-press menu does the same for its own missing songs: **Download Missing Songs** queues them in album order, Cancel Download shows while they download, and Retry Failed once the rest has ended with failures, as a YT Music album's menu does. Albums checked together offer **Download** on the selection bar, for the missing songs of all of them (Core's `itemActions`). An album with no missing songs offers none of these. One song, or all of them, downloads through the download pipeline and its source rule (Core's `downloadSource`): a song with a video id downloads that video while YT Music is on, whatever Download in best quality says, and gets the cover of that video's album; with YT Music off it goes to Soulseek while Download in best quality is on. Any other song goes to Soulseek while Download in best quality is on, else to YouTube Music's search, matched by the rules downloads already use. A downloaded song keeps its place in playlists, favorites and play history, since it's the same Track.
- **The sync service** (`apps/sync`) is a Cloudflare Worker in front of one Turso (libSQL) database, with the account's id on every row. The app never holds a database credential: only the Worker has Turso's token. The Worker imports Core, so it merges by the same rules the app does. Its routes:
  - `POST /v1/session`: Google ID token in, session token out.
  - `DELETE /v1/session`: sign out.
  - `POST /v1/push`: changed rows in, merged on arrival.
  - `GET /v1/pull?since=<cursor>`: every row that changed after the cursor, and the next cursor. The service numbers each accepted change, so a pull never misses one.
  - `PUT /v1/soulseek`: the account's Soulseek account in (the password kept encrypted under `SOULSEEK_KEY`); `GET /v1/soulseek`: it, or none. Only the native module sends them.
  - `DELETE /v1/account`: every row, session and the Soulseek account.
- **Deploying it** is done by hand with `wrangler deploy`. A setup wizard walks through creating the Turso database, Google's OAuth clients (an Android client with the release and debug signing certificates' SHA-1, and the web client id the service checks ID tokens against) and the Worker's secrets.

### Modules

1. **Core** (workspace package, pure TypeScript, **zero runtime dependencies**). Holds all decision-heavy domain logic behind a small public API, so it can be tested anywhere with plain Node:
   - _Queue engine_ — a pure state + command reducer: start playback from a context at a given track; next/previous (with the 3-second restart rule); seek; shuffle on/off (current track stays first, original order restorable; Real Shuffle or Smart Shuffle, see story 25); repeat off/all/one; Play Next; Add to Queue; reorder; remove; track-ended transitions. It outputs the ordered list the native player should hold.
   - _Play-event classifier_ — given start, listened time, duration and end reason, decides whether a play is counted.
   - _Library model_ — grouping (album identity = album title + album artist, album-artist fallback, compilations), sort keys (articles and accents ignored), disc/track ordering, unknown-tag fallbacks, and splitting credits into artists (`creditSplitter`).

2. **Native player** (local Expo module, Kotlin, Media3). A `MediaSessionService` hosting ExoPlayer, so notification, lock screen, Bluetooth/headset buttons, audio focus and becoming-noisy handling come from Media3. The JS side owns ordering (from the queue engine) and pushes the ordered item list; the native side plays it gaplessly and reports state, throttled position, item transitions with a reason (auto-advance, skip, seek, error, crossfade) and errors. Native shuffle is not used. Crossfade opens the next song on a second ExoPlayer well before the overlap, paused and silent; at the handoff it starts playing and becomes the media session's player (the rest of the queue added around its song, audio focus and listeners moved), with no seek, while the player it replaces keeps only the outgoing song and fades it out, so the media session, notification, queue sync and Android Auto still have one current song. The service keeps the overlap JS last set, so it fades without JS too. The audio pipeline keeps a hook for custom audio processors so equalizer and ReplayGain (v2) slot in without restructuring.
3. **Native scanner** (same Kotlin module). Walks the scan roots minus exclusions, reads tags with a JVM tag library that also supports writing (needed by the v2 tag editor), extracts embedded artwork once per unique image (hashed, cached as files) together with a small colour palette (Android Palette) for the Now Playing tint, and finds sidecar `.lrc` files. It streams results to JS in batches of ~500 and supports an incremental mode driven by known path + modification time + size. The same module holds the YT Music client (`yt` package, see YT Music).
4. **Library store** (app, Drizzle on expo-sqlite). The single owner of the schema and migrations. Tables for tracks, albums, artists (with each track's and album's credited artists in credit order), genres, folders, artwork (path + palette), playlists and playlist items, favorites, play events, scan roots/exclusions, recent searches, and persisted playback state. Home sections and play counts are queries over play events.
   - _What the Wave account needs_ (`db/sync.ts`). Nothing syncs from the store itself; it keeps what sync works from. The `phone` table holds this install's phone id, 32 random hex digits made by the migration (or the first launch), never changed. A Track has `recording_key` (Core's `recordingKey`, computed whenever its file is saved; after the update that brought it in, the store keys the existing Library at launch, `keyRecordings`) and `video_id` (set when a YouTube Music download is imported and kept when the file is saved again; null for Soulseek downloads and scanned files). Its `path` is null for a missing song. Song lists, playback, the scan, imports and lookups read only Tracks with a file (`hasFile`, `withFile` in `db/tracks.ts`). A play event has `recording_key`, written when the play is recorded. Plays of songs deleted before the update have none and stay on the phone.
   - Playlists and playlist entries have a `sync_id` made on the phone that created them. An entry has an `order_key` (Core's `orderKeyBetween`) instead of a position, and a playlist lists its entries by `order_key`, then `sync_id`. The update gave existing entries the keys that adding them one by one at the end would have made, in their old order.
   - Playlists, entries and favorites carry `changed_at` (ms, this phone's clock) and `phone_id`. A removal sets `deleted` and leaves the row as a tombstone, which no list, count or total shows. A phone never stamps two changes of one row with the same time: a change made in the same millisecond as the last one takes the next. `preference_changes` holds when each synced setting (`persistedSetting`'s `synced`) last changed and on which phone; the values stay in the key-value store. `selectChangesSince` reads every playlist, entry, favorite and preference changed after a time, tombstones included.
   - Every synced row also has `pushed`: false from a change made here until a push takes it, true for a change pulled from the account. A play event has a `sync_id`, the same on every phone: random for Wave's own plays, `blackplayer:<n>:<recording key>` for a song's n-th imported BlackPlayer play (Core's `blackPlayerPlayId`), so importing the same stats on two phones uploads them once; and `ongoing` for the play going on now, which `latestPlay` picks back up and which waits to go up until it ends. `account_songs` holds the account's songs as the phone knows them (and what its own file with each key was at the last sync), `sync_pending` the pulled rows waiting for a song or playlist, and `sync_state` the account the Library is joined with and the pull cursor (`db/syncRows.ts`: `joinAccount`, `selectUnpushed`, `markPushed`, `planSongs`, `applyPulledPage`, `applyPending`).
5. **App services** (app, Effect). Wrap the native modules as typed services and event streams; orchestrate scans (core reconciliation → store); drive the queue engine and sync it to the native player; record play events through the classifier; persist and restore playback state.
6. **UI** (app, Expo Router). Tabs the owner arranges — Home, then any of Library, Search and the Library's sections (Search rendered as a separate glass pill; see "Editable tab bar"). A custom glass tab bar, a floating glass mini player and a full-screen Now Playing presented over the tabs with an interactive drag-to-dismiss gesture. Detail screens for album, artist, playlist and genre. Settings, a list of pages (see "Settings").
7. **Sync service** (`apps/sync`, Cloudflare Worker, Turso). Checks Google ID tokens, holds sessions, merges pushed rows by Core's rules, serves pulls and deletes accounts. The app's side is an Effect service (`WaveSync`) that hears of local changes, pushes and pulls, and translates local ids to recording keys through the store (see "Wave account and sync").

### Play event shape

One row per playback start: track, started-at, listened milliseconds, track duration, end reason (`completed` | `skipped` | `stopped` | `interrupted`), counted flag, and play context (type + id). Append-only; nothing is aggregated away.

### Importing playlists from a link

- Library › Playlists › Import Playlists asks where the playlist comes from: **From a Link**, **From Files**, **From YT Music** or **From Spotify**. From Files is the M3U import (story 56). With YT Music off there is no From a Link, From YT Music or From Spotify (a Spotify playlist's songs are searched on YouTube Music): Import Playlists opens the file picker at once, as before, and nothing is asked of YouTube (Core's `playlistImportSources`).
- **From YT Music** shows whenever YT Music is on, signed in or not. Signed out (or with a session that expired), it opens the YouTube Music sign-in Settings › Accounts uses, then the picker; leaving the sign-in opens nothing. The picker is a full-screen sheet that rises from the bottom (`YtLibraryPicker.tsx`): **Cancel** at the top, **Import** and **Import & Download** side by side at the bottom, and **Select All** above the list, which reads **Deselect All** while every row is ticked. Its first row is **Liked Songs**, YouTube Music's Liked music (read as the playlist `LM`), always listed, even for an account with no playlists; its cover and count come from the library page's Liked Music when it lists one. Under it is a switch, **Also add to Favorites**: off when the sheet opens, and enabled only while Liked Songs is ticked (unticked, it shows off and dimmed, and ticking Liked Songs again brings back what it was; Core's `toggleYtLibraryFavorites`, `ytLibraryPickerView`). After it come the playlists in the account's YouTube Music library (`YtMusic.libraryPlaylists`, see YT Music, Native shape), in YouTube Music's order: the ones the account made and the ones it saved from others, YouTube Music's editorial playlists (`RDCLAK5uy_…`) included. What YouTube Music generates (Liked Music, which is the Liked Songs row, Episodes for Later, Supermix, My Mix and the other mixes, podcasts) is left out. Each row shows the cover, the name and "N songs", all from the library page; nothing is read per playlist before Import. Every row starts ticked, Liked Songs included, except those already imported on this phone: they show **Imported** and start unticked. Import and Import & Download do nothing while no row is ticked; Cancel, Back or dragging the sheet down imports nothing. While the library loads the sheet shows a spinner. A failed read gives the reason and **Try Again**; a session YouTube no longer accepts says so and offers **Sign In Again** (Core's `ytLibraryFailure`). The rows, the ticks and Select All are Core's (`ytLibraryPicker`, `toggleYtLibraryRow`, `pressYtLibrarySelectAll`, `ytLibraryPickerView`).
  - **Import.** The sheet goes down and the user stays on Playlists. Each ticked playlist is made at once, empty, in the picker's order and named as a link import names it, Liked Songs as "Liked Songs"; a name any other playlist has (or one made just before it in the same Import) goes in under Keep Both's name ("Name 2") without asking (Core's `planYtLibraryImport`). Wave then reads them one at a time in the background, in that order, and fills each as a YouTube Music link import does (`YtMusic.playlist`, `planLinkImport`, `finishPlaylistImport`): a song the Library has as the Library's track, any other as a YT Music entry. Liked Songs has no song cap: it's read whole, however many songs the account liked (`readYtPlaylist`'s `uncapped`). The other playlists stop at the 5,000-song cap (`YT_PLAYLIST_SONG_CAP`), and the page of one that goes on past it says "Only its first 5,000 songs came from YouTube Music" (see Listed twice, below). Nothing is downloaded or searched; there is no notification and no service. The app log gets the import's line per playlist (`importLogLine`), a line for each read that fails (`ytLibraryReadLogLine`), and one each time the batch pauses (`ytLibraryPauseLogLine`). With Also add to Favorites on, Liked Songs' read gives the Favorite star to each liked song that is a Library track at that moment, the ones `libraryFinder` matched for the playlist (Core's `ytLibraryFavorites`), in one write, before its songs go in; the Favorites list shows them, and the star syncs like any other. Each liked song that goes in as a YT Music entry becomes a **pending favorite** instead (as does a YT Music song starred anywhere, see A star means downloaded), kept by its YouTube Music song (its video id) and its recording (title, artists, length), in the same step, before any of its downloads is queued: the library database's `pending_favorites` table, on this phone only, never synced, so it survives Wave closing. Its star means downloaded, as any star does: its download then joins the queue under Favorites (Core's `ytLibraryFavorites` `download`, run by `downloadStarredSongs`), so a liked song already on its way just gets the star, and unstarring it before its file arrives cancels the download. Import & Download asks for none of these: its playlist's own download queues the liked songs under Liked Songs, which the owner asked for, so an unstar leaves them be. When a song enters the Library, by a download (any download: Import & Download's, the song's own, a playlist's, Soulseek's) or by a scan finding its file, the save that writes it (`saveBatch`) checks the pending favorites in the same transaction: a song downloaded from a pending favorite's video is that song whatever its tags say, any other is when `libraryFinder` would match the two (Core's `settlePendingFavorites`). It gets the star then, which syncs like any other, and the pending favorite is cleared, so a star taken off by hand later stays off. A pending favorite never shows in Favorites until it's starred. Importing Liked Songs again keeps each liked song pending once. The import never takes a star away. With it off, no star changes and nothing is kept pending.
  - **Import & Download.** Imports exactly as Import does, and as soon as each playlist has been read and filled, its downloads join the queue as its own Download Playlist would queue them: the songs the Library doesn't have, in playlist order, sharing jobs already on their way (`runYtPlaylistDownloadAction`, Core's `ytPlaylistDownload`, on the playlist as it now stands). A playlist whose read fails queues nothing, and Try Again on its page imports it without downloading (Core's `ytLibraryReadFailed` drops the choice). The choice is each playlist's own (`YtLibraryRead.download`): a playlist refreshed by a later Import while still in the batch takes that Import's choice. Import alone downloads nothing.
  - **Kept on the phone.** The batch is kept in the key-value store ("ytLibraryBatch", never synced; Core's `writeYtLibraryBatch`, `readYtLibraryBatch`), each playlist with its source, its Wave playlist, its name, whether its read stops at the cap (Liked Songs' doesn't) whether it stars (Liked Songs' with Also add to Favorites on, so a resumed read or Try Again still stars; a playlist kept without it stars nothing) and whether it downloads (Import & Download's, so the playlists read after Wave starts again still queue their downloads; a playlist kept without it downloads nothing; one read and filled stays, filled, until its downloads are queued, so Wave stopping in between queues them, after its import is done, as the batch resumes: Core's `ytLibraryDownloadsToQueue`), added as it's made; a playlist refreshed while still in the batch leaves its old place and is read once, in the new order. A read still running when its playlist is refreshed or deleted gives its answer to nothing: no songs, no stars, and the newer read stays as it was (each read carries a token while Wave runs, Core's `isYtLibraryReadQueued`), so a playlist is never filled twice. If Android stops Wave partway through, the playlists not read yet are read once Wave starts again with all-files access, after the first screen shows its content, in the same order; one being read when Wave stopped is read again first (Core's `nextYtLibraryRead`). A playlist leaves the batch once it's read, its songs then going in as a link import's do, or once it's deleted, here or on another phone (`pruneYtLibraryBatch`, as each run of the batch starts; one deleted while the batch runs is read, and its link import ends at once).
  - **Offline, or YT Music off.** A read that finds Wave offline or YT Music turned off pauses the batch (Core's `ytLibraryReadFailed`): that playlist and the rest stay waiting, in order, with no line on their pages, and the batch goes on when Wave starts again, comes back to the foreground or imports again, from the playlist it paused at.
  - **A failed read.** If a playlist's read fails otherwise (refused, an answer Wave can't read), the playlist stays, empty, and the rest of the batch goes on. Its page says "Couldn't read it online" under its length, with **Try Again** while YT Music is on (`YtLibraryReadLine.tsx`, Core's `ytLibraryReadView`). Try Again reads it again at once and fills it as the batch would have (Liked Songs whole, and starred when the switch was on); it reads "Reading…", not tappable, until the read ends, and another failure, offline included, leaves the line as it was. A failed playlist is kept in the batch, so the line is still there after Wave starts again, but the batch doesn't read it again on its own.
  - **Importing again.** Wave remembers which YouTube Music playlist each playlist From YT Music made came from (its id; `LM` for Liked Songs), in the key-value store ("ytLibraryOrigins", Core's `readYtLibraryOrigins`, `writeYtLibraryOrigins`). It stays on this phone: the Wave account doesn't sync it, so on another phone the playlist arrives without its origin, shows no Imported label there, and a new import there only falls back to Keep Both by name. Ticking a row marked Imported refreshes that playlist with a fresh copy, as Replace does: the old playlist keeps its name and is emptied at once (an import still filling it ends), then filled again from the source in its turn, and no second playlist is made (Core's `planYtLibraryImport`). Deleting the playlist forgets its origin, so its row is ticked and unmarked again; one deleted on another phone is forgotten when the picker next opens (`liveYtLibraryOrigins`). A playlist whose origin is gone by Import is made again, as a new one.
  - **What is sent.** The library page's browse requests (and each ticked playlist's id when it's read), with the session, as every signed-in YouTube Music request goes. Nothing of the Library goes out. Wave asks for the library page only when the picker opens.
- **From Spotify** shows whenever YT Music is on, signed in to Spotify or not. Signed out (or with a session that expired), it opens Spotify's sign-in Settings › Accounts uses (see Spotify account), then the picker; leaving the sign-in opens nothing, and a sign-in that fails says why. The picker is From YT Music's sheet, titled From Spotify, with the same Cancel, Select All, Import and Import & Download, ticks, Imported label, spinner, Try Again and Sign In Again (`YtLibraryPicker.tsx`). It lists **Liked Songs** first, always, then the playlists in the account's library in Spotify's order, the ones it made and the ones it follows alike (GraphQL `libraryV3`, playlists only, folders flattened, 50 a page; Core's `readSpotifyLibrary`, `spotifyLibraryPicker`). Folders, playlists Spotify no longer has and Your Episodes are left out. Each row shows the cover and the name; only Liked Songs has "N songs", as only its count comes with the library. There's no Also add to Favorites: the songs the Library lacks go in unsearched, with nothing to star yet. A failed read says why ("Couldn't read your Spotify library. No answer online.", "Spotify changed something. Try again after updating Wave.") with Try Again, and a session Spotify no longer accepts offers Sign In Again (Core's `spotifyLibraryFailure`).

A name already in use asks Replace or Keep Both first, as the M3U import does (Replace empties the old playlist). The songs go in in the source's order: a song the Library has (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover mixes decide) as the Library's, a YouTube Music playlist's other songs as YT Music entries that stream and can be downloaded later (see YT Music, Playlists), a Spotify playlist's other songs as unsearched entries, searched on YouTube Music when they're about to play or by the background fill (below; see YT Music, Unsearched entries), and a Spotify local file with no artist as an unavailable entry in its place (see YT Music, Unavailable entries). A playlist of only Library songs is an ordinary playlist. On Spotify that is usually a re-release under another Spotify id with the same title, artists, album and length.

What the page shows is Core's (`playlistImportView`).

An import a version from before kept going on, waiting or stopped ends once Wave starts with all-files access: the songs it had found stay as they were found, the songs it hadn't searched go in as unsearched entries in their places, and a song it gave the playlist already isn't given again. An import kept from before says it ended as Wave started.
  - **Background fill.** While Wave runs, on screen or playing in the background, it searches the unsearched entries nobody has played or downloaded, so an imported playlist settles over time and its count line ("2,451 songs · N unavailable") becomes right. It has no notification and no service of its own, and stops when Android stops Wave; in the app it starts after the first screen shows its content. Core decides which song goes next, when it waits and what a failure does (`backgroundFill.ts`: `nextFillStep`, `fillAnswered`); the app runs it in `src/library/backgroundFill.ts`.
    - **Pace.** One search a second (`FILL_PACE_MS`), as the fill, the lowest asker in the search allowance: it takes none while the last 15 minutes already hold 800 searches, so 200 stay free for playing and downloads (see YT Music, Search allowance). A 2,451-song playlist settles at about one song a second until the window holds 800, then as the window frees.
    - **Downloads first.** It searches nothing while any download is queued or downloading (Core's `queuedDownloads` over the download journal), gives up a turn it was still waiting for when one starts, and goes on once the queue is empty. With YT Music off it searches nothing and looks again a minute later (`FILL_LOOK_AGAIN_MS`).
    - **Order.** The playlist opened or played from last, top to bottom, then the other playlists with unsearched entries, the most recently opened or played first; a playlist never opened or played comes after those, the newest first. Opening a playlist moves the fill to it at once (a playlist imported since the fill last read the playlists is read then). "Played from" is the play history's (`selectPlaylistsLastPlayed`); "opened" counts while Wave runs. The fill reads every playlist's unsearched entries in one query (`selectUnsearchedEntries`), drops each song it settles from what it read, and reads them again at most every 15 seconds while the playlists change.
    - **Answers.** Found and not found settle the entry as playing does (`searchUnsearchedSong`: a YT Music entry in its place in every playlist, or an unavailable entry). No notice is shown: nobody was waiting for it. The playlist page shows nothing while the fill runs, only the count line changing.
    - **Failures.** A failed search leaves the entry unsearched and puts it at the back of its playlist's line, asked again no sooner than a minute later (`FILL_RETRY_MS`), so a song whose search keeps failing never becomes unavailable and never stops the others. Offline, the entry keeps its place and the fill looks again a minute later. Only several different searches refused in a row (`FILL_REFUSALS`, 3) pause the fill, with growing pauses: 2, 5, 10 and 20 seconds, then 1, 2, 5, 10 and 15 minutes, 15 minutes again after that, each further refusal before an answer pausing it longer (`FILL_PAUSES_MS`). An answer ends the run. Each pause writes a line to the app log (tag `WaveSearch`, Core's `fillPauseLine`): "The background fill pauses for 5 s: YouTube Music refused 4 different searches in a row; it goes on at 13:14:09 UTC".
- **Spotify playlists.** The card's heading says "Import from Spotify". Wave reads the playlist as `docs/spotify-playlists.md` (#156) recommends, keyless and without an account (Core's `readSpotifyPlaylist`). It loads the playlist's embed page, which says whether the playlist is public and gives its name, cover, first 100 songs and an anonymous token. With that token it then reads every song (and its album's cover) through the web player's own `fetchPlaylist` query, 500 a page. A short link is followed first (Core's `spotifyShortLinkTarget`), and one that leads to a song or a profile says so.
  - **Limits.** `fetchPlaylist` is an internal Spotify API: it can change without notice, and its persisted-query hash is a constant in Core. When that call fails (a changed hash answers 412, a refused token 401, or Spotify throttles), the card uses the embed page's songs instead, which stop at 100, and says the playlist goes on past them. Neither source gives the ISRC, so songs are matched by title, artists and length. `fetchPlaylist` has no song limit of Wave's: a 10,000-song playlist is read whole, 20 pages. A private, deleted or unknown playlist says "This playlist doesn't exist or isn't public." Spotify out of reach, or an answer that isn't the playlist, gets Try Again. Using the internal API goes against Spotify's User Guidelines on automated access. The owner accepted that on #156, as YT Music's `youtubei` API already stands.
  - **Finding the songs.** Each song is looked for in the Library before Import (`libraryFinder`, as Search and Discover mixes decide). A song the Library doesn't have goes in unsearched, and is searched when it's needed or by the background fill (see YT Music, Unsearched entries, and Background fill below): one YouTube Music Songs search for its title and artists. Wave takes only the first result that is the same recording, by the rules downloads use (Core's `spotifyYtMatch`). The normalized title must be the same once guest credits are set aside ("(feat. B)", Spotify's "(with B)", or a note naming only the song's artists). Each side's main artist must be credited on the other, with credits split as the Library splits them and "The" ignored. The edition notes must be the same, and so must any guests. Both lengths must be known and within 3 seconds. So a live version, a remix or another edit doesn't count, while a remaster note, an album's edition ("Deluxe") or a soundtrack's "From …" note doesn't tell recordings apart. A song with no match becomes an unavailable entry ("Not found online") in its place. A local file with no artist is never searched and goes in unavailable at once ("Local file on Spotify"). A song listed twice goes in once, so it's searched once. Every search waits its turn in the search allowance every automatic search shares (see YT Music, Search allowance); the Library's songs are never searched, so they spend none of it.
  - **Rate limits, measured from a PC on 2026-10-14**, when imports still searched every song. Four searches at once: a 495-song playlist took 85 seconds, about 6 searches a second, with no refusals. YouTube Music began refusing about one search in eight (HTTP 403, never more than three in a row) only after about 2,300 searches in 15 minutes, and still refused a few for over half an hour after. During that time every refused search went through within three tries. Under that throttling the 495-song playlist still finished, in 188 seconds, with 40 of 513 searches refused and all recovered. 97% of that playlist's songs were found. That is why the allowance is 1,000 in any 15 minutes, and why a 2,451-song import, which used to wait about 12 minutes near song 920, now searches nothing. YouTube Music playlist imports search nothing either and keep their own cap (`readYtPlaylist`).
  - **The card and Import.** Once read, the card shows the cover, the name and "N songs · M in your Library", as for YouTube Music, with Spotify's own count under it when some songs are listed twice or some items aren't songs ("2,451 songs on Spotify · 21 listed twice go in once"); nothing is searched. Import makes the playlist whole at once, in Spotify's order: the Library's songs as its tracks and the others unsearched (a local file with no artist unavailable), so the playlist counts as many songs as the card did. There is no progress or summary card over the screen: the playlist page shows the songs listed twice, if any (above).

Reading a Spotify playlist sends Spotify the playlist id, the page asked for and the token Spotify itself gave (and a short link's code, to follow it); Import sends YouTube Music nothing, and each song the Library doesn't have is searched later, when it's needed: its title and artists. Matching against the Library happens on the phone. An unsearched or unavailable entry's cover is asked of Spotify's image server when it shows, as a YT Music entry's is asked of YouTube's.

- **Sharing to Wave.** Wave is listed in Android's share sheet for shared text and links, and for nothing else (a `SEND` intent filter for `text/plain` on the main activity, from `android.intentFilters` in `app.config.ts`). Wave also publishes an **Import to Wave** sharing shortcut, with the icon that is on, so the share sheet (Android's and Samsung's) shows it in the direct-share row at the top for shared text, without "..." or "see other apps"; another app's own share panel, such as YouTube Music's, still lists whatever that app picks. It is published each time Wave's screen opens, and again after the launcher icon is switched (Android drops a shortcut whose launcher entry is turned off); it keeps one id, so publishing again never adds a second. From Android 13 it isn't in the long-press menu on Wave's icon; before that Android has no way to keep it out. Picking it sends the same share as picking Wave from the app list. Sharing a playlist from the YouTube Music app, which shares a bare link, from the Spotify app (an `open.spotify.com` link or a `spotify.link` short link, with or without words around it), or from a browser, which adds the page title around it, opens Wave on the import card for that playlist, with Import and Cancel as from the Library. It works from a cold start and with Wave already open (the main activity is `singleTask`, so a share reaches the running Wave). Wave takes the first web address (or `spotify:` URI) in the shared text, drops the punctuation after it, and lets `recognisePlaylistLink` name the playlist (Core's `sharedTextImport`). Anything else shared (a song, an album, an artist, another site, text with no link, a bare playlist id) shows a short message saying Wave imports playlists from YouTube Music and Spotify links, and nothing is requested. ### Importing BlackPlayer stats

- Settings › Library › Import BlackPlayer Stats opens the file picker for any file type, several at once, and adds the plays in BlackPlayer's track-stat files (`BlackPlayer EX Track Stat Autosave.bpstat`) to the play history. Each file is a cumulative snapshot: one song per line, `playCount;unknown;title;artist;album;path;addedAtMs;lastPlayedAtMs`, UTF-8. The second field isn't understood and is ignored. A title, album or path can hold a `;` of its own.
- **Dating.** Snapshots are ordered by content: a snapshot's time is its latest last play. The plays a song gained between two consecutive snapshots are spread evenly over that interval (from when it was added, if later); those already in the earliest are spread from when it was added to that snapshot's time. No play is dated after the song's last play. A song's count is the highest any snapshot gave, so a lower count later adds nothing.
- **Matching.** A row is the Library song at its path (case-insensitively, as Android's storage is), else the one with its title and artist as `libraryFinder` compares a YT Music song's, so a moved file still matches. A row has no length, so it can't tell recordings apart: when several Library songs have its title and artist it takes the only one on its album, and otherwise counts as not in the Library rather than guess.
- **Imported plays** are counted plays, listened for the song's length, with no play context, and marked `imported_from = 'blackplayer'`. They count in Stats, Home's Most Played, the artist page's Stats block and play counts like Wave's own. An import adds to each song only the plays beyond those it already has from BlackPlayer, dated after the latest of them, so importing the same or newer snapshots again only adds what's new. They are never the play Wave picks back up after a restart.
- The summary (the M3U import's card) says how many new plays and songs were imported and lists the songs that matched nothing in the Library, and the files that weren't track stats. Core's `blackPlayer.ts` decides all of this.

### Recap

- **Periods.** A monthly Recap covers one calendar month in the phone's local time, from its first instant to its last. It is offered once the month has ended (never the month under way) and only when the month holds at least 3 hours of counted listening; below that, that month has no Recap. A yearly Recap covers one calendar year the same way and needs at least 20 hours of counted listening: the current year's is out on 1 December, covering the year so far, keeps updating through 31 December and is final from 1 January. Past months and years stay available. Core's `recap.ts` decides.
- **Data.** Everything comes from the play history on the phone, counted plays only. Minutes listened are the whole minutes listened over the period's counted plays, deleted songs included. The top songs are the period's 5 songs with the most counted plays, still in the Library, ranked as Stats ranks (`rankPlays`). The top artists (5, credited by track artist) and the top album are ranked the same way, deleted songs' plays included. The top genres are up to 5 genre families (Genre mixes' families, `genreFamily`) ranked by the counted plays of the month's songs still in the Library, a song in n families adding 1/n of its plays to each, each with its share of the plays of songs with a genre; songs without a genre are left out. Habits are the local hour of day and weekday with the most counted listening (the earliest on a tie), the longest run of consecutive days with a counted play within the period, and the artists discovered: those whose first counted play ever falls in the period. The store reads the history's counted listening summed per quarter hour (`countedListening`); a quarter hour never straddles a local month, since every time zone's offset is a whole number of them.
- **Entry.** Stats opens with a "Recaps" row, a tile for every available Recap, newest first with each year's tile ("Year in music") ahead of its months, scrolling sideways; it's hidden while there's none. Home opens with a card when a new Recap is ready ("Your Recap is ready", or "Your year in music is ready"), above its sections, until that Recap is opened from anywhere. Only the Recaps out most recently count as new: on 1 December the year, then November once the year is opened; an older one left unopened isn't announced once a newer one is out. The opened Recaps are kept on this phone (they don't sync).
- **Viewer.** Opening a Recap plays its stories full screen over everything (`/recap/<yyyy-mm>`, `/recap/<yyyy>` for a year): slides that advance on their own every 6 seconds, with a progress bar per slide at the top, the Recap's title and a close button under them. The summary card, the last slide, doesn't move on by itself: its bar fills and it stays until a tap, a swipe down or the close button ends the stories (Core's `recapAutoForward`). A tap on the left third goes back (on the first slide it restarts it), anywhere else forward; past the last slide the stories close. Holding pauses, letting go resumes. Dragging down closes (past 120 dp or flung), otherwise the slide springs back. Each slide's lines rise in one after another (a fade with reduce motion).
- **Music.** If nothing is playing when a Recap opens, it plays in the background: the song the slide on screen features, else the Recap's #1 song (Core's `recapSong`; a top-songs slide features its #1, and a song without a file plays nothing). It plays on its own, in place of the queue, with no "New queue playing" toast, and never counts: it isn't play history, a counted play or in Stats, so watching a Recap never adds to what it reports (Core's `trackPlays` skips the Recap's context). Holding a slide pauses it too. Leaving the Recap stops it and brings back the queue from before, paused where it was. If something is playing when the Recap opens, Wave doesn't interrupt it and the Recap plays no music.
- **Slides**, in order: minutes listened ("In September 2026 you listened for … minutes", "In 2026 …" for a year); then the top songs, #1 highlighted with its cover and play count, #2 to #5 listed under it with their plays (left out when none of the month's songs is in the Library any more); then the top artists, #1 with a large photo and its plays, #2 to #5 under it; the top album with its cover, album artist and plays; the top genres, #1 large with its share of plays, the others listed with theirs; habits ("You listened most on Fridays around 10 PM", and the longest streak in days); and the artists discovered (how many, then the 5 most played of them). A slide with nothing to show (no genres, nothing discovered, its artists or album gone from the Library) is skipped. The last slide is always the summary card, as Wrapped's: the #1 artist's photo, the top 5 artists and top 5 songs side by side, minutes listened and the top genre (each list or the genre left out when there's none).
- **Sharing.** Each slide has Share, beside the close button. It pauses the stories (and their music) and opens a preview of the slide as a 9:16 image (1080 × 1920 px) over them: the slide on its colour, standing still, with the Recap's title at the top and the Wave mark at the bottom. Under it are the lyrics card's destinations, with the same rules (see "Sharing lyrics"): Instagram Stories (the image as the sticker over the slide's colour), WhatsApp, Save image and More. Close or Back returns to the slide, and the stories resume. The image is drawn once the slide's images have loaded; inside it, covers and artist photos are React Native's `Image` (`share/imageCapture.ts`). The file is named `Wave - Recap <title> - <yyyyMMdd-HHmmss>.png`.

### Settings

- Settings (the gear on Home) is a short list of rows. Each opens a page of its own with the usual push animation, and back returns to the list. The pages, in order (Core's `SETTINGS_PAGES`; each also opens from `wave://settings/<page>`):
  - **Library**: Music folders, Rescan library (with the scan's progress) and Import BlackPlayer Stats.
  - **Appearance**: Appearance (System, Light or Dark), Theme, Glass, Motion (tap animation), Touch (long-press delay).
  - **Layout**: the options a long press opens on a screen's title or the tab bar, for whoever doesn't know the long press or finds the card cramped. Its page lists **Home** (Home's editor), **Library** (the shelf under its rows), **Playlists** (List, Grid or Large Cards, then Sort by) and **Tab bar** (the bar's editor), each a page of its own (Core's `LAYOUT_PAGES`, at `/settings/layout/<page>`). Each is the editor or choice list the long press opens, with the same rules, laid out as a page that scrolls (except while a row is dragged) rather than as a card: an editor there has no Done, as each change is saved as it's made and shows at once on the screen it shapes. A change from a long press shows there too. The long presses are unchanged.
  - **Swipe actions**: whether song rows swipe and what each direction runs (see Swipe actions).
  - **Playback**: Crossfade, Don't play explicit and Buffer.
  - **Lyrics and tags**: the switch for online lookups, the whole-Library run with its progress and Cancel, and Artist photos.
  - **Downloads**: Download in best quality (with, while songs are left, the row that offers to replace low-quality songs while it's on, or to free up space while it's off; see YT Music › Download quality), then a **Downloading** section with the songs on their way (Core's `queuedDownloads`), under a line like "2 downloading · 5 waiting" ("2 downloading · 40 saving · 5 waiting" while downloaded songs wait their turn to join the Library; `queuedDownloadCounts`): the ones downloading (or saving) first, then the waiting ones grouped by where they were asked from, each with its artwork, title, artists, the progress line its row shows elsewhere and **Cancel** (as its menu's Cancel Download; none once it's saving). Above them, the **run panel** shows how the run is going (#475): a bar, "1,234 of 2,500" (songs done of all), how many failed and how many are left, and "About 2 h left". The run is the songs queued since the queue was last empty: a song joins it when it's seen on its way, a retried failure is left again, a cancelled song leaves it, a replacement that found no better copy keeps its file and counts as done, and it resets once nothing is left (Core's `followDownloadRun`, followed from launch through every journal change whether the page is open or not, and `downloadRunProgress`). A restart isn't the run finishing: its songs done per group and its failed songs are kept in the key-value store ("downloads.run", about once a second; Core's `keptDownloadRun`), and the next launch goes on from them (`restoredDownloadRun`), its songs left read from the journal again and its pace starting over. The time left is the songs finished (done or failed) per minute over the last 10 minutes, or since the run started if later, applied to the songs left; it's hidden until 3 songs have finished over at least a minute, and while downloads are paused, in the panel and the notification alike: while YouTube is limiting downloads the panel shows that paused line with the minutes to the next try, and otherwise, with songs left and none downloading or saving, it says "Paused" (Core's `downloadTimeLeft`, `downloadsPaused`, `downloadRunPanelLine`). The panel reads the run again about once a second, not on each journal change, and renders only when what it shows changed; its bars are plain views. Each download job remembers its **origin** in the journal, so it survives a restart: the playlist, album (a YT Music album, or a Library album's Download Missing Songs) or Discover mix whose Download, Download Playlist, Download Album or Retry Failed queued it, with its name. A quality replacement run is an origin too (Quality upgrade). A song only its star asked for has **Favorites** (see A star means downloaded). A song asked for on its own (its menu, a search result, the selection bar's Download of songs, Settings › Wave Account's Download All) has none. A song a second source asks for keeps the first one's; asked for again after it ended, it takes the new source's, or keeps its own when asked with none (Settings › Downloads' Retry). The waiting songs follow in one group per origin, under a header with its name, its own bar and its run's counts ("40 of 120 · 2 failed · 80 left", Core's `downloadRunGroupLine`; "12 songs left", its songs downloading now included, for a group the run doesn't count); songs with no origin, jobs queued before downloads remembered it included, go in a group called **Songs**. Groups come in queue order, the one whose next song starts soonest first (one whose songs have all started comes before them), and songs keep their queue order within a group. A group lasts until its last song finishes, fails or is cancelled (Core's `downloadingList`). Dragging a group's header by its handle moves the whole group, its waiting songs with it, before or after another group, and dragging a song moves it within its own group (never into another), which changes the order they start in (Core's `moveDownloadGroup` / `moveDownloadInGroup`, run by the journal's `moveQueuedJobs`); a group whose songs have all started has no handle. Screen readers get Move up / Move down instead. The page is one **virtualized list**, as the Library's song lists are: the download settings above, then the songs downloading, each group's header and its waiting songs as rows of the list (Core's `downloadListRows`), then the failed songs below, all scrolling together, so only the rows around the screen are mounted however long the queue is (#447). A dragged song lifts and follows the finger while its group's other songs step aside; a dragged group lifts its header (its songs leave their place with it) and the groups it passes step aside by its height, header and songs, as before the list was virtualized. Only the rows on screen of what's passed move. What a drop or a Move up / Move down lands is Core's (`dropDownload`, among `movableGroups`), on the list as it is when it lands. Near the list's top or bottom edge a drag scrolls it. Only the rows making room for a dragged song animate; the handles are drawn with plain views rather than an icon. A song leaves for the failed list when it fails and for good once it's in the Library; with nothing on its way the section isn't there. Then the songs whose download failed (Core's `failedDownloads`), as rows of the same virtualized list (Core's `failedDownloadRows`), so only the ones on screen are mounted however many failed (#465). The section starts **collapsed**: one row saying how many failed ("640 songs failed") with **Retry All**, **Clear All** and **Show Songs**. Shown, the songs follow grouped by why they failed, in the order the reasons first come, each group under a header with the reason and how many ("This song is unavailable on YouTube Music · 640") and its own **Retry** and **Remove** for all its songs; **Hide Songs** collapses it again. Each song's row shows the artwork, title, artists, the failure line, the recorded cause when it says more than the line, **Retry**, and **Remove** ("Remove from failed downloads"), which clears that song alone, without asking, as Clear All clears every song. The failed rows are built again only when a failure comes, goes or changes (Core's `failedJobs`), not on each tick of the songs on their way, and a row renders again only when what it shows or does changed (Core's `sameFailedDownload`, which tells two replacements of one Track in different directions apart). No row of the page, queued, group or failed, draws an SVG or carries an animated style of its own: a cover with no artwork is the fill colour alone and the buttons dim while pressed (`PlainTextButton`), because drawing an SVG shape makes Reanimated re-apply its whole registry on the main thread for each layout event, which froze the page into an ANR with hundreds of failed songs (#446). The cause is the one behind the failure the row shows, in the errors' own messages rather than class names, which a release build shortens (Core's `failedDownloads`): YouTube audio of another length says both lengths (`ytAudioRejection`), and Soulseek's cause doesn't outlive the job falling back to YouTube Music, unless Soulseek's failure is the one that stands. The title wraps at large text sizes, so Retry and Remove stay visible. A failure stays listed across days and restarts until it's retried, cleared, or the song reaches the Library some other way; Clear cancels the job in the journal, which forgets cancelled jobs at its next start. Album and playlist Retry Failed are unchanged.
  - **Accounts**: YT Music, Spotify and the Wave account, with the missing songs' downloads.
  - **Developer**: Export Logs (see "App log") and Design preview.
- A row can show one line about its page, kept live. Library says how many music folders there are, or "Scanning…" while a scan runs. Lyrics and tags shows the whole-Library run's progress while it runs. Downloads says how many songs are on their way while any are, then how many failed ("2 downloading · 5 waiting · N songs failed", Core's `downloadsSummary`), and nothing when neither. It reads only counts (`queuedDownloadCounts`, `failedDownloadCount`), never building the failed or queued lists. Accounts names the YT Music, Spotify and Wave accounts the phone is signed into (Core's `accountsSummary`), from the sessions the phone holds, without asking YouTube, Spotify or Wave's server. Swipe actions says "Off" while swipes are off. The other rows show only their title.
- Each Settings row leads with its page's tile, as iOS Settings does: a white Tabler line glyph in a small rounded square of the page's own colour, no two pages sharing one (Library red books, Appearance purple palette, Layout indigo grid, Swipe actions cyan swipe, Playback pink play, Lyrics and tags orange quote, Downloads green download, Accounts blue person, Developer grey code). ### App log

- Every build records synchronous Library reads that held the JS thread for more than 16 ms (info, tag `WaveTiming`): `Read "list songs" held JS 640 ms (12,431 rows) on /(tabs)`. Core decides the budget and formats the line (`slowReadLine`, `slowReadsLine`); it names the store's existing operation, the rows returned, the duration (ms below a second, then seconds with one decimal) and the route showing. Named results such as Home's shelves count their returned rows together. Consecutive slow reads in a one-second window share one entry, separated by semicolons, each retaining its route; the window ends one second after its first slow read, so continuous work still gets reported. Export Logs, backgrounding, a memory warning or a fatal flush also writes the waiting group. Every store read goes through one timing boundary, independent of perf-build instrumentation. A fast read takes only two timestamps and a budget comparison, with no row inspection, route lookup or logging; mutations are separate. Export Logs, backgrounding, memory warnings and fatal flushes collect pending read lines before writing, through the existing batched, redacted app-log writer.
- Wave keeps a log on the phone so a friend can send what went wrong. It records unhandled JS errors and promise rejections with their stack, the errors React reports (a render error shows a screen with Try Again and Export Logs instead of closing Wave), every `console.warn` and `console.error`, and a line each time Wave starts, naming the build: `Wave 0.10.1 (build 1001, PR #438 @ 90970e3) started`, or without the label in a build that has none, such as a local or debug build (Core's `appBuildName`). A message keeps 8,000 characters at most; an object given to `console` is written as JSON, eight levels deep at most, and read only as far as those characters go, so logging a huge one costs no more than a small one (Core's `describeLogArgs`).
- It's one UTF-8 file in Wave's private storage, `files/logs/wave.log`, kept across restarts. Each entry is `<UTC time, ISO 8601 with ms> <I|W|E|F> <tag>: <message>`; a stack goes on the lines after, each indented by two spaces, so only an entry's first line starts with a time (Core's `formatLogEntry`). Native code writes the same shape to the same file (`AppLogFile`).
- Native code logs there too, from the start of every process, before JS (`NativeLog`, started at Application.onCreate). Its warnings go through `WaveLog` (`WaveLog.w(tag, message, error?)`), which writes to logcat and to the log under the same tags (`WaveDownloads`, `WaveYtPoToken`, `WaveAuto`, `WaveResume`, …), redacted the way Core's `redactSecrets` does (`AppLogEntry`, a Kotlin port to keep in step with Core). A call never waits for the file: entries queue for one writer thread of Wave's log (`AppLogWriter`), so a caller on the main thread isn't held up by JS reading or trimming the log; a fatal entry, and Export Logs, write what's queued first. Past 1,000 waiting entries, new ones are dropped and a line says how many.
- An uncaught Kotlin exception, which is also how a fatal JS error ends a release build, is written with its thread and stack before the process dies, and the next launch starts with "The previous session ended in a crash". Each launch also writes what Android recorded about each session that ended since the last launch (`ApplicationExitInfo`; only the latest on the first launch), in plain words with Android's description when it has one (`SessionRecord`): a crash Wave couldn't see itself (native), an ANR with the main thread's stack, a failed start, too many resources, a kill for low memory, a kill by a signal (its number and name), Wave exiting on its own (its status), a kill while frozen in the background, a process it depends on dying, a changed permission, a change in Wave's package state, any other end Android closed it for, and an end for a reason Android didn't record. Each line, Wave's own crash line included, says what Wave was doing at the end, from Android's importance (on screen, a foreground service when playing or downloading, visible, in the background, cached), and its PSS and RSS in MB, where Android recorded them. The previous session's line also says how many songs were downloading and waiting when the download journal was last written, if any were (`savedDownloadCounts`, read without opening the journal, and before anything else at launch, so a download service Android restarts can't rewrite it first). These lines are errors, except an end while Wave was cached (Android trimming its cached processes, "empty for 1800s", or a low-memory kill in the background): routine, it's written as information; a crash, an ANR or a failed start stays an error however Wave stood. Only the user quitting (force stop, or stopping Wave from the task manager), swiping Wave away, and a package update say nothing; a reboot leaves no record.
- It records YouTube Music's search allowance (see YT Music, Search allowance), a few lines per event and never one per search answered. Each wait for it writes a line as it starts and one as it ends (tag `WaveSearch`, Core's `searchWaitLine`): who waited (the song about to play, Search, a download, Try Again, a Discover mix, the background fill), from when until when (UTC, as the log's own times), and how many searches the last 15 minutes held. Each failed search writes who asked, the search and how it failed: offline, YT Music off, or failed (`searchFailedLine`). Each pause the background fill takes after refusals writes how long, why and when it goes on (`fillPauseLine`; see "Importing playlists from a link", Background fill). Native code writes what it saw of the same failure on a line of its own through `WaveLog` (tag `WaveYtMusic`, `YtFailure.failedLine`): the HTTP status YouTube answered, or the exception's class and message, so a failure in the background is logged however slow JS is. Every failed Songs or Artists search gets that native line, a search typed in Search included. A link import has a line of its own (see "Importing playlists from a link", In the app log).
- It says what Home's mixes and the Magic Queue cost on the network (tag `WaveNetwork`, info), so speed-ups and data savings can be measured on the phone: one line per Artist mixes or Genre mixes build, the day's automatic one ("build") or one the owner pulled for ("refresh"), which read only the phone (every host "none"), one per seed the harvest reads ("Mix harvest of Muse (143 songs)", or "no radio"; what it counts is what the harvest's mobile data adds up, `networkUseBytes`) and one per Wi-Fi run for the similar artists' pages it reads for its seeds ("Mix harvest similar artists"), and one per Magic Queue batch that refilled its candidates ("Magic Queue radio (52 songs)", or "no radio"; a batch the catalog serves says nothing). The line gives what the run made (its mixes or candidates, or that it failed, timed out or was stopped), how long it took, and per host (YouTube Music, Deezer) the requests it made and the bytes it received: `Artist mixes build (4 mixes) took 34.2 s: YouTube Music 18 requests, 612.4 KB; Deezer 41 requests, 270.1 KB`, a host it didn't ask saying "none" (Core's `networkUseLine`; KB and MB of 1,024 bytes). YouTube Music's count natively, on the wire: each run starts a meter (`startNetworkMeter`), its artist searches, artist pages and radio pages carry it, and an OkHttp network interceptor counts each request that goes out (a retry or redirect too) and the bytes of its answer as received, still compressed, then the run takes what it counted (`takeNetworkUse`; `NetworkMeters`). Runs at the same time (Artist and Genre mixes build together) each count only their own; a request still going when its run ends counts nowhere. Deezer's lookups count in JS, each answer by its Content-Length, else (Android unzips a gzipped answer and drops it) the size of the body as read (Core's `answerBytes`). Each run of the Wi-Fi pre-download (see YT Music, Streaming) writes one line under the same tag with the songs it fetched, those the cache had already, those that failed and, when it stopped (off Wi-Fi, battery low, out of time) or went offline, how many were left, then its time and the audio bytes it read from the network: `Wi-Fi pre-download (12 songs fetched, 28 cached already) took 84.2 s: 54.30 MB from the network` (`PreDownloadRun.line`, natively, as the job runs without JS).
- A Now Playing artist or album link that has not opened its destination after one second writes a warning under `WaveTiming`, naming the tap and why: `link not ready`, `close taken over by Back`, `close taken over by chevron`, `close taken over by card`, `target gone`, or `unknown` (Core's `lostTapLine`). Each tap has its own deadline, including rapid consecutive links. An accepted screen push or artist-choice card answers the link; revealing the artist page already underneath is an ordinary sheet close. Sheet completion never owns link navigation, so a close taking over a link is a regression. Lost taps also contribute to the speed summary.
- Every build records slow navigation under `WaveTiming` (info), through the same batched, redacted app-log writer. Core's one `TIMING_BUDGETS` table sets response/first-frame budgets of 100/150 ms for tabs, pushes and pops, 50 ms for Now Playing's spring start, 100 ms for Queue and artist-choice cards, 200 ms to a link's first frame, and 400 ms to draw phone-local screen content. At or under budget a tap writes nothing. A slow tap names its kind, source and destination, response, first frame and settle times, overlapping scan/import/download/mix-harvest/Magic Queue work, offline state and the longest observed JS block. A slow screen names its route, first frame and main content times and any reads contributed by read timing (none until available). A screen settles only after its transition completes and its main content is drawn; optional work can subscribe to that signal without following renders. When Wave leaves the screen, at most once every five minutes and only with taps in the window, a speed summary names the version and commit, counts and median/p90 responses per tap kind, median/p90 content time per screen and the slow taps; read/frame/hitch timing can contribute to the same window. No timing counts renders or rows, and no idle frame counter runs.
- Lyrics, genre and tag failures are logged with their cause: the original error's class and message, found through Effect's wrappers (a `TagWriteFailed` or `LookupFailed` around an `UnknownError` around the native or network error) and cut to 1,000 characters (Core's `failureCause`, `describeCause`). A failed Fix Song lookup or lyrics search says which service failed (LRCLIB, Deezer or YT Music) and, for an HTTP error, its status (`lookupFailureLine`). A song whose tags couldn't be filled in says which step failed (reading the file, writing the tags, or re-indexing it), whether the file was there when the write ran with its size (read before writing, as a failed write may cut it short), its extension, and where the song came from: a quality replacement (upgrade or downgrade), an ordinary download, a Fix Song run or the Lyrics view's search (`tagWriteFailureLine`; the native writer resolves the step and the file's facts, `TagWriteFailure`, `TagWriteFile`). A re-index that fails once the tags are written, natively or as JS saves the entry, isn't a failed write: the tags stay, the line names the "reindex" step, the outcome is as written, and the next scan brings the index up to date.
- Android ending a foreground service on its time limit (Android 15+ limits data-sync services: the playlist import, downloads, and lyrics and genre services) writes a line with how long the service ran (`ServiceTimeout`).
- It says where a download's time went (tag `WaveDownloads`): each YouTube attempt, when it ends, writes the bytes it received, its time and speed, and why it ended (finished, failed with its reason, cancelled for silence, out of time, cancelled, stopped with the service); a finished Soulseek download writes its peer, size and time. A transfer the journal cancels for going quiet (or a peer that never started or kept the job queued) writes a line naming the job and the attempt, which also becomes the job's cause, and a job that uses up its six attempts fails with "Out of attempts (6 of 6); last error: …" as its cause. The download service writes a line when it starts (and when Android restarts it after Wave was closed), when it stops, with how long it ran and why (its queue is empty, the last song was cancelled, Android's time limit, Android stopped it), and when Android refuses to start or restart it (`DownloadServiceLines`). About once a minute while it runs, a line says what the download journal's writes cost since the last one: how many, how many were whole (a new snapshot), the bytes written and the time spent writing (`JournalWrites`), and another how many files went to the media scanner in how many batches (`MediaScanBatch`). Failures are warnings; finished transfers and the service's life are info.
- It says how much memory Wave uses while downloads run, so a slowdown or a kill during a big queue can be matched to memory pressure. The download service's start and stop lines end with Wave's memory: the Java heap used and its limit, the native heap and the process's PSS, in MB (binary, as Android sizes a heap; `MemoryUse`, read by `AndroidMemory`). While the service runs, an info line about once a minute (tag `WaveDownloads`, "Memory while downloading") gives the downloading, saving and waiting counts and the same numbers, plus the Hermes heap when JS answers within half a second (native code asks each JS runtime through `WaveAppLog`, `JsHeap`); without JS running, the line goes without it. One tick serves every once-a-minute line of the service (`MinuteLines`), so each comes at most once a minute whatever the size of the queue, the first a minute after the service starts. Android's memory warnings (`onTrimMemory`, `onLowMemory`) are warnings (tag `WaveMemory`), one per level change, with the level and the same numbers (`MemoryWarnings`); Wave leaving the screen isn't a warning, but the next warning after it counts as a change. At every warning JS writes the entries it's holding at once, so the lines before a kill reach the file. Since Android 14, Android no longer sends the running levels, `MODERATE` or `COMPLETE`, nor calls `onLowMemory` (only `UI_HIDDEN` and `BACKGROUND` arrive), so on the phone those warnings never come while a queue runs (`adb shell am send-trim-memory` still sends them). The download service's minute tick therefore checks for itself (`MemoryWarnings.checked`): when Android counts the phone as low on memory (`ActivityManager.MemoryInfo.lowMemory`) or Wave's Java heap is past 85% of its limit, a warning (tag `WaveMemory`, "Memory is short: …" with the reasons and the same numbers) is written once per change of reasons, and JS writes what it holds at every check while memory stays short.
- While Wave is on screen (one of its activities started), a watchdog pings the main thread and the JS thread once a second (`FreezeWatchdog`); in the background, background playback included, it doesn't run; one that doesn't answer for 3 s is written as a freeze, with the route showing and, for the main thread, its stack, then again with how long it was stuck once it answers. A tick that comes 3 s late means Android paused the whole process, which isn't a freeze.
- Release builds keep Wave's exception class names, and source files and line numbers, through R8, so the log's stacks stay readable. Settings › Developer has debug-build triggers for a Kotlin crash and a 5 s JS freeze.
- Past 2 MiB the oldest entries go, whole, until it's down to 1.5 MiB (Core's `trimLog`).
- Passwords, tokens, cookies, Authorization headers, bearer tokens, JWTs and a URL's password never reach it (Core's `redactSecrets`); song titles, artists and paths can.
- Entries wait in memory and are written together every two seconds, off the JS thread, and whenever Wave leaves the screen or Android warns that memory is short; a fatal error is written at once.
- Settings › Developer › Export Logs shares it through the share sheet as `wave-log-<date>-<version>.txt`, which starts with Wave's version, build and build label (see Build and CI) as the "started" line names them, Android's version, the phone and its font scale (Core's `logHeader`).

- **Editable Home.** Home's sections are a catalogue Core knows (`homeSections.ts`), each with an id and a title, in a default order: Discover, Recently added, Recently played, Most played, Most played albums, Most played artists, Favorites, Stats (the entry to Library › Stats) and Listening overview. **Discover** is one section of three rows in a fixed order (Core's `DISCOVER_ROWS`): Magic Queue, Artist mixes and Genre mixes, each under a small Footnote graphite label, one level below the section's title. A row with nothing to show behaves as its section did: skeletons while its mixes build, gone when empty, and the mix rows are gone with YT Music off while the Magic Queue's stays; Discover is gone only when all three are. Each row keeps its pull right to refresh, tiles, taps, long-presses and crossfades. Discover's header carries no share: on its right, an adjustments icon (Tabler's horizontal sliders, as large and as thin as Home's gear, a 48dp target) opens Discover's level card beside it, titled "Discover", with New music ("Mostly familiar · 20% new" to "All new · 100% new"), Genre faithfulness and Popularity (see Artist mixes): one set of values for all three rows, kept on the phone (not synced). A move applies at once: both mix rows re-blend or re-rank offline, and the Magic Queue applies it from its next batch. With YT Music off the icon is dimmed and opens nothing, and TalkBack reads it as "Discover settings, needs YT Music". Home shows the owner's arrangement of it. Long-pressing the Home title opens Home's editor, a card anchored to the title. It lists the sections Home shows, in order, each with a handle to drag it up or down and a button to remove it, then under **Not on Home** the sections Home doesn't show, each with a button that adds it at the bottom. **Done** saves; Back or a tap outside leaves Home as it was. Screen readers get Move up, Move down and Remove on each shown section. The arrangement is kept across restarts on this phone (it doesn't sync). With none saved, Home shows the whole catalogue in its order, which is Home as it was before it could be edited. Reading a saved arrangement drops ids this Wave doesn't know (the Magic Queue, Artist mixes, Genre mixes and Discover mixes sections, now Discover's rows, among them), and a section added to the catalogue after the owner saved shows after the section it follows in the catalogue, on top when none does, while the sections the owner removed stay off. So after the update every Home shows Discover first, whatever its earlier arrangement, and from then on the owner moves or hides it like any other section. A section with nothing to show stays hidden. With nothing played yet, the line asking for a first play sits where the first section that plays fill would be. If the owner removes every section, Home says a long press on Home adds them back. The Settings button stays on Home's title line. Other screens can draw a section of the catalogue by id, with the same data Home shows.
- **Editable tab bar.** The tab bar holds Home and any of Library, Search and the Library's sections (Playlists, Artists, Albums, Songs, Genres, Favorites), each a tab of its own, up to five tabs in all. Home is always there and always first, since Settings opens from it. Search, whenever it's on, keeps its own round pill at the end; the other tabs share Home's pill in the owner's order. Long-pressing any tab opens the bar's editor, a card anchored to the bar: Home first (nothing to press), the tabs after it, each with a handle to drag it up or down and a button to remove it, Search with a button to remove it, then under **Not in the tab bar** the tabs it doesn't hold, each with a button that adds it at the end, before Search (Search, added, takes its pill). While five tabs are on, the add buttons are off. **Done** saves; Back or a tap outside leaves the bar as it was. Screen readers get Move up, Move down and Remove on each tab that can move. A section tab opens its section with its own stack: what it opens (an artist, an album…) is pushed inside the tab, under the tab bar, and stays open while another tab shows; the same screens opened from the Library, Home or Search are pushed over the tabs (Core's `detailPath`). Removing the tab that's showing goes to Home. The bar is a synced preference, stored as its tabs in order. It replaced Settings' Shortcut tab option: until the bar is first saved, it's the bar that option left (Home, Library, the chosen section, Search; Playlists for an install that never chose, no section for None), so with nothing changed the bar is Home, Library, Playlists, Search. Core's `tabBar.ts` holds the rules: `parseTabBar` reads a saved bar (or the old choice) and holds it to them, leaving out unknown tabs and duplicates and keeping no more than five, and `editTabBar` applies the editor's changes.
- **Library shelf.** Under its entry rows, the Library shows one shelf from Home's catalogue, the same section Home draws with the same data, and hidden as there while it has nothing to show. Long-pressing the Library title opens a card of the shelves, by their Home titles: Discover (its three rows, as on Home; a Library that showed Artist mixes or Discover mixes shows it, Core's `storedHomeSectionId`), Recently added, Recently played, Most played albums, Most played artists and Favorites, then **None**, which leaves the Library at its rows. Most played, Stats and Listening overview stay on Home. The choice applies at once, is kept across restarts on this phone (it doesn't sync) and defaults to Recently added; anything stored that isn't one of the card's choices reads as the default (Core's `libraryShelf.ts`). The shelf draws its sections with Home's kind cards (see Kind cards).
- Glass is reserved for what floats: the tab bar, the mini player, the toast, and dialogs and menus, which are floating glass cards (rounded, sized to their content, clear of the system bars) over a lightly dimmed app. A card opened next to what was pressed (a long-press menu, a title's card, an editor opened from the tab bar) sits beside it, a margin clear of the status bar and the gesture or navigation bar; it may draw over the floating chrome (tab bar, mini player, selection bar), as a centred card may, so a row near the bottom gets its menu below it when it fits there; content taller than that room scrolls, with the card's heading and Done staying put (Core's `placeOverlayCard`). They are drawn at the app root, not in a separate window, so the blur shows the app behind live. Below Android 12 (no blur) the cards are an opaque surface. The row, tile or button a card was opened from stays clear of the dimming, washed with the accent, while that card (or one following up on it) is open. The highlight covers it as it sits at rest (not shrunk by its press feedback), square like the row, and never over the floating chrome: a tile partly under the mini player or tab bar shows only its visible part (Core's `visibleHighlight`). The Library | Online toggle is the exception: it is one control, so a long press on either segment highlights the whole toggle in its own pill shape, as tall as the drawn pill (not its taller touch targets) with half that height as its radius, and the card anchors to it. So a long-press menu card has no heading: it starts straight at its play cards. Every long-press menu starts with one row of equal-width cards for its play actions, each an icon over a short label in the menu's ink (Core's `menuLayout`): a collection (album, artist, playlist, genre, Discover mix, YT Music album, Library entry, YT Music artist) has **Play**, **Play Next** and **Add to Queue**, and **Shuffle** is the first row of its list; Play and Shuffle play its songs as its page's Play and Shuffle do. A song (the Library's, a YT Music song, an unsearched entry) has Play Next and Add to Queue only, since a tap already plays it; a missing song and an unavailable entry have no cards. Play Next and Add to Queue are never also list rows. A menu's choices run from the edge nearest the finger that opened it: opened below the row, the cards sit at its top (under any heading) and the list runs down from them; opened above it, the cards sit at its bottom edge and the list runs up from them, Shuffle (or the first entry) next to the cards, while any heading stays at the top. Screen readers keep the logical order (heading, cards, entries). A YT Music artist row (Search) long-presses to such a menu too. Each list entry is a list row: its action's Tabler line icon, the same in every menu that offers it, then its label in the list text size (Body, as a song row's title). Delete, Remove from Library, Delete Playlist and the selection bar's Delete Playlists are in the destructive colour. A Library entry's menu (Songs, Albums…) is the exception: its heading counts the songs it acts on, which the row doesn't say. A menu opened with nothing highlighted (no press, or what was pressed is off screen or entirely under the floating chrome) still starts with the item it acts on: artwork, title and subtitle, ruled off from the entries (Core's `cardShowsHeading`).
- Now Playing background: gradient from the artwork palette, animated on track change; controls use the app palette.

### Build and CI

- GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) on every PR and on `main` and `ti`. Checks installs, typechecks, lints, checks formatting and runs the tests; on PRs it typechecks and tests only the packages the PR changed and the ones that depend on them. wave-native's Kotlin unit tests run in their own job beside the APK build, and on PRs only when something they test changed. The Android APK job builds the APK I install on the phone, uploads it as a build artifact and publishes it to a prerelease (`pr-<n>`, or `latest` for `main`). On a PR it skips the build when nothing in the APK changed, and repacks when only JS changed since a kept full build with the same native side: it swaps the new JS bundle into that APK without Gradle (`scripts/ci/repack.ts`). Anything else builds in full, and so do pushes to `main` and stable release tags. What counts as changed is the change rule in `scripts/ci/changes.ts`. Each job posts a sticky PR comment with its step durations; the Android APK one says whether the build was skipped, repacked or full. Every APK CI builds carries a build label beside its version, which the app log shows: `PR #<n> @ <short commit>` for a PR build, and the branch or tag and the short commit for a push (`ti @ 1a2b3c4`, `main @ 1a2b3c4`), the commit being the one the APK is built from (`scripts/ci/build-label.ts`). Metro inlines it into the JS bundle as `EXPO_PUBLIC_WAVE_BUILD_LABEL`, so a repack carries the label of the run that repacked it, not the kept APK's, and the APK job checks that the packaged bundle holds this run's label. A build made off CI (local or debug) has none.
- **Releases.** Wave's version lives only in the `version` field of `apps/mobile/package.json` (`major.minor.patch`, optionally ending in `-ti`, minor and patch under 100), which `app.config.ts` reads. Android's versionCode follows from it (major × 10000 + minor × 100 + patch, so 0.5.0 is 500), so each version installs over the one before and keeps the Library and settings. Nobody bumps or tags a version by hand: every push to `main` or `ti` that changes the app releases the next one (`ti`'s end in `-ti`, see Test branch and prereleases). The CI's Version job works it out first (`scripts/ci/next-version.ts`): the highest version on any `v*` tag (a `-ti` tag counts by its base), bumped by the PRs merged since the channel's previous tag. The PRs that count are the ones that channel's patch notes list (see Patch notes), so a PR that changes only CI, docs or specs doesn't, less any whose merge commit a `v*` tag of either channel already contains: the shared counter already bumped for it, so a `feat/` PR released on `ti` doesn't bump the minor again when `ti` reaches `main`. If any of them has a branch starting with `feat/`, the bump is minor and the patch goes back to 0 (0.5.3 becomes 0.6.0); otherwise it's a patch bump. The Version job's log names the PR that made the bump minor, or says it's a patch. Major is never automatic. Both branches count from these same tags. A version in `package.json` already above every tag is released as it is, which is how a major bump made by hand ships; with no version tag yet, the one in `package.json` is the first. A patch bump that would reach 100 fails the Version job and asks for a minor or major bump by hand, and a minor bump that would reach 100 asks for a major one. The Version job writes the version into `apps/mobile/package.json`, commits that file alone to the pushed branch as the workflow bot ("Release Wave <version>") and pushes it before anything is checked, together with its `v<version>` tag in one atomic push: both land or neither does (`scripts/ci/bump-version.sh`). The tag takes the number as soon as it's picked, so the next bump on either branch counts past it, even while this run is still building. The rest of the run checks, builds and publishes that commit, so its APK reports the new version. The workflow's token pushes the commit and the tag, so neither starts another run. A push whose changes since its branch's previous release (on `main`, the newest stable tag in its history) are only CI, docs or specs, by the change rule that also leaves PRs out of the patch notes (`scripts/ci/changes.ts`), or only the `version` in `apps/mobile/package.json` (the other branch's version bump merged in), makes no commit and no release: it publishes only the rolling `latest` (or `ti-latest`) build, and the Version job's log says why. Runs on `main` or `ti` don't cancel each other: one runs at a time, the newest push waits, and a push replaced while it waits is released by the next run, since that counts from the previous release. Since both branches share the counter, the Version job also runs one at a time across `main` and `ti`, so a `main` push and a `ti` push close together never pick the same number. If the branch moves on, or the tag is taken, before the bump is pushed, the Version job starts over from the new tip, so the bump commit always lands on top; a push rejected for any other reason fails the job with git's error. A bump whose run fails after the push keeps its tag but has no release: re-running the run's failed jobs publishes it, and so does re-running all of them, since a branch tip that already carries its channel's tag is released as it is, with nothing pushed; the next push to the branch releases the next number. The release job then publishes, on that tag, the GitHub Release "Wave <version>" with that run's `wave-<version>.apk` and patch notes. A stable one becomes the repo's latest release; a `-ti` one is a prerelease and leaves Latest alone. If `v<version>` has since been moved to another commit, the release job publishes nothing, with a warning. Pushing `v<version>` by hand still works (`git tag v0.5.0 <commit> && git push origin v0.5.0`): CI checks the tag against the app's version and that it's on its release branch before building (a mismatch fails the Checks job and says which version the app is at), then publishes as above. Releases are signed with the same debug keystore as every other build. In `.github/workflows/ci.yml` a hand-pushed tag's run has `IS_RELEASE == 'true'`, and a stable release always builds fully. Release publication waits for the APK, native tests and patch notes; when the Patch notes job fails, the release job fails instead of publishing without them. PR builds (`pr-<n>`) and main's rolling `latest` prerelease are unchanged.
- **Test branch and prereleases.** Daily test work targets `ti`, created from `main`. Its app version ends in `-ti`, starting at `0.5.1-ti` (Android versionName `0.5.1-ti`, versionCode 501). Every push to `ti` that changes the app releases the next test version, the way `main` releases (see Releases). The next `ti` version comes from the same counter as `main`'s: the highest version on any `v*` tag, stable or `-ti` (counted by its base), bumped by the PRs merged since `ti`'s previous release as `main`'s is, with the `-ti` suffix. So the sequence runs like 0.5.4-ti, 0.5.5, 0.5.6-ti, and every new APK from either channel has a higher versionCode than the ones before it and installs over them. The bot commits "Release Wave 0.5.6-ti" to `ti`'s `apps/mobile/package.json` and tags it `v0.5.6-ti` in the same push, and the run publishes the GitHub prerelease "Wave 0.5.6-ti" with `wave-0.5.6-ti.apk`, without replacing stable Latest. `ti`'s previous release, which its changes and patch notes count from, is the newest `-ti` tag in its history, or the newest stable one before `ti`'s first. A push that changes only CI, docs or specs since then, or only the version (merging `main` brings its bot bump), makes no commit and no release. Merging `ti` into `main` releases `main`'s next number, a patch bump unless it brings a `feat/` PR no tag holds yet; it doesn't promote ti's version. A conflict on the version line during that merge can be resolved either way, because CI overwrites the version. Pushes to `ti` also publish the rolling `ti-latest` prerelease with `wave-ti.apk`. A hand-pushed `v<version>-ti` tag still publishes too, as long as it names the app version and belongs to `ti`. ti keeps fast builds: ti pushes, ti tags and PRs may repack an exact-key kept APK. Since tags cannot read ti's branch-scoped cache, successful full CI pushes on ti also upload the APK and debug keystore as a `kept-<native key>` artifact for 30 days. A ti tag or PR can restore that artifact only from this repository's successful CI push on ti (`scripts/ci/kept-apk.ts`); a miss or failed download builds fully. The key includes native inputs and Metro resources but not the app version, so a version bump alone doesn't need a full build. The repack writes the run's versionCode and versionName (prebuild's, from `android/app/build.gradle`) into the kept APK's compiled `AndroidManifest.xml` and into Expo's embedded app config, `assets/app.config`, then signs the APK again (`scripts/ci/apk-version.ts`). Main and ti save Gradle, Metro and C++ caches; PRs and tags only read them. Both stable and ti releases wait for native tests before publication. The `-ti` suffix doesn't change the numeric versionCode; the shared counter keeps each release's above the last.
- **Patch notes.** Each release's notes say what's new and what's fixed since the previous version: the PRs merged since the previous tag in that release channel, under "New" and "Fixes" (an empty section is left out), one line per PR, the PR's title linked to the PR. A PR is a fix when its branch starts with `fix/` or an issue it closes has the `bug` label. PRs that change only CI, docs or specs (by the change rule, `scripts/ci/changes.ts`) aren't listed, nor is a feature branch's PR into `main` when its tickets' PRs into that branch are. So PR titles are written as one line a user understands. Stable notes exclude `-ti` tags; ti notes use the previous `-ti` tag, falling back to the last stable tag for the first ti version. The version bump counts from the same previous tag (`scripts/ci/previous-tag.ts` picks it for both). The PRs come from GitHub's search (`scripts/ci/merged-prs.sh`), which lags merges by a minute or so; a commit on the branch's first-parent line that no PR it found was merged by is asked about directly (`commits/<sha>/pulls`), so the PR a push just merged still counts. With no previous version tag (including 0.5.0), notes are the hand-written `docs/first-release-notes.md`. `scripts/ci/patch-notes.sh` builds them (`patch-notes.ts` decides); the CI's Patch notes job shows them in every run's summary, and a run that releases publishes them. A push that releases already has its own tag on its version bump, so its notes leave that tag out and count from the previous tag of its channel, the same as a hand-pushed tag's.

## Testing Decisions

- A good test exercises a module only through its public interface and asserts observable behaviour (resulting queue, counted/not counted, search results order, reconciled changes) — never internals. Tests should survive refactors of the implementation.
- **Primary seam — Core.** Every decision-heavy rule lives in Core and is tested there with `node:test`, run directly by Node's TypeScript type-stripping. Because Core has zero dependencies, these tests run anywhere, including environments without package-registry access. This is where most tests live.
- **Library store.** Migrations and the Home/play-count queries are tested in CI against a real SQLite database through Drizzle, seeded with fixture data.
- **Sync service.** Its routes are tested in CI against a local libSQL database. Merging is Core's, so the rules themselves are tested there.
- **Native module.** Kotlin JVM unit tests in CI for tag reading (small fixture audio files per format) and for scan diffing; Media3's test utilities for the player command/event mapping where practical.
- **UI.** No automated UI tests in v1. Each ticket has acceptance criteria verified manually on the S24+ using the CI-built APK.
- Prior art: none yet (new repository). The first ticket establishes the test setup for each seam.

## Out of Scope

- v2 features: equalizer and presets, ReplayGain, tag editor, smart playlists, M3U export, Last.fm scrobbling, home-screen widgets, album artwork download, statistics/metrics screens (v1 only records the data).
- iOS, desktop, web.
- Streaming services other than YouTube Music (see YT Music), cloud libraries, network shares. Spotify is only read for playlists to import, and nothing streams from it.
- Uploading audio to the Wave account, a web or desktop view of it, sharing between accounts, and signing in with anything but Google.
- Play Store distribution.
- Chromecast / casting; Android Auto beyond what Media3 provides for free. Android Auto is one of the two primary listening scenes, so a browsable Auto library is the first v2 candidate.
- Localisation (English only).

## Further Notes

- **Glass on Android** (no longer a default material; see Visual design) **was the main technical risk.** Sampling the native views behind a surface is not straightforward in React Native Skia. The first UI work is a spike comparing approaches (for example, Android `RenderEffect` blur with an AGSL refraction shader versus a Skia snapshot of the backdrop) for visual quality and 120 Hz performance on the S24+. Acceptable fallback if refraction is too costly: blur + tint + specular rim highlight.
- **Track identity** on the phone is the file path, with moved/renamed files matched by Core's reconciliation so history survives. Between phones it's the recording key (see Wave account and sync).
- **Development environment constraint:** the agent session working on this repository cannot reach the npm registry or the Android/Gradle repositories. GitHub Actions is the build and test environment of record; Core's zero-dependency design keeps its tests runnable inside the agent session as well.
- Library sizes in the tens of thousands of tracks are the design target for scan batching, list virtualisation and query indexes.
